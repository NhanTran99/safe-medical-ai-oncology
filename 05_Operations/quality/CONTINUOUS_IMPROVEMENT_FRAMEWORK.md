# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the Continuous Improvement Framework of the Safe Medical AI System.

The framework establishes a governed organizational learning process that continuously improves the system using operational evidence while preserving patient safety, governance integrity, scientific reproducibility, and technology independence.

---

# 2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Continuous Improvement follows the principles of:

- Governance before optimization
- Evidence-driven improvement
- Organizational learning
- Controlled change management
- Continuous quality improvement
- Technology independence

---

# 3. ROLE

The Continuous Improvement Framework is responsible for:

- collecting improvement opportunities
- governing improvement proposals
- coordinating approval workflows
- preserving organizational learning
- verifying improvement outcomes
- supporting future release planning

The framework is not responsible for:

- direct implementation
- deployment execution
- runtime decision making
- autonomous system optimization

---

# 4. IMPROVEMENT PHILOSOPHY

Continuous Improvement represents governed organizational learning.

System improvements shall originate from governed operational evidence rather than subjective observations or uncontrolled experimentation.

---

# 5. IMPROVEMENT INPUTS

Governed improvement inputs include:

- Monitoring Framework
- Observability Framework
- Incident Management
- System Evaluation
- Clinical Review
- User Feedback

Additional governed evidence sources may be introduced through formal amendments.

---

# 6. IMPROVEMENT CATEGORIES

Every Improvement Proposal shall belong to one primary category.

Standard categories include:

- Clinical Knowledge Improvement
- Architecture Improvement
- Safety Improvement
- Governance Improvement
- Operational Improvement

Categories support planning, ownership, and reporting.

---

# 7. IMPROVEMENT PROPOSAL

Every improvement shall begin with a standardized Improvement Proposal.

Minimum proposal contents include:

- Problem Statement
- Supporting Evidence
- Proposed Change
- Expected Impact
- Risk Assessment

No implementation shall begin without an approved proposal.

---

# 8. EVIDENCE-BASED IMPROVEMENT

Every Improvement Proposal shall reference governed evidence.

Evidence may originate from:

- Monitoring Records
- Evaluation Records
- Incident Records
- Clinical Reviews
- User Feedback

Unsupported improvements shall not proceed to approval.

---

# 9. IMPROVEMENT APPROVAL WORKFLOW

The governed workflow is:

Improvement Proposal

↓

Clinical Review

↓

Governance Review

↓

Approval

↓

Implementation

↓

Evaluation

↓

Release

Workflow stages shall remain fully traceable.

---

# 10. IMPROVEMENT RECORD

Every approved improvement shall generate a standardized Improvement Record.

Minimum metadata include:

- Improvement Identifier
- Proposal Identifier
- Improvement Category
- Current Status
- Approval Date
- Release Version

Additional metadata may be introduced through governed amendments.

---

# 11. OUTCOME VERIFICATION

Following deployment, every improvement shall undergo outcome verification.

Verification shall determine:

- whether intended objectives were achieved
- whether regression occurred
- whether new incidents emerged

Outcome verification supports evidence-based organizational learning.

---

# 12. ORGANIZATIONAL KNOWLEDGE PRESERVATION

Knowledge generated throughout the improvement lifecycle shall be preserved.

Preserved knowledge includes:

- rationale
- decisions
- lessons learned
- successful practices
- unsuccessful approaches

Knowledge preservation supports future governance and onboarding.

---

# 13. CONTINUOUS LEARNING LOOP

Continuous Improvement follows the governed lifecycle:

Monitoring

↓

Observability

↓

Incident Management

↓

System Evaluation

↓

Improvement Proposal

↓

Approval

↓

Implementation

↓

Release

↓

Monitoring

The lifecycle establishes continuous organizational learning while preserving governance integrity.

---

# 14. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

The Continuous Improvement Framework follows:

- Governance-first improvement
- Evidence-driven decision making
- Organizational learning
- Continuous quality improvement
- Traceability
- Separation of concerns
- Technology independence
