# Evidence Classification

## Established / directly source-supported

- Chronic atrophic gastritis is a recognized gastric-cancer risk/precursor condition.
- H. pylori chronic infection can lead to atrophic gastritis and is a cause of noncardia gastric carcinoma.
- Autoimmune gastritis is another pathway to gastric atrophy.
- Atrophic gastritis can be associated with intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia, pernicious anemia, and gastric cancer.
- Low serum pepsinogen can indicate gastric atrophy, but standard abnormal cutoffs are not established in the cited NCI evidence.
- Atrophic gastritis is identified among higher-risk groups in the NCI screening evidence.

## Context-dependent

- The magnitude of individual risk.
- Whether a particular patient requires endoscopic screening/follow-up.
- How much risk reduction is achieved after H. pylori eradication in a particular patient.
- Interpretation of pepsinogen in the presence of H. pylori eradication or PPI use.
- The degree of progression from atrophy to later precursor states.

## Not established universally

- One universal absolute-risk estimate.
- One universal screening interval.
- One universal pepsinogen cutoff.
- Complete reversal of established gastric atrophy.
- Guaranteed prevention of gastric cancer after H. pylori eradication.
