# Clinical Question

> **What is atrophic gastritis, how does it arise, and why is it clinically important for gastric-cancer risk?**

# Educational Intent

Provide a patient-facing, evidence-grounded explanation of chronic atrophic gastritis as a gastric mucosal risk/precursor condition, including its major etiologic pathways, relationship to gastric carcinogenesis, and key limitations around risk interpretation and screening.

# Evidence Notes

## Atrophic Gastritis as a Risk/Precursor Condition

NCI identifies chronic atrophic gastritis as a precursor condition and gastric-cancer risk factor. The evidence therefore supports describing atrophic gastritis as clinically important for cancer prevention and risk recognition, but not as cancer itself. NCI also identifies older adults with atrophic gastritis or pernicious anemia among groups with elevated risk. 

## H. pylori Pathway

NCI states that chronic H. pylori infection produces long-lasting gastric inflammation and can lead to atrophic gastritis. The NCI prevention evidence further identifies H. pylori as a cause of noncardia gastric carcinoma. Detailed testing and eradication decisions are delegated to PP-0012/PP-0160/PP-0161.

## Autoimmune Pathway

The ACS source identifies autoimmune gastritis as a distinct cause of atrophic gastritis. Autoimmune gastric disease can also be associated with pernicious anemia. Detailed autoimmune work-up and B12 management are outside PP-0165.

## Gastric Carcinogenesis

The supplied sources support a conceptual sequence linking chronic gastric injury/atrophy with intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia and gastric cancer. This is a carcinogenesis model, not a deterministic sequence for every patient.

## Serum Pepsinogen

NCI states that low serum pepsinogen levels indicate atrophic gastritis and may identify presumed precursors for intestinal-type gastric cancer. NCI also states that there are no standard cutoff values and that H. pylori eradication and PPI use can change pepsinogen levels. Therefore PP-0165 should treat pepsinogen as a limited marker, not a universal diagnostic rule.

## Screening Context

NCI identifies older people with atrophic gastritis or pernicious anemia as potential higher-risk groups for gastric-cancer screening. The package therefore acknowledges screening relevance but delegates screening algorithms to PP-0170 and downstream screening packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

1. Atrophic gastritis is a chronic gastric mucosal condition.
2. It is associated with increased gastric-cancer risk.
3. H. pylori and autoimmune gastritis are important etiologic pathways.
4. Atrophic gastritis can coexist with or precede intestinal metaplasia and dysplasia.
5. Increased risk does not mean inevitable cancer.
6. Pernicious anemia is an important autoimmune-gastric context.
7. Serum pepsinogen can indicate atrophic gastritis but has important limitations.
8. Screening decisions depend on clinical context and are not defined by this package.
9. H. pylori eradication can reduce gastric-cancer risk, but residual risk may remain after established mucosal damage.

# Evidence Consistency Review

The supplied NCI and ACS materials are consistent in describing atrophic gastritis as a chronic mucosal risk/precursor state and in linking it to H. pylori, autoimmune gastritis, intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia and gastric cancer.

The package deliberately does not convert population-level risk evidence into an individualized absolute risk.

Where the source identifies uncertainty—such as pepsinogen cutoffs, screening benefit, or residual risk—the uncertainty is preserved.

# Evidence Gaps

The supplied sources do not support:

- a universal absolute gastric-cancer risk for every patient with atrophic gastritis;
- a universal endoscopic surveillance interval;
- a universal pepsinogen cutoff;
- a universal diagnostic algorithm for autoimmune gastritis;
- a universal claim that established gastric atrophy is completely reversible;
- a universal claim that H. pylori eradication eliminates future gastric-cancer risk.
