# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what PM Evidence Codes are.
- Understand that PM stands for **Pathogenic Moderate**.
- Recognize that PM Evidence Codes form one evidence-code family within the ACMG/AMP framework.
- Understand that each PM evidence code represents a different type of **moderate scientific evidence** supporting pathogenicity.
- Recognize that PM evidence contributes to—but does not determine—the final variant classification.
- Feel more confident understanding professional genetic variant reports.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Are PM Evidence Codes?

## Patient Explanation

**PM Evidence Codes** are standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant may cause disease.

The letters **PM** stand for **Pathogenic Moderate**, meaning they represent **moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity**.

---

## Clinical Importance

PM Evidence Codes provide standardized categories of moderate scientific evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- PM
- Moderate evidence
- ACMG Evidence Codes

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Are There Multiple PM Codes?

## Patient Explanation

The PM family contains **multiple individual evidence codes**, such as **PM1, PM2, PM3, PM4, PM5, and PM6**.

Each code represents **a different kind of moderate scientific evidence**.

---

## Clinical Importance

Different scientific observations require different evidence codes to ensure consistent interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- PM family
- Multiple evidence categories
- Standardization

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Do PM Evidence Codes Mean a Variant Is Pathogenic?

## Patient Explanation

No.

A PM evidence code **does not mean** that a genetic variant has already been proven to cause disease.

Instead, it contributes **one category of moderate evidence** that specialists evaluate together with many other types of evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification always depends on the complete body of available evidence.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Variant classification
- Professional interpretation

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Are PM Evidence Codes Useful?

## Patient Explanation

PM Evidence Codes help laboratories use the same international system when interpreting genetic variants.

This makes interpretation more consistent and helps different laboratories communicate scientific findings more clearly.

---

## Clinical Importance

Standardized evidence coding improves reproducibility and consistency in clinical genomics.

---

## Key Concepts

- Standardization
- Consistency
- Clinical genomics

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Do Patients Need to Learn Every PM Code?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Patients are **not expected to memorize** individual PM evidence codes.

The important point is understanding that genetics professionals use a structured international framework to evaluate scientific evidence carefully before reaching a conclusion.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding the interpretation process is generally more valuable than remembering individual evidence-code names.

---

## Key Concepts

- Professional interpretation

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

If a report contains a PM evidence code, the variant has already been confirmed as disease-causing.

**Fact**

PM evidence represents **moderate supporting evidence** only. Final classification always depends on the combination of multiple evidence categories evaluated by qualified professionals.

---

# Key Messages

- PM stands for **Pathogenic Moderate**.
- PM Evidence Codes represent moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- The PM family contains multiple individual evidence codes.
- PM evidence contributes to—but does not determine—the final variant classification.
- Professional interpretation always combines many evidence categories.
