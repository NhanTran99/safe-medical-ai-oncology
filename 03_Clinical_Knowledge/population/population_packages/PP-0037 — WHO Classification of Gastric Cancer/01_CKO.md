# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what the WHO Classification of Gastric Cancer is.
- Understand why pathologists use the WHO Classification.
- Recognize several common WHO histologic subtypes.
- Understand that the WHO Classification is determined by microscopic examination of tumor tissue.
- Understand that the WHO Classification may appear in the pathology report.
- Understand that the WHO Classification does not, by itself, determine prognosis or treatment.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is the WHO Classification?

## Patient Explanation

The World Health Organization (WHO) has developed an international system for classifying many diseases, including gastric cancer.

For stomach cancer, the WHO Classification describes the type of cancer based on how the tumor looks under the microscope.

This system helps pathologists around the world use consistent terminology.

---

## Clinical Importance

WHO Classification provides a standardized international language for describing gastric cancer histology.

---

## Key Concepts

- WHO Classification is an international classification system.
- It is based on microscopic examination.
- It helps standardize pathology reporting.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Do Doctors Use the WHO Classification?

## Patient Explanation

Doctors and pathologists use the WHO Classification to describe the type of gastric cancer in a consistent way.

Using the same classification system helps healthcare professionals communicate clearly and compare medical information across hospitals and countries.

---

## Clinical Importance

Standardized terminology improves diagnostic consistency and communication.

---

## Key Concepts

- WHO Classification standardizes pathology reports.
- It improves communication among healthcare professionals.
- It supports consistent diagnosis.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Types Might Be Listed?

## Patient Explanation

Your pathology report may mention names such as:

- **Tubular adenocarcinoma**
- **Papillary adenocarcinoma**
- **Poorly cohesive carcinoma**
- **Mucinous adenocarcinoma**
- **Mixed adenocarcinoma**

These names describe how the cancer cells appear under the microscope.

This Population Package introduces these names only.

Each subtype is explained separately in future Population Packages.

---

## Clinical Importance

WHO Classification includes several recognized histologic subtypes of gastric adenocarcinoma.

---

## Key Concepts

- Several WHO histologic subtypes exist.
- The names describe microscopic appearance.
- Additional explanation requires separate Population Packages.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — How Is the WHO Classification Determined?

## Patient Explanation

The WHO Classification cannot be determined from symptoms, blood tests or imaging studies.

Instead, it is assigned after a pathologist examines tissue obtained during a biopsy or surgery.

The result may then appear in your pathology report.

---

## Clinical Importance

Microscopic examination remains essential for WHO histologic classification.

---

## Key Concepts

- Tissue examination is required.
- A pathologist assigns the classification.
- The result becomes part of the pathology report.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Is the WHO Classification the Same as the Lauren Classification?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Both systems describe gastric cancer, but they answer different questions.

The **Lauren Classification** groups gastric cancer into intestinal, diffuse and mixed types.

The **WHO Classification** describes specific histologic subtypes based on microscopic appearance.

Doctors may use one or both systems when describing a tumor.

---

## Clinical Importance

Different histologic classification systems provide complementary information.

---

## Key Concepts

- WHO and Lauren are different classification systems.
- Both are used in pathology.
- They describe different aspects of the tumor.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Does the WHO Classification Decide My Treatment?

## Patient Explanation

No.

The WHO Classification is only one part of the information doctors use.

Treatment decisions also depend on:

- cancer stage;
- pathology findings;
- biomarker testing;
- imaging studies;
- your overall health;
- and your treatment goals.

Your healthcare team considers all of these factors together.

---

## Clinical Importance

Modern gastric cancer management integrates histology with multiple clinical and pathological factors.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

If I know my WHO subtype, I already know how my cancer will be treated.

**Fact**

The WHO Classification provides important pathological information, but treatment decisions always consider many additional clinical factors.

---

# Key Messages

- WHO Classification is an international system used to classify gastric cancer.
- It is determined by examining tumor tissue under the microscope.
- Several WHO histologic subtypes may appear in a pathology report.
- WHO Classification and Lauren Classification are different systems.
- WHO Classification is different from cancer staging.
- WHO Classification alone does not determine prognosis or treatment.
