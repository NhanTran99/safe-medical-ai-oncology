# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is histologic differentiation and what should patients understand about it?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of histologic differentiation, including what it represents, how it is evaluated from pathological examination, how it relates to tumor grade, and why it is one component of the overall pathological assessment.

This Population Package introduces histologic differentiation without discussing detailed microscopic grading criteria, prognostic implications or treatment recommendations.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Histologic differentiation reflects the degree to which cancer cells resemble normal stomach cells.
- Histologic differentiation is determined through microscopic examination by a pathologist.
- Common descriptive categories include well differentiated, moderately differentiated and poorly differentiated.
- Histologic differentiation is commonly documented in pathology reports.
- Histologic differentiation is related to, but not identical with, tumor grade.
- Histologic differentiation contributes to pathological assessment but does not independently determine prognosis or treatment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition and purpose of histologic differentiation;
- pathological assessment through microscopic examination;
- commonly used differentiation terminology;
- distinction between differentiation, histologic type and TNM staging;
- integration of differentiation into the overall pathological evaluation.

Differences between references are limited to terminology, educational emphasis and explanatory depth.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
