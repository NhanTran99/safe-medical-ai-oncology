# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

1.  Explain in plain language what HER2-targeted therapy means in
    gastric adenocarcinoma.
2.  Understand why HER2 testing is a prerequisite for HER2-directed
    treatment.
3.  Understand the difference between HER2-targeted therapy and
    chemotherapy.
4.  Understand that HER2-targeted therapy includes more than one
    therapeutic approach.
5.  Recognize trastuzumab as the foundational HER2-directed treatment
    paradigm.
6.  Recognize trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) as an important subsequent
    HER2-directed treatment approach.
7.  Understand at a high level why trastuzumab may be combined with
    chemotherapy and, in selected settings, immunotherapy.
8.  Understand why HER2 positivity does not by itself determine the
    complete treatment plan.
9.  Understand the major treatment benefits and limitations without
    interpreting the information as an individualized prescription.
10. Recognize important safety concepts, including cardiac
    considerations with trastuzumab and ILD/pneumonitis considerations
    with T-DXd.
11. Understand that response is not guaranteed and that HER2-directed
    treatment can be followed by progression or resistance.
12. Know which questions to bring to the oncology team.

# Clinical Knowledge Blocks

## Block 1 --- What Is HER2?

HER2 is a protein that can be present at increased levels in some
gastric and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinomas.

HER2 belongs to a family of receptor proteins involved in cell
signaling. When HER2 is increased or otherwise clinically relevant in a
tumor, it can become a treatment target.

The important patient-facing point is not that HER2 is "good" or "bad."

The important point is:

> **HER2 status can help identify whether HER2-directed treatment may be
> relevant.**

This makes HER2 different from a finding that is used only to describe
the tumor.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 2 --- Why HER2 Matters for Treatment

A patient's cancer can be characterized in several ways.

One of those characteristics is HER2 status.

The clinical sequence is:

**Tumor**

→ **HER2 assessment**

→ **HER2 result**

→ **clinical interpretation**

→ **possible HER2-targeted treatment**

The HER2 result is therefore a bridge between tumor biology and
treatment selection.

However, the bridge is not automatic.

A HER2 result must be interpreted together with:

-   disease setting;
-   treatment line;
-   previous treatment;
-   other biomarkers;
-   performance status;
-   comorbidities;
-   toxicity considerations;
-   current guideline recommendations;
-   patient preferences.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 3 --- HER2 Testing Comes Before HER2-targeted Treatment

HER2-targeted therapy is intended for cancers in which the relevant HER2
status has been established using an appropriate testing framework.

This creates an important distinction:

### HER2 Testing

Answers:

> **"What is the HER2 status of this tumor?"**

### HER2-targeted Therapy

Answers:

> **"What does a clinically relevant HER2 result mean for treatment?"**

Detailed HER2 testing is therefore an upstream knowledge package.

PP-0210 should not reproduce the laboratory testing algorithm.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 4 --- HER2-positive Does Not Mean "One Automatic Drug"

A patient may hear:

> "Your tumor is HER2-positive."

It is understandable to think that this immediately determines one
treatment.

In practice, HER2 positivity opens a treatment pathway.

The actual treatment approach depends on:

-   whether the disease is advanced/recurrent/metastatic or in another
    clinical setting;
-   whether this is initial or subsequent systemic therapy;
-   whether the patient has already received trastuzumab;
-   whether chemotherapy is appropriate;
-   whether immunotherapy is appropriate;
-   other biomarkers;
-   previous toxicities;
-   overall health;
-   current evidence and guidelines.

Therefore:

> **HER2-positive is a treatment-relevant finding, not a complete
> treatment prescription.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 5 --- HER2-positive Versus HER2-negative

A clinically meaningful HER2-positive result can identify a patient for
whom HER2-directed treatment is relevant.

A HER2-negative result generally means that the HER2-directed pathway
being discussed is not the applicable treatment pathway.

But:

> **HER2-negative does not mean "there are no treatment options."**

Other biomarkers and clinical factors may identify other treatment
approaches.

This package therefore does not equate HER2 status with the totality of
gastric-cancer treatment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 6 --- Predictive Versus Prognostic Meaning

A biomarker can have different clinical roles.

### Prognostic

A prognostic factor is associated with disease behavior or expected
outcome.

### Predictive

A predictive factor helps identify the likelihood of benefit from a
particular treatment.

For PP-0210, the treatment-relevant role of HER2 is the key concept.

The package should not imply that HER2 positivity automatically means a
particular prognosis.

The supplied guideline framework distinguishes treatment relevance from
prognostic interpretation.

Therefore:

> **HER2 is important here primarily because it can identify a
> treatment-relevant biological feature.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 7 --- What Is HER2-targeted Therapy?

HER2-targeted therapy means treatment designed to exploit HER2 as a
biological target.

At a high level:

**HER2-positive cancer cell**

→ **HER2-directed agent recognizes or acts through HER2**

→ **HER2-associated tumor biology is therapeutically targeted**

→ **tumor growth may be reduced or tumor cells may be damaged**

Different HER2-directed technologies can use this target in different
ways.

This is why HER2-targeted therapy is a treatment family rather than one
single drug.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 8 --- Trastuzumab: The Foundational HER2-directed Approach

Trastuzumab is a monoclonal antibody directed against HER2.

In advanced HER2-positive gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma, trastuzumab has an
established role in combination with chemotherapy in appropriate
first-line treatment settings.

The pivotal ToGA trial established the clinical proof of concept for
adding trastuzumab to chemotherapy.

The important patient-facing message is:

> **Trastuzumab showed that identifying HER2-positive disease could lead
> to a treatment strategy that improves outcomes compared with
> chemotherapy alone in the appropriate population.**

Detailed trastuzumab information belongs to the dedicated Trastuzumab
package.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 9 --- Why Trastuzumab Is Commonly Combined With Chemotherapy

Targeted therapy and chemotherapy do not necessarily compete with one
another.

In HER2-positive advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma, trastuzumab may be
integrated with a chemotherapy backbone.

Conceptually:

**Chemotherapy**

→ attacks cancer cells through cytotoxic mechanisms.

**Trastuzumab**

→ adds HER2-directed biological targeting.

The combination can therefore attack the cancer through complementary
mechanisms.

This does not mean that every patient should receive the same
chemotherapy combination.

Detailed chemotherapy selection belongs to chemotherapy and
systemic-treatment packages.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 10 --- ToGA: Why It Matters

ToGA was a pivotal randomized phase III trial in HER2-positive advanced
gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

Patients received chemotherapy with or without trastuzumab.

The NCI evidence synthesis reports median overall survival of
approximately:

-   13.8 months with trastuzumab plus chemotherapy;
-   11.1 months with chemotherapy alone;

with a hazard ratio of 0.74.

The important educational meaning is:

> **The trial provided high-level evidence that adding HER2-directed
> treatment to chemotherapy can improve outcomes in appropriately
> selected HER2-positive advanced disease.**

The numerical result should not be interpreted as an individualized
prediction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 11 --- HER2-targeted Therapy Can Also Interact With Immunotherapy

The modern HER2 treatment landscape is not limited to:

**HER2 + chemotherapy.**

KEYNOTE-811 evaluated pembrolizumab with trastuzumab and chemotherapy in
HER2-positive advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

This established an important concept:

> **In selected patients, HER2-directed treatment can be incorporated
> into a broader systemic-treatment combination that also includes
> immunotherapy.**

PP-0210 introduces this concept.

Detailed immunotherapy selection belongs to the immunotherapy branch.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 12 --- Trastuzumab Deruxtecan (T-DXd)

T-DXd is an antibody-drug conjugate.

Conceptually, it combines:

1.  a HER2-directed antibody component;
2.  a linker;
3.  a cytotoxic payload.

The antibody component helps direct the drug toward HER2-expressing
cancer cells.

The payload then contributes cytotoxic activity.

This illustrates an important principle:

> **The same biological target can be exploited by different therapeutic
> technologies.**

Detailed ADC biology and T-DXd pharmacology belong downstream.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 13 --- Why T-DXd Matters

T-DXd is particularly important because it provides a subsequent
HER2-directed treatment strategy for selected patients after prior
therapy.

The supplied NCCN evidence includes DESTINY-Gastric01 and
DESTINY-Gastric02.

DESTINY-Gastric01 showed clinically meaningful activity and improved
outcomes compared with physician's-choice chemotherapy in previously
treated HER2-positive advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

The patient-facing message is:

> **Progression after one HER2-directed treatment does not necessarily
> mean that every HER2-directed option has disappeared.**

Whether another HER2-directed treatment is appropriate depends on
treatment history and the current clinical situation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 14 --- DESTINY-Gastric01: Benefit and Limitations

DESTINY-Gastric01 compared T-DXd with physician's-choice chemotherapy
after prior treatment that included trastuzumab.

The NCCN evidence reports:

-   ORR approximately 40.5% with T-DXd versus 11% with
    physician's-choice chemotherapy;
-   median OS approximately 12.5 versus 8.4 months;
-   median PFS approximately 5.6 versus 3.5 months.

These are trial-level results.

They are not a promise that an individual patient will experience the
same outcome.

The trial also demonstrated clinically important toxicity, including
hematologic adverse events and ILD/pneumonitis.

Therefore:

> **A treatment can have meaningful efficacy and still carry important
> risks.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 15 --- DESTINY-Gastric02

DESTINY-Gastric02 provides complementary evidence for T-DXd in Western
patients with unresectable/metastatic HER2-positive gastric/GEJ
adenocarcinoma after progression on or after first-line therapy.

The study reinforces several principles:

-   treatment line matters;
-   previous HER2-directed treatment matters;
-   patient population matters;
-   response is variable;
-   subsequent HER2-directed treatment is not automatically appropriate
    for every patient.

Detailed trial analysis belongs to the dedicated trial package.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 16 --- What Happens if Treatment Works?

When HER2-targeted therapy is effective, the cancer may:

-   shrink;
-   stop growing for a period;
-   produce symptom improvement;
-   delay progression;
-   contribute to longer survival.

The exact benefit depends on the treatment, patient population, disease
setting, and individual response.

Treatment is therefore monitored over time.

A response does not necessarily mean the cancer has been permanently
eliminated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 17 --- What Happens if Treatment Does Not Work?

A cancer may fail to respond from the beginning.

This can be described conceptually as primary resistance or lack of
sensitivity.

Alternatively, a cancer may initially respond and later begin growing
again.

This can occur because cancer biology can change over time.

The important patient-facing message is:

> **HER2 positivity does not guarantee durable treatment response.**

Detailed mechanisms of HER2 resistance belong to the dedicated
resistance package.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 18 --- HER2 Heterogeneity

Tumors are not always biologically uniform.

Different parts of the same tumor may show different levels of HER2
expression.

This is one reason why a HER2 result should be interpreted in the
context of:

-   the specimen tested;
-   the testing method;
-   tumor sampling;
-   the overall clinical situation.

PP-0210 introduces this limitation.

Detailed heterogeneity and testing interpretation belong to the HER2
Testing package.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 19 --- Why a HER2 Result Is Not the Whole Treatment Plan

A treatment decision may require information about:

-   HER2;
-   PD-L1;
-   MSI/MMR;
-   CLDN18.2;
-   other molecular findings where relevant;
-   disease stage;
-   prior treatment;
-   performance status;
-   organ function;
-   patient goals.

Therefore:

> **HER2 is one important piece of the treatment decision, not the
> entire decision.**

This is the central boundary between PP-0210 and the broader
biomarker-directed treatment-selection package.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 20 --- Safety: Trastuzumab

Trastuzumab is not a risk-free treatment.

An important safety consideration is cardiac function.

Patients may therefore undergo assessment and monitoring of cardiac
function depending on the treatment context.

PP-0210 should communicate the principle:

> **HER2-targeted treatment can require organ-specific safety
> monitoring.**

Detailed cardiac monitoring, thresholds, treatment interruption, and
management belong to the dedicated Cardiac Monitoring package.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 21 --- Safety: T-DXd

T-DXd has important treatment-specific adverse effects.

These can include:

-   blood-count abnormalities;
-   gastrointestinal effects;
-   fatigue and other treatment-related symptoms;
-   interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis.

ILD/pneumonitis is particularly important because it can be serious.

PP-0210 therefore includes the safety-awareness message:

> **New or worsening respiratory symptoms during T-DXd treatment require
> prompt clinical attention.**

The detailed diagnostic and management algorithm belongs downstream.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 22 --- Targeted Does Not Mean Harmless

A common misconception is:

> "Because targeted therapy is more precise, it has no serious side
> effects."

This is incorrect.

Targeted therapies can affect normal tissues and can have
treatment-specific toxicities.

Precision refers to the biological target.

It does not mean:

-   zero toxicity;
-   zero monitoring;
-   zero treatment burden;
-   guaranteed benefit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 23 --- Targeted Therapy Does Not Mean Chemotherapy-Free

Some patients may think:

> "If my cancer has a target, I will not need chemotherapy."

In gastric cancer, HER2-targeted treatment may be combined with
chemotherapy.

Therefore the correct conceptual model is:

**targeted therapy + chemotherapy**

in selected settings,

rather than:

**targeted therapy instead of chemotherapy** in every patient.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 24 --- What if HER2 Is Negative?

If HER2 is not positive under the applicable testing framework,
HER2-directed treatment is generally not the relevant pathway being
discussed.

But the patient may still have other treatment-relevant features.

Possible treatment considerations may include other biomarkers or
non-HER2 systemic treatment options.

Therefore:

> **HER2-negative is not the same as treatment-negative.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 25 --- What if HER2 Is Positive but the Patient Has Already Received Trastuzumab?

This is an important treatment-line question.

Prior trastuzumab exposure changes the context.

The next treatment cannot simply be selected from the initial-treatment
pathway.

For selected patients with progression after prior
trastuzumab-containing treatment, T-DXd may be relevant.

This is why treatment history is essential to interpretation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 26 --- Why Clinical Context Matters More Than a Single Test Result

Two patients can have the same HER2 result but different treatment
decisions because they may differ in:

-   stage;
-   treatment line;
-   prior therapy;
-   performance status;
-   comorbidities;
-   organ function;
-   other biomarkers;
-   treatment preferences.

Therefore:

> **The same biomarker result does not necessarily produce the same
> treatment decision for every patient.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 27 --- Shared Decision-Making

When a HER2-directed treatment is being considered, patients may want to
understand:

-   expected benefit;
-   likelihood of response;
-   treatment duration;
-   treatment burden;
-   important adverse effects;
-   monitoring requirements;
-   alternatives;
-   what happens if the cancer progresses.

The oncology team integrates these questions with the patient's clinical
status and goals.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Block 28 --- Patient Questions to Bring to the Care Team

1.  Is my tumor HER2-positive under the appropriate gastric-cancer
    testing criteria?
2.  What exactly was my HER2 result?
3.  What specimen was tested?
4.  Is HER2 the main biomarker guiding this treatment?
5.  What treatment line am I in?
6.  Have I received trastuzumab before?
7.  Why is this particular HER2-directed treatment appropriate now?
8.  Will it be combined with chemotherapy?
9.  Is immunotherapy part of the proposed treatment?
10. What benefit can realistically be expected?
11. What are the most important side effects?
12. Do I need cardiac monitoring?
13. If T-DXd is being considered, what should I know about lung
    toxicity?
14. How will response be monitored?
15. What would we do if the cancer progresses?
16. Are there clinical trials relevant to my HER2 status?
17. Are there other biomarkers that should be considered before choosing
    treatment?

# Common Misconceptions

## Myth 1

**"HER2-positive means I automatically receive trastuzumab."**

### Fact

HER2 positivity makes HER2-directed treatment relevant, but treatment
choice depends on clinical setting, treatment line, prior therapy, other
biomarkers, safety, and current guidance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 2

**"HER2-targeted therapy replaces chemotherapy."**

### Fact

In important first-line advanced HER2-positive settings, trastuzumab is
combined with chemotherapy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 3

**"All HER2-targeted drugs work in exactly the same way."**

### Fact

Trastuzumab is a monoclonal antibody, while T-DXd is an antibody-drug
conjugate with a cytotoxic payload.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 4

**"If HER2 is positive, treatment will definitely work."**

### Fact

HER2 positivity identifies a treatment-relevant population, but response
varies and resistance can occur.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 5

**"Targeted therapy has no serious side effects."**

### Fact

Trastuzumab has important cardiac safety considerations, and T-DXd can
cause serious toxicities including ILD/pneumonitis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 6

**"If trastuzumab stops working, there are no HER2-directed treatments
left."**

### Fact

Selected patients may have later-line HER2-directed options, including
T-DXd.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 7

**"HER2-negative means there is no treatment."**

### Fact

HER2-negative means the HER2-directed pathway is not the relevant
pathway being discussed. Other treatment options may still exist.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 8

**"A HER2 result alone tells the doctor exactly what to prescribe."**

### Fact

HER2 is one part of treatment selection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 9

**"Targeted therapy means the treatment only affects cancer cells."**

### Fact

Targeted therapies can still affect normal tissues and cause adverse
effects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Myth 10

**"If treatment works, the cancer is permanently cured."**

### Fact

Response and disease control do not necessarily mean permanent
eradication of cancer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Clinical Safety Boundary

This package provides general education.

It does not tell an individual patient to:

-   start treatment;
-   stop treatment;
-   switch treatment;
-   repeat testing at a specific interval;
-   accept or refuse a specific drug.

An individual HER2-directed treatment decision requires the full
clinical context and current oncology guidance.
