# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand that preparation for a CT scan depends on the type of examination.
- Know why following the healthcare team's instructions is important.
- Recognize that some CT scans require fasting while others do not.
- Know which health information should be shared before the examination.
- Understand that preparation may differ when contrast material is planned.
- Recognize that medications should not be stopped unless specifically instructed.

# Knowledge Block 1 — Does Every CT Scan Require the Same Preparation?

## Patient Explanation

No.

The preparation depends on **why the CT scan is being performed** and **which part of your body is being examined**.

Some CT scans require very little preparation, while others have specific instructions.

---

## Clinical Importance

Preparation is individualized according to the examination and clinical purpose.

---

## Key Concepts

- Preparation varies.
- Follow individual instructions.
- Not every CT is the same.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Should I Follow the Instructions from My Healthcare Team?

## Patient Explanation

Your healthcare team provides instructions that are specific to your examination.

Following these instructions helps:

- obtain the best possible images;
- reduce the chance that the examination must be repeated;
- improve the quality of the information available to your doctors.

---

## Clinical Importance

Appropriate preparation supports high-quality imaging and efficient patient care.

---

## Key Concepts

- Follow instructions carefully.
- Better preparation improves image quality.
- Preparation may prevent delays.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Will I Need to Fast Before My CT Scan?

## Patient Explanation

Sometimes.

Some CT scans require you to **avoid eating for several hours beforehand**, especially if contrast material will be used.

Many other CT examinations **do not require fasting**.

Always follow the specific instructions you receive.

---

## Clinical Importance

Fasting requirements depend on the examination protocol.

---

## Key Concepts

- Some CT scans require fasting.
- Many do not.
- Individual instructions are most important.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — What Information Should I Tell My Healthcare Team?

## Patient Explanation

Before your CT scan, you may be asked whether you:

- are pregnant or might be pregnant;
- have had a previous reaction to contrast material;
- have kidney disease;
- have other important medical conditions;
- are taking certain medicines.

Providing accurate information helps your healthcare team prepare safely for your examination.

---

## Clinical Importance

Pre-examination assessment supports appropriate use of CT and contrast material.

---

## Key Concepts

- Share important health information.
- Inform your healthcare team honestly.
- Safety assessment is individualized.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Should I Stop My Medicines Before a CT Scan?

## Patient Explanation

Do **not** stop or change your medicines unless your healthcare team specifically tells you to do so.

If you are unsure, ask your doctor or the imaging department before your appointment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Medication management should always follow professional medical advice.

---

## Key Concepts

- Do not stop medicines on your own.
- Ask if you are uncertain.
- Follow professional instructions.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Everyone must prepare for a CT scan in exactly the same way.

**Fact**

Preparation depends on the type of CT examination, whether contrast material will be used, and your individual medical situation.

---

# Key Messages

- CT preparation varies from one examination to another.
- Follow the instructions provided by your healthcare team.
- Some CT scans require fasting, while many do not.
- Tell your healthcare team about pregnancy, previous contrast reactions, kidney disease and other important medical conditions.
- Do not stop your medicines unless you are specifically instructed to do so.
