# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what **Stable Disease (SD)** means.
- Understand that Stable Disease is one of the standardized RECIST response categories.
- Recognize that SD means the cancer has neither shrunk enough to be considered a Partial Response nor grown enough to be considered Progressive Disease.
- Understand that Stable Disease does not necessarily mean treatment has failed.
- Understand that maintaining Stable Disease may be an important treatment goal for many patients.
- Understand that doctors interpret SD together with many other clinical findings.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Stable Disease (SD)?

## Patient Explanation

**Stable Disease (SD)** is one of the standardized categories used to describe **how cancer responds to treatment**.

It means that, according to standardized assessment methods, **the cancer has remained relatively unchanged**. It has **not become small enough to be called a Partial Response**, but it has **not grown enough to be called Progressive Disease**.

---

## Clinical Importance

SD represents disease stability within standardized RECIST assessment.

---

## Key Concepts

- SD describes treatment response.
- Cancer remains relatively stable.
- Neither significant shrinkage nor significant growth is present.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Does Stable Disease Mean Treatment Is Not Working?

## Patient Explanation

Not necessarily.

For many patients, especially those with advanced cancer, **preventing the cancer from growing can be an important treatment goal**.

Stable Disease may indicate that treatment is helping keep the cancer under control.

---

## Clinical Importance

Disease stabilization may represent meaningful clinical benefit depending on the patient's overall situation.

---

## Key Concepts

- SD does not automatically mean treatment failure.
- Keeping cancer stable can be beneficial.
- Clinical context remains important.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Is Stable Disease Good or Bad?

## Patient Explanation

Stable Disease is **not automatically good or bad**.

Its meaning depends on your overall condition, the type of cancer, your treatment goals and how long the disease remains stable.

For many patients, maintaining Stable Disease is considered a positive treatment outcome.

---

## Clinical Importance

The clinical significance of SD depends on the overall treatment context rather than RECIST classification alone.

---

## Key Concepts

- SD must be interpreted individually.
- Treatment goals differ among patients.
- Stability may be a successful outcome.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — What Happens After Stable Disease?

## Patient Explanation

Your healthcare team will usually continue to monitor your condition with regular follow-up visits, imaging studies and other tests.

Treatment may continue, be adjusted or change depending on your overall clinical situation.

---

## Clinical Importance

Ongoing assessment is essential because treatment planning continues beyond a single response evaluation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Monitoring continues.
- Treatment decisions remain individualized.
- SD is only one point in the treatment journey.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Is Stable Disease the Only Information Doctors Use?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Doctors also consider:

- your symptoms;
- physical examination;
- imaging studies;
- laboratory tests;
- pathology results;
- your overall health.

Stable Disease is **only one part** of the complete clinical assessment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Clinical decisions are never based on RECIST response categories alone.

---

## Key Concepts

- SD is one assessment result.
- Multiple clinical factors are considered.
- Comprehensive evaluation remains essential.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Stable Disease means the treatment is not helping.

**Fact**

Stable Disease means the cancer has remained relatively unchanged according to standardized assessment. For many patients, preventing further cancer growth is an important treatment goal.

---

# Key Messages

- Stable Disease (SD) is a standardized treatment response category.
- SD means the cancer remains relatively stable during standardized assessment.
- SD is different from both Partial Response and Progressive Disease.
- Stable Disease does not automatically mean treatment has failed.
- Continued treatment, monitoring and follow-up remain important parts of cancer care.
