# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does Stable Disease (SD) mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Stable Disease (SD)**, including what the term means, why it is used as a standardized treatment response category, why maintaining stable disease may represent a meaningful treatment outcome, and why Stable Disease should not automatically be interpreted as either treatment failure or treatment success in isolation.

This Population Package introduces the concept of Stable Disease without explaining RECIST technical response criteria, tumor measurement thresholds, response algorithms, prognosis, recurrence risk or treatment strategy.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of Stable Disease
- Stable Disease as a standardized RECIST response category
- Meaning of disease stability under standardized assessment
- Difference between Stable Disease, Partial Response and Progressive Disease
- Explanation that Stable Disease does not automatically indicate treatment failure
- Importance of continued monitoring and comprehensive clinical assessment

---

## Excluded

- RECIST 1.1 technical criteria for Stable Disease
- Percentage thresholds
- Target lesion measurement
- Non-target lesion assessment
- Lymph node criteria
- Progressive Disease (PD)
- RECIST response algorithms
- Post-treatment management
- Cancer recurrence
- Prognostic significance

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Stable Disease is one of the standardized RECIST response categories.
- It describes disease that remains relatively unchanged according to standardized assessment.
- Stable Disease is different from both Partial Response and Progressive Disease.
- Stable Disease does not automatically indicate treatment failure.
- Ongoing monitoring and clinical assessment remain essential.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of Stable Disease;
- its role as a standardized treatment response category;
- the need to interpret Stable Disease within the overall clinical context;
- the importance of continued assessment during treatment.

Differences between references relate mainly to technical RECIST measurement criteria and disease-specific management recommendations, which are intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted RECIST 1.1 concepts without introducing technical response criteria.
