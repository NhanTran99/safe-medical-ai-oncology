# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **How is a patient followed after treatment for gastric cancer?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded, patient-facing framework for understanding gastric-cancer surveillance after treatment.

The package must explain:

- what surveillance is;
- why it is performed;
- why it differs by stage and treatment;
- which major surveillance components may be used;
- how the pathway changes after different forms of treatment;
- how surveillance relates to recurrence detection and survivorship;
- what the evidence limitations are.

The package must not convert general guideline information into an individualized surveillance prescription.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Direct Disease-Specific Guideline

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

This is the dominant source for PP-0220.

The supplied guideline explicitly contains:

- GAST-6 Post-Treatment Assessment;
- GAST-7 Follow-up/Surveillance;
- GAST-8 Recurrence;
- GAST-H Principles of Surveillance;
- GAST-I Principles of Survivorship.

The architecture itself supports the separation:

**Post-treatment assessment**
→ **surveillance**
→ **recurrence**
→ **survivorship**.

The NCCN GAST-7 pathway identifies three surveillance groups:

1. Tis successfully treated by ER.
2. pStage I (T1a/T1b, N0) treated by surgical resection or ER as applicable.
3. pStage II/III or ypStage I–III treated with neoadjuvant ± adjuvant therapy.

NCCN also provides a special footnote for total gastrectomy.

Source traceability: `1. Gastric Cancer_v.2.2026_NCCN-3-109.pdf`, GAST-7/GAST-H.

# Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

## Evidence Note 1 — GAST-7 is the direct source of truth for surveillance architecture

The NCCN v2.2026 document explicitly labels GAST-7 as:

> **FOLLOW-UP/SURVEILLANCE**

and places it between:

- GAST-6 Post-Treatment Assessment
- GAST-8 Recurrence.

This is important because it independently validates the package architecture.

PP-0220 therefore should not absorb post-treatment imaging, recurrence diagnosis or recurrent-treatment management.

---

## Evidence Note 2 — Surveillance is stage- and treatment-specific

GAST-7 identifies distinct groups.

This prevents an unsafe simplification such as:

> “Every gastric-cancer survivor gets CT every six months.”

The structured CT interval applies specifically to the pStage II/III or ypStage I–III pathway treated with neoadjuvant ± adjuvant therapy.

Other pathways use clinically indicated imaging.

This distinction must remain visible in the final knowledge product.

---

## Evidence Note 3 — Endoscopic resection has a distinct surveillance pathway

For Tis successfully treated by ER, NCCN specifies upper-GI endoscopy every six months for one year, then annually for three years.

For pStage I treated by ER, NCCN specifies EGD every six months for one year, then annually for up to five years, thereafter according to symptoms and/or radiographic findings.

This is stronger than treating endoscopy as a generic optional follow-up test.

---

## Evidence Note 4 — Total gastrectomy changes the endoscopic surveillance boundary

NCCN explicitly states that total gastrectomy for curative intent follows surveillance recommendations except for endoscopy.

Endoscopy is clinically indicated unless the patient is symptomatic.

This is an anatomy-dependent distinction and should be preserved.

---

## Evidence Note 5 — Nutritional monitoring is part of the surveillance interface

NCCN links post-gastrectomy surveillance to monitoring for nutritional deficiency.

The guideline lists examples including:

- vitamin B12;
- iron;
- zinc;
- calcium;
- vitamin D.

It also directs lifelong monitoring and management of potential nutritional sequelae.

This supports including nutritional monitoring in PP-0220 while delegating detailed nutrition management.

---

## Evidence Note 6 — The first two years matter most for recurrence timing

NCCN GAST-H summarizes evidence showing approximately 70–80% of recurrences occur within two years after completion of local therapy.

This explains why early surveillance is structured more closely.

The number should not be interpreted as an individualized recurrence probability.

It is a population-level summary of recurrence timing in the cited literature.

---

## Evidence Note 7 — Five years is not a biological guarantee

NCCN states that approximately 90% of recurrences occur by five years but also notes potentially actionable relapse beyond five years.

Therefore:

> five years is a surveillance framework boundary, not a biological “zero recurrence” threshold.

The package must preserve this nuance.

---

## Evidence Note 8 — Evidence uncertainty is itself clinically important

NCCN states that surveillance strategies after curative-intent R0 resection remain controversial, with sparse prospective data.

The guideline balances:

- benefit;
- risk;
- cost.

It therefore uses retrospectively analyzed literature and expert consensus.

The evidence package must not present the schedule as if it were derived from a single definitive randomized trial.

---

## Evidence Note 9 — Surveillance and recurrence detection are sequentially related

The source architecture provides:

**GAST-7 Follow-up/Surveillance**
→ **GAST-8 Recurrence**.

This is the basis for the package boundary.

The inference is architectural, not a new clinical claim.

---

## Evidence Note 10 — Surveillance and survivorship are parallel downstream domains

The GAST-7 pathway can lead to:

- Recurrence;
- Survivorship.

NCCN also has a dedicated GAST-I Principles of Survivorship section.

Therefore PP-0220 should transition toward survivorship but should not own it.

3. H&P is a major surveillance component.

# Evidence Consistency Review

## NCCN GAST-7 vs GAST-H

These components are complementary.

**GAST-7** provides operational stage/treatment-specific surveillance pathways.

**GAST-H** provides the evidence interpretation and surveillance principles, including:

- controversy;
- sparse prospective evidence;
- retrospective evidence;
- expert consensus;
- recurrence timing;
- late recurrence.

No material conflict is identified.

---

## NCCN GAST-7 vs GAST-I

These are complementary rather than competing.

GAST-7 addresses cancer-specific surveillance.

GAST-I addresses survivorship and long-term health.

The package preserves this distinction.

---

## NCCN vs NCI

NCI treatment material supports the broader treatment and post-treatment context.

NCCN remains the dominant source for the specific surveillance framework.

No material conflict is identified for the locked scope.

---

## NCCN vs ESMO-ASCO

NCCN supplies the gastric-cancer-specific surveillance framework.

ESMO-ASCO supplies broader oncology educational context.

No material conflict is identified.

---

## NCCN vs ACS

ACS is used only for patient-facing context and does not override the disease-specific NCCN surveillance framework.

# Evidence Limitations

1. The NCCN guideline itself acknowledges sparse prospective evidence for surveillance after curative-intent R0 resection.
2. Stage-specific recommendations are based on retrospectively analyzed literature and expert consensus.
3. The evidence does not establish one universally optimal surveillance intensity for all patients.
4. Recurrence timing statistics describe populations and should not be treated as individual risk estimates.
5. “Clinically indicated” decisions require patient-specific assessment not contained in this package.
6. The project source set does not establish a universal tumor-marker surveillance schedule.
7. Modality-specific imaging limitations are delegated to imaging PPs.
8. Detailed nutritional treatment is delegated to nutrition/supportive-care PPs.

---

# Evidence Gaps

The following gaps are intentionally not filled with unsupported general knowledge:

1. A single universally optimal surveillance strategy for every stage and treatment group.
2. Definitive prospective evidence that a more intensive surveillance program improves survival in every population.
3. A universal role for routine tumor markers across all gastric-cancer surveillance pathways.
4. A universal imaging schedule applicable to all post-treatment patients.
5. A universal endoscopy schedule applicable to all surgical anatomies.
6. A universal strategy for follow-up beyond five years.
7. Individualized surveillance decisions for a specific patient without their full clinical context.

These gaps do not block PP-0220 because the locked scope is designed to teach the guideline framework and its limitations rather than to prescribe individual care.

# Evidence-to-Patient Translation

## Technical guideline statement

Surveillance strategies are stage- and treatment-specific.

## Patient translation

> **Your follow-up schedule depends on what type of gastric cancer you had and how it was treated.**

---

## Technical guideline statement

CT is used routinely in selected pStage II/III or ypStage I–III pathways.

## Patient translation

> **Some patients need regular CT scans as part of surveillance, while others have imaging only when symptoms or other findings make it appropriate.**

---

## Technical guideline statement

Total gastrectomy changes the role of endoscopy.

## Patient translation

> **If your entire stomach was removed, routine surveillance endoscopy is usually not part of the standard pathway unless it is clinically indicated or you have symptoms.**

---

## Technical guideline statement

Nutritional sequelae require lifelong monitoring.

## Patient translation

> **After stomach surgery, follow-up may include checking for long-term vitamin and mineral deficiencies, not only checking for cancer recurrence.**

---

## Technical guideline statement

Late relapse can occur beyond five years.

## Patient translation

> **The risk of recurrence becomes much lower over time, but five years does not mean recurrence is impossible.**
