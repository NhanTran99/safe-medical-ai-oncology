# Clinical-Educational Intent

PP-0220 explains the **surveillance phase after gastric-cancer treatment**.

The package is designed to answer:

> What is surveillance, why is it performed, what does it include, why does it differ by stage and treatment, and what happens when surveillance identifies a concerning finding?

It is deliberately not a recurrence-treatment package.

It is also deliberately not an imaging-methodology package.

# Knowledge Units

## KU-01 — Definition

Surveillance is planned follow-up after gastric-cancer treatment.

## KU-02 — Purpose

Monitor for recurrence-related findings and treatment consequences while maintaining longitudinal care.

## KU-03 — Pathway Dependence

Surveillance differs by stage, treatment modality and anatomy.

## KU-04 — Clinical Visits

H&P is a central component.

## KU-05 — Laboratory Follow-up

CBC and chemistry are clinically indicated.

## KU-06 — EGD

EGD has pathway-specific roles, particularly after endoscopic resection.

## KU-07 — Imaging

CT surveillance is structured in selected higher-stage post-treatment pathways and clinically indicated in other pathways.

## KU-08 — Total Gastrectomy

Routine surveillance endoscopy is not part of the standard pathway unless clinically indicated or symptomatic; nutritional monitoring becomes particularly important.

## KU-09 — Nutritional Sequelae

Vitamin B12, iron, zinc, calcium and vitamin D deficiencies are examples of nutritional consequences requiring monitoring.

## KU-10 — Recurrence Interface

A concerning surveillance finding may lead to recurrence evaluation.

## KU-11 — Time Window

Most recurrences occur within two years and approximately 90% occur by five years, but late actionable relapse can occur.

## KU-12 — Five-Year Boundary

Routine gastric-cancer-specific surveillance does not necessarily continue beyond five years; additional follow-up may be considered according to risk factors and comorbidities.

## KU-13 — Evidence Limitation

Surveillance strategies after curative-intent R0 resection remain controversial and are supported substantially by retrospective evidence and expert consensus.

## KU-14 — Survivorship Transition

End of routine cancer-specific surveillance does not mean end of healthcare.

# Evidence Classification

## Established / Guideline-Supported

- GAST-7 defines stage/treatment-specific surveillance.
- H&P schedule.
- Clinically indicated CBC/chemistry.
- Pathway-specific EGD.
- Selected CT surveillance.
- Nutritional monitoring after gastrectomy.
- Five-year framework.
- Late recurrence can occur.
- Survivorship and follow-up can continue beyond the routine cancer-specific surveillance window.

## Context-Dependent

- Exact test selection.
- EGD after surgical resection.
- Imaging outside the structured pathway.
- Follow-up after five years.
- Nutritional management.
- Earlier assessment for symptoms.

## Emerging / Uncertain

- Optimal intensity of surveillance after curative-intent R0 resection.
- Comparative benefit of more intensive versus less intensive surveillance.
- Full prospective evidence for surveillance strategies.

## Not Established / Excluded

- Universal surveillance calendar for every patient.
- Universal tumor-marker schedule.
- Surveillance intensity as a guarantee of survival benefit.
- Normal test as proof of permanent cure.

# Safety / Runtime Rules

1. Do not convert NCCN pathway examples into an individualized patient schedule.
2. Do not state that a normal surveillance test guarantees cure.
3. Do not state that an abnormal surveillance test proves recurrence.
4. Do not imply that all patients need the same tests.
5. Do not invent a universal tumor-marker schedule.
6. Do not use surveillance as a substitute for recurrence evaluation.
7. Do not provide detailed recurrence treatment.
8. Do not provide detailed imaging methodology.
9. Do not provide individualized nutritional prescriptions.
10. Preserve uncertainty around surveillance benefit.
11. Preserve treatment/stage dependence.
12. Route questions about established recurrence to PP-0221/PP-0222.
13. Route broader long-term survivorship questions to PP-0229/PP-0230.
