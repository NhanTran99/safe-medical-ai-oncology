# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does "mass" mean in a CT report?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the medical term **"mass"**, emphasizing that it is a **descriptive imaging term** referring to a visible tissue abnormality rather than a diagnosis.

This Population Package helps reduce unnecessary anxiety by explaining that a mass may have many different causes and that imaging findings alone usually cannot determine exactly what a mass represents.

---

- Appropriate patient interpretation

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- A mass is an imaging description rather than a diagnosis.
- Many different medical conditions can produce a mass.
- A mass does not automatically indicate cancer.
- Imaging findings require interpretation together with the patient's history, examination, laboratory studies and, when appropriate, pathology.
- Professional medical evaluation is essential before conclusions are made.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the descriptive meaning of the term "mass";
- multidisciplinary interpretation of imaging findings;
- integration of imaging into comprehensive clinical assessment;
- avoidance of diagnostic conclusions based on imaging terminology alone.

Differences between references primarily concern organ-specific imaging characteristics and diagnostic pathways, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- multidisciplinary oncology recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- radiology terminology principles from ACR and RSNA.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- imaging features suggesting malignancy;
- lesion versus mass versus nodule;
- biopsy indications;
- RECIST terminology;
- TNM staging;
- treatment planning.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
