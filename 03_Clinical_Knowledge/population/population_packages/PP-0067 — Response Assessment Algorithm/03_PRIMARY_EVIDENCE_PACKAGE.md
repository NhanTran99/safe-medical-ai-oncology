# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **How do doctors determine whether cancer has Complete Response (CR), Partial Response (PR), Stable Disease (SD), or Progressive Disease (PD)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **how doctors perform standardized treatment response assessment**, including why multiple evaluations are compared, how existing and new disease are assessed, why standardized response categories are used, and why the final response category represents only one component of overall clinical decision-making.

This Population Package introduces the overall response assessment workflow without explaining RECIST technical measurement rules, response algorithms, imaging methodology or treatment decision algorithms.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Purpose of standardized response assessment
- Comparison of current and previous evaluations
- Assessment of existing lesions
- Assessment for newly detected lesions
- Classification into CR, PR, SD or PD
- Importance of integrating response assessment with the complete clinical picture

---

## Excluded

- RECIST 1.1 technical algorithm
- RECIST measurement rules
- Percentage thresholds
- Target lesion criteria
- Non-target lesion criteria
- Lymph node criteria
- Imaging protocols
- Treatment selection after response assessment
- Prognostic significance
- Survival statistics

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Treatment response is evaluated using a standardized process.
- Doctors compare current findings with previous evaluations.
- Both existing disease and possible new disease are assessed.
- Response is categorized as Complete Response, Partial Response, Stable Disease or Progressive Disease.
- Response assessment is an important part of care but does not independently determine treatment decisions.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the use of standardized response assessment;
- longitudinal comparison of imaging findings;
- classification into four standardized response categories;
- integration of response assessment into broader clinical decision-making.

Differences between references relate primarily to detailed RECIST implementation and disease-specific management recommendations, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted RECIST 1.1 concepts without introducing technical response criteria.
