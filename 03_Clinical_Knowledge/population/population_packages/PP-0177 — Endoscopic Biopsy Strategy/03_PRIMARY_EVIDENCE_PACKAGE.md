# Clinical Question

> **How should suspicious gastric findings be sampled so that tissue is adequate and representative for diagnosis and, when needed, downstream molecular or biomarker testing?**

# Educational Intent

Explain biopsy as a clinical strategy rather than merely a procedure. The package should help readers understand:

- why suspicious gastric findings are sampled;
- why multiple samples are recommended;
- what targeted and context-specific random/mapping sampling mean;
- why biopsy location and representativeness matter;
- what adequate tissue means;
- why ulcerated, diffuse, microscopic and heterogeneous disease can create sampling limitations;
- why a negative biopsy is not identical to an inadequate biopsy;
- why additional sampling may sometimes be needed;
- why tissue adequacy matters for downstream molecular testing;
- how routine biopsy differs from larger endoscopic specimens such as EMR/ESD.

# Evidence Notes

## 1. NCCN defines the central biopsy strategy

NCCN's gastric-cancer diagnostic endoscopy section states that suspicious lesions should be biopsied and recommends multiple 6–8 biopsies using standard-size endoscopy forceps. The stated goal is adequately sized material for histologic and molecular interpretation, with particular importance for ulcerated lesions. NCCN also notes that larger forceps may improve yield.

This is the principal evidence anchor for PP-0177.

## 2. Biopsy is a tissue-acquisition strategy, not merely a yes/no procedure

The guideline recommendation establishes several distinct concepts:

**suspicious lesion**

→ **multiple samples**

→ **adequate material**

→ **histologic interpretation**

→ **molecular interpretation when needed.**

Therefore PP-0177 appropriately owns both quantity and quality/representativeness.

## 3. Biopsy site is clinically meaningful

NCCN's GAST-A evidence list includes a specific study on the importance of the site of endoscopic gastric biopsy in ulcerating lesions and another on biopsy number in the diagnosis of esophageal and gastric carcinoma.

This supports treating biopsy location and number as separate but related dimensions of sampling strategy.

## 4. Ulcerated lesions deserve explicit treatment in the package

Ulcerated lesions can contain nonrepresentative surface material. NCCN explicitly emphasizes adequate tissue in this setting.

PP-0177 therefore includes ulcerated-lesion sampling as a major knowledge block without inventing a universal edge/base formula not established by the supplied source set.

## 5. Adequacy is broader than simply “tissue was obtained”

A specimen can be technically obtained but remain insufficient because it is:

- too small;
- superficial;
- nonrepresentative;
- necrotic;
- from an inappropriate location.

This is especially relevant when the clinical question extends beyond the initial cancer diagnosis to molecular/biomarker assessment.

## 6. Diffuse gastric cancer illustrates a major sampling limitation

NCI describes diffuse gastric cancer/linitis plastica and notes that a very superficial biopsy may be negative for cancer cells.

This directly supports the distinction between:

**negative sample**

and

**adequate exclusion of disease.**

The latter cannot be inferred automatically from the former.

## 7. HDGC demonstrates the role of random sampling in a selected context

NCI HDGC PDQ reports that early diffuse gastric cancer has no definitive identifiable endoscopic features and that targeted biopsies may detect signet-ring-cell disease at low rates. It describes the use of a liberal number of random biopsies because the epithelium overlying signet-ring-cell disease can appear normal.

This evidence is clinically important but must remain bounded to HDGC surveillance. PP-0159 owns the surveillance protocol itself.

## 8. Normal-appearing mucosa does not universally exclude microscopic disease

NCI describes surveillance cohorts in which signet-ring-cell carcinoma was identified despite visibly normal mucosa.

The patient-facing implication is not that every normal stomach requires random biopsy. The correct implication is that biopsy strategy depends on the clinical context and that selected microscopic diseases cannot be excluded by visual inspection alone.

## 9. Tissue can have downstream molecular value

NCCN explicitly links adequate biopsy material to molecular interpretation. ACS also explains that additional tests may be performed on biopsy material after cancer is identified.

Therefore PP-0177 must preserve the concept of **downstream tissue utility** while delegating test-specific interpretation to biomarker packages.

## 10. Routine biopsy and endoscopic resection are not interchangeable

NCCN describes EMR/ESD as providing a larger specimen in selected small lesions and notes that this can provide more information about differentiation, lymphovascular invasion and depth of infiltration.

PP-0177 therefore includes this as a transition point but delegates procedure-specific indications and technique to PP-0192–0194.

## 11. Post-treatment tissue has special limitations

NCCN notes that biopsies performed after chemotherapy or radiation may not accurately diagnose residual disease, although they may still provide useful information.

This is included only as a limitation and interface. Response assessment and post-treatment surveillance belong downstream.

# Clinical Claims Summary

1. Suspicious gastric lesions should be sampled for tissue diagnosis.
2. NCCN recommends multiple 6–8 biopsies using standard-size endoscopy forceps.
3. The purpose is to obtain adequate material for histologic and molecular interpretation.
4. Ulcerated lesions require particular attention to tissue adequacy.
5. Larger forceps may improve yield.
6. Biopsy site can influence representativeness, particularly in ulcerated lesions.
7. Targeted and random/mapping biopsies answer different clinical questions.
8. Random biopsy is context-dependent and has an important selected role in HDGC surveillance.
9. Diffuse and microscopic gastric cancer can be missed by superficial or targeted sampling.
10. A negative biopsy is not identical to an inadequate or nondiagnostic biopsy.
11. Persistent suspicion after nondiagnostic sampling may lead to additional tissue evaluation.
12. Biopsy tissue may need to support downstream molecular/biomarker testing.
13. Selected lesions may require larger endoscopic specimens rather than routine forceps biopsy.
14. Biopsy strategy does not determine pathology classification, biomarker interpretation or treatment.

# Evidence Consistency Review

The supplied sources converge on the same clinical architecture:

**suspicious lesion → multiple/appropriate tissue sampling → adequate representative specimen → histologic diagnosis → additional molecular/biomarker testing when indicated.**

NCCN provides the strongest direct guideline-level evidence for the number and adequacy of biopsies. NCI provides the key limitation evidence for microscopic and diffuse disease. ACS supports the patient-facing explanation of pathology and additional testing.

The evidence does not justify:

- a universal random-biopsy protocol for ordinary gastric cancer;
- a universal repeat-biopsy interval;
- a guarantee that 6–8 samples detect every cancer;
- a universal edge/base formula for every ulcer;
- individualized procedural instructions.

# Evidence Gaps

The supplied source set does not establish:

- one universal biopsy algorithm for every gastric lesion;
- one universal biopsy site formula for every ulcer;
- one universal repeat-biopsy interval;
- one universal random/mapping strategy outside defined high-risk contexts;
- a universal threshold of tissue quantity that guarantees all downstream tests;
- a universal rule for when routine biopsy should always be replaced by EMR/ESD.
