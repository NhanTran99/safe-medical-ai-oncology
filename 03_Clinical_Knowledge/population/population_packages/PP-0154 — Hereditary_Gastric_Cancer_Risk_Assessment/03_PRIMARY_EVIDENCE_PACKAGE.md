# Primary Clinical Question

> **How is hereditary gastric-cancer risk assessed from an individual's personal and family history, and how does that assessment guide genetic evaluation and risk management?**

# Core Evidence: Comprehensive Risk Assessment

NCI describes cancer genetic risk assessment as a structured process incorporating:

- detailed medical history;
- detailed family history;
- assessment of cancer risk;
- determination of whether genetic testing is indicated;
- education/counseling;
- risk-management planning.

Therefore PP-0154 is an **assessment layer**, not a genetic-testing package.

---

# Core Evidence: Personal History

Relevant personal-history elements include:

- current age;
- personal cancer history;
- age at diagnosis;
- tumor type;
- pathology/histology;
- prior biopsies;
- polyps or precursor lesions;
- previous genetic testing;
- previous tumor testing;
- relevant medical history.

For gastric cancer, pathology and histology are particularly important because different histologic patterns can point toward different hereditary syndromes.

---

# Core Evidence: Three-Generation Family History

NCI recommends detailed family-history collection, commonly structured as a three-generation pedigree.

Important fields include:

- cancer type;
- histologic subtype;
- age at diagnosis;
- gastric polyps;
- associated cancers;
- relationship to the patient;
- maternal versus paternal lineage.

The pedigree should not be limited to one side of the family.

---

# Core Evidence: Why Histology Matters

A family-history label such as:

> “relative had stomach cancer”

is often insufficient for precise hereditary-risk assessment.

When possible, assess whether the cancer was:

- diffuse;
- signet-ring cell;
- poorly differentiated;
- intestinal-type;
- associated with polyposis or another syndrome-specific phenotype.

Histology may determine which hereditary pathway is appropriate.

---

# Core Evidence: Verification

Self-reported cancer history may be inaccurate or incomplete.

NCI recommends verification of important cancer diagnoses and histology through appropriate sources such as:

- pathology reports;
- medical records;
- cancer registries;
- death records when appropriate.

Verification is especially important when a syndrome-specific criterion depends on histologic subtype.

---

# Core Evidence: Hereditary-Cancer Red Flags

NCI identifies features that increase suspicion of inherited cancer susceptibility, including:

- unusually early cancer onset;
- multiple primary cancers;
- bilateral cancers;
- multiple close relatives with cancer;
- clustering across generations;
- rare or unusual cancers;
- unusual histology;
- characteristic cancer combinations;
- relevant ancestry;
- congenital anomalies or precursor lesions.

No single feature is sufficient in every case.

The overall pattern should be assessed.

---

# Core Evidence: Hereditary Gastric-Cancer Differential

NCI identifies multiple hereditary pathways relevant to gastric cancer and gastric polyps.

Examples include:

- **CDH1**
- **CTNNA1**
- **APC**
- **STK11**
- **SMAD4**
- Lynch syndrome genes including **MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, EPCAM**

The purpose of PP-0154 is not to interpret these genes individually.

Instead:

> **Different clinical patterns may lead to different hereditary pathways.**

---

# Core Evidence: Family History Is Not the Same as a Genetic Diagnosis

A positive family history can indicate increased cancer risk even when no hereditary syndrome is identified.

Therefore:

**family clustering**

does not automatically mean:

**pathogenic germline variant**

and:

**pathogenic variant probability**

does not automatically equal:

**specific cancer risk.**

---

# Core Evidence: Incomplete Family History

Family-history information may be limited by:

- small family size;
- adoption;
- missing records;
- early deaths;
- limited knowledge of relatives;
- variable penetrance;
- genetic heterogeneity;
- de novo variants;
- other pedigree limitations.

Therefore, an apparently negative family history should not automatically be interpreted as proof of low hereditary risk.

---

# Core Evidence: Two Different Risk Questions

Genetic risk assessment can address at least two distinct questions:

### Probability of a pathogenic variant

How likely is it that the person/family carries a clinically relevant germline pathogenic variant?

### Probability of cancer

How likely is the person to develop cancer?

These probabilities may be related but are not interchangeable.

---

# Core Evidence: Risk Models

Risk assessment may incorporate:

- empirical data;
- penetrance;
- Mendelian inheritance;
- pedigree structure;
- age;
- cancer pattern;
- statistical models;
- Bayesian approaches;
- clinical judgment.

Risk models have limitations.

They should support, not replace, clinical assessment.

The Source Materials do not support creation of a universal gastric-cancer-specific risk calculator within PP-0154.

---

# Core Evidence: Transition to Genetic Testing

Risk assessment can lead to:

**hereditary suspicion**

↓

**genetic counseling/testing when appropriate**

The decision to test should consider:

- suspected syndrome;
- personal/family history;
- interpretability of the test;
- potential effect on management;
- ability to clarify family risk.

Exact testing criteria are delegated to disease-specific packages.

---

# Core Evidence: Tumor Testing as a Clue

Previous tumor genomic testing can sometimes reveal findings that raise suspicion for germline susceptibility.

However:

> **a somatic tumor finding is not automatically a germline finding.**

Tumor molecular data should therefore be treated as a potential clue within the broader assessment.

Detailed somatic testing and variant interpretation belong to dedicated molecular/genetic packages.

# Evidence Gaps

The Source Materials do not justify:

- a universal gastric-cancer hereditary-risk score;
- a universal percentage threshold for ordering germline testing;
- a single risk calculator applicable to every gastric-cancer family;
- replacing clinical judgment with a numerical model;
- inferring germline status from a tumor-only finding;
- using family history alone to diagnose a hereditary syndrome.

These limitations are intentionally preserved.

# Primary Evidence Conclusion

The Source Materials support PP-0154 as a distinct **assessment-layer package**.

The cleanest architecture is:

> **PP-0154 = ASSESS HEREDITARY RISK**

↓

> **PP-0152 = APPLY HDGC TESTING CRITERIA**

↓

> **PP-0150 / PP-0151 = INTERPRET CDH1 / CTNNA1**

↓

> **PP-0149 / PP-0153 = MANAGE THE RESULTING HDGC STATE**

This avoids duplication with the immediately adjacent hereditary-gastric-cancer packages while filling a genuine clinical knowledge gap.
