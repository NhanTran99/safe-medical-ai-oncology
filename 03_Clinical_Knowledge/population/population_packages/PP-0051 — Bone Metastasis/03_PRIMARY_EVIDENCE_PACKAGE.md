# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is bone metastasis?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **bone metastasis**, including what bones are, how gastric cancer can spread to bones, why bone metastasis remains gastric cancer rather than primary bone cancer, and why appropriate medical evaluation is required before confirming bone metastasis.

This Population Package introduces the concept of bone metastasis without discussing imaging studies, bone biopsy, skeletal-related events, bone-directed therapies or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of bones
- Definition of bone metastasis
- Relationship between gastric cancer and bones
- Difference between primary bone cancer and bone metastasis
- Relationship between bone metastasis and metastatic gastric cancer
- Importance of appropriate medical evaluation

- Skeletal-related events (SREs)
- Bone-modifying agents
- Radiation therapy

- Systemic therapy

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Bones are essential living tissues that provide structure and protection for the body.
- Bone metastasis means gastric cancer has spread to one or more bones.
- Bone metastasis remains gastric cancer because it originated in the stomach.
- Bone metastasis should not be confused with primary bone cancer.
- Not every patient develops bone metastasis.
- Doctors determine whether bone metastasis is present through appropriate clinical assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- bone metastasis as a form of distant metastatic disease;
- the distinction between metastatic gastric cancer and primary bone cancer;
- the role of bone metastasis in advanced gastric cancer;
- the importance of confirming metastatic disease through appropriate clinical evaluation.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

- systemic treatment;
- molecular mechanisms;
