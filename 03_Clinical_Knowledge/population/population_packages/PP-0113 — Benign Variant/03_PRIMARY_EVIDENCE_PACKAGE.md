# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a Benign Variant?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of a **Benign Variant** as a genetic variant for which **sufficient scientific evidence demonstrates that it does not cause disease**.

This Population Package explains that a benign classification represents the **highest-confidence non-disease-causing category** within standardized variant classification systems while emphasizing that the classification applies to a **specific genetic variant**, not to an entire gene or to every aspect of a person's health.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Benign is an evidence-based scientific classification.
- Benign variants are not considered disease-causing.
- Benign represents the highest-confidence non-disease-causing classification.
- The classification applies to a specific variant rather than the entire gene.
- Clinical interpretation remains part of comprehensive genomic assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently define a **Benign Variant** as a genetic variant supported by sufficient evidence demonstrating that it does not cause disease. Guidance consistently distinguishes benign classification from likely benign on the basis of evidence strength while emphasizing that standardized classifications are designed to promote consistent laboratory reporting and clinical communication. Current recommendations also recognize that classification systems remain evidence-based and subject to periodic scientific review, although benign classifications are generally highly stable.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- classification standards from ACMG and AMP;
- clinical genomics guidance from ClinGen;
- molecular pathology principles from CAP;
- oncology implementation guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- international consistency with ESMO.
