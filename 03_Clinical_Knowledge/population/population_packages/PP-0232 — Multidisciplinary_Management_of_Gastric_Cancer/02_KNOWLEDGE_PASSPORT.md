# Knowledge Classification

## Knowledge Type

Patient-facing clinical education / multidisciplinary-care literacy.

## Atomic Knowledge Question

> **How do different medical and supportive-care specialists work together to plan and reassess gastric-cancer management?**

## Primary Function

This PP is the **gastric-cancer-specific multidisciplinary integration node**.

It explains:

- why multiple specialties may be involved;
- what information is brought into multidisciplinary review;
- what each major discipline contributes;
- how information is synthesized;
- how treatment coordination and sequencing are considered;
- how patient goals are integrated;
- why multidisciplinary review may recur.

It does not own detailed treatment content.

# Clinical Knowledge Units

## KU-01 — Definition

Multidisciplinary management is coordinated care in which relevant specialists jointly integrate clinical information and expertise.

## KU-02 — Why It Matters

Gastric cancer can involve interacting diagnostic, surgical, systemic, radiation, nutritional, supportive, and patient-centred decisions.

## KU-03 — Core Team

Core disciplines identified by NCCN include surgical oncology, medical oncology, gastroenterology, radiation oncology, radiology, and pathology.

## KU-04 — Supporting Team

Nutrition, nursing, social work, palliative care, and other disciplines may contribute according to patient needs.

## KU-05 — Data Integration

MDT integrates diagnosis, staging, pathology, imaging, biomarkers, clinical condition, nutrition, and patient preferences.

## KU-06 — Treatment Integration

MDT coordinates consideration of surgery, systemic treatment, radiation, endoscopic options, supportive care, and palliative care.

## KU-07 — Treatment Sequencing

MDT can discuss the relationship and timing between modalities without replacing detailed treatment-specific algorithms.

## KU-08 — Dynamic Review

MDT may reassess the strategy when disease, response, toxicity, resectability, or patient circumstances change.

## KU-09 — Patient Role

The patient remains central to shared decision-making.

## KU-10 — Safety

MDT education does not constitute individualized treatment advice.

# Evidence Classification

## Established / Guideline-Supported

- NCCN has a dedicated Principles of Multidisciplinary Team Approach for Esophagogastric Cancers.
- Multidisciplinary treatment decision-making is supported as an important infrastructure for esophagogastric cancer care.
- NCCN identifies core disciplines including surgical oncology, medical oncology, gastroenterology, radiation oncology, radiology, and pathology.
- Nutritional services, social workers, nursing, palliative-care specialists, and other support disciplines may also be desirable.
- Joint review of actual medical data is emphasized.
- Multidisciplinary review appears directly in gastric-cancer treatment pathways.
- Complex peritoneal/HIPEC decisions may require multidisciplinary tumor-board discussion.

## Context-Dependent

- Exact team membership.
- Frequency of meetings.
- Local institutional workflow.
- Use of virtual/digital MDTs.
- Referral thresholds.
- Quality metrics for MDT performance.
- Which supporting specialties are present for an individual case.

## Not Established by the Current Package

- A universal survival benefit attributable solely to MDT participation.
- A universal meeting frequency that applies to every institution.
- A universal algorithm defining which specialist must attend every case.

# Runtime Safety Rules

1. Do not convert MDT education into individualized treatment selection.
2. Do not claim that a tumor board guarantees a single correct answer.
3. Do not imply that every patient requires every specialist.
4. Do not imply that palliative care means treatment cessation.
5. Do not provide treatment regimens or dosing from this PP.
6. Do not interpret a patient's imaging, pathology, biomarker, or RECIST result.
7. Refer individualized decisions to the treating clinical team.
8. Preserve uncertainty where the source evidence is context-dependent.
9. Distinguish MDT integration from modality-specific treatment ownership.
10. Never infer a patient's treatment plan from this package alone.

# Evidence Gaps

1. Exact MDT structures differ by institution.
2. The source set does not establish one universal meeting frequency for all gastric-cancer services.
3. The source set does not support a universal MDT performance metric.
4. Not every patient needs every specialist.
5. MDT does not eliminate uncertainty or guarantee consensus.
6. The project materials do not support a universal quantitative survival effect attributable to MDT alone.

These gaps are intentionally preserved rather than filled by unsupported assumptions.
