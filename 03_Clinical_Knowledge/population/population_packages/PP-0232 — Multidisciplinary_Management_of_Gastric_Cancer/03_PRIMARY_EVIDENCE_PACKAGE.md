# 1. Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **How do different medical and supportive-care specialists integrate clinical information to formulate and reassess a coordinated management strategy for gastric cancer?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded framework for understanding multidisciplinary gastric-cancer care without turning the package into an individualized treatment algorithm.

The package is deliberately positioned as an **integration layer** between:

**diagnostic / pathology / imaging / biomarker information**

and

**modality-specific treatment, supportive-care, response, recurrence, surveillance, and survivorship packages.**

# 3. Primary Sources

## Source 1 — NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026

### Role

**Highest-priority disease-specific guideline source.**

The supplied NCCN material identifies a dedicated:

> **Principles of Multidisciplinary Team Approach for Esophagogastric Cancers (GAST-E)**

and incorporates multidisciplinary review into the gastric-cancer treatment pathway.

### Key source-supported points

- Multidisciplinary treatment decision-making is an important component of esophagogastric cancer care.
- Relevant disciplines may include surgical oncology, medical oncology, gastroenterology, radiation oncology, radiology, and pathology.
- Nutrition, nursing, social work, palliative care, and other support disciplines may also contribute.
- Long-term treatment strategies are best developed after adequate staging and ideally before therapy.
- Joint review of actual clinical data is emphasized.
- Multidisciplinary review is explicitly represented in gastric-cancer pathways.
- Peritoneal-only disease and selected HIPEC/cytoreduction decisions require multidisciplinary consultation/review.

### Evidence classification

**Established / guideline-supported.**

The supplied NCCN GAST-E material describes an infrastructure that encourages multidisciplinary treatment decision-making.

The source identifies disciplines that may include:

- surgical oncology;

- gastroenterology;
- radiation oncology;
- radiology;

It additionally identifies:

- nutritional services;

- ideally establishing strategy before treatment;
- joint review of actual medical data rather than relying only on reports.

### Interpretation for PP-0232

> **Multidisciplinary management is an information-integration and coordinated-decision process, not merely a list of specialists.**

# 6. Primary Source Detail — NCCN GAST-10

The supplied NCCN gastric-cancer material describes multidisciplinary consultation in peritoneal-only metastatic disease.

The pathway includes:

- history and physical examination;
- CT staging;
- consideration of diagnostic laparoscopy;
- PET/CT when clinically indicated;
- pathology review;
- multidisciplinary consultation;
- systemic therapy;
- response/restaging;
- selected cytoreductive surgery and intraperitoneal chemotherapy/HIPEC;
- clinical-trial consideration;
- best supportive care according to clinical circumstances.

The source discussion states that treatment decisions for selected IC/HIPEC circumstances should be made in the context of a multidisciplinary tumor board.

### Interpretation

Peritoneal disease demonstrates why MDT is particularly valuable when several clinical modalities must be integrated.

It also provides a concrete example without transferring HIPEC ownership to PP-0232.

# 10. Clinical Interpretation

## 10.1 MDT Is an Integration Mechanism

The strongest evidence-supported interpretation is that MDT exists to bring together complementary expertise.

This matters because gastric cancer treatment can involve multiple interdependent domains.

A single isolated report may be technically accurate while still being insufficient for the full treatment decision.

Examples:

- Imaging may describe anatomy, but surgery must translate anatomy into resectability and operative feasibility.
- Pathology may define histology and biomarkers, but medical oncology integrates these findings with treatment context.
- Nutritional assessment may identify a major treatment-readiness issue that is not visible in a tumor report.
- Radiation expertise may affect local-control strategy and treatment timing.
- Palliative-care input may alter how symptoms and goals are addressed without necessarily ending active treatment.

Therefore:

> **MDT creates a clinical context in which individual data streams can be interpreted together.**

---

# 11. Clinical Interpretation — Joint Review of Data

NCCN's GAST-E emphasis on reviewing actual medical data rather than simply reading reports has an important educational implication.

A multidisciplinary discussion may involve direct review of:

- imaging;
- pathology;
- endoscopy;
- staging information;
- biomarker information;
- prior treatment history;
- clinical condition.

This reduces the risk that one specialty interprets another specialty's information without the necessary context.

The package should therefore explain the distinction between:

**“The report says…”**

and

**“The multidisciplinary team integrates the underlying clinical information to decide what it means for the overall plan.”**

---

# 12. Clinical Interpretation — Staging Before Strategy

NCCN's GAST-E framework supports development of long-term therapeutic strategies after adequate staging and ideally before therapy.

This does not mean every treatment decision is fixed permanently before treatment begins.

It means that:

> **A coordinated strategy should be based on an adequate understanding of the disease before major treatment decisions are made whenever feasible.**

The plan may later be revised.

---

# 13. Clinical Interpretation — Treatment Coordination

MDT can coordinate multiple modalities.

Conceptually:

**Surgery**

↔

**Systemic therapy**

↔

**Radiation**

↔

**Endoscopic therapy**

↔

**Supportive / palliative care**

The arrows indicate coordination, not that every modality is used for every patient.

The correct educational message is:

> **The MDT determines which disciplines need to contribute for the patient's particular clinical problem.**

---

# 14. Clinical Interpretation — Patient Factors

Disease biology is not the only input.

The multidisciplinary strategy can also consider:

- performance status;
- comorbidity;
- nutritional status;
- organ function;
- prior treatment;
- treatment tolerance;
- psychosocial circumstances;
- patient goals.

This is why two patients with apparently similar tumor stages may not receive identical management.

PP-0232 should explain this principle without prescribing individualized treatment.

---

# 15. Clinical Interpretation — Treatment Sequencing

Sequencing is a legitimate MDT question.

Examples:

- whether systemic therapy should precede surgery;
- whether radiation has a role before or after another modality;
- whether a response should trigger re-evaluation;
- whether toxicity requires modification;
- whether a patient remains a candidate for a previously considered local intervention.

However, PP-0232 does not own the detailed answer for each clinical scenario.

Those answers remain in dedicated treatment packages.

---

# 16. Clinical Interpretation — Reassessment

The value of MDT is not limited to the initial diagnosis.

New information may change:

- stage;
- resectability;
- biomarker status/availability;
- response;
- progression;
- toxicity;
- functional status;
- patient goals.

A multidisciplinary team may therefore need to revisit the case.

This creates an iterative model:

**Review**

→ **Plan**

→ **Treat**

→ **Reassess**

→ **Reintegrate**

→ **Adjust strategy**

The model is conceptual and should not be presented as a rigid schedule.

# 18. Evidence Gaps

### Gap 1 — Exact MDT frequency

NCCN GAST-E discusses regular review and gives examples of frequent meetings, but this should not be converted into a universal institutional rule.

### Gap 2 — Exact membership

Core disciplines are identified, but not every patient requires every discipline.

### Gap 3 — MDT outcome metrics

The current project materials do not establish a single universal metric set.

### Gap 4 — Consensus

Multidisciplinary discussion does not guarantee agreement.

### Gap 5 — Patient-specific decision

No source can replace review of the complete individual record by the treating team.

# 21. Patient-Facing Evidence Translation

The evidence should be translated into the following core messages:

### Message 1

Several specialists may be involved because gastric cancer can require several types of expertise.

### Message 2

The purpose is coordination, not simply collecting opinions.

### Message 3

The team may review actual imaging, pathology, and other clinical information together.

### Message 4

Not every patient needs every specialist.

### Message 5

The plan can change when new information appears.

### Message 6

Patient goals remain part of the final individualized decision.

---

# 22. Patient Safety Interpretation

A patient-facing system using this PP should avoid statements such as:

- “The tumor board will decide your treatment.”
- “Your surgeon will decide everything.”
- “If palliative care is involved, cancer treatment is ending.”
- “MDT means you will receive combined treatment.”
- “MDT guarantees the best treatment.”

Safer wording:

> “Your clinical team may bring together several specialists so that the different aspects of your cancer and your overall health can be considered together.”

# 26. Final Evidence Conclusion

The supplied project evidence supports PP-0232 as a distinct and necessary gastric-cancer Population Package.

The strongest source is NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026, which provides both:

1. a dedicated multidisciplinary-team principles section; and
2. explicit placement of multidisciplinary review within gastric-cancer treatment pathways.

ESMO-ASCO further supports multidisciplinary tumour-board participation and coordination of treatment modalities.

The evidence therefore supports the following final proposition:

> **Multidisciplinary management is the clinical integration mechanism through which relevant specialists jointly review disease and patient information, coordinate treatment strategy, and reassess the plan as circumstances change.**

This package should remain at the integration level and should not absorb detailed ownership from modality-specific Population Packages.
