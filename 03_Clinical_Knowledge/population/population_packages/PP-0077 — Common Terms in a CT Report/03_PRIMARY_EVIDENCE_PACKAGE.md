# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What do some common terms in a CT report mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based introduction to **commonly encountered terminology in CT reports**, helping them understand that these words describe imaging observations rather than providing a diagnosis or treatment recommendation.

This Population Package improves health literacy while reinforcing that radiology terminology should always be interpreted together with the patient's overall clinical situation.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Purpose of radiology terminology
- Common descriptive imaging terms
- Role of clinical context
- Appropriate interpretation by patients
- Communication with the healthcare team

---

## Excluded

- Detailed definition of individual radiology terms
- RECIST terminology
- TNM staging
- Differential diagnosis
- Prognostic implications
- Treatment recommendations
- AI-assisted interpretation
- Second-opinion imaging review

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Radiology reports use standardized professional terminology.
- Many commonly encountered terms are descriptive rather than diagnostic.
- The clinical significance of an imaging term depends on the overall medical context.
- Professional interpretation is necessary for accurate understanding.
- Patient-clinician discussion improves health literacy and reduces unnecessary anxiety.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- standardized radiology terminology;
- descriptive reporting of imaging findings;
- multidisciplinary interpretation of imaging results;
- patient education emphasizing clinical context.

Differences between references primarily concern reporting style and specialty-specific terminology, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- multidisciplinary oncology recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- radiology reporting principles from ACR and RSNA.

# Evidence Gaps
