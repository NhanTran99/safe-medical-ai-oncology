# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **How is imaging used after treatment for gastric cancer to reassess the disease, support restaging, and inform the next clinical assessment?**

---

# Educational Intent

This Evidence Package supports a patient-facing Population Package that explains **post-treatment imaging as a clinical reassessment event**.

The evidence synthesis must preserve the distinction between:

1. imaging performed after a treatment milestone;
2. formal response assessment;
3. RECIST-based assessment;
4. long-term surveillance;
5. recurrence detection;
6. treatment decision-making.

The package therefore uses guideline evidence to explain:

**treatment**
→ **appropriate timing**
→ **post-treatment imaging**
→ **comparison**
→ **restaging/response information**
→ **clinical interpretation**

without converting imaging evidence into individualized treatment advice.

# Primary Evidence Sources

## Source 1 — NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

### Role

**Primary disease-specific guideline source.**

### Relevant content

NCCN separates:

- response assessment;
- post-treatment assessment/restaging;
- follow-up/surveillance;
- recurrence.

This architectural separation is directly relevant to PP-0219.

### GAST-6 — Post-treatment Assessment

The relevant NCCN pathway specifies restaging with:

- chest/abdomen/pelvis CT with oral and IV contrast;
- CBC and comprehensive chemistry profile;
- FDG-PET/CT as clinically indicated.

The pathway then distinguishes outcomes such as resectable disease, unresectable disease, metastatic disease, surgery, follow-up, and palliative management.

### GAST-3 / relevant treatment pathways

NCCN states that response assessment guides additional management after primary treatment.

For patients who are medically fit after perioperative systemic therapy and before surgery, chest/abdomen/pelvis CT with contrast is used for response assessment, with FDG-PET/CT as clinically indicated.

For selected MSI-H/dMMR patients receiving preoperative/perioperative immune checkpoint inhibition, NCCN includes:

- FDG-PET/CT as clinically indicated;
- EGD and biopsy;
- chest/abdomen CT with oral and IV contrast, with pelvis CT when clinically indicated.

NCCN specifies a relevant assessment window of approximately 5–8 weeks after therapy in that pathway.

### Important contextual note

NCCN also notes that CT with contrast should be considered when pre-treatment CT findings require more accurate anatomic assessment after treatment, such as small pulmonary nodules, or when post-treatment PET identifies findings needing better anatomical characterization.

### GAST-7 — Follow-up/Surveillance

NCCN separates long-term follow-up/surveillance from post-treatment assessment.

This is critical evidence for the PP-0219 versus PP-0220 boundary.

# Source 2 — ESMO-ASCO Recommendations for a Global Curriculum in Medical Oncology, Edition 2023

### Role

**Professional oncology framework supporting response assessment, imaging selection and clinical interpretation.**

### Relevant content

The response-evaluation section states that:

- RECIST was developed to harmonize response definitions;
- measurement error is an issue in response assessment;
- imaging modality at baseline and during follow-up is important;
- target and non-target disease contribute to assessment;
- new lesions are relevant;
- iRECIST exists for trials testing immunotherapeutics;
- RECIST was developed for clinical trials;
- treatment benefit for an individual patient should be based on medical judgment integrating clinical, imaging and laboratory data;
- tumour-board discussion with imaging specialists is relevant.

### Relevance to PP-0219

This source supports the principle that post-treatment imaging should be:

- clinically contextualized;
- comparable with baseline/prior imaging;
- interpreted with awareness of measurement error;
- integrated with other clinical data.

It also supports the explicit PP-0219 boundary against making imaging or RECIST a stand-alone treatment algorithm.

# Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

## Evidence Note 1 — Post-treatment assessment is a defined clinical node

NCCN GAST-6 explicitly labels a **POST-TREATMENT ASSESSMENT** pathway and identifies restaging as a major component.

This provides direct evidence that PP-0219 is not an artificial educational subdivision.

The clinical concept exists as a distinct step between treatment and later management.

---

## Evidence Note 2 — Restaging is more than “checking whether the tumor shrank”

The NCCN pathway connects restaging with clinically meaningful disease-state categories, including:

- resectable;
- unresectable;
- metastatic.

Therefore PP-0219 must not reduce post-treatment imaging to a simplistic “smaller versus bigger” concept.

Imaging can reassess the overall disease state and the feasibility of subsequent management.

---

## Evidence Note 3 — CT is a major modality

NCCN directly specifies chest/abdomen/pelvis CT with oral and IV contrast in relevant restaging pathways.

This supports a strong but qualified educational message:

> CT is a major post-treatment/restaging modality in gastric cancer.

It does **not** support:

> Every patient must undergo CT after every treatment.

The latter would exceed the evidence and scope.

---

## Evidence Note 4 — PET/CT is clinically selected

NCCN repeatedly uses the phrase **as clinically indicated** for FDG-PET/CT in relevant pathways.

Therefore PET/CT should be presented as a selected tool.

The package should not imply:

- PET/CT is always superior;
- PET/CT is mandatory;
- PET/CT is required for all patients.

---

## Evidence Note 5 — Treatment-specific timing

The NCCN source contains a specific 5–8 week assessment window in a defined preoperative/perioperative immunotherapy pathway.

This is useful because it demonstrates that timing is tied to the treatment context.

The evidence does not support turning that number into a universal gastric-cancer imaging schedule.

---

## Evidence Note 6 — Baseline comparison

ESMO-ASCO explicitly identifies measurement error and the importance of imaging modality at baseline and during follow-up.

This supports the package's longitudinal-comparison framework.

The practical educational implication is:

> A post-treatment scan becomes more informative when it can be interpreted against appropriate prior imaging.

---

## Evidence Note 7 — Imaging is not the entire response assessment

ESMO-ASCO explicitly states that for an individual patient, treatment benefit should be based on medical judgment integrating clinical, imaging and laboratory data.

Therefore PP-0219 should never present:

> scan result = treatment decision.

The scan is one component of the assessment.

---

## Evidence Note 8 — Immunotherapy creates a special context

ESMO-ASCO identifies iRECIST for trials testing immunotherapeutics.

NCCN separately provides a gastric-cancer MSI-H/dMMR pathway with imaging plus EGD/biopsy and a defined post-treatment assessment window.

Together, these sources justify an immunotherapy-specific interface while supporting delegation of technical iRECIST rules.

---

## Evidence Note 9 — Post-treatment assessment versus surveillance

NCCN GAST-6 and GAST-7 are adjacent but distinct.

This is direct evidence for the architectural boundary:

**PP-0219 = post-treatment assessment**

**PP-0220 = surveillance**

---

## Evidence Note 10 — Negative imaging must be interpreted conservatively

A scan assesses what is detectable by the modality at the time of examination.

It cannot establish the absence of every microscopic cancer cell.

Therefore the package uses conservative wording:

> “no detectable evidence of disease on this examination”

rather than:

> “all cancer is gone.”

This is an evidence-integrity and patient-safety distinction.

# Clinical Claims Summary

## Established Clinical Messages

1. Post-treatment imaging is used in gastric-cancer pathways to reassess disease after treatment.
2. Restaging is an important purpose of post-treatment assessment.
3. CT is a major modality for relevant restaging pathways.
4. FDG-PET/CT has selected clinically indicated roles.
5. Selected pathways may use EGD and biopsy alongside imaging.
6. Timing depends on treatment context.
7. Baseline and prior imaging are important for comparison.
8. Imaging modality and measurement error influence response assessment.
9. RECIST is an assessment framework and not a substitute for overall clinical judgment.
10. Surveillance is distinct from immediate/defined post-treatment assessment.

## Context-Dependent Messages

1. Exact imaging timing.
2. CT versus PET/CT versus MRI.
3. Contrast use.
4. Additional EGD/biopsy.
5. Laparoscopy.
6. Interpretation of residual abnormality.
7. Interpretation of new or indeterminate findings.
8. Imaging after immunotherapy.

# Evidence Consistency Review

The primary sources are highly consistent on the major architecture:

### Consistent

- post-treatment assessment exists as a distinct clinical step;
- restaging is clinically meaningful;
- CT is a major imaging modality;
- PET/CT is selected when indicated;
- imaging contributes to response assessment;
- imaging does not replace clinical judgment;
- surveillance is a distinct downstream concept.

### Contextual differences

The sources differ in emphasis because they serve different functions:

- NCCN provides treatment-pathway-specific clinical guidance.
- ESMO-ASCO provides a professional curriculum and response-assessment framework.
- NCI/ACS provide patient-facing education.

These are complementary rather than contradictory.

---

# Evidence Limitations

1. NCCN recommendations are pathway-specific rather than universal.
2. Imaging timing cannot be generalized from one treatment context to all patients.
3. PET/CT use is explicitly clinically indicated rather than universal.
4. Imaging findings can remain indeterminate.
5. Imaging cannot establish microscopic disease status in every circumstance.
6. Response assessment frameworks such as RECIST have defined applicability and do not replace clinical judgment.
7. Patient-specific treatment decisions require information beyond this evidence package.
8. Detailed modality methodology is intentionally outside scope.
