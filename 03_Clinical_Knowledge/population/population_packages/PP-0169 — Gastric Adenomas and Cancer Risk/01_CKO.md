## Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain the central concept of gastric adenomas and cancer risk.
2. Distinguish risk, screening, and diagnosis where relevant.
3. Understand the main evidence-supported findings.
4. Understand important limitations and uncertainties.
5. Avoid interpreting study-specific findings as universal rules.
6. Recognize how the topic connects to adjacent gastric-cancer knowledge packages.
7. Understand what questions require individualized clinical assessment.

# Clinical Knowledge Blocks

## Knowledge Block 1 — What Is a Gastric Adenoma?

### Patient Explanation

A gastric adenoma is a type of gastric polyp with epithelial neoplastic features. Gastric adenomas make up approximately 10% of gastric polyps in the NCI Genetics PDQ evidence base.

### Clinical Importance

The package establishes the adenoma as a clinically meaningful precursor-type lesion rather than treating all gastric polyps as equivalent.

### Key Concepts

- What Is a Gastric Adenoma?
- Evidence-supported interpretation
- Appropriate uncertainty

## Knowledge Block 2 — Are All Gastric Polyps Adenomas?

### Patient Explanation

No. Gastric polyps are a heterogeneous group. The presence of a polyp does not by itself establish that it is an adenoma or that it has the same cancer risk.

### Clinical Importance

This distinction prevents overgeneralization from adenomas to all gastric polyps.

### Key Concepts

- Are All Gastric Polyps Adenomas?
- Evidence-supported interpretation
- Appropriate uncertainty

## Knowledge Block 3 — Why Do Gastric Adenomas Matter for Cancer Risk?

### Patient Explanation

Gastric adenomatous polyps are recognized by NCI as a gastric-cancer risk/precursor condition. Their significance depends on their pathological characteristics rather than simply on the fact that a polyp is present.

### Clinical Importance

Risk should therefore be interpreted through the lesion's histology and other clinical context.

### Key Concepts

- Why Do Gastric Adenomas Matter for Cancer Risk?
- Evidence-supported interpretation
- Appropriate uncertainty

## Knowledge Block 4 — What Determines Malignant Potential?

### Patient Explanation

The NCI Genetics PDQ states that malignant potential is determined by histological subtype and size. Pyloric gland adenomas and intestinal-type gastric adenomas have the highest malignant potentials in the cited evidence, while foveolar-type adenomas have the lowest.

### Clinical Importance

These differences mean that 'gastric adenoma' is not a single-risk category.

### Key Concepts

- What Determines Malignant Potential?
- Evidence-supported interpretation
- Appropriate uncertainty

## Knowledge Block 5 — Why Does Histologic Subtype Matter?

### Patient Explanation

Different adenoma subtypes have different observed malignant potential. Histology therefore provides clinically important information about the nature of the lesion.

### Clinical Importance

Detailed histopathologic classification belongs to downstream pathology packages.

### Key Concepts

- Why Does Histologic Subtype Matter?

## Knowledge Block 6 — Why Does Size Matter?

### Patient Explanation

The NCI states that size contributes to malignant potential. The package does not create a universal size threshold because the supplied source does not establish one universal patient-facing cutoff.

### Clinical Importance

Size is therefore a risk-modifying feature, not a standalone diagnosis.

### Key Concepts

## Knowledge Block 7 — How Do Adenomas Relate to Dysplasia?

### Patient Explanation

Gastric adenomas may contain dysplastic epithelial changes. Dysplasia represents abnormal epithelial development and is an important component of the precursor-lesion framework.

### Clinical Importance

Detailed grading and pathology interpretation are delegated downstream.

### Key Concepts

- How Do Adenomas Relate to Dysplasia?
- Evidence-supported interpretation

## Knowledge Block 8 — How Do Adenomas Fit Into Gastric Carcinogenesis?

### Patient Explanation

The broader gastric carcinogenesis pathway may involve chronic gastritis, atrophy, intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia, and ultimately cancer. Adenomas are a distinct precursor-type lesion and should not be collapsed into the Correa cascade.

### Clinical Importance

This distinction preserves ownership with PP-0165/0166 and downstream pathology.

### Key Concepts

- How Do Adenomas Fit Into Gastric Carcinogenesis?
- Evidence-supported interpretation
- Appropriate uncertainty

## Knowledge Block 9 — Do Adenomas Mean Cancer Is Inevitable?

### Patient Explanation

No. Increased risk or malignant potential does not mean that every adenoma becomes cancer.

### Clinical Importance

The patient-facing message should avoid deterministic language.

### Key Concepts

- Do Adenomas Mean Cancer Is Inevitable?

## Knowledge Block 10 — Which Clinical Contexts Increase Concern?

### Patient Explanation

The supplied NCI sources identify sporadic gastric adenomas as one subgroup associated with elevated gastric-cancer risk. Familial adenomatous polyposis and other hereditary contexts can also involve gastric lesions and increased risk.

### Clinical Importance

Hereditary syndrome assessment is outside this package.

## Knowledge Block 11 — What About Familial Adenomatous Polyposis?

### Patient Explanation

NCI Genetics PDQ describes gastric polyps and adenomas as part of the lesion spectrum in familial adenomatous polyposis. High-risk lesions include tubular adenomas, polyps with high-grade dysplasia, and pyloric gland adenomas.

### Clinical Importance

The syndrome-level genetic assessment belongs to hereditary/genetics packages.

## Knowledge Block 12 — What About GAPPS?

### Patient Explanation

NCI Genetics PDQ identifies gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach (GAPPS) as an APC-associated hereditary syndrome with significantly increased gastric-cancer risk, while noting that exact risk numbers are unknown.

### Clinical Importance

This package uses GAPPS only as context for why adenomas can occur in hereditary settings.

### Patient Explanation

## Knowledge Block 16 — How Should Patients Understand the Finding?

### Patient Explanation

A gastric adenoma is not synonymous with gastric cancer, but it is not an incidental finding that can be interpreted without context. Its subtype, size, dysplasia and clinical setting matter.

### Clinical Importance

The appropriate next step is professional evaluation rather than assuming either cancer or no risk.

# Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1
**“A gastric adenoma is already gastric cancer.”**

**Fact:** False. An adenoma is a distinct lesion; malignant potential is not the same as established carcinoma.

### Myth 2
**“Every gastric adenoma has the same cancer risk.”**

**Fact:** False. NCI identifies subtype and size as determinants of malignant potential.

### Myth 3
**“A small adenoma can never matter.”**

**Fact:** False. Size contributes to risk, but size alone does not determine the complete clinical significance.

### Myth 4
**“All gastric polyps are adenomas.”**

**Fact:** False. Gastric polyps are heterogeneous.

### Myth 5
**“An adenoma always becomes cancer.”**

**Fact:** False. Malignant potential is not inevitability.


# Key Messages

1. Gastric Adenomas and Cancer Risk is a clinically meaningful concept, but it should not be interpreted as a diagnosis by itself.
2. The supplied evidence must be interpreted in its population and study context.
3. Study-specific estimates should not be converted into universal thresholds unless the source supports that use.
4. Adjacent clinical decisions belong to their dedicated Population Packages.
5. When evidence is uncertain, the uncertainty should remain explicit.
