# 3. Primary Educational Question

> **What are immune checkpoint inhibitors, how do they work, which checkpoint-inhibitor agents and treatment strategies are used in gastric cancer, and what should patients understand about their benefits, limitations, response patterns and characteristic immune-related toxicities?**

# 4. Intended Runtime Usage

This package is intended to support:

- patient education about checkpoint inhibitors;
- clinician-facing retrieval of foundational checkpoint concepts;
- explanation of PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 mechanisms;
- explanation of why checkpoint inhibitors may be combined with chemotherapy;
- explanation of biomarker-treatment relationships;
- explanation of major gastric-cancer checkpoint-inhibitor evidence;
- patient preparation before treatment discussions;
- safe explanation of immune-related adverse-event awareness;
- linkage to biomarker-testing and response/toxicity packages.

It is not intended to function as:

- a prescribing monograph;
- a dosing reference;
- an irAE management protocol;
- a biomarker laboratory manual;
- a RECIST/iRECIST manual;
- an individualized treatment recommender.

# 7. Knowledge Units

## KU-01 — Immune checkpoint concept

**Type:** Foundational  
**Priority:** Core

An immune checkpoint is a regulatory pathway that limits immune activation. Cancer can exploit these pathways to reduce immune attack.

---

## KU-02 — PD-1/PD-L1

**Type:** Mechanistic  
**Priority:** Core

PD-1 on immune cells interacts with PD-L1 and can reduce T-cell activity. Blocking PD-1 or PD-L1 can increase anti-tumor immune activity.

---

## KU-03 — CTLA-4

**Type:** Mechanistic  
**Priority:** Core/supporting

CTLA-4 is another immune checkpoint. Its gastric-cancer relevance is principally represented by dual-checkpoint therapy with nivolumab.

---

## KU-04 — Major agents

**Type:** Treatment-class  
**Priority:** Core

Nivolumab, pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, durvalumab, dostarlimab and selected ipilimumab combination use.

---

## KU-05 — Combination therapy

**Type:** Clinical application  
**Priority:** Core

Checkpoint inhibitors can be combined with chemotherapy and, in selected settings, targeted therapy.

---

## KU-06 — Biomarker context

**Type:** Precision oncology  
**Priority:** Core

PD-L1 and MSI-H/dMMR can materially change the context in which checkpoint inhibitors are considered.

---

## KU-07 — Landmark evidence

**Type:** Evidence interpretation  
**Priority:** Core

CheckMate-649, KEYNOTE-859, RATIONALE-305, KEYNOTE-811 and MATTERHORN.

---

## KU-08 — Response

**Type:** Monitoring concept  
**Priority:** Core

Durable response, pseudoprogression, mixed response and hyperprogression.

---

## KU-09 — Resistance

**Type:** Treatment limitation  
**Priority:** Core

Primary and acquired resistance.

---

## KU-10 — Safety

**Type:** Patient safety  
**Priority:** Core

Immune-related adverse events, infusion reactions and prompt symptom reporting.

# 8. Evidence Classification

## Established / guideline-supported

- checkpoint inhibitors are an established immunotherapy class in selected gastric-cancer settings;
- PD-1/PD-L1 blockade is central to current gastric-cancer checkpoint treatment;
- nivolumab, pembrolizumab and tislelizumab have major advanced-disease evidence;
- durvalumab has perioperative evidence;
- pembrolizumab has a selected HER2-positive combination role;
- MSI-H/dMMR supports selected checkpoint-inhibitor use;
- PD-L1 is relevant to several first-line ICI strategies;
- checkpoint inhibitors can be combined with chemotherapy;
- selected checkpoint inhibitors can be combined with targeted therapy;
- immune-related adverse events are a characteristic toxicity class.

## Context-dependent

- exact agent choice;
- monotherapy versus combination;
- treatment line;
- perioperative use;
- dual-checkpoint therapy;
- treatment duration;
- later-line use;
- rechallenge.

## Evidence-limited / caution required

- TMB-H as a gastric-specific predictive biomarker;
- cross-trial comparison of different checkpoint inhibitors;
- universal claims that one ICI is superior;
- assuming biomarker positivity guarantees response;
- assuming apparent progression is pseudoprogression.

# 13. Safety / Runtime Rules

1. Do not interpret the package as an individualized treatment plan.
2. Do not provide dosing from this PP.
3. Do not infer treatment eligibility solely from one biomarker.
4. Do not treat PD-L1 CPS, TAP and other scoring systems as interchangeable.
5. Do not state that MSI-H guarantees response.
6. Do not label all apparent progression as pseudoprogression.
7. Do not minimize new symptoms during checkpoint treatment.
8. Direct users with concerning new symptoms to their treating clinical team.
9. Do not use this package to override a current disease-specific guideline or treating-team recommendation.
10. Retrieve PP-0231 for detailed toxicity management.
11. Retrieve PP-0215 for detailed MSI-H/dMMR treatment context.
12. Retrieve PP-0216 for detailed PD-L1-guided treatment context.
13. Retrieve PP-0217–0219 for formal response assessment.
14. Retrieve PP-0210 for detailed HER2-targeted therapy.
15. Keep patient-facing explanations neutral and evidence-calibrated.

# 14. Runtime Decision Logic

When a user asks:

**“What is a checkpoint inhibitor?”**

→ use KU-01 to KU-03.

When a user asks:

**“Which checkpoint inhibitors are used in gastric cancer?”**

→ use KU-04 plus treatment-setting context.

When a user asks:

**“Why do I need PD-L1 testing?”**

→ explain the treatment-context bridge, then retrieve PP-0183/PP-0216 for detail.

When a user asks:

**“Why does MSI-H matter?”**

→ explain the checkpoint-treatment bridge, then retrieve PP-0182/PP-0215.

When a user asks:

**“What side effect should I worry about?”**

→ provide high-level safety awareness and retrieve PP-0231 for detailed management.

When a user asks:

**“Has my tumor progressed?”**

→ do not use PP-0214 as the response-assessment authority; retrieve PP-0217–0219.

When a user asks:

**“Which ICI is best for me?”**

→ do not provide an individualized selection; explain that selection depends on clinical and biomarker context and should be discussed with the treating team.
