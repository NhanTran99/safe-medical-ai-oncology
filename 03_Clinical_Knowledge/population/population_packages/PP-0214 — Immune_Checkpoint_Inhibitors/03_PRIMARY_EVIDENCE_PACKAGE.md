# 6. Landmark Evidence Summaries

## 6.1 CheckMate-649

**Population:** treatment-naïve advanced HER2-negative gastric/EGJ/esophageal adenocarcinoma.

**Intervention:** nivolumab + chemotherapy.

**Comparator:** chemotherapy.

**Key evidence represented in NCCN v2.2026:**

- 3-year OS in PD-L1 CPS ≥5: 14.4 vs 11.1 months;
- HR 0.70;
- PFS 8.3 vs 6.1 months in the reported population;
- ORR around 60% in the cited analysis.

**MSI-H subgroup:**

- median OS 38.7 vs 12.3 months;
- HR 0.34 in all randomized MSI-H patients.

**Dual-checkpoint subgroup:**

Nivolumab + ipilimumab did not improve broad-population outcomes over chemotherapy, but MSI-H tumors showed improved OS and response.

**Interpretive safeguard:**

Do not treat the MSI-H subgroup result as evidence that dual checkpoint inhibition benefits all gastric cancers.

---

## 6.2 KEYNOTE-859

**Population:** treatment-naïve advanced HER2-negative gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

**Intervention:** pembrolizumab + chemotherapy.

**Key evidence represented in project source:**

PD-L1 CPS ≥10:

- OS 15.7 vs 11.8 months;
- HR 0.65;
- PFS 8.1 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.62;
- ORR 61% vs 43%.

PD-L1 CPS ≥1:

- OS 13.0 vs 11.4 months;
- HR 0.74;
- PFS 6.9 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.72;
- ORR 52% vs 43%.

**Interpretive safeguard:**

The trial does not establish that pembrolizumab is universally superior to all other checkpoint inhibitors.

---

## 6.3 RATIONALE-305

**Population:** previously untreated unresectable locally advanced or metastatic gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

**Intervention:** tislelizumab + chemotherapy.

**Comparator:** placebo + chemotherapy.

**Evidence represented in project source:**

- OS 15.0 vs 12.9 months;
- HR 0.80;
- PFS 6.9 vs 6.2 months;
- HR 0.78.

PD-L1 TAP ≥5%:

- OS 17.2 vs 12.6 months;
- HR 0.74;
- PFS 7.2 vs 5.9 months;
- HR 0.67.

**Interpretive safeguard:**

TAP and CPS are not automatically interchangeable scoring systems.

---

## 6.4 KEYNOTE-811

**Population:** advanced HER2-positive gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

**Intervention:** pembrolizumab + trastuzumab + chemotherapy.

**Evidence represented in project source:**

- median PFS 10.0 vs 8.1 months;
- PD-L1 CPS ≥1 subgroup PFS 10.8 vs 7.2 months;
- HR 0.70 in that subgroup;
- ORR 72.6% vs 59.8%;
- median duration of response 11.2 vs 9 months.

**Interpretive safeguard:**

The checkpoint-inhibitor component belongs to PP-0214; detailed HER2 therapy belongs to PP-0210.

---

## 6.5 MATTERHORN

**Clinical context:** perioperative gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

**Intervention concept:** durvalumab integrated with FLOT around surgery.

**Clinical significance:**

This trial provides a major evidence bridge from metastatic checkpoint treatment to perioperative treatment.

**Interpretive safeguard:**

Do not convert this into a universal recommendation for every resectable patient.

# 7. Biomarker Evidence

## 7.1 PD-L1

Current NCCN v2.2026 places PD-L1 in multiple checkpoint-inhibitor treatment pathways.

Examples include:

- nivolumab + chemotherapy in selected HER2-negative advanced disease;
- pembrolizumab + chemotherapy in selected HER2-negative advanced disease;
- tislelizumab + chemotherapy in selected HER2-negative advanced disease;
- pembrolizumab + trastuzumab + chemotherapy in selected HER2-positive advanced disease.

The exact scoring and testing rules are not owned by this Evidence Package.

**Delegation:** PP-0183 and PP-0216.

---

## 7.2 MSI-H/dMMR

Current NCCN v2.2026 supports multiple checkpoint-inhibitor strategies for MSI-H/dMMR tumors.

These include:

- pembrolizumab;
- nivolumab + chemotherapy;
- nivolumab + ipilimumab;
- dostarlimab;
- selected perioperative checkpoint strategies.

The key clinical principle is that MSI-H/dMMR can be treatment-defining independently of PD-L1 in selected contexts.

**Delegation:** PP-0182 and PP-0215.

---

## 7.3 TMB-H

The project source supports pembrolizumab in selected TMB-H tumor-agnostic settings.

However, NCCN explicitly notes that the KEYNOTE-158 TMB analysis contained no gastroesophageal cancer patients.

Therefore:

**TMB-H = relevant but evidence-limited gastric-specific context.**

**Delegation:** TMB-specific package.

Potential organ systems include:

- lungs;
- intestines;
- liver;
- endocrine glands;
- kidneys;
- skin;
- other organs.

## Professional curriculum

ESMO-ASCO emphasizes:

- immune-related adverse events;
- distinguishing toxicity from progression;
- skin, endocrine, gastrointestinal, pulmonary and hepatic systems;
- multidisciplinary management.

**Boundary:**

This Evidence Package identifies and contextualizes safety; it does not prescribe toxicity management.

# 9. Response Evidence

ESMO-ASCO recognizes:

- late responses;
- regression after apparent progression;
- pseudoprogression;
- mixed response;
- hyperprogression.

These concepts are relevant to PP-0214 because they are characteristic of checkpoint-inhibitor treatment.

However:

> Formal response classification belongs to PP-0217 and PP-0218.

---

# 10. Resistance Evidence

ESMO-ASCO recognizes:

- primary resistance;
- adaptive/acquired resistance;
- combination strategies intended to overcome resistance.

PP-0214 should explain these concepts but should not create a detailed molecular resistance algorithm.

---

# 11. Evidence Gaps

1. The supplied Source Files do not provide one universal framework for selecting among all checkpoint inhibitors in every clinical scenario.
2. Cross-trial comparisons are limited because trials differ in population, biomarker criteria, comparator, treatment backbone and endpoint.
3. TMB-H evidence has explicit gastric-specific limitations.
4. PD-L1 scoring systems differ between trials; CPS and TAP should not be treated as universally interchangeable.
5. Individual response cannot be guaranteed by a biomarker.
6. Individual treatment selection requires current clinical assessment.
7. Detailed irAE management is delegated.
8. Detailed formal response assessment is delegated.
9. Detailed resistance mechanisms are delegated.
10. Regulatory approval and guideline positioning can change and require periodic review.

These gaps do not block PP-0214 because the approved scope is an educational treatment-class package rather than an individualized decision algorithm.

# 16. Clinical Claims Summary

The project evidence supports the following high-confidence conclusions:

1. Immune checkpoint inhibitors are a major class of immunotherapy.
2. PD-1/PD-L1 blockade is central to gastric-cancer checkpoint treatment.
3. CTLA-4 inhibition has a selected role in dual-checkpoint therapy.
4. Nivolumab, pembrolizumab and tislelizumab have major advanced-disease evidence.
5. Durvalumab has perioperative evidence.
6. Dostarlimab has an MSI-H/dMMR treatment context.
7. Checkpoint inhibitors may be used alone or in combination.
8. Chemotherapy combinations are central to current treatment.
9. Pembrolizumab can be combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy in selected HER2-positive disease.
10. PD-L1 status can influence checkpoint-inhibitor treatment context.
11. MSI-H/dMMR can be treatment-defining independently of PD-L1 in selected contexts.
12. TMB-H has a more limited gastric-specific evidence base.
13. CheckMate-649, KEYNOTE-859 and RATIONALE-305 support major advanced-disease strategies.
14. KEYNOTE-811 supports checkpoint inhibition integrated with HER2-targeted therapy.
15. MATTERHORN provides perioperative checkpoint evidence.
16. Checkpoint inhibitors can produce durable responses in selected patients.
17. Resistance can be primary or acquired.
18. Pseudoprogression and other unconventional response patterns can occur.
19. Immune-related adverse events are a distinctive safety issue.
20. Individual treatment selection cannot be reduced to one biomarker or one drug.
