# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is PM3 (Detected in Trans with a Pathogenic Variant for Recessive Disorders)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **PM3 (Detected in Trans with a Pathogenic Variant for Recessive Disorders)** as one of the standardized **ACMG/AMP Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes**.

This Population Package explains that **PM3 represents moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity when a genetic variant is identified on the opposite copy of a gene (in trans) from a known pathogenic variant in an appropriate recessive genetic disorder, according to accepted professional standards.** It emphasizes that PM3 represents **one category of scientific evidence**, not a diagnosis or final variant classification.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- PM3 is one standardized ACMG/AMP evidence code.
- Most people have two copies of most genes.
- Finding variants on opposite gene copies may provide moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity in appropriate recessive disorders.
- PM3 contributes one category of evidence within structured variant interpretation.
- Final variant classification always requires comprehensive professional evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **PM3** as one of the standardized **Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes** within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that PM3 may be applied when a variant is identified **in trans** with a known pathogenic variant in an appropriate recessive disorder. ClinGen SVI recommendations further refine the application of PM3 while emphasizing that **the configuration of variants across the two copies of a gene represents one component of structured evidence-based interpretation rather than an independent diagnostic conclusion.**
