After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what BP5 is.
- Understand what an alternate molecular basis for disease means.
- Understand why finding another convincing genetic cause may provide supporting benign evidence.
- Recognize why the alternative cause must actually explain the patient's disease.
- Understand why BP5 requires particular caution in recessive disorders.
- Recognize that BP5 alone cannot determine that a variant is benign.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is BP5?

## Patient Explanation

**BP5** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant is likely to be **benign (not disease-causing).**

BP5 represents **Supporting Benign Evidence**.

It may be considered when a patient has another genetic finding that provides a convincing **alternative molecular explanation** for the disease.

---

## Clinical Importance

BP5 can provide supporting evidence that the variant being evaluated may not be the cause of the patient's disease.

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Is an Alternate Molecular Basis?

## Patient Explanation

Sometimes the same disease or a similar clinical condition can have **more than one genetic cause**.

An alternate molecular basis means that another genetic finding has been identified that provides a convincing explanation for why the patient has the disease.

When this alternative explanation fits the patient's clinical situation, the original variant may be less likely to be responsible for the disease.

---

## Clinical Importance

The presence of another genetic finding must be interpreted in the context of the patient's actual disease.

---

## Key Concepts

- Alternative genetic cause

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Can BP5 Support a Benign Interpretation?

## Patient Explanation

If another genetic finding already provides a convincing explanation for the patient's disease, there may be less reason to believe that the variant being evaluated is the cause of that disease.

This can provide **supporting benign evidence** for the variant under evaluation.

However, this conclusion depends on whether the alternative genetic cause truly explains the patient's disease.

---

## Clinical Importance

BP5 is a **context-dependent** evidence criterion rather than an automatic rule.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Must BP5 Be Used Carefully?

## Patient Explanation

Finding another genetic variant does **not automatically** mean that the first variant is harmless.

For example, in some **recessive disorders**, a person may carry a disease-causing variant in one gene while the actual disease is caused by genetic changes in another gene.

In addition, some diseases can involve more than one genetic factor.

Therefore, genetics professionals must determine whether the alternative molecular finding really explains the patient's disease before BP5 is applied.

---

## Clinical Importance

The ACMG/AMP framework and ClinGen guidance specifically caution against treating every alternate genetic finding as sufficient evidence for BP5. BP5 is generally stronger in appropriate dominant-disorder contexts and requires additional caution in recessive disorders. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does BP5 Mean the Variant Is Definitely Benign?

## Patient Explanation

No.

BP5 **does not prove** that a variant is benign.

It provides one piece of supporting evidence that must be interpreted together with the patient's clinical findings and other genetic and scientific evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification requires integration of multiple evidence categories.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

If another pathogenic genetic variant is found, the variant being evaluated must be benign.

**Fact**

Not necessarily.

The alternative genetic finding must provide a **convincing explanation for the patient's disease**. Some disorders can involve multiple genetic factors, and special caution is needed in recessive disorders.

---

# Key Messages

- BP5 is a **Supporting Benign Evidence** criterion.
- BP5 considers whether another genetic finding provides an alternate molecular explanation for disease.
- The alternative explanation must fit the patient's clinical situation.
- BP5 is context-dependent and requires particular caution in recessive disorders.
- BP5 alone cannot establish that a variant is benign.
- Final variant classification requires integration of multiple types of evidence.
