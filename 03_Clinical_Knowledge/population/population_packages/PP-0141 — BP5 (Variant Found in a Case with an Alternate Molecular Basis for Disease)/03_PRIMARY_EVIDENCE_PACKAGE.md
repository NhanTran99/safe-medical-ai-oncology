# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is BP5 (Variant Found in a Case with an Alternate Molecular Basis for Disease)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **BP5 (Variant Found in a Case with an Alternate Molecular Basis for Disease)** as one of the standardized **Supporting Benign Evidence** criteria within the **ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework**.

BP5 is used when a variant is found in a patient who has a **clear alternate genetic cause of the disease**. In this situation, the alternate molecular finding may provide a more convincing explanation for the patient's phenotype, thereby supporting the interpretation that the variant under evaluation is less likely to be disease-causing. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

However, the presence of another genetic finding does **not automatically** make the evaluated variant benign. The alternative molecular explanation must be clinically appropriate, and BP5 requires particular caution in disorders where more than one genetic variant may contribute to disease or where an individual may carry an unrelated pathogenic variant. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- BP5 is an individual Supporting Benign Evidence criterion.
- BP5 considers whether another molecular finding provides an alternate explanation for disease.
- A convincing alternative molecular diagnosis can reduce the likelihood that the evaluated variant explains the patient's phenotype.
- The alternative explanation must be clinically appropriate.
- BP5 is context-dependent and requires caution in recessive and genetically complex disorders.
- BP5 alone cannot establish benign classification.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

The core ACMG/AMP framework defines BP5 as evidence arising when a variant is found in a case with an **alternate molecular basis for disease**. The guideline specifically cautions that an individual may carry an unrelated pathogenic variant for a recessive disorder and that some disorders can involve multiple variants contributing to disease. Therefore, BP5 should not be treated as an automatic consequence of finding another pathogenic variant. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

ClinGen-derived and disease-specific implementations further demonstrate that BP5 is **context-dependent**. For example, expert-panel specifications may restrict BP5 according to the penetrance of the alternate disorder and whether the patient's personal or family history is compatible with overlapping phenotypes. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Accordingly, this Population Package deliberately presents BP5 as a **conditional supporting evidence concept**, rather than as a universal rule that an alternative pathogenic finding makes the evaluated variant benign.
