# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does "nodule" mean in a CT report?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the medical term **"nodule"**, emphasizing that it is a **descriptive imaging term** referring to a relatively small focal tissue abnormality rather than a diagnosis.

This Population Package aims to reduce misunderstanding by explaining that nodules have many possible causes and that imaging findings alone usually cannot determine exactly what a nodule represents.

---

- Nodule as descriptive terminology

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- A nodule is an imaging description rather than a diagnosis.
- Many different medical conditions can produce a nodule.
- A nodule does not automatically indicate cancer.
- Small size alone cannot determine clinical significance.
- Accurate diagnosis requires integration of imaging findings with the patient's complete clinical picture.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the descriptive meaning of the term "nodule";
- multidisciplinary interpretation of imaging findings;
- integration of imaging with clinical evaluation;
- avoidance of diagnostic conclusions based solely on imaging terminology.

Differences between references primarily concern organ-specific definitions, follow-up algorithms and imaging protocols, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- multidisciplinary oncology recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- radiology terminology principles from ACR and RSNA.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- lesion versus mass versus nodule;
- pulmonary, thyroid or liver nodule guidelines;
- imaging features suggesting malignancy;
- surveillance intervals;
- biopsy;
- treatment planning.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
