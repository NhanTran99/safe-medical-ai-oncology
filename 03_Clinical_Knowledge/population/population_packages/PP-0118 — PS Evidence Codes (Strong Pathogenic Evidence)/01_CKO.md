# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what PS Evidence Codes are.
- Understand that PS codes represent strong evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- Recognize that PS codes are a family of ACMG Evidence Codes rather than a single evidence code.
- Understand that different PS codes represent different categories of scientific evidence.
- Recognize that PS codes contribute to, but do not determine, the final variant classification.
- Feel more confident understanding discussions about professional genomic interpretation.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Are PS Evidence Codes?

## Patient Explanation

**PS Evidence Codes** are a family of standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** that represent **strong scientific evidence supporting the possibility that a genetic variant causes disease**.

Instead of being a single code, PS refers to several different evidence codes that each represent a different type of strong evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

PS codes provide standardized documentation of strong pathogenic evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- PS
- Strong evidence
- ACMG Evidence Codes

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Are There Several PS Codes?

## Patient Explanation

Different types of scientific evidence can support the conclusion that a variant may be disease-causing.

Rather than treating every type of evidence the same way, the ACMG framework assigns different PS codes to different categories of strong evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

Different evidence types are evaluated separately before being integrated into the overall interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Different evidence types
- Structured evaluation
- Scientific evidence

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Do PS Codes Mean the Variant Is Already Classified as Pathogenic?

## Patient Explanation

No.

PS codes represent **strong evidence**, but they are **not the final classification**.

Healthcare professionals combine PS codes with many other evidence codes before deciding whether a variant should be classified as pathogenic, likely pathogenic, uncertain, likely benign, or benign.

---

## Clinical Importance

Professional interpretation always considers the complete body of available evidence.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Final classification
- Professional interpretation

# Knowledge Block 4 — Do Patients Need to Learn Individual PS Codes?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Most patients do not need to know the differences between PS1, PS2, PS3, or PS4.

The important point is that experts are using an internationally standardized system to evaluate different kinds of scientific evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding the overall framework is generally more useful than memorizing individual evidence codes.

---

## Key Concepts

- Patient education
- Standardization
- Clinical genomics

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Are PS Evidence Codes Important?

## Patient Explanation

PS Evidence Codes help laboratories evaluate genetic variants consistently.

Using standardized evidence codes improves communication between laboratories, healthcare professionals, and patients.

---

## Clinical Importance

Standardized evidence codes improve transparency, reproducibility, and consistency across genomic medicine.

---

## Key Concepts

- Transparency
- Reproducibility
- Standardized reporting

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

A PS code means the laboratory has already proven that a genetic variant causes disease.

**Fact**

A PS code represents one category of **strong supporting evidence**. Final classification always depends on the combined evaluation of multiple evidence sources.

---

# Key Messages

- PS represents a family of ACMG Evidence Codes.
- PS codes indicate strong evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- Different PS codes represent different categories of scientific evidence.
- PS codes contribute to—but do not determine—the final variant classification.
- PS codes are professional tools used within the ACMG framework.
