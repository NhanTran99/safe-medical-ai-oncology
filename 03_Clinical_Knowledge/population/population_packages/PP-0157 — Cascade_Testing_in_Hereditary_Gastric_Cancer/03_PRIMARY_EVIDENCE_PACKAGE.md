# Primary Clinical Question

> **What is cascade genetic testing in hereditary gastric cancer, who may be offered testing after a familial pathogenic variant is identified, how does testing move through a family, and what factors can affect whether relatives complete testing?**

# Core Evidence 1 — Definition and Starting Point

NCI defines cascade testing as offering genetic testing to biological family members at risk of inheriting a familial pathogenic variant previously identified in a relative.

The process can repeat as additional pathogenic-variant carriers are identified.

This establishes the central PP-0157 pathway:

**Known familial pathogenic variant**

→ **at-risk relatives**

→ **familial-variant testing**

→ **additional carriers**

→ **their at-risk relatives**. fileciteturn24file13

---

# Core Evidence 2 — Why Cascade Testing Matters

NCI states that cascade testing can identify pathogenic-variant carriers before cancer presentation, creating opportunities for:

- cancer prevention;
- early detection;
- risk reduction;
- improved health outcomes.

The package therefore frames cascade testing as an **identification pathway**, not as a treatment itself. fileciteturn24file13

---

# Core Evidence 3 — Family Disclosure

NCI states that dissemination of genetic-risk information from the proband to at-risk relatives is essential for cascade-testing uptake.

Traditional approaches include:

- discussion between the genetics provider and proband;
- encouraging the proband to relay results;
- family letters;
- educational materials.

fileciteturn24file13

This supports family communication as a core component of PP-0157.

---

# Core Evidence 4 — Cascade Uptake Is Incomplete

The NCI review summarizes studies largely conducted in hereditary breast and ovarian cancer and Lynch syndrome families.

Reported disclosure and cascade-testing uptake varied considerably.

Therefore the package uses the general conclusion:

> **Identification of a familial pathogenic variant does not guarantee that every at-risk relative will complete testing.**

It does **not** transfer disease-specific percentages from HBOC/Lynch studies to HDGC.

This preserves evidence integrity.

---

# Core Evidence 5 — Communication Barriers

NCI identifies barriers including:

- loss of contact information;
- lack of close relationships;
- guilt;
- anxiety;
- concern that relatives will not understand results;
- fear of emotional distress;
- negative family dynamics.

Communication strength and comfort discussing health information can influence disclosure. fileciteturn24file15

This supports inclusion of communication barriers as a substantive knowledge block.

---

# Core Evidence 6 — Information Comprehension

NCI reports that some first-degree relatives found information about a proband's genetic results difficult to understand and that some relatives could not accurately recall the result.

Therefore cascade testing is not simply an information-transfer problem.

It is also a:

> **comprehension + decision-making + access problem.**

fileciteturn24file15

---

# Core Evidence 7 — Strategies to Facilitate Cascade Testing

NCI describes several approaches, including:

- written materials;
- family letters;
- facilitated cascade testing;
- telephone counseling;
- direct provider involvement in selected models.

These approaches are presented as strategies that may improve disclosure/testing, not as a universal mandatory workflow.

fileciteturn24file13turn24file16

---

# Core Evidence 8 — International Variation

NCI explicitly notes that cascade-testing protocols vary internationally.

The current NCI section focuses on the United States.

Therefore PP-0157 should distinguish:

### Universal principle

> Identify at-risk relatives and offer appropriate familial-variant testing.

### Context-dependent implementation

- who contacts relatives;
- how counseling is delivered;
- how testing is accessed;
- privacy/legal framework;
- health-system workflow.

fileciteturn24file13

---

# Core Evidence 9 — Gastric-Cancer Context

The gastric-genetics and HDGC materials establish hereditary gastric cancer as a clinically meaningful germline cancer-susceptibility context, including CDH1- and CTNNA1-associated disease. fileciteturn24file15turn24file18

This provides the disease-specific setting in which the general cascade-testing framework is applied.

The current source set does **not** establish one standalone HDGC-specific cascade-testing protocol or universal uptake percentage.

---

# Core Evidence 10 — Positive Cascade Result

A relative who carries the known familial pathogenic variant becomes a carrier within the family cascade.

That result can:

- identify hereditary susceptibility;
- trigger syndrome-specific risk management;
- create a new opportunity to test that carrier's at-risk biological relatives.

PP-0157 owns the **cascade transition**.

Gene-specific interpretation and management belong downstream.

---

# Core Evidence 11 — Negative Cascade Result

A relative who does not carry the known familial pathogenic variant does not become a carrier node for that specific familial variant.

However:

> **negative for the known familial variant is not equivalent to zero cancer risk from all causes.**

The broader meaning of personal/family risk remains outside this package.

---

# Core Evidence 12 — Counseling Boundary

Genetic counseling is an important component of cascade testing.

But the ownership distinction is:

### PP-0156

> **Counseling and informed decision-making.**

### PP-0157

> **Family-level cascade-testing pathway.**

PP-0157 can explain why counseling is offered to relatives without duplicating the full counseling package.

---

# Core Evidence 13 — Privacy and Ethical Context

Family genetic information creates potential tension between:

- informing relatives who may benefit;
- protecting the tested person's privacy;
- respecting autonomy;
- following local legal and ethical requirements.

The NCI source discusses ethical/legal issues around communication to relatives and healthcare-professional responsibilities.

PP-0157 therefore keeps this as general context.

Jurisdiction-specific legal duties are excluded.

# Evidence Consistency Review

The NCI counseling source provides the strongest direct evidence for cascade-testing principles.

The gastric-specific NCI sources establish the hereditary gastric-cancer context.

No clinically meaningful contradiction was identified.

The main limitation is that cascade-uptake evidence is predominantly derived from HBOC and Lynch syndrome populations rather than HDGC-specific cohorts.

This limitation is explicitly preserved.

# Final Evidence Decision

**SUFFICIENT — GOLD READY**

The source set supports PP-0157 as a distinct **family-level cascade-testing package**.

The evidence does not justify creating a universal HDGC-specific operational cascade protocol or transferring HBOC/Lynch uptake statistics to HDGC.

The package therefore remains focused on the evidence-supported core:

> **Known familial pathogenic variant → at-risk relatives → familial-variant testing → carrier identification → cascade continuation → downstream management.**
