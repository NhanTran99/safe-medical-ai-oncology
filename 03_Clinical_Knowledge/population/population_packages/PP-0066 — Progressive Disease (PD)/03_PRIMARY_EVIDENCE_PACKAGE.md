# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does Progressive Disease (PD) mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Progressive Disease (PD)**, including what the term means, why it is used as a standardized treatment response category, what disease progression represents under standardized assessment, why Progressive Disease does not automatically mean that all treatment options have been exhausted, and why reassessment of the overall clinical situation remains essential.

This Population Package introduces the concept of Progressive Disease without explaining RECIST technical response criteria, response algorithms, treatment sequencing, prognosis or end-of-life care.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of Progressive Disease
- Progressive Disease as a standardized RECIST response category
- Meaning of disease progression under standardized assessment
- Explanation that progression may involve tumor growth or new lesions
- Clarification that Progressive Disease does not automatically mean there are no remaining treatment options
- Importance of comprehensive reassessment and individualized treatment planning

---

## Excluded

- RECIST 1.1 technical criteria for Progressive Disease
- Percentage increase thresholds
- Target lesion measurement
- Non-target lesion assessment
- Lymph node criteria
- RECIST response algorithms
- Treatment after Progressive Disease
- Second-line therapy
- Cancer recurrence
- Prognostic significance
- End-of-life care

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Progressive Disease is one of the standardized RECIST response categories.
- It describes disease progression according to standardized assessment.
- Progression may involve growth of existing disease and/or appearance of new disease.
- Progressive Disease should not be interpreted as meaning all treatment options have ended.
- Continued evaluation and individualized treatment planning remain essential.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of Progressive Disease;
- its role as a standardized treatment response category;
- the need for comprehensive reassessment following disease progression;
- the distinction between disease progression and overall prognosis.

Differences between references relate primarily to disease-specific management recommendations and technical RECIST measurement criteria, which are intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted RECIST 1.1 concepts without introducing technical response criteria.
