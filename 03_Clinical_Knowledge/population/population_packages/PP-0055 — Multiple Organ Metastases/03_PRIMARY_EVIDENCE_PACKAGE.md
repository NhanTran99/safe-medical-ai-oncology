# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does it mean if gastric cancer has spread to multiple organs?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **multiple organ metastases**, including what the term means, why metastatic gastric cancer may involve more than one distant organ, why metastatic patterns differ between patients, and why physicians evaluate many clinical factors rather than relying only on the number of organs involved.

This Population Package introduces the concept of multiple organ metastases without discussing oligometastatic disease, tumor burden, RECIST, treatment strategies or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of multiple organ metastases
- Explanation that metastatic gastric cancer may involve more than one distant organ
- Individual variation in metastatic patterns
- Relationship between multiple organ metastases and metastatic gastric cancer
- Importance of comprehensive clinical evaluation
- Explanation that the number of involved organs is only one aspect of assessment

---

## Excluded

- Oligometastatic disease
- Tumor burden
- Number of metastatic lesions
- RECIST
- TNM staging details
- Treatment
- Palliative care
- Molecular mechanisms of metastasis
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Metastatic gastric cancer may involve one or multiple distant organs.
- Patterns of metastatic spread vary considerably between patients.
- Not every patient with metastatic disease has multiple organ involvement.
- The number of organs involved should not be interpreted in isolation.
- Physicians evaluate the complete clinical picture when discussing treatment options and disease assessment.

---

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.
