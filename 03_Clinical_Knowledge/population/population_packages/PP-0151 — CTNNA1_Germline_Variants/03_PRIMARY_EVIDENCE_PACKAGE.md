# Primary Clinical Question

> **What is a CTNNA1 germline pathogenic variant, how is CTNNA1 used in the evaluation of hereditary diffuse gastric cancer, what is known about associated gastric-cancer risk, and what remains uncertain?**

# Core Evidence Statements

## 1. CTNNA1 Gene

CTNNA1 encodes alpha-catenin, a protein involved in cell-adhesion structures.

CTNNA1 is recognized as a second gene associated with HDGC after CDH1.

---

## 2. CTNNA1 Germline Pathogenic Variants

Germline pathogenic CTNNA1 variants have been identified in a small number of families with HDGC-related phenotypes.

The evidence base is substantially smaller than for CDH1.

---

## 3. Role in HDGC Genetic Testing

When an individual meets HDGC genetic-testing criteria but no CDH1 pathogenic variant is identified, CTNNA1 testing is recommended in the NCI-described framework.

CTNNA1 may also be included in hereditary cancer multigene testing.

This is the strongest established clinical role of CTNNA1 in the current source set.

---

# Gastric-Cancer Risk Evidence

Published CTNNA1 families have included diffuse gastric cancer.

NCI reports ages of gastric-cancer onset ranging from approximately **22 to 72 years** among confirmed pathogenic-variant carriers.

A registry-based study estimated up to approximately **57% lifetime gastric-cancer risk** in its specific family population.

### Interpretation boundary

This estimate is:

- family/registry based;
- derived from limited evidence;
- not a universal CTNNA1 penetrance estimate;
- not an individualized risk calculator.

The package therefore preserves the estimate only as contextual evidence.

---

# Breast-Cancer Evidence

NCI states that it remains unclear whether CTNNA1 pathogenic variants increase breast-cancer risk.

Therefore the evidence does not justify importing the CDH1-associated lobular breast-cancer risk framework into CTNNA1.

This uncertainty is a core part of the knowledge package.

---

# Penetrance and Natural History

Available evidence is limited regarding:

- CTNNA1 prevalence;
- penetrance;
- natural history;
- cancer spectrum.

The first major penetrance study provided estimates in a specific registry-based family population, but the available evidence is insufficient for the level of individual prediction available for better-characterized hereditary syndromes.

---

# Clinical Testing Availability

NCI notes that many commercial laboratories do not offer clinical CTNNA1 testing because of limited knowledge regarding prevalence and penetrance.

This should be interpreted as an evidence/availability issue, not as proof that CTNNA1 testing is universally unavailable.

Testing availability can evolve as evidence and laboratory practice change.

---

# Negative CDH1 / CTNNA1 Boundary

An HDGC-like family can remain clinically suggestive even when neither CDH1 nor CTNNA1 pathogenic variants are identified.

Therefore:

**CDH1 negative**
→ **CTNNA1 evaluation when indicated**
→ **both negative**
→ **clinical HDGC-like phenotype may still remain**

This prevents false reassurance from negative molecular testing.

---

# Pathogenic Versus Uncertain Findings

The package distinguishes:

- pathogenic/likely pathogenic;
- uncertain findings;
- benign/likely benign.

A CTNNA1 sequence variant should not automatically be treated as a disease-causing variant.

Detailed variant classification is delegated to the Variant Interpretation branch.

---

# Management Evidence Boundary

The current source set does **not** support a CTNNA1-specific management algorithm equivalent to the CDH1 pathway.

Therefore the package does not establish:

- a CTNNA1-specific prophylactic gastrectomy rule;
- a CTNNA1-specific endoscopic schedule;
- a CTNNA1-specific breast-surveillance protocol.

Instead:

> **Management should be individualized within specialist hereditary gastric-cancer care because the evidence base is limited.**

|---|---|---|

| Evidence volume | Extensive | Limited |
| Gastric-cancer association | Well established | Supported but limited |

This comparison is included specifically to prevent inappropriate extrapolation.

# Family Implications

A pathogenic germline CTNNA1 variant can have implications for biological relatives.

Appropriate genetic counseling can help determine:

- who may be at risk;
- whether familial testing is appropriate;
- how results should be interpreted.

Detailed cascade-testing methodology is delegated.

# Evidence Gaps

The source materials do not establish:

- a universal CTNNA1 lifetime gastric-cancer risk;
- robust CTNNA1 penetrance estimates across populations;
- a confirmed CTNNA1-associated breast-cancer risk;
- a CDH1-equivalent management pathway;
- reliable variant-specific risk prediction;
- comprehensive CTNNA1 variant interpretation methodology.

These gaps are intentionally preserved.

---

# Primary Evidence Conclusion

The Source Materials provide sufficient evidence to justify a dedicated CTNNA1 package, but only if the package is explicitly framed as **evidence-limited**.

The strongest established role is:

**HDGC criteria**
→ **CDH1 testing**
→ **CTNNA1 testing when CDH1 is negative and criteria remain appropriate**

The most important knowledge boundary is:

> **CTNNA1 is clinically relevant but substantially less well characterized than CDH1.**

**Evidence Status: SUFFICIENT FOR LIMITED-SCOPE GOLD PACKAGE**
