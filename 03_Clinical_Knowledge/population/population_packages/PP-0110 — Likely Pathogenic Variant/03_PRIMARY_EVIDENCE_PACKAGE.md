# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a Likely Pathogenic Variant?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of a **Likely Pathogenic Variant** as a genetic variant supported by **strong scientific evidence** suggesting a disease-causing role, while acknowledging that the available evidence has not yet reached the highest level required for a **Pathogenic** classification.

This Population Package explains the scientific meaning of the classification, emphasizes that it reflects the **strength of available evidence rather than disease severity**, and reinforces that clinical interpretation always requires consideration of the patient's complete medical context.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Likely pathogenic is an evidence-based scientific classification.
- Strong evidence supports a disease-causing role, although evidence is not yet at the highest classification level.
- The classification does not predict disease certainty.
- Clinical interpretation requires consideration of the complete clinical context.
- Scientific classifications are periodically reviewed as new evidence emerges.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently describe a likely pathogenic variant as one supported by strong—but not yet definitive—scientific evidence for a disease-causing role. Guidance consistently distinguishes the **degree of supporting evidence** from the **clinical severity of disease**, and emphasizes that standardized classifications should always be interpreted alongside the patient's medical history, clinical findings, and other relevant diagnostic information. Recommendations also recognize that classifications may change when additional scientific evidence becomes available.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- classification standards from ACMG and AMP;
- clinical genomics guidance from ClinGen;
- molecular pathology principles from CAP;
- oncology implementation guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- international consistency with ESMO.
