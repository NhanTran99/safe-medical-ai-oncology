## Clinical Question

> **What is risk-reducing total gastrectomy in hereditary diffuse gastric cancer, why is it considered for people with CDH1 pathogenic variants, when may it be considered, what are its potential benefits and burdens, and what should patients understand when choosing surgery versus continued surveillance?**

## Educational Intent

## Evidence Notes

### Rationale for RRTG

The NCI source explains that early HDGC may contain microscopic SRCC that cannot be consistently identified by endoscopy. This is the central rationale for risk-reducing removal of the stomach.

### Occult disease

In the systematic review summarized by NCI, 85.4% of 224 RRTG specimens contained gastric cancer; 72.6% had at least one SRCC focus and 12.8% contained invasive tumor. The source also summarizes a retrospective series of 101 asymptomatic CDH1 carriers in which pT1a SRCC was found in 85%.

These figures support the existence of occult disease but must not be converted into an individual's predicted cancer probability.

### Surgery versus surveillance

NCI states that RRTG remains the gold-standard recommendation, while multidisciplinary discussion is favored because of emerging surveillance evidence, uncertain progression of microscopic lesions, and surgical morbidity.

### Postoperative burden

NCI reports universal weight loss, nutritional consequences, gastrointestinal symptoms and other surgical complications. Therefore RRTG must be presented as a major irreversible intervention rather than a simple preventive procedure.

### Long-term adaptation

A cited series of 54 patients showed very good median quality of life after 4.6 years, supporting a balanced message: substantial adaptation is possible, but lifelong nutritional and medical follow-up remains necessary.

## Clinical Claims Summary

### Established / guideline-supported

- RRTG is the principal risk-reducing surgical strategy for CDH1-associated HDGC.
- Microscopic SRCC is frequently present in RRTG specimens.
- Endoscopy may fail to detect microscopic disease.
- RRTG has meaningful surgical and long-term functional morbidity.
- Weight and nutritional status require monitoring after surgery.
- Long-term follow-up is required.
- Selected patients who defer or decline surgery may undergo endoscopic surveillance.

### Individualized / context-dependent

- Age and timing.
- Surgical approach.
- Readiness for irreversible surgery.
- Whether surveillance is appropriate.
- Management of non-CDH1 hereditary variants.

### Not established by the current source set

- A universal individualized age threshold.
- A universal CTNNA1-specific RRTG indication.
- A universal surgery-versus-surveillance outcome advantage for every patient.
- A universal postoperative risk percentage applicable to every center and patient.

## Evidence Consistency Review

The core sources are consistent in presenting RRTG as the principal risk-reducing approach for CDH1-associated HDGC while recognizing:

- surgical morbidity;
- limitations of endoscopy;
- the role of surveillance for selected patients;
- the need for multidisciplinary decision-making.

The sources do not support treating all hereditary gastric-cancer syndromes identically.

## Evidence Gaps

1. Optimal individualized timing remains incompletely defined.
2. CTNNA1-specific evidence for RRTG is less established than CDH1-specific evidence.
3. Comparative long-term outcomes of immediate RRTG versus surveillance remain an evolving evidence area.
4. Contemporary surgical morbidity varies by center and technique; historical estimates should not be presented as universal current risk.
5. The current project source set does not establish a single international postoperative nutrition protocol.
