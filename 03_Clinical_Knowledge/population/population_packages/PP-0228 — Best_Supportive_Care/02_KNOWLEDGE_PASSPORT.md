# 3. Clinical Question

> **What supportive interventions and supportive-care processes can help a person with gastric cancer manage symptoms, complications, nutritional and functional problems, psychosocial burden and treatment burden?**

---

# 4. Core Knowledge Units

## KU-01 — BSC Definition

BSC is active clinical care intended to relieve suffering, manage symptoms and complications, and support quality of life.

## KU-02 — BSC Is Active Care

BSC may involve medications, procedures, nutrition, rehabilitation and psychosocial support.

## KU-03 — BSC Is Longitudinal

Supportive needs can occur throughout the cancer trajectory.

## KU-04 — BSC Can Be Concurrent

BSC can be provided alongside anticancer treatment.

## KU-05 — BSC Can Be Principal Care

In selected clinical circumstances, BSC may be the principal active-care approach.

## KU-06 — Assessment Before Intervention

Symptoms require clinical assessment and cause identification.

## KU-07 — Bleeding

Acute severe gastric-cancer bleeding requires prompt assessment and may require endoscopic, radiologic, radiation or selected surgical palliation.

## KU-08 — Obstruction

Malignant gastric obstruction may require symptom relief, decompression, restoration of passage or nutritional support.

## KU-09 — Nausea/Vomiting

Nausea/vomiting may have disease-, treatment- or obstruction-related causes.

## KU-10 — Pain

Pain requires assessment and multimodal supportive management.

## KU-11 — Fatigue

Fatigue is often multifactorial and may require cause-directed supportive care.

## KU-12 — Nutrition

Nutrition is a core supportive-care domain.

## KU-13 — Hydration

Hydration needs may arise from poor intake, vomiting, obstruction or treatment.

## KU-14 — Function

Functional decline is a meaningful supportive-care outcome.

## KU-15 — Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation can support mobility, strength and independence.

## KU-16 — Psychosocial Care

Emotional and social needs require recognition and appropriate support.

## KU-17 — Caregiver Support

Caregiver education and support are part of comprehensive supportive care.

## KU-18 — Reassessment

Supportive interventions require reassessment because symptoms and goals change.

## KU-19 — Multidisciplinary Care

BSC may require multiple professional disciplines.

## KU-20 — Boundary

PP-0228 owns the practical BSC layer, while adjacent PPs own palliative framework, systemic therapy, treatment toxicity and detailed specialty interventions.

# 8. Patient-Journey Position

PP-0228 is longitudinal.

### Diagnosis

Supportive needs may already be present.

### Treatment

BSC may support symptoms, nutrition and function.

### Recurrence

BSC may become more prominent as symptom burden increases.

### Metastatic disease

BSC may address complex symptom and functional needs.

### End of life

BSC may remain active while goals become increasingly comfort-focused.

# 10. Source Passport

## NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026

Primary gastric-cancer-specific authority.

The supplied GAST-J section states that BSC aims to prevent and relieve suffering and support quality of life regardless of disease stage or need for other therapies.

It specifically addresses:

- bleeding;
- obstruction;
- symptom-directed care;
- interdisciplinary palliative/BSC approach.

The NCCN advanced-disease framework also describes BSC alone or with systemic therapy according to clinical context.

## ESMO-ASCO 2023

Supports:

- supportive-care needs across the cancer trajectory;
- individual symptom assessment;
- nutrition;
- psychosocial care;
- multidisciplinary care;
- patient/caregiver education;
- rehabilitation;
- supportive/end-of-life principles.

## NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ

Supports the broader palliative-treatment interface and examples of palliative local interventions.

## American Cancer Society

Supports patient-facing interpretation of supportive/palliative care and continued care when cancer treatment changes or stops.

# 14. Runtime Rules

1. Never say BSC means no treatment.
2. Never say BSC is only for terminal disease.
3. Never equate BSC with hospice.
4. Never provide individualized treatment decisions.
5. Never provide medication dosing.
6. Never provide procedural instructions.
7. Always distinguish symptom from cause.
8. Preserve the PP-0227 boundary.
9. Preserve the PP-0231 toxicity boundary.
10. Preserve the PP-0208 systemic-therapy boundary.
11. Refer acute deterioration to clinical assessment.
12. Preserve uncertainty when intervention choice is context-dependent.
