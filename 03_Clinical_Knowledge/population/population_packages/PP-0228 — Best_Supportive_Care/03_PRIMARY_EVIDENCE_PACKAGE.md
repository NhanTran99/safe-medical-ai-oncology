# 1. Clinical Question

> **What is Best Supportive Care in gastric cancer, what clinical problems does it address, how is it integrated with anticancer treatment, and what supportive interventions can be considered without duplicating the palliative-care, systemic-treatment or treatment-toxicity packages?**

---

# 2. Evidence Intent

PP-0228 translates guideline-supported BSC principles into a structured clinical-supportive framework.

The package prioritizes:

1. gastric-cancer-specific NCCN evidence;
2. supportive-care concepts from ESMO-ASCO;
3. palliative-treatment context from NCI;
4. patient-facing interpretation;
5. explicit boundaries with adjacent PPs.

# 3. Primary Evidence Sources

## 3.1 NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026

### Primary role

Gastric-cancer-specific BSC authority.

The supplied NCCN GAST-J section states:

- BSC aims to prevent and relieve suffering;
- BSC supports the best possible quality of life;
- this applies regardless of disease stage or need for other therapies;
- gastric-cancer symptom-relieving interventions may sometimes prolong life;
- multidisciplinary care is encouraged.

The same section addresses bleeding and obstruction.

### Evidence role

**Primary / guideline-supported.**

---

## 3.2 NCCN Advanced Disease Framework

The supplied NCCN gastric-cancer material states that unresectable, recurrent and metastatic disease may be managed with systemic therapy and/or chemoradiation and should incorporate symptom-directed BSC.

The decision to provide BSC alone or with systemic therapy depends on performance status and clinical context.

### Evidence role

**Primary / guideline-supported.**

# 6. Detailed Evidence Appraisal

## Evidence 1 — NCCN BSC Definition

The supplied NCCN GAST-J states that the goal of BSC is to prevent and relieve suffering and support the best possible quality of life for patients and families regardless of disease stage or need for other therapies.

### Interpretation

This establishes:

- active supportive care;
- longitudinal applicability;
- patient/family focus;
- compatibility with other therapies.

### PP implication

This is the foundation of PP-0228.

---

## Evidence 2 — Multimodality Interdisciplinary Care

NCCN encourages a multimodality interdisciplinary approach.

### Interpretation

BSC is not a single medication pathway.

It may involve:

- oncology;
- nursing;
- palliative care;
- nutrition;
- rehabilitation;
- psychosocial professionals;
- procedural specialists.

### Boundary

Operational MDT organization belongs to PP-0232.

---

## Evidence 3 — Bleeding

NCCN identifies acute bleeding as common in gastric cancer and notes that bleeding may arise directly from tumor or as a consequence of therapy.

Acute severe bleeding should receive prompt endoscopic assessment.

Potential treatment categories include:

- endoscopic therapy;
- interventional radiology/embolization;
- palliative gastrectomy in selected patients;
- radiation.

NCCN also notes limitations of endoscopic evidence and recurrent bleeding risk.

### Interpretation

PP-0228 should present bleeding as a supportive-care problem with multiple possible intervention pathways.

It should not present one procedure as universally preferred.

---

## Evidence 4 — Obstruction

NCCN identifies the primary palliative goals for malignant gastric obstruction as:

1. reducing nausea and vomiting;
2. allowing resumption of oral diet when possible.

Potential approaches include endoscopic and surgical strategies and supportive nutritional/decompressive approaches.

### Interpretation

Obstruction is a major PP-0228 domain because it affects:

- symptoms;
- nutrition;
- hydration;
- function;
- quality of life.

---

## Evidence 5 — Symptom Assessment

ESMO-ASCO emphasizes clinical and individual assessment of symptoms.

### Interpretation

The same symptom may have several causes.

Therefore a safe supportive-care architecture requires:

**symptom**

→ **assessment**

→ **cause**

→ **intervention**

→ **reassessment**

---

## Evidence 6 — Nutrition

ESMO-ASCO identifies nutritional support and nutritional screening within supportive care.

### Interpretation

Nutrition is not a peripheral add-on.

It may affect:

- strength;
- treatment tolerance;
- recovery;
- function;
- quality of life.

### Boundary

Detailed nutritional prescription remains outside PP-0228.

---

## Evidence 7 — Psychosocial Care

ESMO-ASCO recognizes psychosocial needs and multidisciplinary supportive care.

### Interpretation

BSC must not be reduced to physical symptoms.

---

## Evidence 8 — Rehabilitation

ESMO-ASCO includes prehabilitation and rehabilitation across the cancer trajectory.

### Interpretation

Function is a legitimate supportive-care outcome.

---

## Evidence 9 — Systemic Therapy + BSC

NCCN describes systemic therapy together with BSC and shows that BSC can be used alone or alongside systemic treatment according to clinical status.

### Interpretation

BSC should not be framed as the opposite of anticancer treatment.

# 7. Evidence-Supported Clinical Use Model

## Use 1 — Identify the Burden

Examples:

- bleeding;
- obstruction;
- nausea/vomiting;
- pain;
- fatigue;
- malnutrition;
- dehydration;
- functional decline;
- distress.

↓

## Use 2 — Assess the Cause

Consider:

- tumor;
- treatment;
- obstruction;
- medication;
- comorbidity;
- mixed causes.

↓

## Use 3 — Assess Severity and Impact

Consider:

- symptom intensity;
- oral intake;
- hydration;
- mobility;
- independence;
- hospital burden;
- caregiver burden.

↓

## Use 4 — Select Supportive Intervention

Potential categories:

- pharmacologic;
- endoscopic;
- interventional radiology;
- radiation;
- surgery;
- nutrition;
- hydration;
- rehabilitation;
- psychosocial support.

↓

## Use 5 — Reassess

Determine whether:

- symptoms improved;
- function improved;
- burden decreased;
- intervention introduced new burden;
- goals remain aligned.

This is a conceptual clinical-use model, not a universal treatment algorithm.

# 8. Clinical Evidence by Problem

## 8.1 Bleeding

### Evidence-supported

- prompt assessment for severe bleeding;
- endoscopic evaluation;
- possible endoscopic treatment;
- embolization in selected situations;
- radiation in selected situations;
- surgery in selected patients.

### Uncertainty

Endoscopic evidence in gastric-cancer bleeding is limited and recurrent bleeding may be common.

### Boundary

No procedural technique.

---

## 8.2 Malignant Obstruction

### Evidence-supported

- symptom assessment;
- nausea/vomiting control;
- restoration of oral intake when feasible;
- endoscopic intervention;
- surgical bypass in selected patients;
- decompression;
- nutritional support.

### Uncertainty

No universal procedure applies to all patients.

---

## 8.3 Nausea/Vomiting

### Evidence-supported

- symptom management;
- assessment of obstruction and other causes;
- treatment-related cause assessment;
- reassessment.

### Boundary

No detailed antiemetic dosing.

---

## 8.4 Pain

### Evidence-supported

- systematic assessment;
- multimodal support;
- pharmacologic and non-pharmacologic approaches;
- selected local palliation.

### Boundary

No individualized opioid dosing or conversion.

---

## 8.5 Fatigue

### Evidence-supported

- assessment of reversible contributors;
- nutritional support;
- rehabilitation;
- psychosocial support;
- treatment-burden assessment.

### Boundary

No universal pharmacologic fatigue algorithm.

---

## 8.6 Nutrition

### Evidence-supported

- nutritional screening;
- individualized nutritional assessment;
- oral support when feasible;
- escalation when needed;
- multidisciplinary care.

### Boundary

No individualized prescription.

---

## 8.7 Hydration

### Evidence-supported

- assessment;
- correction of clinically relevant dehydration;
- oral or parenteral support according to context;
- reassessment.

### Boundary

No universal fluid protocol.

---

## 8.8 Function and Rehabilitation

### Evidence-supported

- functional assessment;
- rehabilitation;
- mobility/strength support;
- independence preservation.

### Boundary

Detailed rehabilitation protocols are delegated.

---

## 8.9 Psychosocial and Caregiver Support

### Evidence-supported

- distress recognition;
- psychosocial support;
- caregiver education;
- social support;
- referral.

### Boundary

Detailed psychiatric/psychological treatment is delegated.

# 10. Evidence Limitations

1. BSC is a broad clinical domain rather than one standardized intervention.
2. Individual supportive interventions depend on anatomy, disease status and patient goals.
3. Evidence for some symptom-specific interventions is limited.
4. Gastric-cancer bleeding may recur after endoscopic therapy.
5. Obstruction management requires individualized procedural selection.
6. Nutritional needs vary substantially.
7. Treatment toxicity can overlap with disease-related symptoms.
8. Healthcare-resource availability varies.
9. Hospice and end-of-life operational rules vary by jurisdiction.
10. This package does not quantify individualized prognosis.

---

# 11. Evidence Gaps

## Gap 1 — Universal intervention algorithm

No single algorithm applies to every patient.

## Gap 2 — Procedural selection

Stent, bypass, decompression and other interventions require clinical/anatomical context.

## Gap 3 — Nutrition

Optimal escalation differs according to prognosis, GI function and goals.

## Gap 4 — Hydration

The balance between benefit and burden varies by clinical state.

## Gap 5 — Psychosocial care

Access and models differ between healthcare systems.

## Gap 6 — Resource availability

The availability of specialist palliative, nutrition, rehabilitation and psychosocial services varies.
