# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is Variant Reclassification?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Variant Reclassification** as the process of **updating the classification of a genetic variant when new scientific evidence becomes available**.

This Population Package explains that reclassification reflects the normal evolution of scientific knowledge rather than laboratory error, introduces the concept that variant classifications are evidence-based and dynamic, and reinforces that updated classifications should always be interpreted within the patient's complete clinical context.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Variant classification is an evidence-based process that may evolve over time.
- Reclassification usually reflects scientific progress rather than testing errors.
- New research and accumulated clinical evidence can change classification.
- Many variants remain stable for long periods and are never reclassified.
- Updated classifications should always be interpreted by qualified healthcare professionals.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize that genomic variant classification is dynamic because scientific knowledge continues to evolve. Guidance consistently explains that reclassification is driven by newly accumulated evidence—including published research, clinical observations, functional data, and expert consensus—rather than deficiencies in the original laboratory testing. Recommendations also emphasize that any updated classification should be interpreted together with the patient's clinical presentation and other diagnostic information.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- classification standards from ACMG and AMP;
- clinical genomics guidance from ClinGen;
- molecular pathology principles from CAP;
- oncology implementation guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- international consistency with ESMO.
