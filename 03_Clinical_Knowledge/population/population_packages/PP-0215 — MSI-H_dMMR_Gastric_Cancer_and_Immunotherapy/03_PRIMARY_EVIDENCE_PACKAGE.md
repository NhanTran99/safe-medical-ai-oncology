# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What does MSI-H/dMMR mean in gastric cancer, and why does it matter for immunotherapy?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded explanation of MSI-H/dMMR as a gastric-cancer phenotype and its treatment relevance, especially the evidence supporting immune checkpoint inhibition.

The package is intentionally positioned between:

**MSI/MMR testing**

and

**MSI-H/dMMR-specific clinical treatment application.**

It does not reproduce laboratory testing methodology, general immune-checkpoint biology, PD-L1 testing, TMB testing, or individualized treatment selection.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Direct Gastric-Cancer Guideline Evidence

### NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026

The supplied NCCN v2.2026 guideline is the dominant evidence source.

It reports that MSI-H/dMMR gastric tumors have distinct clinicopathologic characteristics and more favorable prognostic associations than MSS/MMR-proficient tumors.

It also provides explicit MSI-H/dMMR treatment recommendations and discusses evidence from KEYNOTE and CheckMate programs.

---

# Core Evidence Synthesis

## 1. MSI-H/dMMR phenotype

NCCN describes MSI-H/dMMR as a clinically relevant gastric-cancer subgroup.

The guideline notes associations with:

- older age;
- intestinal histology;
- distal gastric location;
- earlier-stage disease in relevant datasets;
- favorable prognosis compared with MSS/pMMR disease.

These are population-level associations and must not be used as diagnostic rules.

---

## 2. General biological rationale

The project evidence supports the established biological concept that mismatch-repair deficiency produces genomic instability and can create a tumor environment in which immune recognition is more relevant.

The educational chain is:

**dMMR**
→ **microsatellite instability**
→ **altered tumor genomic/antigenic landscape**
→ **greater potential immune recognition**
→ **checkpoint blockade can be particularly effective in selected tumors.**

This is a mechanistic rationale and not an individual response guarantee.

# Pembrolizumab Evidence

## KEYNOTE-059

The NCCN source reports a phase II cohort of previously treated recurrent/metastatic gastric and EGJ cancers.

Among seven patients with MSI-H tumors:

- 4 had objective responses;
- ORR was 57.1%;
- median OS was not reached;
- median PFS was not reached.

The small subgroup size limits precision.

### Evidence interpretation

**Strength:** clinically meaningful signal.

**Limitation:** very small MSI-H subgroup.

---

## KEYNOTE-061

This phase III study compared pembrolizumab with paclitaxel in previously treated advanced gastric/EGJ cancer.

Among the approximately 5.3% of patients with MSI-H tumors:

| Outcome | Pembrolizumab | Paclitaxel |
|---|---:|---:|
| Median OS | Not reached | 8.1 months |
| Median PFS | 17.8 months | 3.5 months |
| ORR | 46.7% | 16.7% |

These results provide important randomized evidence for the MSI-H subgroup.

They should not be interpreted as an individual survival prediction.

---

## KEYNOTE-062

This phase III trial evaluated treatment-naïve advanced gastric cancer.

Among approximately 7% of patients with MSI-H tumors:

| Outcome | Pembrolizumab | Pembrolizumab + chemotherapy | Chemotherapy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Median OS | Not reached | Not reached | 8.5 months |
| Median PFS | 11.2 months | Not reached | 6.6 months |
| ORR | 57.1% | 64.7% | 36.8% |

The MSI-H subgroup findings support substantial checkpoint-inhibitor activity.

Because MSI-H represented a minority subgroup, the results must be interpreted as subgroup evidence rather than as a universal treatment effect for all gastric cancer.

---

# KEYNOTE-158

The updated MSI-H/dMMR analysis included:

- 351 patients;
- 28 tumor types;
- overall ORR 30.8%;
- median duration of response 47.5 months;
- 42 patients with gastric cancer;
- gastric-cancer ORR 31%.

### Evidence interpretation

This supports the broader tumor-agnostic biological activity of pembrolizumab in MSI-H/dMMR tumors.

However, it must be labeled as **tumor-agnostic evidence**, not equivalent to a gastric-specific randomized trial.

---

# Nivolumab + Chemotherapy — CheckMate-649

The supplied NCCN v2.2026 source reports particularly strong outcomes in the MSI-H subgroup.

### MSI-H + PD-L1 CPS ≥5

- n = 34;
- median OS 44.8 vs 8.8 months;
- HR 0.29.

### All randomized MSI-H patients

- n = 44;
- median OS 38.7 vs 12.3 months;
- HR 0.34.

NCCN therefore recommends nivolumab plus fluoropyrimidine/oxaliplatin as a category 2A preferred first-line option for MSI-H/dMMR tumors independently of PD-L1 status.

### Interpretation

This is one of the strongest disease-specific evidence blocks in PP-0215.

However, the MSI-H sample is still a subgroup, so the numerical effect should not be converted into an individual prognosis.

---

# Nivolumab + Ipilimumab — CheckMate-649

The supplied NCCN source reports:

- MSI-H median OS: not reached vs 10 months;
- HR 0.28;
- ORR 70% vs 57%;
- no comparable survival benefit in the microsatellite-stable cohort.

NCCN v2.2026 recommends nivolumab plus ipilimumab as a category 2A preferred first-line option and as an option useful in certain circumstances in later-line MSI-H/dMMR disease.

### Key clinical meaning

The contrasting MSI-H versus MSS result reinforces the principle that MSI-H/dMMR can function as a treatment-relevant biomarker for checkpoint blockade.

---

# Neoadjuvant / Perioperative Evidence

## NEONIPIGA

NCCN describes the GERCOR NEONIPIGA phase II study:

- 32 patients;
- locally advanced gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma;
- confirmed MSI-H/dMMR;
- neoadjuvant nivolumab + ipilimumab;
- adjuvant nivolumab;
- 29 R0 resections;
- approximately 59% pathologic complete response;
- 97% survival benefit among 31 evaluated patients at reported follow-up.

NCCN recommends nivolumab + ipilimumab followed by nivolumab as a category 2A option useful in certain circumstances for MSI-H/dMMR tumors in the neoadjuvant/perioperative setting.

### Evidence grade

**Important phase II evidence; not equivalent to large phase III randomized evidence.**

---

## Neoadjuvant pembrolizumab

NCCN also describes a phase II study of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab in locally advanced MSI-H/dMMR solid tumors.

Among 33 assessed patients:

- ORR 82%;
- CR 30%;
- among 17 surgically resected patients, pathologic CR 65%.

However, most patients had colorectal cancer rather than gastric cancer.

Therefore this evidence is supportive of the MSI-H/dMMR biological principle but should not be represented as gastric-specific evidence of the same strength as NEONIPIGA.

---

# Perioperative Evidence — Important Uncertainty

The supplied NCCN source explicitly frames perioperative immunotherapy as an option for **certain patients**.

This wording must be preserved.

The evidence does not establish:

> every MSI-H/dMMR gastric cancer should receive perioperative immunotherapy.

Likewise, a complete response does not automatically resolve whether surgery is required.

The package should preserve this uncertainty rather than convert evolving evidence into a universal algorithm.

---

# Subsequent-line Evidence

NCCN v2.2026 identifies selected options for MSI-H/dMMR tumors in second/subsequent-line treatment, including:

- pembrolizumab;
- nivolumab + ipilimumab;
- dostarlimab-gxly.

The exact selection depends on prior treatment and clinical context.

The Evidence Package deliberately does not create a universal sequencing algorithm.

---

# Biomarker Distinctions

## MSI-H/dMMR versus PD-L1

| Feature | MSI-H/dMMR | PD-L1 |
|---|---|---|
| Biological concept | Mismatch repair / microsatellite instability | Immune checkpoint ligand expression |
| Testing question | Is the tumor MSI-H/dMMR? | Is PD-L1 expressed at the relevant level? |
| Treatment relevance | Strong ICI relevance in selected gastric cancer contexts | Treatment relevance in selected guideline-defined contexts |
| Relationship | Independent biomarker | Independent biomarker |
| Key safety message | MSI-H/dMMR can support selected PD-L1-independent ICI strategies | PD-L1 pathway remains separately owned |

---

## MSI-H/dMMR versus TMB-H

MSI-H/dMMR and TMB-H can overlap biologically.

They are not interchangeable.

NCCN discusses TMB-H separately and notes that the KEYNOTE-158 TMB analysis did not include gastroesophageal cancer patients.

Therefore the package should not use TMB-H evidence as though it were direct gastric MSI-H evidence.

---

# Hereditary / Lynch Bridge

An MSI-H/dMMR tumor can raise the possibility of an inherited mismatch-repair condition.

However:

**tumor MSI-H/dMMR**
≠
**confirmed Lynch syndrome**

NCI genetics material supports the role of genetic risk assessment and counselling when hereditary risk is suspected.

The detailed hereditary pathway is delegated.

# Evidence Gaps

The following evidence gaps are intentionally preserved:

1. Optimal perioperative immunotherapy strategy remains unsettled.
2. The optimal duration of checkpoint inhibition in every MSI-H/dMMR setting is not established by one universal rule.
3. The role of surgery after complete response to neoadjuvant immunotherapy remains uncertain in selected contexts.
4. MSI-H subgroup sample sizes in several randomized trials are small.
5. Tumor-agnostic evidence is not equivalent to gastric-specific evidence.
6. Cross-trial numerical comparisons are limited by different populations, treatment settings, comparators, endpoints and follow-up.
7. Not every MSI-H/dMMR tumor responds.
8. The Source Materials do not establish a universal algorithm for treatment after checkpoint-inhibitor resistance.
9. Detailed mechanisms of primary and acquired resistance are outside the approved scope.
10. Hereditary implications require dedicated genetic evaluation rather than inference from tumor status alone.

These gaps do not block PP-0215 because they are explicitly incorporated into the educational boundary.
