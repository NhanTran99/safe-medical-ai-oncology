# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **How should I read my pathology report?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based guide for approaching a pathology report in a safe and understandable way.

This Population Package explains that pathology reports follow a structured format, introduces the major sections commonly found in these reports, and emphasizes that they should always be interpreted together with clinical findings by the healthcare team.

---

# Scope

## Included

- General organization of pathology reports
- Common report sections
- Importance of the Final Diagnosis section
- Appropriate patient approach
- Professional interpretation

---

## Excluded

- Interpretation of individual pathology findings
- Tumor grade
- Tumor stage
- Margin status
- Lymphovascular invasion
- Perineural invasion
- Biomarker results
- Molecular pathology
- Synoptic pathology reports
- CAP cancer protocols

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Pathology reports are structured medical documents.
- Most reports contain recognizable sections.
- Final Diagnosis usually summarizes the principal findings.
- Pathology reports should not be interpreted in isolation.
- Healthcare professionals should explain specialized pathology terminology.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence consistently emphasizes standardized pathology reporting while recognizing that interpretation requires integration with imaging, clinical findings, laboratory investigations and patient history.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- pathology reporting standards from CAP;
- multidisciplinary oncology guidance consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- interpretation of individual pathology findings;
- tumor grading;
- staging;
- biomarker testing;
- molecular pathology;
- prognosis.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
