# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand that antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) can still cause side effects.
- Explain why targeted therapy does not mean side-effect-free treatment.
- Recognize the common categories of ADC-related side effects.
- Understand why regular monitoring during treatment is important.
- Recognize symptoms that should be reported promptly to the healthcare team.
- Appreciate the importance of early detection and management.
- Participate more confidently in shared decision-making.

# Knowledge Block 1 — Can ADCs Cause Side Effects?

## Patient Explanation

Yes.

Although antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are designed to deliver treatment more selectively than conventional chemotherapy, they are still powerful cancer medicines.

Like all cancer treatments, they may cause side effects.

The type and severity of side effects vary from person to person.

---

## Clinical Importance

Patients should understand that targeted treatment does not eliminate treatment-related risks.

---

## Key Concepts

- Targeted does not mean risk-free
- Side effects remain possible
- Individual experiences differ

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Can ADCs Still Cause Side Effects?

## Patient Explanation

ADCs are designed to improve treatment precision by directing medicine toward cancer cells.

However, no cancer treatment affects only cancer cells.

For this reason, side effects can still occur during treatment.

Most side effects can be monitored and managed when recognized early.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding this principle helps patients develop realistic expectations.

---

## Key Concepts

- Precision improves selectivity
- Side effects remain possible
- Early recognition matters

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Types of Side Effects May Occur?

## Patient Explanation

Different ADC medicines may cause different side effects.

General categories include:

- tiredness;
- nausea;
- changes in blood cell counts;
- digestive symptoms;
- other organ-related side effects depending on the medicine.

Your healthcare team will explain which side effects are most relevant to your treatment.

---

## Clinical Importance

The exact toxicity profile depends on the specific ADC being used.

---

## Key Concepts

- Different medicines
- Different side effects
- Individualized monitoring

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Is Regular Monitoring Important?

## Patient Explanation

Regular follow-up appointments help your healthcare team identify problems before they become more serious.

Monitoring may include:

- physical examinations;
- blood tests;
- symptom review;
- additional investigations when needed.

---

## Clinical Importance

Routine monitoring improves treatment safety.

---

## Key Concepts

- Scheduled follow-up
- Early detection
- Safer treatment

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — When Should Patients Contact Their Healthcare Team?

## Patient Explanation

Patients should promptly inform their healthcare team if they develop:

- new or worsening symptoms;
- persistent fever;
- difficulty breathing;
- severe nausea or vomiting;
- unusual bleeding;
- any symptom that causes concern.

Patients should never ignore important new symptoms during treatment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Early reporting may allow earlier evaluation and treatment.

---

## Key Concepts

- Report symptoms early
- Do not wait
- Communication is essential

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Why Does Early Detection Matter?

## Patient Explanation

Many treatment-related side effects can be managed more effectively when identified early.

Reporting symptoms promptly may help prevent complications and support continuation of treatment when appropriate.

---

## Clinical Importance

Early intervention improves patient safety.

---

## Key Concepts

- Early recognition
- Early management
- Better outcomes

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Common Questions About ADC Side Effects

## Patient Explanation

Some patients believe targeted therapy has no side effects.

This is incorrect.

Targeted therapy may reduce certain unwanted effects compared with some treatments, but it does not eliminate risk.

Every treatment should be monitored carefully.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

Because ADCs are targeted therapies, they are free of side effects.

**Fact**

All cancer treatments, including ADCs, may cause side effects and require regular monitoring.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Should Patients Remember?

Patients should understand:

- ADCs remain powerful cancer medicines.
- Side effects vary between individuals.
- Early reporting improves safety.
- Regular monitoring is an important part of treatment.
- Questions should always be discussed with the healthcare team.
- Patients should never stop treatment without medical advice.

---

## Clinical Importance

Patient education supports safer treatment and shared decision-making.

---

# Key Messages

- ADCs may still cause side effects.
- Different ADC medicines have different safety profiles.
- Regular monitoring is an essential part of treatment.
- Report new or worsening symptoms promptly.
- Early detection often allows better management.
- Never change or stop treatment without consulting your healthcare team.
