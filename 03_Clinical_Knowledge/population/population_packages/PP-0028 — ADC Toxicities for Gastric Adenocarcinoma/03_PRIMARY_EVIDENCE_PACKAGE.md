# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> Why can antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) cause side effects, and what should patients with gastric adenocarcinoma understand about treatment safety?

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an evidence-based overview of the general safety principles associated with antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

This Population Package explains why ADCs can still produce adverse effects despite being targeted therapies, introduces the common categories of side effects at a high level, emphasizes the importance of routine monitoring, and encourages early communication with the healthcare team.

The package intentionally avoids drug-specific toxicities, detailed management algorithms and specialist toxicity grading.

---

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- ADCs remain potent anti-cancer medicines despite their targeted design.
- Side effects are possible and differ among medicines and patients.
- Routine monitoring is an essential part of treatment.
- Early reporting of symptoms supports timely assessment and management.
- Patients should never change or discontinue treatment without medical guidance.
- Safe treatment depends on ongoing communication between patients and the healthcare team.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary authoritative references.

All major references consistently emphasize that targeted therapies, including ADCs, require careful safety monitoring and patient education throughout treatment.

Differences among references primarily involve:

- toxicity profiles of individual ADCs;
- management recommendations;
- dose adjustment strategies;
- drug-specific adverse events.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- clinical recommendations from NCCN;
- evidence interpretation from JNCCN;
- patient-oriented explanations from NCI and ACS;
- internationally harmonized terminology from ESMO.
