# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is molecular tumor profiling and what can a molecular profile tell us about gastric cancer?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **molecular tumor profiling** as an integrated approach to describing the molecular characteristics of a cancer.

The package explains that a molecular profile can include multiple types of molecular information, including genomic alterations and selected biomarker findings, and that these findings must be interpreted together with pathology and clinical context.

The package deliberately separates the **concept of a molecular tumor profile** from individual testing technologies such as NGS and from downstream interpretation or treatment algorithms.

# Evidence Notes

## 1. Molecular Tumor Profiling Is an Integrated Concept

The evidence supports treating molecular tumor profiling as a **clinical characterization concept**, not as the name of a single laboratory assay.

The ESMO/ASCO curriculum separates molecular oncology, molecular biology/pathology, and genetic/genomic testing into related but distinct educational competencies. It also emphasizes review of pathology, molecular pathology, and other diagnostic materials within clinical oncology practice.

This supports the PP-0238 architecture:

**tumor**

→ **molecular findings**

→ **integrated molecular profile**

→ **clinical interpretation**

rather than:

**NGS = molecular tumor profiling**.

---

## 2. Gastric Cancer Requires Multidimensional Molecular Characterization

NCCN gastric-cancer guidance places pathologic review and biomarker testing within the gastric-cancer clinical pathway.

The 2026 guideline update specifically notes that NGS can assess numerous mutations simultaneously and other molecular events such as amplification, deletions, TMB, and MSI. The same guideline separately addresses HER2, MSI/MMR, PD-L1, CLDN18.2, and NGS testing.

This is the core evidence supporting the package's central educational message:

> **A gastric-cancer molecular profile can contain multiple types of molecular information rather than only a single mutation result.**

---

## 3. NGS Is a Method, Not the Definition of the Profile

The NCCN framework distinguishes preferred initial approaches for specific biomarkers from broader NGS testing that may be considered later in the clinical course when appropriate.

Therefore the package deliberately teaches:

> NGS can generate molecular information that contributes to a molecular profile.

It does not teach:

> Molecular tumor profiling means NGS.

This distinction is important for avoiding overlap with PP-0101 — NGS and other technology-specific Population Packages.

---

## 4. Biomarkers Are Used as Examples, Not as the Package's Main Subject

HER2, MSI/MMR, PD-L1, and CLDN18.2 are included only to demonstrate that clinically relevant tumor characterization can extend beyond simple mutation lists.

The package intentionally does not reproduce:

- biomarker-specific thresholds;
- assay scoring systems;
- laboratory workflows;
- companion diagnostic rules;
- treatment algorithms.

Those subjects belong to dedicated biomarker packages.

---

## 5. Pathology Remains Essential

ESMO/ASCO describes clinical oncology training that includes review of imaging, pathology, molecular pathology, and other diagnostic materials, together with multidisciplinary tumor-board participation.

PP-0238 therefore treats molecular information as one layer of integrated cancer characterization.

The evidence does not support presenting molecular profiling as a replacement for:

- histologic diagnosis;
- staging;
- imaging;
- clinical assessment.

---

## 6. Clinical Actionability Is Context-Dependent

A molecular finding may be clinically relevant or potentially actionable, but the existence of an alteration does not automatically determine the treatment that should be given.

The clinical meaning depends on:

- tumor type;
- exact molecular finding;
- evidence supporting its relevance;
- clinical context;
- available therapies;
- current guidelines.

Detailed interpretation is delegated to PP-0107 and downstream precision-oncology packages.

---

## 7. Tumor Heterogeneity and Evolution

A molecular profile describes the material that was tested.

Different areas of a tumor can contain different molecular characteristics, and tumor biology can evolve over time.

This provides the conceptual bridge to PP-0237 — ctDNA and future molecular-monitoring packages without turning PP-0238 into a longitudinal monitoring package.

---

## 8. Tissue and Blood-Based Molecular Information

Blood-based molecular testing can provide tumor-derived molecular information in selected settings.

The package uses this only to establish the relationship:

**Molecular Tumor Profiling**

↔

**Tissue-based molecular information**

↔

**Blood/ctDNA-based molecular information**

Detailed liquid-biopsy and ctDNA content is delegated to PP-0236 and PP-0237

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Molecular tumor profiling is an integrated way of describing molecular characteristics of a tumor.
- It is broader than one individual genetic test.
- A profile can contain multiple molecular findings.
- NGS can be one way to obtain molecular information but is not synonymous with molecular profiling.
- Gastric-cancer characterization can include genomic alterations and clinically relevant biomarker information.
- Molecular findings must be interpreted in the context of pathology and the patient's clinical situation.
- Not every molecular finding is actionable.
- Tumor heterogeneity, sample limitations, assay limitations, and tumor evolution can affect molecular characterization.
- Blood-based molecular information can complement tissue-based testing in selected circumstances.
- Molecular profiling does not replace comprehensive clinical assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

NCCN provides the strongest disease-specific framework for gastric-cancer biomarker and molecular testing. ESMO/ASCO provides the broader oncology educational framework in which molecular oncology, molecular pathology, and genetic/genomic testing are integrated with pathology and other diagnostic information.

The sources are therefore complementary:

- **NCCN** anchors gastric-cancer-specific clinical use.
- **ESMO/ASCO** anchors the conceptual oncology framework.
- **NCI** provides supporting cancer-genetics context.
- **ACS** supports patient-facing explanation.

The package intentionally does not infer that every molecular profiling application is standard of care for every gastric-cancer patient.

---

# Evidence Gaps

The current package intentionally does not establish:

- universal use of broad genomic profiling for every patient;
- universal use of NGS;
- a complete gastric-cancer biomarker catalogue;
- biomarker-specific testing thresholds;
- molecular subtype assignment;
- treatment selection from individual molecular findings;
- longitudinal ctDNA monitoring;
- MRD or recurrence-detection algorithms.

These areas require dedicated, context-specific evidence packages.
