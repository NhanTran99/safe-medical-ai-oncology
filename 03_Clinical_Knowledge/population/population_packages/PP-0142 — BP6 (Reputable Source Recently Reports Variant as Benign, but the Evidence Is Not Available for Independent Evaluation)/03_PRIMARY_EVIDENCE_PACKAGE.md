# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is BP6 (Reputable Source Recently Reports Variant as Benign, but the Evidence Is Not Available for Independent Evaluation)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **BP6** as a historical **Supporting Benign Evidence Code** in the original ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework, while clearly communicating its **current governance status**.

The original ACMG/AMP framework included BP6 for situations in which a reputable source reported a variant as benign but the underlying evidence was not available to the laboratory for independent evaluation.

Subsequent ClinGen Sequence Variant Interpretation (SVI) guidance recommended that **BP6 not be used**, because variant interpretation should rely on evidence that can be independently evaluated rather than simply relying on another source's assertion.

Therefore, this Population Package intentionally distinguishes between:

> **A source reporting a classification**

and

> **Evidence that can be independently evaluated.**

BP6 is retained for historical and educational understanding but must not be presented as a currently recommended standalone benign evidence criterion.

# Historical Evidence Position

The original ACMG/AMP framework defined BP6 as a **Supporting Benign Evidence** criterion for a situation in which a reputable source had recently reported a variant as benign but the underlying evidence was not available to the laboratory for independent evaluation.

This historical definition is important for understanding the structure and evolution of the ACMG/AMP evidence-code system.

However, retaining the historical definition must not be interpreted as endorsing BP6 for current routine variant classification.

---

# Current Governance Position

The current governance position is:

> **BP6 is not recommended for use.**

ClinGen SVI subsequently recommended removing BP6 from active use because laboratories should evaluate the underlying evidence independently rather than rely solely on a classification asserted by another source.

This creates an important distinction:

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Source assertion | Another source reports a variant as benign |
| Independently evaluated evidence | The underlying evidence can be reviewed and assessed |
| BP6 historical role | Captured the former situation where the assertion existed but underlying evidence was unavailable |
| Current position | BP6 should not be used as an active evidence criterion |

---

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence supports the following educational messages:

- BP6 was part of the original ACMG/AMP framework.
- BP6 concerned a benign classification reported by a reputable source without independently evaluable underlying evidence.
- ClinGen SVI subsequently recommended that BP6 not be used.
- The underlying evidence is more important than simply repeating another source's classification.
- BP6 should therefore be understood as **historical knowledge**, not as a currently recommended standalone benign criterion.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

The historical ACMG/AMP definition and current ClinGen governance position represent different stages in the development of variant interpretation standards.

There is therefore no contradiction that needs to be hidden:

1. **Original framework:** BP6 existed as a Supporting Benign Evidence criterion.
2. **Subsequent governance development:** ClinGen SVI reviewed the criterion and recommended that it not be used.
3. **Current educational interpretation:** BP6 should be understood historically, while current variant interpretation should prioritize independently evaluable evidence.

This distinction is essential for safe patient education and prevents a historical ACMG/AMP criterion from being presented as a current recommendation.
