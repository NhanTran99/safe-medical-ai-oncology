# Knowledge Block 1 — What Was BP6?

## Patient Explanation

**BP6** was one of the **Supporting Benign Evidence Codes** in the original 2015 ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework.

It described a situation in which a **reputable source reported a genetic variant as benign**, but the underlying evidence was not available to the laboratory for independent evaluation.

---

## Clinical Importance

BP6 was intended to capture information from a trusted source when the laboratory could not independently review the evidence behind that source's conclusion.

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does "Reputable Source" Mean?

## Patient Explanation

A reputable source may be a trusted organization, laboratory, database, or other established source that reports information about genetic variants.

However, a source having a good reputation does **not** mean that every reported classification can automatically be accepted as proven.

The evidence supporting the classification is also important.

---

## Clinical Importance

Variant interpretation should consider the quality and availability of the underlying evidence, not only who reported the conclusion.

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Was BP6 Reconsidered?

## Patient Explanation

The main concern with BP6 is that a laboratory may be asked to rely on another source's conclusion when it cannot examine the evidence that led to that conclusion.

For example:

> A source reports that a variant is benign, but the underlying scientific evidence is not available for independent review.

In this situation, it is difficult for another laboratory to determine whether the conclusion is appropriate.

---

## Clinical Importance

Independent evaluation of underlying evidence helps make variant interpretation more transparent and reproducible.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Is BP6 Still Recommended for Use?

## Patient Explanation

**No.**

Although BP6 appeared in the original ACMG/AMP framework, the **ClinGen Sequence Variant Interpretation (SVI) Working Group recommended that BP6 not be used**.

ClinGen's general variant curation guidance states that BP6 and PP5 should not be applied because variant interpretation should rely on **primary evidence that can be independently evaluated**, rather than simply relying on another source's assertion.

---

## Clinical Importance

This represents an important evolution in genetic variant interpretation:

> **The evidence supporting a classification is more important than simply repeating another source's classification.**

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does a Reputable Source Saying "Benign" Prove That a Variant Is Benign?

## Patient Explanation

No.

A reputable source reporting a variant as benign can be useful information, but the report itself does not necessarily provide independently verifiable evidence.

Qualified professionals should consider the underlying evidence and other relevant information when determining the variant's classification.

---

## Clinical Importance

A reported classification and independently evaluated evidence are **not the same thing**.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Why Is This Important for Patients?

## Patient Explanation

Genetic test reports may contain information from databases, laboratories, publications, or other sources.

It is important to understand that:

> **A classification reported by another source is not automatically the same as a new piece of independently verified evidence.**

Modern variant interpretation therefore places importance on reviewing the evidence behind a classification whenever that evidence is available.

---

## Clinical Importance

This helps prevent overinterpretation of database or literature classifications and supports safer genomic interpretation.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

If a trusted laboratory or database says that a variant is benign, the variant has been proven benign.

**Fact**

Not necessarily.

The classification may be useful information, but the underlying evidence should be considered whenever possible. BP6 was ultimately not recommended for use because it could encourage reliance on an assertion without independent evaluation of the evidence behind it.

---

# Key Messages

- BP6 was part of the **original 2015 ACMG/AMP framework**.
- BP6 described a reputable source reporting a variant as benign when the underlying evidence was not available for independent evaluation.
- **ClinGen SVI recommends that BP6 not be used.**
- A source's classification is not automatically equivalent to independently evaluated evidence.
- Modern variant interpretation emphasizes evaluating the underlying evidence whenever possible.
- BP6 should **not** be presented as a currently recommended benign evidence criterion.
