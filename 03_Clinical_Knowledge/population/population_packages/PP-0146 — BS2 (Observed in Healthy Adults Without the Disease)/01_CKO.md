# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what BS2 is.
- Understand why finding a genetic variant in a genuinely healthy adult can sometimes provide evidence that the variant is unlikely to cause disease.
- Understand why the person's age and health status matter.
- Understand why BS2 is particularly relevant when a disorder is expected to have high penetrance and early onset.
- Understand why a healthy adult does not automatically make a variant benign.
- Understand why disease-specific context, inheritance pattern, penetrance, and adequate phenotyping matter.
- Understand that BS2 is Strong Benign Evidence rather than an automatic final classification.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is BS2?

## Patient Explanation

**BS2** is an ACMG/AMP **Strong Benign Evidence** criterion.

It can be considered when a genetic variant is found in a **genuinely healthy adult** in a genetic state that would normally be expected to cause a particular disorder.

If the disorder is expected to have **high or full penetrance at an early age**, finding the variant in an appropriately evaluated healthy adult can provide strong evidence that the variant is unlikely to cause that disorder.

---

## Clinical Importance

The reasoning behind BS2 is simple:

> If a variant were expected to cause an early-onset, highly penetrant disorder, finding that same variant in an appropriately evaluated healthy adult would be unexpected.

That observation can therefore provide evidence against pathogenicity.

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Does Finding a Variant in a Healthy Adult Matter?

## Patient Explanation

Genetic variants are interpreted partly by comparing what is observed in a person with what would be expected if the variant truly caused a disease.

If a variant is found in an adult who has been adequately evaluated and does not have a disorder that should already have appeared by that age, this can provide evidence against the variant being disease-causing.

This is especially informative when the disorder is expected to be highly penetrant and to appear early in life.

---

## Clinical Importance

The observation becomes meaningful because the person's health status is inconsistent with what would be expected if the variant were strongly disease-causing.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Does Age Matter?

## Patient Explanation

Being healthy at one age does not necessarily mean that a person will remain free of disease.

Some genetic disorders:

- develop during childhood or early adulthood;
- develop only later in life;
- affect only some people who carry a disease-causing variant.

Therefore, the age of the healthy adult matters.

A healthy 25-year-old may provide very different information from a healthy 75-year-old depending on the disorder being evaluated.

---

## Clinical Importance

BS2 is most informative when the disorder would be expected to have manifested by the person's age if the variant were truly pathogenic.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Does Penetrance Matter?

## Patient Explanation

**Penetrance** describes how often people carrying a particular disease-causing genetic variant actually develop the associated condition.

If a disorder has **full or very high penetrance**, most or all people carrying a truly disease-causing variant are expected to develop the disorder.

In that situation, finding the variant in a genuinely healthy adult can be strong evidence against pathogenicity.

If a disorder has **reduced penetrance**, however, some people carrying a disease-causing variant may remain healthy.

In that situation, finding the variant in a healthy adult may provide much less evidence against pathogenicity.

---

## Clinical Importance

This is why the original BS2 criterion specifically refers to disorders where **full penetrance is expected at an early age**.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Does the Inheritance Pattern Matter?

## Patient Explanation

The meaning of a variant can depend on how a disorder is inherited.

The original ACMG/AMP framework describes BS2 in relation to the relevant genetic state, including:

- heterozygous variants for appropriate dominant disorders;
- homozygous variants for appropriate recessive disorders;
- hemizygous variants for appropriate X-linked disorders.

The relevant genotype therefore has to match the disease's inheritance pattern.

---

## Clinical Importance

A variant found in a healthy person cannot be interpreted correctly without knowing what genetic state would normally be expected to cause the disorder.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Why Must the Person Really Be "Healthy"?

## Patient Explanation

A person should not simply be labelled "healthy" because a database contains no diagnosis for them.

For BS2, it may be important to know whether the person has been appropriately evaluated for the disorder.

Depending on the disease, this may involve:

- appropriate clinical history;
- relevant examination;
- age-appropriate screening;
- other disease-specific evaluation.

The more confidently the person's unaffected status can be established, the more meaningful the observation can be.

---

## Clinical Importance

A missing diagnosis is not always the same thing as evidence that a person does not have the disease.

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Does Finding a Variant in a Healthy Adult Mean It Is Benign?

## Patient Explanation

No.

A healthy adult carrying a variant does **not automatically** mean that the variant is benign.

The observation is meaningful only when the person's:

- age;
- genotype;
- inheritance pattern;
- disease characteristics;
- penetrance;
- and clinical evaluation

make it genuinely unexpected for that person to carry a disease-causing variant without having the disorder.

---

## Clinical Importance

Reduced penetrance, late-onset disease, variable expression, incomplete phenotyping, and other disease characteristics can weaken or prevent the use of BS2.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — Why Is This Important in Gastric Cancer Genetics?

## Patient Explanation

Some patients with gastric cancer undergo genetic testing because their cancer may be related to an inherited cancer-predisposition condition.

When interpreting a variant associated with a hereditary cancer syndrome relevant to gastric cancer, genetics professionals may consider whether that variant is observed in people who are appropriately evaluated and do not show the expected syndrome.

However, a healthy adult observation must always be interpreted according to the **specific hereditary cancer disorder**, including its age of onset and penetrance.

---

## Clinical Importance

The Safe Medical AI System must not generalize evidence from one hereditary gastric-cancer syndrome to another.

A healthy adult observation that is informative for one syndrome may have much less significance for another syndrome with later onset or reduced penetrance.

# Common Misconceptions

**Myth 1**

A healthy person carries the variant, so the variant is benign.

**Fact**

Not necessarily. The interpretation depends on the disease, age, penetrance, inheritance pattern, genotype, and quality of clinical evaluation.

---

**Myth 2**

Anyone who is an adult and healthy can be used for BS2.

**Fact**

The person must be appropriate for the disease model being evaluated. Age and expected disease onset are important.

---

**Myth 3**

BS2 applies equally to every hereditary cancer syndrome.

**Fact**

No. BS2 is disease-specific and may be modified, restricted, or not applicable depending on penetrance, age of onset, and disease-specific ClinGen guidance.

---

# Key Messages

- **BS2** is a **Strong Benign Evidence** criterion.
- Finding a variant in a genuinely healthy adult can provide evidence against pathogenicity when the disease should already have appeared if the variant were truly disease-causing.
- Age matters.
- Penetrance matters.
- Inheritance pattern and genotype matter.
- Adequate phenotyping matters.
- A healthy adult does not automatically make a variant benign.
- In hereditary gastric-cancer interpretation, BS2 must be considered in the context of the **specific cancer-predisposition disorder**.
- Disease-specific ClinGen recommendations may modify or restrict how BS2 is applied.

# Governance Status

**Current ACMG/AMP Evidence Criterion — CONTEXT-DEPENDENT APPLICATION**

BS2 should not be interpreted as a universal rule that any healthy adult carrying a variant provides benign evidence.

Its interpretation depends on the specific disorder, genotype, inheritance pattern, age, expected penetrance, age of onset, and quality of phenotyping.

Where available, disease- or gene-specific ClinGen Expert Panel specifications should take precedence over generic interpretation.

---

# Runtime Safety Rule

The Safe Medical AI System must not generate:

> "A healthy person with the variant means the variant is benign."

Instead, it should communicate:

> **A healthy adult carrying a variant may provide strong benign evidence when the disorder would be expected to have manifested by that age and the person's genotype and clinical evaluation are appropriate for the disease model.**
