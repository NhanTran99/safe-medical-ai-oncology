# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What is neoadjuvant therapy in gastric cancer, why may treatment be given before definitive surgery, how does it fit within the overall treatment sequence, what evidence supports preoperative treatment, and how are response, resectability, tumor biology, and patient fitness integrated into this strategy?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded, patient-facing explanation of treatment delivered before definitive surgery.

The package must allow a patient to understand:

- why treatment may be given before surgery;
- why preoperative treatment is not synonymous with unresectable disease;
- how neoadjuvant treatment differs from perioperative and adjuvant treatment;
- how chemotherapy and selected immunotherapy strategies fit within the concept;
- how response and reassessment connect treatment to surgery;
- why treatment decisions are individualized.

The package must not become a regimen manual.

# Primary Sources

## 1. Gastric Cancer v2.2026 — NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology

Primary current gastric-cancer guideline.

### Directly relevant source domains

- Primary treatment pathway.
- Perioperative systemic therapy.
- Neoadjuvant/perioperative immunotherapy.
- MSI-H/dMMR.
- Response assessment after preoperative therapy.
- Surgical pathway after systemic treatment.
- Multidisciplinary management.

NCCN states that:

- ER/EMR/ESD is the primary treatment option for Tis/T1a tumors;
- medically fit patients with resectable T1b or higher tumors may undergo surgery with D2 lymph-node dissection;
- perioperative systemic therapy is a category 1 recommendation for resectable T2 or higher tumors, any N;
- neoadjuvant/perioperative immune checkpoint inhibition may be considered for certain MSI-H/dMMR tumors.

This establishes the stage-dependent treatment architecture.

## 3. Treatment of Stomach Cancer — NCI

Patient-facing source.

Directly relevant concepts:

- treatment before surgery is called preoperative or neoadjuvant therapy;
- chemotherapy may be given before surgery;
- treatment before and after surgery is called perioperative therapy;
- treatment after surgery intended to lower recurrence risk is called adjuvant therapy.

This source provides the clearest patient-facing terminology for the core definition.

## 4. Chemotherapy for Stomach Cancer — ACS

Patient-facing source.

Directly relevant concepts:

- potentially resectable stomach cancer requires accurate staging before surgery;
- chemotherapy and/or immunotherapy may be used before surgery;
- treatment before and after surgery is perioperative therapy;
- selected MSI-H/dMMR tumors may receive immunotherapy before surgery followed by surgery and additional treatment.

# Detailed Evidence Notes

# Evidence Note 1 — Definition of Neoadjuvant Therapy

NCI patient-facing material explicitly defines treatment given before surgery as preoperative or neoadjuvant therapy.

This is the cleanest source for patient-facing terminology.

### Evidence position

**Established.**

### Interpretation

The term describes timing.

It does not identify:

- one drug;
- one chemotherapy regimen;
- one tumor stage;
- one treatment intent.

---

# Evidence Note 2 — Definition of Perioperative Therapy

NCI and ACS describe treatment before and after surgery as perioperative therapy.

Therefore:

**preoperative treatment**

is a component of:

**perioperative therapy**

when postoperative treatment is also planned.

### Evidence position

**Established.**

### Boundary implication

PP-0206 should not duplicate the complete perioperative strategy.

---

# Evidence Note 3 — Current NCCN Treatment Architecture

The current NCCN source identifies:

- perioperative systemic therapy as category 1 for resectable T2 or higher disease in medically fit patients;
- neoadjuvant/perioperative immunotherapy as an option in certain MSI-H/dMMR tumors.

### Evidence position

**Current guideline-supported.**

### Interpretation

Neoadjuvant therapy is clinically embedded in the broader multimodality treatment pathway.

---

# Evidence Note 4 — MAGIC

NCI PDQ reports the randomized phase III MAGIC trial.

Population:

- stage II or higher adenocarcinoma of stomach or lower third of esophagus.

Intervention:

- three cycles of ECF before and after surgery.

Comparator:

- surgery alone.

Results:

- PFS HR 0.66;
- OS HR 0.75;
- 5-year OS 36.3% versus 23%.

### Evidence position

**Level A1 randomized evidence as reported by NCI PDQ.**

### Limitation

The trial evaluated **perioperative chemotherapy**, not isolated neoadjuvant therapy.

Therefore it supports the importance of the preoperative component without becoming a pure neoadjuvant trial.

---

# Evidence Note 5 — FLOT4

NCI PDQ reports randomized phase III FLOT4:

Population:

- 716 patients;
- stage IB–III;
- resectable gastric or gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma.

Intervention:

- perioperative FLOT.

Comparator:

- ECF/ECX.

Results:

- median OS 50 vs 35 months;
- HR 0.77;
- 95% CI 0.63–0.94;
- P=.012;
- margin-free resection 85% vs 78%.

### Evidence position

**Level A1 randomized evidence as reported by NCI PDQ.**

### Limitation

The trial evaluated a perioperative regimen.

It does not establish that the preoperative component alone is responsible for the entire observed effect.

---

# Evidence Note 6 — FLOT4 and PP Ownership

FLOT4 is relevant to PP-0206 because FLOT includes treatment before surgery.

But the evidence belongs primarily to:

- perioperative strategy;
- FLOT regimen.

Therefore PP-0206 uses the evidence only to establish the role of preoperative treatment within a broader strategy.

---

# Evidence Note 7 — MSI-H/dMMR

NCCN identifies neoadjuvant or perioperative immunotherapy as useful in certain circumstances for MSI-H/dMMR gastric tumors.

Listed options include:

- dostarlimab-gxly for neoadjuvant therapy only;
- nivolumab + ipilimumab followed by nivolumab;
- pembrolizumab;
- tremelimumab + durvalumab for neoadjuvant therapy only.

### Evidence position

**Current guideline-supported, selected population.**

### Safety interpretation

This is not a universal gastric-cancer treatment rule.

---

# Evidence Note 8 — NEONIPIGA

NCCN summarizes NEONIPIGA:

- phase II;
- 32 patients;
- locally advanced gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma;
- confirmed MSI-H/dMMR;
- neoadjuvant nivolumab + ipilimumab;
- adjuvant nivolumab.

After neoadjuvant therapy:

- 29 underwent R0 resection;
- approximately 59% had pathologic complete response.

NCCN recommends this strategy as a category 2A option useful in certain circumstances.

### Evidence limitation

The study was phase II and specialized to MSI-H/dMMR disease.

It does not justify extrapolation to unselected gastric cancer.

---

# Evidence Note 9 — Neoadjuvant Pembrolizumab

NCCN summarizes a phase II study of locally advanced MSI-H/dMMR solid tumors.

Among 33 assessed patients:

- ORR 82%;
- CR 30%.

Among 17 surgically resected patients:

- pathologic CR 65%.

### Critical limitation

Most patients had stage III colorectal cancer.

Therefore this is not a gastric-cancer-specific randomized result.

It is included only to contextualize the neoadjuvant MSI-H/dMMR immunotherapy landscape.

---

# Evidence Note 10 — Response Assessment

NCCN treatment pathways include response assessment after preoperative therapy.

Assessment can include:

- CT;
- FDG-PET/CT when clinically indicated;
- EGD and biopsy.

The purpose is to determine the current disease state and inform subsequent management.

### Evidence position

**Current guideline pathway.**

---

# Evidence Note 11 — Timing of Reassessment

The current NCCN pathway includes assessment after completion of preoperative therapy.

The package deliberately does not establish a universal timing rule outside the source-supported pathway.

### Boundary

Detailed response-assessment timing and imaging methodology belong to downstream response packages.

---

# Evidence Note 12 — Complete Response After Neoadjuvant Immunotherapy

NCCN explicitly notes uncertainty regarding the role of surgery after biopsy-proven and radiologic/metabolic complete response to neoadjuvant immunotherapy in MSI-H/dMMR disease.

### Evidence position

**Explicit guideline uncertainty.**

### Patient-safety implication

Do not teach:

> “Complete response means surgery is unnecessary.”

---

# Evidence Note 13 — Pathologic Response

NCCN states that pathologic response and histologic tumor regression after neoadjuvant therapy have been shown to be predictors of survival in gastric adenocarcinoma.

### Evidence position

**Guideline-supported prognostic relevance.**

### Boundary

The package does not define detailed tumor-regression grading methodology.

---

# Evidence Note 14 — Accurate Staging

ACS emphasizes accurate staging before potentially curative surgery.

The source describes:

- CT;
- PET;
- EUS;
- staging laparoscopy.

### Interpretation

Treatment sequencing should be based on a reliable understanding of disease extent.

---

# Evidence Note 15 — Multidisciplinary Care

NCCN strongly recommends multidisciplinary management for localized gastric cancer.

ESMO-ASCO also emphasizes multidisciplinary discussions on treatment sequencing and treatment planning.

### Evidence position

**Strong professional-framework support.**

---

# Evidence Note 16 — Early Disease

NCCN identifies ER/EMR/ESD as the primary treatment option for Tis/T1a tumors.

This demonstrates that treatment sequence is stage-dependent.

### Boundary implication

PP-0206 must not generalize neoadjuvant therapy to all gastric cancers.

---

# Evidence Note 17 — T1b and Higher

NCCN states that medically fit patients with resectable T1b or higher tumors may receive surgery with D2 lymph-node dissection.

This creates a distinction between early disease and more advanced resectable disease.

### Boundary implication

Neoadjuvant treatment should be discussed in the appropriate stage and clinical context.

---

# Evidence Note 18 — Resectable T2 or Higher

NCCN states that perioperative systemic therapy is category 1 for resectable T2 or higher tumors, any N, in medically fit patients.

### Evidence position

**Current guideline-supported.**

### Interpretation

This is the major current systemic-treatment anchor for the preoperative component.

---

# Evidence Note 19 — Patient Fitness

Treatment selection depends on medical fitness.

Relevant considerations include:

- functional status;
- nutritional status;
- comorbidities;
- organ function;
- treatment tolerance.

### Evidence position

**Clinical framework support.**

### Boundary

No universal threshold is defined by PP-0206.

---

# Evidence Note 20 — Neoadjuvant Treatment Is Not a Guarantee of Surgery

The pathway may change after treatment because of:

- progression;
- new metastatic disease;
- persistent unresectability;
- treatment intolerance;
- other clinical factors.

Therefore:

**neoadjuvant therapy → surgery**

is an intended pathway, not a guaranteed outcome.

---

# Evidence Note 21 — Treatment Response Is Not the Same as Treatment Success in Isolation

Response can be:

- radiologic;
- endoscopic;
- pathologic.

These measures answer different questions.

A response should be interpreted in relation to:

- disease extent;
- resectability;
- treatment plan;
- surgical pathology;
- subsequent clinical course.

---

# Evidence Note 22 — Why the Package Does Not Predict Individual Benefit

Trial outcomes describe populations.

They do not determine:

- an individual patient's exact survival;
- an individual's probability of complete response;
- an individual's recurrence risk.

PP-0206 therefore reports trial evidence without converting it into personal predictions.

---

# Evidence Note 23 — Why Historical Evidence Must Be Distinguished From Current Guidance

MAGIC is historical randomized evidence.

FLOT4 is newer randomized evidence.

NCCN is the current guideline framework.

The package preserves all three levels rather than treating the oldest trial as the current prescription.

---

# Evidence Note 24 — Evidence Language

Use:

- “showed” for trial findings;
- “supports” for evidence;
- “recommends” only when current guideline wording supports it;
- “may be considered” for conditional pathways;
- “unclear” where the guideline explicitly states uncertainty.

Avoid:

- “always”;
- “never”;
- “guarantees”;
- “cures”;
- “everyone.”

---

# Evidence Note 25 — Source-Grounded Limitations

The supplied source set supports the strategy and major evidence.

It does not support a universal:

- regimen choice for every patient;
- dose;
- cycle schedule;
- toxicity algorithm;
- individualized response prediction;
- individualized surgical decision.

These remain outside the PP.

# Evidence Note 27 — Evidence-to-Patient Translation

### Technical statement

“Perioperative chemotherapy improved OS.”

### Patient-facing translation

> “In randomized studies, some treatment strategies given before and after surgery improved survival compared with surgery alone.”

The second statement preserves the evidence without turning a trial result into a personal guarantee.

---

# Evidence Note 28 — Evidence-to-Patient Translation: FLOT

### Technical statement

“FLOT HR 0.77.”

### Patient-facing translation

> “In a randomized study of resectable gastric or gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, perioperative FLOT was associated with longer overall survival than the comparison regimen.”

Do not state:

> “FLOT will increase your survival by a fixed amount.”

---

# Evidence Note 29 — Evidence-to-Patient Translation: MSI-H/dMMR

### Technical statement

“NEONIPIGA showed approximately 59% pathologic CR.”

### Patient-facing translation

> “In a small phase II study of MSI-H/dMMR gastric or EGJ cancer, many patients had a strong treatment response, including a substantial proportion with no viable tumor found in the surgical specimen. This evidence applies to a selected group and does not mean the same result occurs in every patient.”

---

# Evidence Gaps

The source set does not establish:

1. A single universal neoadjuvant regimen for all gastric cancer.
2. A universal preoperative treatment duration.
3. A universal number of cycles.
4. A universal dose-modification algorithm.
5. A universal surgery rule after complete response.
6. A universal strategy for progression during treatment.
7. Individual patient response probabilities.
8. Individual patient survival predictions.
9. Universal treatment thresholds based solely on stage.
10. A universal role for neoadjuvant immunotherapy in biomarker-unselected disease.

These are intentionally left outside PP-0206.
