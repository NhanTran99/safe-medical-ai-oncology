## Clinical Question

> **What is peritoneal carcinomatosis in gastric cancer, how is it recognized and characterized, what problems can it cause, and why does it matter clinically?**

## Educational Intent

This package converts the project Source Materials into a patient-facing, evidence-traceable knowledge node focused specifically on peritoneal carcinomatosis.

The package is intentionally positioned between:

**PP-0048 Peritoneal Metastasis**
→ **PP-0224 Peritoneal Carcinomatosis**
→ **PP-0225 Peritoneal Carcinoma as Only Disease**
→ **PP-0226 HIPEC**

The goal is to deepen clinical understanding without duplicating the ownership of these adjacent packages.

## Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

### Evidence Note 1 — Peritoneal disease as a clinically important metastatic pattern

NCCN v2.2026 identifies a high risk of peritoneal disease in locally advanced/metastatic gastric cancer and describes it as an adverse prognostic feature.

The evidence supports making peritoneal carcinomatosis a dedicated knowledge node rather than treating it as a minor subtype of metastatic disease.

### Evidence Note 2 — Positive cytology

The NCCN staging material is especially important because it prevents a common misconception:

**visible peritoneal deposits are not required for peritoneal metastatic disease to be clinically relevant.**

Positive cytology in the absence of visible implants is considered M1 disease in the supplied NCCN framework. fileciteturn31file12turn31file13

### Evidence Note 3 — Laparoscopy

NCCN notes that FDG-PET does not replace staging laparoscopy for detection of peritoneal disease and that laparoscopy can detect radiographically occult metastases. fileciteturn31file12

This supports inclusion of diagnostic laparoscopy as a core conceptual block.

### Evidence Note 4 — PCI

NCCN v2.2026 specifically requests documented PCI in the peritoneal-only pathway and uses PCI thresholds in treatment architecture. fileciteturn31file7turn31file15

Therefore PCI must be visible in PP-0224.

However, detailed scoring methodology would create a technical package and overlap with downstream treatment-specific ownership.

### Evidence Note 5 — Systemic therapy and reassessment

NCCN v2.2026 places systemic treatment before reassessment in the dedicated peritoneal-only pathway.

This supports a high-level longitudinal model:

**characterize → systemic therapy → reassess → multidisciplinary review**

without reproducing regimen details. fileciteturn31file15

### Evidence Note 6 — Extraperitoneal disease

The NCCN pathway differentiates patients with peritoneal-only disease from those with extraperitoneal metastases or higher-burden/progressive disease. fileciteturn31file7turn31file15

This is a central reason PP-0224 must explain the distinction without owning the full PP-0225 algorithm.

### Evidence Note 7 — Cytoreduction

NCCN links selected cytoreduction to the feasibility of complete removal of visible disease.

The package therefore explains cytoreduction conceptually but does not teach operative technique. fileciteturn31file15turn31file18

### Evidence Note 8 — HIPEC

NCCN v2.2026 states that IC/HIPEC should be pursued only in selected circumstances and after multidisciplinary discussion. It also distinguishes higher-burden disease and clinical-trial contexts. fileciteturn31file14turn31file18

This supports introducing HIPEC but delegating the dedicated evidence and treatment package to PP-0226.

### Evidence Note 9 — PIPAC

NCCN explicitly identifies PIPAC as investigational and restricts it to clinical-trial context. fileciteturn31file14

This is included as evidence maturity/boundary context rather than as a treatment recommendation.

## Clinical Claims Summary

1. Peritoneal carcinomatosis is a clinically important manifestation of gastric-cancer spread.
2. It remains gastric cancer.
3. Visible deposits are not the only form of clinically relevant peritoneal involvement.
4. Positive cytology can represent M1 disease.
5. Imaging is important but does not necessarily exclude occult peritoneal disease.
6. Laparoscopy can provide direct peritoneal assessment.
7. Washings provide cytologic information.
8. PCI provides a structured description of disease extent.
9. Symptoms may include abdominal distension, pain/discomfort, early satiety, nausea/vomiting and ascites.
10. Peritoneal disease may cause bowel complications.
11. Disease burden and extraperitoneal disease affect management context.
12. Systemic treatment is an important component of management.
13. Selected patients may be assessed for cytoreduction.
14. HIPEC is selective rather than universal.
15. PIPAC is investigational in the supplied NCCN framework.
16. Multidisciplinary assessment is central to selected treatment decisions.

## Evidence Consistency Review

### NCCN v2.2026 versus NCCN v2.2025

Both guideline versions support:

- the importance of peritoneal disease;
- systemic therapy;
- PCI-based assessment;
- selected cytoreduction;
- selective IC/HIPEC;
- multidisciplinary decision-making;
- investigational status of PIPAC.

The v2.2026 source is treated as the current primary guideline anchor.

**Result: CONSISTENT.**

### NCCN versus NCI

NCI patient-facing material describes HIPEC as a regional approach being studied and available at selected centers, while NCCN provides the more detailed selection framework.

**Result: CONSISTENT, with NCCN retaining higher disease-specific authority.**

## Evidence Limitations

1. Peritoneal carcinomatosis is heterogeneous.
2. Imaging sensitivity is imperfect for small-volume disease.
3. PCI is useful but does not capture every clinically relevant variable.
4. Evidence for intraperitoneal treatment remains evolving.
5. HIPEC evidence includes heterogeneous studies and selected populations.
6. PIPAC remains investigational in the supplied guideline.
7. Organ-specific complications may require separate evidence.
8. Individual treatment decisions require clinical context beyond the package.

## Evidence Gaps

### Gap 1 — Universal treatment rule based on PCI

The source does not support PCI as a standalone treatment determinant.

### Gap 2 — Universal HIPEC benefit

The supplied NCCN material supports selected use rather than universal benefit.

### Gap 3 — PIPAC standardization

The source classifies PIPAC as investigational.

### Gap 4 — Imaging exclusion

A negative imaging study cannot be treated as a universal exclusion of occult peritoneal disease.

### Gap 5 — Individual prognosis

No single peritoneal-disease feature can safely generate an individualized prognosis.

## Evidence-to-Patient Translation

### Why is peritoneal disease important?

Because it means the gastric cancer has spread to the peritoneal surfaces and may behave differently from disease confined to the stomach.

### Why might more tests be needed?

Because the team needs to understand the extent of disease and whether there are metastatic sites that imaging does not fully characterize.

### Why is PCI mentioned?

Because the amount and distribution of peritoneal disease matter when considering treatment options.

### Why is HIPEC not automatic?

Because it is a specialized treatment for selected circumstances and requires assessment of disease extent, response, surgical feasibility and patient factors.
