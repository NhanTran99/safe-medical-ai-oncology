## Atomic Clinical Question

> **What is peritoneal carcinomatosis in gastric cancer, how is it recognized and characterized, what problems can it cause, and why does it matter clinically?**

## Educational Objectives

After reading this package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain what peritoneal carcinomatosis means in gastric cancer.
2. Distinguish peritoneal carcinomatosis from a new primary cancer of the abdomen.
3. Understand how gastric cancer can disseminate across the peritoneal surfaces.
4. Recognize common clinical manifestations, including abdominal symptoms and ascites.
5. Understand why imaging may not completely characterize small-volume peritoneal disease.
6. Explain the conceptual roles of diagnostic laparoscopy, peritoneal washings, cytology and biopsy.
7. Understand what the Peritoneal Cancer Index (PCI) represents and why disease burden matters.
8. Understand the difference between peritoneal disease alone and peritoneal disease accompanied by extraperitoneal metastases.
9. Understand the high-level role of systemic treatment, reassessment, multidisciplinary discussion and selected local approaches.
10. Understand why HIPEC is a specialized downstream topic rather than an automatic treatment for all peritoneal disease.
11. Recognize important limitations and patient-facing misconceptions.
12. Avoid interpreting general information as an individualized treatment or prognosis decision.

## Scope

### Included

PP-0224 owns the clinical disease-state concept of **peritoneal carcinomatosis**. It extends the foundational PP-0048 Peritoneal Metastasis package into a deeper clinical layer covering disease distribution, visible peritoneal deposits, positive cytology, symptoms, ascites, diagnostic characterization, laparoscopy, washings, PCI, disease burden, clinical significance, systemic-treatment architecture, selected cytoreductive concepts, multidisciplinary review, clinical-trial context and patient-facing interpretation.

### Not Included

PP-0224 does not own detailed PCI scoring, operative laparoscopy technique, cytology laboratory methods, detailed systemic regimens, detailed biomarker-directed therapy, detailed cytoreductive surgery, HIPEC evidence/technical methodology, PIPAC technique, the dedicated peritoneal-only disease algorithm, detailed palliative procedures, or individualized treatment/prognosis.

## Clinical Knowledge Blocks

### 1. What is the peritoneum?

The peritoneum is a thin lining associated with the abdominal cavity and the surfaces of many abdominal organs. In gastric cancer, it is clinically important because tumor cells can reach and implant on peritoneal surfaces.

At patient-facing level, the most important idea is simple:

> The peritoneum is part of the abdominal environment in which gastric cancer can spread.

The package does not require detailed operative anatomy. The purpose of the anatomy is to make the later concepts of peritoneal deposits, washings, ascites and laparoscopy understandable.

### 2. Peritoneal metastasis versus peritoneal carcinomatosis

**Peritoneal metastasis** is the broad concept of gastric cancer spreading to the peritoneal lining.

**Peritoneal carcinomatosis** describes a clinically important pattern in which metastatic tumor involves multiple areas of the peritoneal surfaces, potentially producing nodules, plaques, implants, diffuse involvement and associated fluid or symptoms.

The terms can overlap in clinical use, but PP-0224 uses “peritoneal carcinomatosis” as the more clinically developed disease-state concept.

PP-0048 remains the foundational package for the basic question:

> What is peritoneal metastasis?

PP-0224 answers the next-level question:

> What does clinically significant peritoneal involvement look like, how is it characterized, and why does it change the clinical picture?

### 3. It remains gastric cancer

Peritoneal carcinomatosis from gastric cancer is not automatically a new primary cancer of the peritoneum.

The tumor remains biologically related to the original gastric cancer.

This distinction matters because treatment planning is based on the primary cancer and its metastatic pattern, not on relabeling the peritoneal deposits as an unrelated cancer.

### 4. How gastric cancer reaches the peritoneum

Gastric cancer can disseminate through the abdominal cavity and establish tumor deposits on peritoneal surfaces.

Conceptually:

**Gastric primary tumor**
→ **tumor cells gain access to the peritoneal environment**
→ **dissemination within the abdominal cavity**
→ **implantation on peritoneal surfaces**
→ **peritoneal deposits**
→ **progressive peritoneal disease in some patients**

This is a conceptual disease-biology explanation.

PP-0224 does not attempt to teach detailed molecular adhesion pathways, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, molecular subtypes or experimental mechanisms.

### 5. Visible peritoneal disease

Peritoneal disease can appear as:

- small nodules;
- larger tumor deposits;
- plaques;
- clustered implants;
- more diffuse surface involvement.

The distribution may be focal or extensive.

The amount and distribution of disease matter because the clinical implications are not determined solely by the fact that peritoneal spread exists.

### 6. Positive peritoneal cytology

Peritoneal washings can contain malignant gastric-cancer cells even when obvious visible implants are not identified.

NCCN v2.2026 treats positive peritoneal cytology, even without visible implants, as M1 disease. fileciteturn31file12turn31file13

This is an important distinction:

**No obvious visible implant**
does not necessarily mean
**no peritoneal metastatic disease**.

The package explains the clinical concept but excludes laboratory cytology methodology.

### 7. Why diagnostic laparoscopy can matter

Cross-sectional imaging is important, but small-volume peritoneal disease may be difficult to characterize completely by imaging.

NCCN v2.2026 notes that FDG-PET does not replace staging laparoscopy for detection of peritoneal disease and that laparoscopy can detect occult metastatic disease. fileciteturn31file12

Diagnostic laparoscopy may therefore provide:

- direct visualization of the peritoneal surfaces;
- an opportunity to assess distribution;
- peritoneal washings;
- cytology;
- biopsy when indicated;
- information relevant to PCI.

The PP deliberately stops before operative technique.

### 8. Imaging does not provide the whole story

CT chest/abdomen/pelvis is a central part of staging and advanced-disease evaluation.

FDG-PET/CT can be considered in selected contexts.

However, neither should be interpreted as a guarantee that all microscopic or very small-volume peritoneal disease has been excluded.

The patient-facing message is:

> A scan is an important part of the assessment, but the treating team may need other information when peritoneal disease is suspected or when treatment planning depends on its exact extent.

### 9. Peritoneal washings

Peritoneal washings involve sampling fluid from the peritoneal cavity for cytologic assessment.

The purpose is to detect tumor cells that may not be visible as obvious deposits.

Washings therefore complement visual assessment.

The package does not own:

- specimen-processing protocols;
- cytology staining;
- microscopic interpretation criteria;
- laboratory validation.

### 10. Biopsy

Biopsy may be used when tissue confirmation is clinically indicated.

The need for biopsy depends on the clinical situation and whether confirmation would change management.

The important educational principle is:

> A suspicious peritoneal finding is interpreted in context rather than through a single test in isolation.

### 11. Peritoneal Cancer Index

The **Peritoneal Cancer Index (PCI)** is a structured way of describing the extent and distribution of peritoneal disease.

PCI is clinically useful because “peritoneal carcinomatosis” does not tell the team how extensive the disease is.

The package explains PCI conceptually.

It does not provide a detailed scoring manual or surgical mapping tutorial.

### 12. Disease burden is multidimensional

Peritoneal disease burden can involve:

- number of deposits;
- distribution;
- size of deposits;
- confluent disease;
- ascites;
- symptoms;
- bowel involvement;
- response or progression;
- presence or absence of disease outside the peritoneum.

PCI is one structured measure within this broader clinical picture.

### 13. Lower versus higher peritoneal burden

NCCN v2.2026 uses PCI categories in the dedicated peritoneal-only pathway, including a lower-burden group at PCI ≤10 and a higher-burden group at PCI >10. fileciteturn31file7turn31file15

These categories should not be misunderstood as an automatic treatment rule.

PCI does not independently determine whether a patient should receive cytoreductive surgery or HIPEC.

Treatment consideration also depends on:

- disease response or progression;
- extraperitoneal metastases;
- predicted ability to achieve complete cytoreduction;
- patient fitness;
- multidisciplinary assessment;
- clinical-trial context.

### 14. Symptoms of peritoneal carcinomatosis

Peritoneal disease can produce symptoms through tumor deposits, inflammation, fluid accumulation or effects on abdominal organs.

Possible manifestations include:

- abdominal distension;
- abdominal discomfort or pain;
- reduced appetite;
- early satiety;
- nausea;
- vomiting;
- altered bowel function;
- weight loss;
- ascites;
- symptoms associated with obstruction in advanced disease.

These symptoms are not specific enough to diagnose peritoneal carcinomatosis by themselves.

### 15. Ascites

**Ascites** means abnormal accumulation of fluid within the abdominal cavity.

When associated with peritoneal cancer, ascites can contribute to:

- abdominal enlargement;
- discomfort;
- early fullness;
- reduced appetite;
- impaired mobility;
- shortness of breath when severe.

The presence of ascites does not by itself establish that the fluid is malignant. Clinical evaluation may be required.

PP-0224 provides the disease-state context.

Detailed drainage, diuretic and procedural management is delegated.

### 16. Bowel dysfunction and obstruction

Advanced peritoneal disease can affect bowel movement or compress/involve bowel structures.

Possible consequences include:

- abdominal distension;
- nausea/vomiting;
- reduced oral intake;
- constipation or altered bowel function;
- obstruction.

The package explains the mechanism at a high level.

Detailed obstruction management, stenting, bypass surgery, venting procedures and emergency algorithms belong to dedicated treatment/palliative packages.

### 17. Nutritional consequences

Peritoneal disease may worsen nutrition through:

- reduced appetite;
- early satiety;
- nausea/vomiting;
- abdominal discomfort;
- obstruction;
- systemic effects of advanced cancer.

Nutritional status can in turn influence:

- physical function;
- tolerance of treatment;
- recovery;
- quality of life.

The package therefore includes nutritional context but does not provide an individualized nutrition prescription.

### 18. Relationship to overall metastatic disease

Peritoneal carcinomatosis may occur:

- as the dominant metastatic pattern;
- together with liver metastases;
- together with lung or other distant disease;
- as peritoneal disease with positive cytology;
- as peritoneal-only metastatic disease.

This distinction is critical for downstream routing.

PP-0223 owns the overall metastatic-disease framework.

PP-0225 owns the specialized situation in which peritoneal disease is the only metastatic disease.

### 19. Peritoneal disease and prognosis

NCCN v2.2026 describes peritoneal disease as an adverse prognostic feature in locally advanced or metastatic gastric cancer. fileciteturn31file15

However, prognosis is not determined by the word “carcinomatosis” alone.

Important variables include:

- disease burden;
- PCI;
- response to systemic therapy;
- extraperitoneal metastases;
- performance status;
- nutritional status;
- tumor biology;
- treatment options;
- patient goals.

No individual survival estimate should be derived from this package.

### 20. Systemic therapy remains central

For appropriate patients with peritoneal metastatic gastric cancer, systemic treatment is an important part of management.

NCCN's peritoneal-only pathway begins with systemic therapy in selected patients before restaging and further consideration. fileciteturn31file15

PP-0224 owns this **architecture**, not the drug regimen.

Detailed systemic treatment belongs to PP-0208 and therapy-specific packages.

### 21. Reassessment after treatment

Peritoneal disease is dynamic.

A conceptual sequence is:

**Baseline characterization**
→ **systemic treatment**
→ **reassessment**
→ **stable/improved disease or progression**
→ **multidisciplinary review when appropriate**
→ **continue/adapt treatment or consider selected additional approaches**

This is not a universal algorithm.

Detailed response assessment and RECIST belong to PP-0217 and PP-0218.

### 22. Cytoreductive surgery

Cytoreductive surgery aims to remove visible peritoneal tumor deposits.

Its relevance is highly selected.

The question is not simply:

> “Does the patient have peritoneal disease?”

It is:

> “Can a complete or clinically appropriate cytoreduction realistically be achieved in this patient's overall disease context?”

NCCN v2.2026 links selected cytoreductive approaches to low-burden disease, response/stability, absence of extraperitoneal disease in the dedicated peritoneal-only pathway and predicted complete cytoreduction. fileciteturn31file7turn31file15

Detailed surgery belongs downstream.

### 23. HIPEC — why it appears in this PP but is not owned here

**HIPEC** is hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy, a specialized intraperitoneal treatment used in conjunction with cytoreductive surgery in selected settings.

PP-0224 needs to introduce HIPEC because it is a major concept encountered when patients learn about peritoneal carcinomatosis.

But:

> **Peritoneal carcinomatosis does not automatically mean HIPEC.**

NCCN v2.2026 states that IC/HIPEC should be pursued only in selected circumstances and after multidisciplinary discussion; for certain patients with PCI ≤10 and candidates for complete cytoreduction it is an option, while higher-burden situations may be considered in a clinical-trial context. fileciteturn31file14turn31file18

Detailed HIPEC evidence and treatment selection belong to PP-0226.

### 24. PIPAC

**PIPAC** is pressurized intraperitoneal aerosolized chemotherapy.

The project must preserve the distinction between:

- established/selected treatment approaches;
- investigational approaches.

NCCN v2.2026 states that PIPAC is investigational and should only be performed in the context of a clinical trial. fileciteturn31file14

PP-0224 therefore mentions PIPAC only as downstream context.

### 25. Multidisciplinary assessment

Peritoneal disease can require coordinated input from:

- medical oncology;
- surgical oncology;
- radiology;
- pathology;
- gastroenterology/endoscopy;
- nutrition/supportive care;
- other specialists as clinically relevant.

NCCN v2.2026 explicitly places treatment decisions for selected peritoneal disease in multidisciplinary discussion/tumor-board context. fileciteturn31file15turn31file18

PP-0224 owns the principle.

PP-0232 owns detailed MDT workflow.

## Patient-Facing Questions

### Is peritoneal carcinomatosis a new cancer?

Usually no. In this context it means the gastric cancer has spread to the peritoneal surfaces.

### Can peritoneal disease be present when CT does not clearly show it?

Yes. Small-volume or occult disease may be difficult to characterize completely with imaging.

### Why might I need a laparoscopy?

A laparoscopy can allow direct assessment of the peritoneal surfaces and may allow washings, cytology and biopsy when indicated.

### What is PCI?

PCI is a structured measure used to describe the extent of peritoneal disease.

### Does PCI alone decide treatment?

No. PCI is one part of a broader assessment.

### What is ascites?

Ascites is abnormal fluid accumulation in the abdominal cavity. In advanced gastric cancer it may occur with peritoneal disease, but not every ascites is malignant.

### Does peritoneal carcinomatosis mean there is no treatment?

No. Treatment may include systemic therapy, selected local approaches, clinical trials and supportive care depending on the overall situation.

### Does everyone with peritoneal disease receive HIPEC?

No. HIPEC is a specialized option for selected circumstances and requires multidisciplinary assessment.

### Does positive cytology matter if no implants are visible?

Yes. NCCN considers positive peritoneal cytology in the absence of visible implants to represent M1 disease. fileciteturn31file13

## Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1
**“Peritoneal carcinomatosis is a new cancer of the abdomen.”**

**Fact:** In gastric cancer, it generally represents spread of the gastric cancer to the peritoneal surfaces.

### Myth 2
**“A negative CT proves that there is no peritoneal disease.”**

**Fact:** Small-volume or occult peritoneal disease may be difficult to characterize completely with imaging.

### Myth 3
**“Positive cytology means there must be a visible tumor deposit.”**

**Fact:** Malignant cells may be detected in peritoneal washings even without obvious visible implants.

### Myth 4
**“PCI alone decides treatment.”**

**Fact:** PCI is one component of a broader assessment that includes disease distribution, response, extraperitoneal disease, feasibility of complete cytoreduction, patient fitness and multidisciplinary judgment.

### Myth 5
**“Peritoneal carcinomatosis means treatment is impossible.”**

**Fact:** Management can include systemic treatment, selected local approaches, clinical trials and supportive care.

### Myth 6
**“Everyone with peritoneal disease should have HIPEC.”**

**Fact:** HIPEC is a specialized approach used selectively and is not appropriate for every patient.

### Myth 7
**“Ascites automatically means the cancer has spread to the peritoneum.”**

**Fact:** Ascites has multiple possible causes and requires clinical evaluation.

### Myth 8
**“More extensive peritoneal disease automatically means one specific treatment.”**

**Fact:** Disease burden is one important factor, but management is multidimensional.

## Clinical Reasoning Model

A safe conceptual model is:

**Confirm disease**
→ **Map peritoneal distribution**
→ **Assess burden**
→ **Assess cytology/biopsy when indicated**
→ **Assess extraperitoneal disease**
→ **Assess patient fitness and goals**
→ **Review treatment history**
→ **Systemic treatment / reassessment**
→ **Multidisciplinary consideration of selected additional approaches**

This is a reasoning framework, not a patient-specific treatment algorithm.

## Clinical Safety Boundary

PP-0224 provides population-level education.

It does not:

- diagnose peritoneal carcinomatosis;
- interpret an individual's CT or laparoscopy;
- determine whether cytology is malignant in an individual case;
- calculate an individualized PCI from an operative report;
- determine HIPEC eligibility;
- prescribe systemic therapy;
- recommend surgery for an individual;
- estimate individual survival;
- replace multidisciplinary clinical assessment.

## Key Messages

1. Peritoneal carcinomatosis is a clinically important pattern of gastric-cancer spread to the peritoneal surfaces.
2. It remains gastric cancer rather than becoming a new primary peritoneal cancer.
3. Visible disease is only one way peritoneal involvement may be detected.
4. Positive peritoneal cytology can represent M1 disease even without visible implants.
5. Imaging is important but may not fully characterize small-volume peritoneal disease.
6. Diagnostic laparoscopy and peritoneal washings can provide information that imaging may miss.
7. PCI describes the extent of peritoneal disease and is one component of treatment planning.
8. Peritoneal disease can cause ascites, abdominal symptoms and bowel complications.
9. Systemic therapy remains an important management component for appropriate patients.
10. Selected patients may undergo multidisciplinary evaluation for additional local approaches.
11. HIPEC is specialized and is not automatically appropriate for every patient.
12. PIPAC remains investigational in the supplied NCCN framework.
13. Detailed treatment decisions belong to dedicated downstream packages and the treating team.
