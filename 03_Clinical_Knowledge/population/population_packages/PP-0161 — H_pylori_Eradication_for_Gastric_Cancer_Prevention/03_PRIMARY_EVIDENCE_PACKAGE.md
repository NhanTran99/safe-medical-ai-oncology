## Clinical Question

> How is H. pylori eradication used as a gastric-cancer prevention intervention, who may receive it in relevant clinical contexts, what evidence supports it, and what should be considered after treatment?

## Key Evidence

### Shandong randomized trial

NCI reports that short-term amoxicillin plus omeprazole treatment reduced gastric-cancer incidence by 39% during 15 years of follow-up. At 22 years, H. pylori treatment was associated with absolute reductions in gastric-cancer incidence and mortality.

The regimen is retained here as **historical trial evidence**, not as a current prescription.

### Family-history randomized trial

NCI reports a randomized trial in individuals with a first-degree relative with gastric cancer. Gastric-cancer incidence was 2.7% with placebo versus 1.2% after H. pylori treatment (HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.21–0.94).

The historical regimen included lansoprazole, amoxicillin, and clarithromycin for 7 days. It is not presented as a contemporary universal regimen.

### Early gastric cancer / secondary prevention

NCI reports that patients treated for early gastric cancer who received H. pylori eradication were approximately half as likely to develop metachronous gastric cancer.

NCCN v2.2026 states that H. pylori-positive patients after curative endoscopic resection or gastric subtotal resection should receive an eradication regimen.

### Current regimen limitation

NCCN directs regimen choice and duration to the latest ACG or Maastricht H. pylori guidance. The project source PDFs do not contain those detailed contemporary regimens.

Therefore:
- exact first-line regimen = **source gap**;
- exact duration = **source gap**;
- detailed salvage pathway = **source gap**;
- exact test-of-cure timing = **source gap**.

These gaps are intentionally preserved.

## Clinical Claims Summary

### Established / guideline-supported
- H. pylori eradication reduces gastric-cancer risk.
- Eradication has primary and secondary prevention relevance.
- Eradication is recommended for H. pylori-positive patients after curative ER or gastric subtotal resection.
- Contemporary regimen choice should follow current ACG/Maastricht guidance.
- Antimicrobial stewardship matters.

### Supported / context-dependent
- Benefit in asymptomatic infection.
- Benefit in family-history populations.
- Benefit in people with atrophic gastritis/intestinal metaplasia.
- Household transmission may contribute to recurrence.
- Confirmation of eradication is clinically important.

### Not established by current project sources
- One universal regimen.
- One universal treatment duration.
- One universal salvage algorithm.
- One universal test-of-cure protocol.
- Universal family-wide eradication.

## Evidence Gaps

1. Current ACG/Maastricht regimen details are not included in the supplied project PDFs.
2. Contemporary resistance-guided therapy is not sufficiently detailed.
3. Exact test-of-cure timing and methodology are not sufficiently detailed.
4. Population-specific reinfection rates vary.
5. Optimal family/household eradication strategy remains context-dependent.
