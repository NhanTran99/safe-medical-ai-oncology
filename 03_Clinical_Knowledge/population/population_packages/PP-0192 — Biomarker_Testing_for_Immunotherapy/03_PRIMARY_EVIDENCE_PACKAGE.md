# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **Which biomarker testing is relevant when immunotherapy is being considered for gastric adenocarcinoma?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with a source-grounded framework for understanding why immunotherapy biomarker testing is performed, which biomarker domains are central, how the tests relate to one another, where broader molecular testing fits, and why results must be interpreted within the clinical context.

The package is deliberately positioned:

**after foundational biomarker/immunotherapy concepts**

→ **before detailed biomarker interpretation**

→ **before downstream immunotherapy treatment application**.

It is not an individualized treatment algorithm.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Direct Disease-Specific Guideline

### NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026

NCCN is the primary source for PP-0192 because it provides the most direct gastric-cancer framework for biomarker testing and treatment integration.

The supplied NCCN Version 2.2026 material establishes:

1. Universal MSI testing by PCR/NGS or MMR testing by IHC for newly diagnosed gastric cancer.
2. Universal PD-L1 IHC testing for newly diagnosed patients who are candidates for PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitors.
3. PD-L1 assessment using FFPE tumor tissue and a companion-diagnostic framework.
4. IHC/ISH/targeted gene PCR as preferred initial approaches for specified biomarkers.
5. NGS as a broader molecular-testing approach that may be considered in selected circumstances.
6. NGS can include assessment of TMB and MSI.
7. MSI-H/dMMR tumors have immunotherapy-relevant treatment options in appropriate clinical settings.
8. TMB-H is a selected immunotherapy-relevant molecular context.
9. Treatment pathways can incorporate more than one biomarker simultaneously.

These points establish the clinical architecture of PP-0192.

# Evidence Notes

## Note 1 — PD-L1 and MSI/MMR occupy different biological domains

PD-L1 testing evaluates a protein-expression/immune-checkpoint feature.

MSI/MMR testing evaluates mismatch-repair/microsatellite-instability status.

Because these are different biological questions, the package explicitly avoids treating them as interchangeable.

This is central to the clinical logic of PP-0192.

---

## Note 2 — Universal testing versus treatment eligibility

The phrase **universal testing** must not be confused with:

> “Every patient automatically receives immunotherapy.”

NCCN testing recommendations identify when biomarker information should be obtained.

Treatment eligibility remains a separate clinical decision.

This distinction is particularly important for patient-facing safety.

---

## Note 3 — PD-L1 testing is context-dependent despite its routine testing position

NCCN recommends universal PD-L1 IHC testing for newly diagnosed patients who are candidates for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor treatment.

However, the clinical meaning of the resulting PD-L1 score depends on the relevant treatment setting and guideline context.

PP-0192 therefore teaches the **role of the test**, while PP-0183 owns detailed scoring and interpretation.

---

## Note 4 — MSI/MMR testing is broader than an “immunotherapy test”

NCCN recommends universal MSI/MMR testing at diagnosis.

Therefore the package should not imply:

> “MSI/MMR is only tested if immunotherapy is already planned.”

The result may become relevant later in the disease course.

---

## Note 5 — TMB has a different evidence position

TMB is included because NCCN identifies TMB assessment within the NGS framework and recognizes TMB-H as an immunotherapy-relevant context.

However, TMB is not placed on the same universal-testing level as PD-L1 and MSI/MMR.

The package therefore uses the classification:

> **selected broader molecular-testing consideration**.

---

## Note 6 — NGS is a platform, not a biomarker

NGS can assess multiple molecular events.

It therefore should not be treated as if it were itself an immunotherapy biomarker.

The package distinguishes:

**NGS = testing platform**

from

**PD-L1/MSI/MMR/TMB = biological information generated or assessed by specific testing approaches**.

---

## Note 7 — EBV must remain emerging/contextual

The project Registry assigns EBV-associated gastric cancer and EBV testing to a dedicated package.

PP-0192 therefore does not promote EBV to the same routine status as PD-L1 or MSI/MMR.

This avoids overclaiming from biologic association to clinical routine.

---

## Note 8 — Multi-biomarker context

ACS and NCCN materials illustrate that treatment planning may involve multiple biomarker dimensions.

Therefore PP-0192 explicitly teaches:

> **immunotherapy testing occurs within a broader precision-oncology profile.**

HER2 and CLDN18.2 remain owned by their dedicated packages.

---

## Note 9 — Specimen adequacy

NCCN specifies FFPE tissue and adequacy requirements for PD-L1 and tissue-based testing frameworks for MSI/MMR.

The educational implication is:

> an inadequate specimen may leave the clinical question unanswered.

This is distinct from a true negative result.

---

## Note 10 — Companion diagnostics

NCCN identifies a companion-diagnostic framework for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor treatment.

PP-0192 introduces the concept only.

Regulatory and assay-specific companion-diagnostic methodology is outside the package.

---

## Note 11 — Tissue versus blood

NCCN recognizes blood-based ctDNA testing as a liquid-biopsy approach that may be considered in selected situations.

This does not establish universal substitution for tissue PD-L1 or MSI/MMR testing.

The package therefore uses cautious language:

> blood-based molecular testing may provide additional or alternative genomic information in selected circumstances, depending on the clinical question.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The project Source Materials support the following major claims:

1. Biomarker testing can help predict response to selected immunotherapy approaches.
2. PD-L1 and MSI/MMR are the principal routine immunotherapy-relevant biomarker domains in the current gastric-cancer framework.
3. PD-L1 and MSI/MMR are complementary rather than interchangeable.
4. MSI/MMR testing is recommended universally at newly diagnosed gastric cancer in NCCN guidance.
5. PD-L1 testing is recommended universally in newly diagnosed patients who are candidates for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.
6. TMB can be relevant in selected circumstances and may be assessed through broader NGS.
7. NGS is a broader testing platform rather than a single immunotherapy biomarker.
8. EBV remains emerging/non-routine in the current clinical framework.
9. HER2 and CLDN18.2 may influence the overall treatment plan alongside immunotherapy biomarkers.
10. Specimen adequacy and assay choice influence the usefulness of biomarker results.
11. Negative and inadequate results should not be treated as equivalent.
12. Biomarker results must be interpreted within clinical context.
13. Biomarker results inform treatment decisions but do not independently prescribe treatment.

# Evidence Gaps

The following gaps are acknowledged and intentionally not filled with unsupported general knowledge:

1. There is no single universal immunotherapy biomarker algorithm that applies identically to every gastric-cancer patient and treatment setting.
2. PD-L1 thresholds and scoring frameworks may be treatment-context specific.
3. TMB has a different clinical position from PD-L1 and MSI/MMR.
4. The optimal use of broad NGS depends on clinical context, available tissue, and the question being asked.
5. Tissue and blood-based testing are not universally interchangeable.
6. Biomarker combinations may produce context-specific treatment pathways.
7. EBV is not currently established as a routine immunotherapy-testing requirement.
8. Detailed assay-specific performance characteristics belong to dedicated testing packages.
9. Individualized treatment selection requires clinical information beyond the biomarker result.

These gaps do not block PP-0192 because its atomic scope is a **biomarker-testing strategy package**, not an individualized immunotherapy algorithm.
