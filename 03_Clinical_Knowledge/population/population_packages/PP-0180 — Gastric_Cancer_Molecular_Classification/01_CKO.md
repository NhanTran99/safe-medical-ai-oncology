## B. Educational Objectives

After reading this package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain what molecular classification of gastric cancer means.
2. Understand why gastric cancer is molecularly heterogeneous.
3. Understand why molecular classification complements histopathology and Lauren classification.
4. Understand genome instability at a conceptual level.
5. Understand MSI and chromosomal instability as major molecular concepts.
6. Understand the TCGA framework at a high level.
7. Recognize the four broad TCGA groups: EBV-positive, MSI, GS, and CIN.
8. Understand the difference between molecular classification and individual biomarker testing.
9. Understand the difference between molecular classification and NGS.
10. Understand common genomic alteration types.
11. Understand why assay scope and tissue adequacy matter.
12. Distinguish somatic tumor alterations from inherited germline findings.
13. Understand how molecular characterization can support precision oncology.
14. Avoid interpreting molecular classification as a standalone treatment or prognosis decision.
15. Understand why detailed molecular-subtype and biomarker packages are separate downstream knowledge assets.

## D. Clinical Knowledge Blocks

### Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Molecular Classification of Gastric Cancer?

**Patient Explanation**

Molecular classification groups gastric cancers according to important molecular features of the tumor. It adds information about tumor biology to the information obtained from microscopic examination.

**Clinical Importance**

It answers a different question from histology: instead of asking mainly what the tumor looks like, it asks what molecular characteristics help define the biology of the tumor.

**Key Concepts**

- Molecular classification is a higher-level biological framework.
- Histology, Lauren classification, molecular classification and individual biomarkers answer different questions.
- Molecular test results must be interpreted within assay scope and clinical context.
- Research classification does not automatically equal a routine clinical test.

**Evidence Anchor**

NCCN states that pathologic review and biomarker testing contribute to diagnosis, classification, and molecular characterization of gastric cancer. fileciteturn31file13

## Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1

**“Molecular classification is the same as genetic testing.”**

**Fact:** Molecular classification is a framework; genetic and molecular tests are tools used to measure selected molecular features.

### Myth 2

**“There is one molecular test that classifies every gastric cancer.”**

**Fact:** Different tests answer different molecular questions, and no single routine assay necessarily captures every feature.

### Myth 3

**“HER2-positive is one of the four TCGA molecular subtypes.”**

**Fact:** HER2 is a clinically relevant biomarker and is not equivalent to the TCGA four-group framework.

### Myth 4

**“MSI-H is the same thing as the whole molecular classification.”**

**Fact:** MSI is one major molecular characteristic and classification dimension, not the entire molecular landscape.

### Myth 5

**“Lauren classification has been replaced by molecular classification.”**

**Fact:** Lauren and molecular classification describe different biological dimensions and can coexist.

### Myth 6

**“TCGA subtype is automatically written on every patient's pathology report.”**

**Fact:** TCGA is a foundational research classification framework; routine clinical testing may instead report specific biomarkers.

### Myth 7

**“A negative molecular test means there are no mutations.”**

**Fact:** A negative result means the tested feature was not detected within that assay's scope and limitations.

### Myth 8

**“A mutation found in the tumor is automatically inherited.”**

**Fact:** Tumor alterations can be somatic and acquired; germline testing addresses inherited risk.

### Myth 9

**“Molecular classification alone determines treatment.”**

**Fact:** Treatment requires integration of validated biomarkers, disease setting, stage and patient-specific clinical factors.

### Myth 10

**“Molecular classification alone determines prognosis.”**

**Fact:** Molecular features may be associated with outcomes, but individual prognosis requires broader clinical context.

### Myth 11

**“NGS detects every genetic abnormality.”**

**Fact:** NGS assays have defined scopes and may detect only selected genes or alteration types.

### Myth 12

**“One biopsy necessarily represents the entire molecular profile.”**

**Fact:** Tumor heterogeneity and specimen limitations can affect what is detected.

### Myth 13

**“EBV-positive automatically means PD-L1-positive.”**

**Fact:** Associations may exist, but one molecular feature does not automatically establish another biomarker.

### Myth 14

**“Molecular classification and biomarker testing are interchangeable.”**

**Fact:** Classification is a framework; biomarker testing answers a specific patient-level question.

### Myth 15

**“A molecular result can be interpreted without clinical context.”**

**Fact:** Molecular findings should be interpreted according to assay scope, pathology, clinical setting and validated evidence.

## Key Messages

- Molecular classification adds a molecular-biology layer to gastric cancer characterization.
- It complements, rather than replaces, histopathology and Lauren classification.
- Gastric cancer is molecularly heterogeneous.
- Genome instability includes major patterns such as chromosomal instability and microsatellite instability.
- The TCGA framework established four broad molecular groups: EBV-positive, MSI, genomically stable and chromosomal instability.
- The four TCGA groups are a classification framework, not four routine tests performed separately on every patient.
- Individual biomarkers such as HER2, PD-L1 and CLDN18.2 are not interchangeable with molecular subtypes.
- NGS is a testing technology, not a synonym for molecular classification.
- Assay scope and specimen adequacy limit what molecular testing can detect.
- Somatic tumor findings are not automatically inherited germline findings.
- Molecular classification can support precision oncology but does not independently determine treatment or prognosis.
- Detailed molecular-subtype biology belongs to PP-0188; individual biomarker testing belongs to PP-0181–PP-0187.
