# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What are non-target lesions?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **non-target lesions**, including what they are, why they are not selected for standardized measurement, how they continue to contribute to treatment response assessment, and why their classification does not reflect their clinical importance.

This Population Package introduces the concept of non-target lesions without explaining RECIST technical response criteria, unequivocal progression, response algorithms or treatment decisions.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of non-target lesions
- Difference between target and non-target lesions
- Continued monitoring during treatment
- Clinical importance of non-target lesions
- Integration into overall disease assessment
- Explanation that classification does not indicate severity

---

## Excluded

- RECIST 1.1 technical criteria for non-target lesions
- Complete Response (CR)
- Progressive Disease (PD)
- Unequivocal progression
- Measurable disease
- Target lesions
- RECIST calculations
- Imaging protocols
- Treatment decisions
- Prognostic significance

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Non-target lesions remain part of routine disease assessment throughout treatment.
- Their classification reflects the method of assessment rather than biological importance.
- Target lesions and non-target lesions complement one another in standardized response evaluation.
- Doctors interpret imaging findings together with symptoms, examination findings and other clinical information.
- Patients should not interpret lesion classifications without professional guidance.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of non-target lesions;
- their continued role during follow-up;
- the distinction between lesion classification and clinical significance;
- the need for comprehensive assessment using multiple sources of clinical information.

Differences between references relate primarily to detailed RECIST response rules, which are intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted RECIST 1.1 concepts without introducing technical response criteria.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- Complete Response (CR);
- Progressive Disease (PD);
- unequivocal progression;
- RECIST response algorithms;
- treatment decisions;
- prognosis.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
