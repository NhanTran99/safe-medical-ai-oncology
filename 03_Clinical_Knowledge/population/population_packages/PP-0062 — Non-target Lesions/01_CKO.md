# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what non-target lesions are.
- Understand that non-target lesions are lesions that are not selected as target lesions for standardized measurement.
- Understand that non-target lesions continue to be monitored throughout treatment.
- Recognize that non-target lesions remain clinically important.
- Understand that treatment response is assessed using information from target lesions, non-target lesions and other clinical findings.
- Understand that patients should not interpret the classification of lesions on their own.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Are Non-target Lesions?

## Patient Explanation

**Non-target lesions** are cancer lesions that are **not selected as target lesions for standardized measurement**.

Even though they are not measured in the same way, doctors continue monitoring them during treatment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Non-target lesions remain an important part of evaluating the overall disease.

---

## Key Concepts

- Non-target lesions are still monitored.
- They are not measured like target lesions.
- They remain clinically relevant.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Aren't They Measured Like Target Lesions?

## Patient Explanation

Not every lesion is suitable for standardized measurement.

Some lesions are followed in other ways using professional medical assessment rather than repeated size measurements.

---

## Clinical Importance

Different lesions require different approaches to assessment.

---

## Key Concepts

- Different lesions are evaluated differently.
- Measurement is only one part of assessment.
- Standardized follow-up remains important.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Are Non-target Lesions Important?

## Patient Explanation

Yes.

Being classified as a **non-target lesion does not mean the lesion is less important**.

Doctors continue evaluating these lesions because they contribute to understanding how your cancer is changing.

---

## Clinical Importance

All clinically relevant disease contributes to treatment response assessment.

---

## Key Concepts

- Non-target lesions remain important.
- They influence overall clinical assessment.
- Classification does not indicate severity.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — How Do Doctors Use Information from Non-target Lesions?

## Patient Explanation

Doctors combine information from:

- target lesions;
- non-target lesions;
- imaging studies;
- symptoms;
- physical examination;
- laboratory tests;
- other clinical findings.

This provides a more complete picture of your disease.

---

## Clinical Importance

Comprehensive assessment requires integration of multiple clinical data sources.

---

## Key Concepts

- Multiple sources of information are used.
- Non-target lesions contribute to the overall evaluation.
- Clinical judgment remains essential.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Should Patients Focus Only on Target Lesions?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Although target lesions are measured using standardized methods, doctors also pay close attention to non-target lesions.

Both types of lesions help healthcare teams understand how your cancer is responding to treatment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Treatment response assessment extends beyond standardized measurements alone.

---

## Key Concepts

- Target and non-target lesions are complementary.
- Overall assessment is comprehensive.
- Patients should rely on professional interpretation.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

A non-target lesion is less serious than a target lesion.

**Fact**

The terms **target** and **non-target** describe **how lesions are evaluated**, not how serious they are. Both may be clinically important.

---

# Key Messages

- Non-target lesions are not selected for standardized measurement.
- They continue to be monitored throughout treatment.
- Non-target lesions remain clinically important.
- Doctors evaluate both target and non-target lesions together.
- Overall treatment response depends on the complete clinical picture.
