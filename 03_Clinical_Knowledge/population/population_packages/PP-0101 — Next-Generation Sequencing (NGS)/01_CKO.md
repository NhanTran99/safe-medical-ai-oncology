# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) is.
- Recognize why NGS may be recommended in cancer care.
- Understand how NGS relates to molecular testing and biomarker testing.
- Recognize that NGS can analyze many genes simultaneously.
- Understand that NGS is not needed for every patient.
- Feel more confident discussing NGS with the healthcare team.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS)?

## Patient Explanation

**Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS)** is a laboratory technology that can examine **many genes or genetic regions at the same time**.

Instead of looking at only one genetic change, NGS can analyze many possible changes in a single test.

---

## Clinical Importance

NGS is a high-throughput molecular testing technology used in modern oncology.

---

## Key Concepts

- Next-Generation Sequencing.
- Multiple genes.
- Molecular analysis.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Is NGS Used?

## Patient Explanation

Depending on the clinical situation, NGS may help doctors:

- identify important molecular changes;
- support biomarker testing;
- guide precision medicine;
- determine whether additional treatment options may be appropriate.

The role of NGS depends on the type of cancer and the clinical question.

---

## Clinical Importance

NGS provides broad molecular information that may support individualized cancer care.

---

## Key Concepts

- Molecular alterations.
- Biomarkers.
- Precision medicine.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Samples Can Be Used?

## Patient Explanation

NGS may be performed using:

- tissue collected during a biopsy or surgery;
- blood samples in selected situations;
- other biological specimens when appropriate.

The sample depends on the type of test being performed.

---

## Clinical Importance

Different NGS tests require different specimen types.

---

## Key Concepts

- Tissue specimen.
- Blood specimen.
- Sample selection.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does Everyone Need NGS?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Whether NGS is recommended depends on:

- the type of cancer;
- available treatment options;
- current clinical guidelines;
- your individual medical situation.

Many patients receive excellent cancer care without NGS.

---

## Clinical Importance

NGS should be performed according to evidence-based clinical indications.

---

## Key Concepts

- Appropriate testing.
- Individualized care.
- Clinical guidelines.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does NGS Replace Other Cancer Tests?

## Patient Explanation

No.

NGS is only one part of cancer evaluation.

Doctors also use:

- pathology;
- imaging;
- cancer staging;
- physical examination;
- medical history;
- many other clinical findings.

---

## Clinical Importance

NGS complements comprehensive clinical assessment rather than replacing established diagnostic methods.

---

## Key Concepts

- Integrated evaluation.
- Clinical judgment.
- Comprehensive care.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

NGS is the most important cancer test and replaces all other testing.

**Fact**

NGS provides valuable molecular information, but doctors still rely on pathology, imaging, staging, and many other clinical findings to make treatment decisions.

---

# Key Messages

- NGS is a technology that analyzes many genes simultaneously.
- NGS is one type of molecular testing.
- NGS may support biomarker testing and precision medicine.
- Not every patient requires NGS.
- NGS complements, rather than replaces, other cancer tests.
