# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain what tumor mutational burden (TMB) means in plain language.
2. Understand why TMB is expressed as mutations per megabase.
3. Explain what TMB-high (TMB-H) means in the current gastric-cancer clinical framework.
4. Understand that TMB is a genomic biomarker rather than an individual gene mutation, protein stain, or cancer stage.
5. Understand the relationship between TMB and genomic testing, including next-generation sequencing (NGS).
6. Understand why a high mutational burden may be relevant to immune recognition and immunotherapy.
7. Understand the current clinical relevance of TMB-H in advanced gastric cancer.
8. Understand the current NCCN gastric-cancer TMB-H threshold of ≥10 mutations/megabase in the treatment pathway.
9. Understand that TMB-H may make pembrolizumab an option in a defined clinical setting, but does not by itself determine treatment.
10. Distinguish TMB from MSI/MMR, PD-L1, HER2, CLDN18.2, and broader molecular classification.
11. Recognize important limitations of TMB testing and interpretation.
12. Interpret a TMB result without assuming that a high or low result guarantees a particular outcome.

# Clinical Knowledge Blocks

## Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Tumor Mutational Burden?

Tumor mutational burden, or **TMB**, is a measure of how many mutations are found in the DNA of a tumor within the genomic territory assessed by a test.

In simple terms, TMB asks:

> **How many DNA changes are present in this tumor relative to the amount of DNA examined?**

TMB is therefore a **quantitative genomic biomarker**. It is not one particular mutation and it is not a diagnosis.

The NCCN Gastric Cancer guideline identifies TMB-H status among biomarkers implicated in the clinical management of advanced gastric cancer. It also describes NGS as a testing approach that can assess TMB together with multiple mutations and other molecular events.

---

## Knowledge Block 2 — What Does “Mutational Burden” Mean?

Cancer cells can accumulate DNA changes as they develop and evolve.

Some tumors contain relatively few mutations, whereas others contain many mutations. TMB summarizes this overall mutational burden into a quantitative result.

A TMB result therefore does not tell the patient:

- which single mutation caused the cancer;
- whether one particular mutation is dangerous;
- whether the tumor is a particular histologic type;
- whether the cancer is early or advanced.

Instead, it describes one characteristic of the tumor's genomic landscape.

---

## Knowledge Block 3 — Why Is TMB Reported as Mutations per Megabase?

TMB is commonly expressed as **mutations per megabase (mutations/Mb)**.

A megabase is a unit representing approximately one million DNA bases.

Expressing TMB per megabase allows the reported mutation burden to be normalized to the amount of genomic sequence assessed.

For patient education, the important concept is:

> A TMB value represents an estimated number of mutations relative to the amount of DNA examined.

The exact laboratory and computational process used to produce a TMB value is outside this package and belongs to NGS Biomarker Testing.

---

## Knowledge Block 4 — What Does TMB-High Mean?

**TMB-high (TMB-H)** means that the tumor's reported mutational burden meets a defined threshold.

In the current NCCN gastric-cancer treatment framework, TMB-H is defined as:

> **≥10 mutations per megabase.**

This threshold should be presented in its clinical context.

It should not be described as a universal biological cutoff that applies identically to every cancer, every assay, or every possible clinical question.

The meaning of a TMB value depends on the assay, reporting framework, disease context, and clinical question.

---

## Knowledge Block 5 — Is TMB a Gene Mutation?

No.

TMB is not:

- a gene;
- a single mutation;
- a protein;
- a gene fusion;
- a histologic subtype;
- a cancer stage.

It is a **summary measure of tumor mutational burden**.

A tumor can therefore be TMB-H while also having separate findings such as HER2 status, MSI/MMR status, PD-L1 expression, CLDN18.2 status, or specific genomic alterations.

---

## Knowledge Block 6 — How Is TMB Related to NGS?

TMB can be assessed through genomic testing.

NCCN states that NGS offers the opportunity to assess numerous mutations simultaneously along with other molecular events such as amplification, deletions, TMB, and MSI status. NCCN also notes that a validated NGS assay performed in a CLIA-approved laboratory may be considered later in the clinical course when sufficient tumor tissue is available.

The important patient-facing distinction is:

**PP-0185 explains what the TMB result means.**

**PP-0187 explains NGS biomarker testing in greater depth.**

This package therefore does not reproduce technical sequencing or bioinformatics methodology.

---

## Knowledge Block 7 — Why Might a High TMB Matter to the Immune System?

A tumor containing many mutations may produce more abnormal proteins or altered peptides that can potentially be recognized as different from normal cells.

This provides a biological rationale for why tumors with high mutational burden may sometimes be more visible to the immune system and may have increased sensitivity to immune checkpoint blockade.

However:

> **TMB-H does not guarantee an immune response or treatment response.**

TMB is one biomarker among several factors that can influence the clinical behavior of a tumor and its response to therapy.

---

## Knowledge Block 8 — Why Can TMB Matter in Advanced Gastric Cancer?

NCCN identifies TMB-H status among biomarkers implicated in the clinical management of advanced gastric cancer.

In the systemic-treatment pathway for unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic disease, NCCN lists pembrolizumab for TMB-H tumors as an option that may be useful in certain circumstances.

The current NCCN threshold is:

**TMB-H: ≥10 mutations/megabase.**

This makes TMB a treatment-relevant biomarker rather than simply a descriptive genomic measurement.

---

## Knowledge Block 9 — What Does TMB-H Mean for Pembrolizumab?

NCCN discusses TMB-H tumors in relation to pembrolizumab.

The guideline describes the June 2020 FDA approval of pembrolizumab for metastatic TMB-H solid tumors identified by an FDA-approved test in patients whose disease had progressed after prior treatment and who had no satisfactory alternative treatment options.

The NCCN gastric-cancer discussion states that pembrolizumab may be used in second-line or subsequent treatment of TMB-H gastroesophageal tumors.

This is an important example of a biomarker being used to identify a clinically relevant treatment population.

It does **not** mean:

> “TMB-H automatically means pembrolizumab should be given.”

Treatment decisions require the complete clinical context.

---

## Knowledge Block 10 — What Is the Evidence Limitation for Gastric Cancer?

The pivotal TMB analysis cited by NCCN came from KEYNOTE-158.

Among 102 patients with TMB-H tumors:

- objective response rate was 29%;
- complete response rate was 4%;
- median duration of response was not reached;
- 50% of responders had response lasting at least 24 months.

However, NCCN explicitly notes that **no patients with gastroesophageal cancer were included in this TMB analysis of KEYNOTE-158**.

Therefore, the evidence should not be represented as a gastric-cancer-specific prospective efficacy dataset.

This limitation is part of the correct interpretation of TMB-H in gastric cancer.

---

## Knowledge Block 11 — Is TMB the Same as MSI/MMR?

No.

TMB and MSI/MMR are distinct biomarkers.

- **MSI/MMR** evaluates microsatellite instability or mismatch-repair status.
- **TMB** summarizes tumor mutational burden.

They can be biologically related in some tumors, but they are not interchangeable tests.

PP-0182 owns MSI/MMR testing.

PP-0185 only explains the distinction and high-level relationship.

---

## Knowledge Block 12 — Is TMB the Same as PD-L1?

No.

PD-L1 is a protein-expression biomarker assessed by pathology-based testing.

TMB is a genomic biomarker reflecting mutational burden.

They answer different questions and should not be treated as interchangeable.

PP-0183 owns PD-L1 Testing.

---

## Knowledge Block 13 — Is TMB the Same as HER2 or CLDN18.2?

No.

HER2 and CLDN18.2 are specific molecular/protein biomarkers.

TMB is a quantitative genomic measure.

A patient can therefore have an independently reported:

- HER2 result;
- MSI/MMR result;
- PD-L1 result;
- CLDN18.2 result;
- TMB result.

These results can contribute different information to precision oncology.

---

## Knowledge Block 14 — Is TMB a Molecular Classification of Gastric Cancer?

No.

TMB-H is a **biomarker result**, not a complete molecular subtype or classification system.

Gastric cancer molecular classification is a separate knowledge question.

PP-0180 owns Gastric Cancer Molecular Classification.

PP-0185 owns the meaning and clinical relevance of TMB/TMB-H.

---

## Knowledge Block 15 — Why Can TMB Results Depend on the Test Used?

TMB is an assay-derived quantitative result.

Different genomic tests may examine different genomic regions and use different technical and computational approaches.

Therefore, the same tumor may not necessarily receive an identical numerical TMB value from every possible assay.

This is one reason why a TMB cutoff must be interpreted in the context of the assay and clinical framework in which it is being used.

Detailed assay harmonization belongs to PP-0187.

---

## Knowledge Block 16 — What Does a TMB Result Not Tell You?

A TMB result alone does not tell you:

- whether the cancer is curable;
- the cancer stage;
- the tumor's histologic type;
- the presence or absence of a particular actionable mutation;
- whether immunotherapy will definitely work;
- whether treatment should automatically be changed;
- whether another biomarker is positive or negative;
- the patient's prognosis with certainty.

TMB should be interpreted as one component of the complete clinical and molecular picture.

---

## Knowledge Block 17 — What Does a Positive or High TMB Result Mean?

A high TMB result means that the tumor meets the relevant threshold for high mutational burden.

In the current NCCN gastric-cancer treatment framework, a value of ≥10 mutations/Mb qualifies as TMB-H.

It may therefore have treatment-selection relevance in the appropriate advanced-disease setting.

It does not independently prescribe treatment.

---

## Knowledge Block 18 — What Does a Low TMB Result Mean?

A low TMB means the reported mutational burden does not meet the defined high-burden threshold.

It should not be interpreted as proof that:

- the tumor has no mutations;
- the tumor cannot respond to immunotherapy;
- all other biomarkers are negative;
- no treatment option exists.

TMB is only one component of molecular characterization.

---

## Knowledge Block 19 — Can TMB Change Over Time?

Tumors can evolve genetically over the course of disease and treatment.

This provides a biological reason why genomic findings can potentially differ between specimens obtained at different times.

However, PP-0185 does not establish a routine serial-TMB monitoring algorithm.

Questions about longitudinal molecular monitoring, ctDNA, resistance monitoring, or treatment-switching based on genomic dynamics belong to other Population Packages.

---

## Knowledge Block 20 — Patient Explanation

A patient may be told:

> **“TMB is a measure of how many DNA mutations are found in your tumor. A high TMB means that the tumor has a relatively large number of mutations according to the test's definition. In some advanced cancers, including selected gastric-cancer situations, a high TMB can be relevant when doctors consider immunotherapy. It does not by itself determine which treatment you should receive.”**

# Clinical Importance

TMB is important because it links **tumor genomic information** with **potential treatment relevance**.

Its position in the gastric-cancer knowledge architecture is:

**Tumor genomic information**
→ **TMB measurement**
→ **TMB-H classification**
→ **potential immune relevance**
→ **selected treatment relevance**

The final clinical decision remains broader than TMB alone.

# Key Concepts

1. TMB is a quantitative genomic biomarker.
2. TMB describes the number of mutations relative to the genomic territory assessed.
3. TMB is commonly expressed as mutations per megabase.
4. TMB-H means a tumor meets a defined high-burden threshold.
5. NCCN currently uses ≥10 mutations/Mb for TMB-H in its gastric-cancer treatment pathway.
6. TMB is not a single mutation or molecular subtype.
7. TMB can be assessed through genomic testing such as NGS.
8. TMB-H may have clinical relevance for immunotherapy in selected advanced-disease settings.
9. TMB-H does not guarantee response.
10. TMB is distinct from MSI/MMR, PD-L1, HER2, and CLDN18.2.
11. TMB results can be influenced by assay characteristics.
12. TMB must be interpreted in clinical context.

# Common Misconceptions

## Myth 1 — “TMB-H means there is one specific mutation.”

**Fact:** TMB-H describes a high overall mutational burden, not one specific mutation.

## Myth 2 — “TMB-H and MSI-H are the same.”

**Fact:** They are different biomarkers measuring different molecular features.

## Myth 3 — “TMB is an IHC stain.”

**Fact:** TMB is a genomic biomarker generally derived from molecular genomic testing.

## Myth 4 — “TMB-H guarantees that immunotherapy will work.”

**Fact:** TMB-H can identify a treatment-relevant population but does not guarantee response.

## Myth 5 — “TMB-H means immunotherapy is mandatory.”

**Fact:** TMB-H may make pembrolizumab an option in a defined clinical context; it does not independently prescribe treatment.

## Myth 6 — “A TMB of 10 mutations/Mb is a universal cutoff for every assay and cancer.”

**Fact:** ≥10 mutations/Mb is the current NCCN gastric-cancer treatment-context threshold for TMB-H; assay and clinical context remain important.

## Myth 7 — “TMB-H is a gastric-cancer molecular subtype.”

**Fact:** TMB-H is a biomarker classification, not a complete molecular classification.

## Myth 8 — “Low TMB means immunotherapy can never work.”

**Fact:** TMB is only one biomarker and should not be interpreted in isolation.

## Myth 9 — “TMB alone determines treatment.”

**Fact:** Treatment decisions require the complete clinical, pathological, and molecular context.

## Myth 10 — “The pivotal TMB evidence was specifically generated in gastric cancer.”

**Fact:** NCCN notes that no gastroesophageal cancer patients were included in the KEYNOTE-158 TMB analysis it cites.

# Key Messages

- **TMB measures the mutational burden of a tumor.**
- **TMB-H means the reported value meets a defined high-burden threshold.**
- **For the current NCCN gastric-cancer treatment pathway, TMB-H is ≥10 mutations/Mb.**
- **TMB is a genomic biomarker, not an individual mutation, IHC result, or cancer stage.**
- **TMB-H can have treatment-selection relevance in selected advanced gastric-cancer settings.**
- **TMB-H does not guarantee benefit from immunotherapy.**
- **TMB must be interpreted together with the patient's other clinical and biomarker information.**
