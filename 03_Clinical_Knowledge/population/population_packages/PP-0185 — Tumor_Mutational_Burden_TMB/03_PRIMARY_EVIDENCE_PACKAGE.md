# Clinical Question

> **What is tumor mutational burden (TMB), what does TMB-high (TMB-H) mean, and why can it matter in gastric cancer?**

---

# Educational Intent

This evidence package supports a patient-centered Population Package that explains TMB as a genomic biomarker and clarifies its clinical relevance without converting the package into a technical NGS manual or an individualized treatment algorithm.

The evidence package must allow the reader to understand:

1. what TMB measures;
2. what TMB-H means;
3. the current gastric-cancer TMB-H threshold;
4. how TMB relates to genomic testing;
5. why TMB may be relevant to immunotherapy;
6. what TMB-H can mean clinically;
7. what TMB does not establish;
8. how TMB differs from other biomarkers;
9. why assay and evidence limitations matter.

# Evidence Notes

## Note 1 — Definition and role of TMB

The source materials support TMB as a clinically relevant genomic biomarker in advanced gastric cancer. The NCCN guideline places TMB-H alongside other biomarkers used in the clinical management of advanced gastric cancer.

The package therefore treats TMB as an applied biomarker rather than merely a research descriptor.

---

## Note 2 — TMB and NGS

NCCN states that NGS offers the opportunity to assess numerous mutations simultaneously together with other molecular events, including TMB and MSI.

This supports the conceptual statement that TMB can be generated through genomic testing.

The source does not require PP-0185 to reproduce NGS methodology. That material is explicitly delegated to PP-0187.

---

## Note 3 — TMB-H threshold

The current NCCN gastric-cancer treatment pathway identifies TMB-H as:

**≥10 mutations/megabase.**

The package must preserve the contextual nature of this threshold.

It should not be generalized into a universal biological cutoff for all assays, cancers, or clinical questions.

---

## Note 4 — Treatment relevance

NCCN lists pembrolizumab for TMB-H tumors as a “useful in certain circumstances” option in the systemic therapy pathway for unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic disease.

This establishes treatment relevance but does not establish automatic treatment selection.

---

## Note 5 — KEYNOTE-158 evidence

The NCCN discussion reports:

- 102 TMB-H patients;
- ORR 29%;
- CR 4%;
- median duration of response not reached;
- 50% of responders with duration ≥24 months.

These findings support the clinical relevance of TMB-H as a treatment-selection biomarker.

---

## Note 6 — Critical gastric-cancer evidence limitation

The NCCN discussion explicitly states that **no patients with gastroesophageal cancer were included in the KEYNOTE-158 TMB analysis**.

Therefore:

- the evidence must not be described as a gastric-cancer-specific prospective TMB efficacy cohort;
- the limitation should be visible in the patient-facing educational synthesis;
- the package should avoid overclaiming gastric-specific magnitude of benefit.

---

## Note 7 — Relationship to MSI/MMR

TMB and MSI/MMR are separate biomarkers.

The package can explain that they may be biologically associated in some cancers, but it must not equate them.

Detailed MSI/MMR testing remains PP-0182 ownership.

---

## Note 8 — Relationship to PD-L1

TMB and PD-L1 provide different types of information.

TMB is genomic.

PD-L1 is a protein-expression biomarker assessed by pathology-based testing.

Detailed PD-L1 testing remains PP-0183 ownership.

---

## Note 9 — Relationship to HER2 and CLDN18.2

HER2 and CLDN18.2 are specific biomarker findings.

TMB is an aggregate genomic measure.

The package should therefore teach patients to read these findings as complementary rather than interchangeable.

---

## Note 10 — Assay dependence

TMB is an assay-derived quantitative result. Different assays can evaluate different genomic territories and use different analytical approaches.

The package therefore teaches the concept of assay/context dependence without entering technical harmonization.

Detailed NGS assay methodology belongs to PP-0187.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The following claims are considered sufficiently supported for Gold educational use:

1. TMB is a quantitative genomic biomarker describing tumor mutational burden.
2. TMB is commonly expressed as mutations per megabase.
3. TMB-H means the reported value meets a defined high-burden threshold.
4. NCCN currently uses ≥10 mutations/Mb as the TMB-H threshold in the gastric-cancer treatment pathway.
5. TMB can be assessed through genomic testing such as NGS.
6. TMB-H is implicated in the clinical management of advanced gastric cancer.
7. Pembrolizumab may be considered for TMB-H tumors in selected circumstances.
8. TMB-H does not independently determine treatment.
9. TMB is distinct from MSI/MMR, PD-L1, HER2, and CLDN18.2.
10. The KEYNOTE-158 TMB evidence cited by NCCN was not generated in gastroesophageal cancer patients.
11. TMB should be interpreted in the context of the assay and overall clinical picture.
12. A TMB result should not be used alone to guarantee or exclude treatment response.

# Evidence Gaps

1. The source package does not establish a gastric-specific randomized TMB-H efficacy trial.
2. The KEYNOTE-158 TMB analysis cited by NCCN did not include gastroesophageal cancer patients.
3. The source package does not establish a universal assay-harmonized TMB measurement standard for every platform.
4. The source package does not establish routine serial TMB monitoring as standard gastric-cancer care.
5. The source package does not support individualized response prediction from TMB alone.
6. The source package does not establish TMB-H as a complete gastric-cancer molecular subtype.
7. The source package does not support universal TMB testing in every gastric-cancer patient.

These gaps are intentionally preserved rather than filled with unsupported general knowledge.
