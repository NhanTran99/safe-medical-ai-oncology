## Clinical Question

**How is gastric cancer characterized under the microscope, what do the
major histopathologic terms mean, and what additional information can
pathology provide from biopsy, endoscopic resection and gastrectomy
specimens?**

## Educational Intent

This Evidence Package supports a patient-facing but clinically governed
explanation of gastric histopathologic characterization. It is designed
to establish a clean bridge:

**tissue acquisition → histopathology → histologic characterization →
specimen-specific pathological features → Lauren / molecular
classification / biomarker testing / staging integration.**

It deliberately does not turn PP-0178 into a complete pathology-report,
TNM, Lauren, WHO, molecular-testing or treatment package.

## Evidence Notes

### Evidence Note 1 --- Histologic type and grade are separate

NCCN explicitly lists histologic type and grade as separate
pathology-report elements. This supports teaching them as distinct
concepts rather than merging "type" and "grade" into one label.

### Evidence Note 2 --- Specimen type determines available information

NCCN's pathology table is explicitly specimen-specific. Biopsy, EMR and
gastrectomy specimens do not provide the same set of reportable
pathological features. This is central to explaining why a later
resection report can contain more information than an initial biopsy.

### Evidence Note 3 --- Intestinal versus diffuse morphology

NCCN and NCI both support intestinal and diffuse patterns. NCCN
describes intestinal tumors as variably differentiated and arranged in
tubular/glandular patterns and diffuse tumors as poorly differentiated
and discohesive with diffuse wall infiltration.

### Evidence Note 4 --- Signet-ring morphology

Signet-ring morphology is a histopathologic observation that is
particularly relevant to diffuse/poorly cohesive gastric carcinoma and
HDGC. It should not be treated as synonymous with hereditary disease.

### Evidence Note 5 --- Histopathology is not staging

Pathology contributes observations used in staging, especially invasion
and lymph-node status. The complete TNM system remains outside PP-0178.

### Evidence Note 6 --- Biomarker interface

NCCN directly links pathology review with biomarker testing. Histology
can provide clinical context, but the presence or absence of an
individual biomarker must be established by its dedicated testing
pathway.

### Evidence Note 7 --- Clinical-pathologic discordance

ESMO--ASCO expects oncology clinicians to recognize discordance between
pathology and clinical findings and to discuss such cases with
pathology. This supports a dedicated supporting knowledge block without
creating a universal second-opinion mandate.

## Clinical Claims Summary

1.  Histopathology characterizes tissue microscopically.
2.  Gastric adenocarcinoma is the dominant gastric cancer histology.
3.  Intestinal and diffuse are major morphologic patterns.
4.  Diffuse tumors can be discohesive and show signet-ring or
    non-signet-ring morphology.
5.  Histologic type, grade and invasion are distinct pathological
    dimensions.
6.  A biopsy may establish important diagnostic information without
    providing complete assessment of the entire tumor.
7.  EMR and gastrectomy specimens permit additional pathological
    assessment.
8.  Pathology contributes to staging but is not equivalent to TNM stage.
9.  Histopathology interfaces with Lauren classification, molecular
    classification and biomarker testing.
10. Histology alone does not determine individualized prognosis or
    treatment.

## Evidence Gaps

The current supplied source set is sufficient for the locked PP-0178
scope, but it does not justify exhaustive treatment of:

-   every current WHO gastric histologic subtype;
-   detailed microscopic diagnostic thresholds for rare entities;
-   full grade-specific algorithms;
-   detailed IHC antibody panels;
-   detailed molecular classification algorithms;
-   assay-specific biomarker scoring systems;
-   individualized prognostic estimates by histology.

These gaps are deliberately preserved rather than filled by unsupported
general knowledge.
