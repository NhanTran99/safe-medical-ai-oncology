## Clinical Question

> **What is subtotal gastrectomy, when can it be used to treat gastric cancer, why might it be chosen instead of total gastrectomy, what does the operation involve at a conceptual level, and what should patients understand about its cancer-control goals, reconstruction, risks, and long-term digestive and nutritional effects?**

## Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded patient-facing framework for understanding subtotal gastrectomy without converting the package into an operative manual or individualized surgical recommendation.

## Scope

### Included

- Definition of subtotal/partial gastrectomy.
- Distal and selected proximal subtotal gastrectomy.
- Tumor-location and extent rationale.
- Adequate resection and negative microscopic margins.
- Subtotal versus total gastrectomy.
- Regional lymphadenectomy as an integrated concept.
- Omentum, selected adjacent-organ and spleen-preservation context.
- Reconstruction concept.
- Open, laparoscopic, and robotic approaches.
- Curative versus selected palliative use.
- Major risks, eating changes, nutritional consequences, dumping-type symptoms, and feeding support.
- Pathology and downstream treatment interfaces.
- Patient-facing questions and misconceptions.

### Excluded

- Detailed operative technique and anatomy.
- Detailed D1/D2 station mapping or technique.
- Sentinel-node mapping.
- Detailed reconstruction technique.
- Exact nutritional prescriptions or supplement doses.
- Systemic therapy regimens and treatment algorithms.
- Individualized treatment and prognosis.
- Detailed surveillance and recurrence management.

## Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Direct disease-specific guidance

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

NCCN is the highest-priority disease-specific source in this package. The supplied 2026 guideline states that resectable T1b–T3 disease requires adequate gastric resection to achieve negative microscopic margins together with lymphadenectomy, and lists distal, subtotal, and total gastrectomy as surgical options. It also states that gastric resection should include regional lymphatics, that routine splenectomy is not indicated unless specific disease/nodal circumstances apply, and that minimally invasive approaches may be considered for selected cases with appropriate surgeon experience. fileciteturn15file4

### NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ

The NCI PDQ directly supports the clinical selection logic: when a lesion is not at the cardioesophageal junction and does not diffusely involve the stomach, subtotal gastrectomy is described as the procedure of choice in the relevant stage I–III treatment context. For cardia disease, proximal subtotal or total gastrectomy may be used; diffuse disease may require total gastrectomy. The NCI also states that subtotal gastrectomy demonstrated equivalent survival compared with total gastrectomy with decreased morbidity in the cited stage I context. fileciteturn15file0turn15file6

### Level II — Patient-facing clinical sources

### American Cancer Society — Stomach Cancer

ACS describes subtotal gastrectomy as removal of part of the stomach, most commonly distal when the cancer is in the lower stomach, with proximal gastrectomy as a less common option for selected upper-stomach disease. It describes reconnection of the remaining stomach to the small intestine and explains open, laparoscopic, and robotic approaches. fileciteturn15file1

### American Cancer Society — Chemotherapy for Stomach Cancer

ACS supports the patient-facing explanation of lymph-node removal, selected palliative subtotal gastrectomy for symptom relief, and feeding-tube support when adequate oral intake is not possible. fileciteturn15file17

### American Cancer Society — Immunotherapy for Stomach Cancer

ACS provides context that potentially resectable gastric cancers may receive systemic treatment before and/or after subtotal or total gastrectomy and that surgery may follow an inadequate endoscopic resection. fileciteturn15file14

### Vietnamese gastric-cancer guideline

The supplied Vietnamese guideline provides additional local clinical framing for gastric resection, margins, lymphadenectomy, and surgical extent. It is treated as supporting context rather than as a reason to convert one local numeric rule into a universal rule.

## Evidence Notes

### Note 1 — Subtotal is not simply 'less surgery'.

The source set frames subtotal gastrectomy as an oncologic resection whose adequacy depends on margins, lymphatic management, and tumor extent. It should not be taught as merely a smaller operation.

### Note 2 — The survival comparison is context-specific.

The NCI PDQ reports equivalent survival and decreased morbidity for subtotal versus total gastrectomy in the cited stage I setting. The package avoids generalizing this statement to every tumor location or stage.

### Note 3 — Margin distance is not universal.

The project sources support the principle of adequate microscopic margins. Exact distances vary by context and are therefore not elevated into a universal patient-facing rule.

### Note 4 — Lymphadenectomy is integrated but separately owned.

NCCN and ACS make regional lymph-node removal part of gastric cancer surgery. PP-0199–0201 own detailed lymphadenectomy content.

### Note 5 — Minimally invasive surgery is selection-dependent.

NCCN supports minimally invasive approaches in selected cases with experienced teams and generally does not recommend them for T4b or bulky N2 disease. This prevents an oversimplified 'laparoscopic is always better' message.

### Note 6 — Reconstruction is necessary but technical details are out of scope.

ACS states that the remaining stomach is reattached to the small intestine. The package explains the functional meaning but does not teach anastomotic technique.

### Note 7 — Palliative and curative intent are distinct.

NCCN and ACS support selected palliative gastric resection for symptom control. The package explicitly separates symptom relief from cancer cure.

### Note 8 — Nutritional effects are real but individualized.

NCCN identifies weight loss, diarrhea, nutritional deficiencies and dumping syndrome among long-term issues after gastrectomy. Exact supplementation and management are intentionally delegated.

## Clinical Claims Summary

- Subtotal gastrectomy is an established surgical option for appropriately selected gastric cancer.
- Distal subtotal gastrectomy is the principal partial-gastrectomy form for selected distal disease.
- Proximal subtotal gastrectomy is a selected option in appropriate proximal/cardia disease.
- Adequate resection with negative microscopic margins is the central oncologic requirement.
- Regional lymph-node management is part of oncologic gastric surgery.
- Subtotal gastrectomy may preserve function compared with total gastrectomy in selected disease, but it does not guarantee normal digestion.
- Minimally invasive approaches are appropriate only when disease and expertise permit safe oncologic surgery.
- Long-term nutrition and gastrointestinal function require monitoring.
- Palliative subtotal gastrectomy is symptom-directed and is not equivalent to curative resection.

## Evidence Consistency Review

### NCCN versus NCI

The two sources are complementary rather than contradictory for the package's core scope. NCCN 2026 emphasizes adequate resection, negative microscopic margins, regional lymphatics, selected minimally invasive surgery, and disease extent. NCI provides explicit location-based selection language and the cited subtotal-versus-total outcome comparison.

### NCCN/NCI versus ACS

ACS translates the same broad surgical concepts into patient-facing language: partial stomach removal, reconstruction, lymph-node removal, surgical approach, recovery, and symptom-focused palliative surgery. The package uses ACS for patient explanation while retaining NCCN/NCI as the principal clinical framework.

### Vietnamese guideline versus international sources

The Vietnamese source adds useful local terminology and surgical detail. Where exact numeric or technical rules could vary by guideline, the package preserves the broader principle of adequate oncologic resection rather than silently universalizing a single numeric rule.

## Evidence Gaps

- No project source establishes one universal margin distance for every subtotal gastrectomy.
- No project source supports a universal reconstruction method for all subtotal gastrectomies.
- The precise extent of lymphadenectomy is delegated to dedicated packages.
- The project sources do not provide individualized postoperative nutrition plans.
- The optimal surgical approach depends on disease and expertise and cannot be selected from this PP alone.
- Individual prognosis cannot be inferred from subtotal gastrectomy alone.

# Extended Evidence Analysis

## Claim-by-Claim Interpretation Rules

### Subtotal gastrectomy is an established operation
State as established, then immediately add 'in appropriately selected gastric cancer'.


### Distal subtotal gastrectomy
Use for selected lower/distal disease; do not imply all subtotal gastrectomies are distal.


### Proximal subtotal gastrectomy
Use selected/context-dependent wording because the source describes it as less common/selected.


### Equivalent survival
Always retain the NCI context: the cited stage I setting and appropriate disease selection.


### Decreased morbidity
Do not convert the NCI comparison into a universal promise for every patient or surgical approach.


### Negative microscopic margins
Treat as a core oncologic principle; do not invent one universal distance.


### Lymphadenectomy
State that regional lymph-node management is part of gastric cancer surgery; delegate technical extent.


### Minimally invasive surgery
Use selected-case and experience-dependent wording; retain NCCN limitations for T4b/bulky N2.


### Reconstruction
Describe restoration of gastrointestinal continuity; avoid technical anastomotic instruction.


### Nutrition
State that nutritional consequences can occur; avoid doses, schedules, or individual prescriptions.


### Palliative subtotal gastrectomy
Use symptom-relief language and distinguish it from cure.

## Evidence-Level Calibration

### Established / guideline-supported
The existence and core principles of subtotal gastrectomy, adequate resection, negative microscopic margins, regional lymphadenectomy, and selected minimally invasive surgery are directly supported.


### Supported but context-dependent
Exact procedure selection by tumor location, proximal versus distal approach, surgical approach, palliative use, and the extent of additional resection are context-dependent.


### Patient-facing inference
Statements such as 'the goal is not to remove the maximum amount of stomach' are educational interpretations of the source-supported oncologic principle and are not presented as literal guideline quotations.


### Not established by this package
The package does not establish individualized prognosis, universal margin distances, universal reconstruction choice, or universal superiority of any surgical approach.


## Negative-Claim Audit

- No claim that subtotal gastrectomy is always superior to total gastrectomy.

- No claim that subtotal gastrectomy is always possible in distal disease.

- No claim that all proximal tumors require total gastrectomy.

- No claim that minimally invasive surgery is appropriate for all patients.

- No claim that a negative margin guarantees cure.

- No claim that all patients need feeding tubes.

- No claim that nutritional deficiency follows in every patient.

- No claim that palliative resection is curative.


## Clinical Claim Decomposition

### Claim: subtotal gastrectomy can preserve part of the stomach
Definition layer: ACS describes removal of part of the stomach. Clinical layer: NCI/NCCN use subtotal resection as an option in selected resectable disease. Boundary: the package does not determine how much stomach an individual should retain.


### Claim: adequate margins determine whether preservation is acceptable
The source hierarchy places oncologic adequacy before preservation. NCCN directly links resectable surgery to adequate gastric resection and negative microscopic margins.


### Claim: lymphadenectomy is part of the operation
NCCN, NCI and ACS support regional lymph-node management. Detailed D1/D2 station ownership is deliberately transferred to PP-0199–0201.


### Claim: minimally invasive surgery may be considered
NCCN explicitly conditions this on case selection and surgeon experience. The package therefore avoids a general superiority claim.


### Claim: subtotal may have functional advantages over total
NCI reports equivalent survival and decreased morbidity in the cited stage I comparison. This is framed as evidence for selected disease, not as a universal promise.


### Claim: nutritional effects remain possible
NCCN survivorship guidance identifies weight loss, nutritional deficiencies and dumping syndrome after gastrectomy. The package therefore includes these as expected educational issues without prescribing treatment.

## Evidence Conflict / Non-Conflict Review

### No material conflict on the definition
All relevant sources describe subtotal/partial gastrectomy as removal of part of the stomach.


### No material conflict on margin principle
NCCN and the Vietnamese guideline both emphasize adequate resection; the exact numeric expression should not be universalized.


### No material conflict on regional nodes
NCCN, NCI and ACS all place regional lymph-node management within oncologic gastrectomy.


### Different levels of detail are complementary
NCCN is more guideline/selection oriented; NCI provides treatment framing; ACS provides patient-facing explanation; the Vietnamese source provides local clinical detail.


### Potential apparent difference in lymph-node framing
The package deliberately avoids choosing between detailed regional-node frameworks because PP-0199–0201 own those questions.


## Evidence Gaps Revisited

- The supplied sources do not establish one universal subtotal-gastrectomy reconstruction method.

- The supplied sources do not establish one universal margin distance applicable to all histologies and locations.

- The supplied sources do not justify individualized prediction of postoperative nutritional needs.

- The supplied sources do not justify individualized choice of open versus laparoscopic versus robotic surgery without clinical context.

- The supplied sources do not justify a universal statement that subtotal gastrectomy is superior to total gastrectomy.

- The supplied sources do not establish an individual patient's prognosis from operation type alone.
