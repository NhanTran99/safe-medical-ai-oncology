# 6. Detailed Evidence Notes

## 6.1 Unresectable, recurrent, and metastatic disease

The NCI PDQ identifies stage IV, inoperable, and recurrent gastric cancer as settings in which treatment can include cytotoxic therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and palliative locoregional therapy.

The NCCN framework similarly provides a systemic-treatment pathway for unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, and metastatic disease when local therapy is not indicated.

**Implication for PP-0208:** this is the principal population and treatment context.

---

## 6.2 Systemic therapy provides more than tumor shrinkage

The NCCN source explicitly describes systemic therapy as potentially providing:

- palliation;
- improved survival;
- enhanced quality of life.

Therefore the package must not reduce treatment goals to objective tumor response alone.

---

## 6.3 Chemotherapy versus supportive care

The NCI PDQ states that multiple randomized trials have supported the conclusion that chemotherapy provides several months of additional survival on average compared with supportive care in selected metastatic gastric-cancer patients.

This evidence supports the statement that:

> palliative systemic therapy can provide meaningful clinical benefit.

It does not establish that every patient should receive chemotherapy.

---

## 6.4 First-line treatment intensity

NCCN recommends two-drug cytotoxic regimens preferentially because of lower toxicity.

Three-drug cytotoxic regimens are reserved for selected medically fit patients with excellent performance status and access to frequent toxicity evaluations.

This supports the educational principle:

> **Treatment intensity must be individualized to clinical fitness and expected benefit.**

---

## 6.5 HER2

The NCI PDQ and NCCN materials support HER2-directed treatment in appropriate HER2-positive advanced disease.

The key PP-0208 claim is not the mechanism of trastuzumab.

The key claim is:

> **HER2 status can redirect systemic treatment.**

Detailed HER2 treatment belongs to PP-0210.

---

## 6.6 PD-L1

NCCN incorporates PD-L1 status into selected immunotherapy-containing treatment pathways.

PP-0208 uses this as an example of biomarker-directed systemic treatment.

It does not establish a universal rule that every patient with a particular CPS must receive one specific regimen.

Detailed PD-L1-guided treatment belongs to PP-0216.

---

## 6.7 CLDN18.2

NCCN and NCI support zolbetuximab-containing treatment for selected CLDN18.2-positive advanced gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

This demonstrates that a biomarker can redirect first-line treatment.

Detailed CLDN18.2-targeted therapy belongs to PP-0211.

---

## 6.8 MSI-H/dMMR

NCI and NCCN identify immunotherapy options for selected MSI-H/dMMR gastric cancers.

The PP uses this as a treatment-architecture example.

Detailed disease/biomarker-specific immunotherapy belongs to PP-0215.

---

## 6.9 Second-line therapy

NCI states that there is no single standard second-line option for all patients after progression on first-line palliative chemotherapy.

Accepted regimens include selected:

- paclitaxel with or without ramucirumab;
- docetaxel;
- irinotecan with or without 5-FU/leucovorin;
- pembrolizumab in dMMR/MSI-H tumors;
- trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-positive disease.

This supports a central PP-0208 concept:

> **subsequent treatment is a new clinical decision informed by prior treatment and current patient/tumor status.**

---

## 6.10 Ramucirumab

NCCN identifies ramucirumab plus paclitaxel as a preferred second-line regimen.

NCCN also states that first-line addition of ramucirumab is not recommended based on the RAINFALL results.

This distinction is clinically important because it demonstrates treatment-line dependence.

---

## 6.11 Trastuzumab deruxtecan

The supplied NCCN evidence summary describes DESTINY-Gastric01 in previously treated HER2-positive disease.

Reported outcomes include:

- ORR 40.5% versus 11%;
- OS 12.5 versus 8.4 months;
- PFS 5.6 versus 3.5 months.

The trial also showed clinically important toxicity, including neutropenia, anemia, leukopenia, and interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis.

The evidence is therefore useful not only for efficacy but also for the principle:

> **benefit must be considered alongside treatment burden.**

---

## 6.12 Third-line therapy

NCCN and NCI identify trifluridine/tipiracil as a later-line treatment option.

This demonstrates that the treatment pathway can extend beyond second-line therapy for selected patients.

---

## 6.13 Best supportive care

NCCN explicitly includes best supportive care in the palliative-management pathway.

NCI also reports evidence comparing chemotherapy with supportive care.

Therefore best supportive care is not an “absence of treatment.”

It is a legitimate clinical pathway focused on symptoms, function, comfort, and quality of life.

---

## 6.14 Palliative local therapy

NCI identifies:

- endoluminal laser therapy;
- endoluminal stent placement;
- gastrojejunostomy

as potentially helpful for selected gastric obstruction.

NCI also notes that palliative radiation may alleviate:

- bleeding;
- pain;
- obstruction.

These are included only as interfaces because PP-0208 is a systemic-treatment package.

# 10. Clinical Claims Summary

1. Palliative systemic therapy is active treatment for advanced gastric cancer.
2. It is used principally when disease is unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic and curative local treatment is not feasible.
3. Chemotherapy remains a major treatment component.
4. Targeted therapy and immunotherapy can be integrated when clinically appropriate.
5. Biomarkers may redirect treatment.
6. Treatment line matters.
7. Prior therapy matters.
8. Performance status matters.
9. Toxicity matters.
10. Patient goals matter.
11. Treatment may be changed after progression or unacceptable toxicity.
12. Best supportive care is a legitimate pathway.
13. Palliative care can coexist with anticancer treatment.
14. Palliative treatment is not synonymous with end-of-life care.
15. Individual treatment selection requires clinical context.

# 18. Evidence Interpretation Rules

## Rule 1 — Population-level evidence is not individualized advice

Trial outcomes describe selected study populations.

They do not establish that the same magnitude of benefit will occur in every patient.

---

## Rule 2 — Guideline preference is not universal prescription

A guideline-listed preferred option still requires clinical selection.

---

## Rule 3 — Biomarker association is not automatic treatment eligibility

A biomarker must be interpreted according to the relevant clinical and testing context.

---

## Rule 4 — Treatment line matters

The same drug may have different roles in different lines.

---

## Rule 5 — Efficacy and toxicity must be interpreted together

A treatment can demonstrate activity while also carrying substantial toxicity.

---

## Rule 6 — Supportive care is not synonymous with no care

Supportive care is an active clinical strategy.

# 21. Evidence Matrix — Treatment-Burden Principle

The source set supports a consistent clinical reasoning principle:

**Potential benefit**

must be weighed against

**treatment burden**

which may include:

- toxicity;
- monitoring;
- treatment visits;
- functional burden;
- cumulative treatment exposure.

This principle is especially important in later-line therapy and in patients with declining performance status.

# 22. Selected Trial Evidence Register

## Chemotherapy versus Best Supportive Care

NCI summarizes randomized evidence showing longer survival on average with salvage chemotherapy compared with supportive care in selected advanced gastric-cancer populations.

**Use in PP-0208:** establishes that palliative systemic treatment can provide meaningful benefit.

---

## RAINBOW / Ramucirumab

NCCN and NCI support ramucirumab-containing later-line treatment.

**Use in PP-0208:** illustrates treatment-line-specific targeted therapy.

---

## RAINFALL

NCCN notes that first-line addition of ramucirumab did not reduce progression/death risk in treatment-naive metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma.

**Use in PP-0208:** illustrates that a later-line therapy is not automatically a first-line therapy.

---

## DESTINY-Gastric01

NCCN summarizes improved ORR, OS, PFS, and DOR with fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan compared with physician-choice chemotherapy in previously treated HER2-positive gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma, with greater toxicity.

**Use in PP-0208:** demonstrates benefit-burden balancing.

---

## CLDN18.2-directed therapy evidence

NCCN/NCI support zolbetuximab-containing first-line treatment in selected CLDN18.2-positive disease.

**Use in PP-0208:** demonstrates biomarker-directed systemic treatment.

# 23. Evidence Gaps — Expanded

The following are intentionally unresolved:

### Gap 1

No single systemic regimen is appropriate for every advanced gastric-cancer patient.

### Gap 2

Exact treatment duration is context-dependent.

### Gap 3

The sequence of later-line therapies can change as new therapies become available.

### Gap 4

Biomarker-defined treatment eligibility may depend on assay and guideline context.

### Gap 5

Trial populations may differ from real-world patients.

### Gap 6

Performance status is clinically important but does not alone determine treatment.

### Gap 7

Patient goals may change over time.

### Gap 8

Treatment decisions may involve multiple biomarkers simultaneously.

These gaps are reasons to preserve a decision framework rather than construct a deterministic algorithm.
