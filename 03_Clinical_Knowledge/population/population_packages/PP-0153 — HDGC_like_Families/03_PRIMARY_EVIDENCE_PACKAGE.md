# Primary Clinical Question

> **What is an HDGC-like family, what does it mean when no CDH1 or CTNNA1 pathogenic variant is identified, and how is gastric-cancer risk managed when the hereditary cause remains unresolved?**

# Core Evidence: Formal Definition

The 2020 IGCLC defines an HDGC-like family when all of the following apply:

### 1.
No **CDH1 or CTNNA1 pathogenic variant** is identified.

### 2.
There is at least **one confirmed case of diffuse gastric cancer** in the family.

### 3.
There is another **gastric cancer or lobular breast cancer** in first-degree or second-degree relatives.

This definition is the canonical scope of PP-0153.

---

# Why Negative Testing Does Not End the Clinical Assessment

NCI reports that CDH1 germline pathogenic variants are found in approximately **30%–40% of clinically defined HDGC families** from different ethnic backgrounds.

Therefore, clinically defined HDGC can exist without an identified CDH1 pathogenic variant.

The HDGC-like framework extends this concept to families in which neither CDH1 nor CTNNA1 pathogenic variants are identified.

---

# Clinical Meaning of HDGC-like

HDGC-like should be understood as:

> **a clinically suggestive hereditary gastric-cancer family phenotype with no identified CDH1/CTNNA1 pathogenic variant.**

It is not:

- a molecular diagnosis;
- proof of another specific causative gene;
- proof that cancer will occur;
- proof that gastric-cancer risk equals CDH1-positive HDGC.

---

# Gastric-Cancer Risk Evidence

The NCI source states that the level of gastric-cancer risk in HDGC-like families is **uncertain**.

This uncertainty is the key reason management differs from confirmed CDH1-associated HDGC.

The evidence does not support assigning a single universal lifetime-risk percentage to HDGC-like families.

---

# Endoscopic Surveillance Evidence

In HDGC-like families:

- affected individuals and their first-degree relatives **may be considered** for yearly endoscopic surveillance;
- this is described for at least **2 years** after a negative CDH1 genetic-test result.

The wording is conditional:

> **may be considered**

It is not an automatic universal mandate.

---

# Surveillance Timing

The NCI summary of the IGCLC framework states:

**Begin at age 40 years**

or

**10 years before the earliest gastric cancer in the family**

as the relevant timing framework.

HDGC surveillance can begin at, but not before, **age 18 years**.

These thresholds should remain exact in the knowledge artifact.

---

# Surveillance Interval After Two Years

After annual surveillance for 2 years:

> surveillance intervals can be prolonged at the discretion of the endoscopist.

The decision may consider:

- previous endoscopy findings;
- family history.

Therefore, annual surveillance should not be represented as a rigid lifelong schedule.

---

# Risk-Reducing Total Gastrectomy Evidence

The NCI source explicitly states:

> **Risk-reducing total gastrectomy is not advised when endoscopies are negative in HDGC-like families because the level of gastric-cancer risk is uncertain.**

This is a critical management boundary.

---

# Why HDGC-like Management Differs From CDH1-Positive HDGC

### CDH1 pathogenic variant identified

→ genetically established HDGC risk  
→ substantially characterized hereditary syndrome  
→ established management framework

### HDGC-like

→ qualifying clinical phenotype  
→ no identified CDH1/CTNNA1 pathogenic variant  
→ risk remains uncertain  
→ surveillance may be considered  
→ negative endoscopy does not justify automatic RRTG

The package must not collapse these two states into one.

---

# Endoscopy Limitations

NCI describes limitations of endoscopic detection in HDGC.

Early diffuse gastric cancer and precursor lesions may not have obvious endoscopic features, and the mucosa may appear normal despite microscopic signet-ring cell carcinoma.

Therefore:

> **A normal-looking endoscopy does not completely exclude microscopic disease.**

Detailed surveillance technique and biopsy strategy are delegated to the dedicated endoscopy/biopsy architecture.

---

# Intestinal-Type Gastric Cancer Boundary

A family history dominated by intestinal-type gastric cancer should not automatically be classified as HDGC-like.

NCI notes that intestinal-type gastric cancer is usually not associated with CDH1 pathogenic variants and that surveillance in such families should be individualized.

This is a separate clinical context from the formal HDGC-like definition.

---

# Future Reassessment

Because the hereditary basis remains unresolved, future specialist reassessment may be appropriate as:

- testing technologies evolve;
- variant interpretation changes;
- additional hereditary cancer knowledge emerges.

No fixed repeat-testing interval is established by the current Source Materials.

Therefore, no artificial testing schedule is included.

# Evidence Gaps

The Source Materials do not establish:

- a universal lifetime gastric-cancer risk for HDGC-like families;
- a universal lifelong endoscopic interval;
- a fixed repeat-genetic-testing interval;
- a specific unidentified gene responsible for HDGC-like families;
- an automatic indication for RRTG in endoscopy-negative HDGC-like families.

These gaps are intentionally preserved.

# Primary Evidence Conclusion

The Source Materials provide sufficient, direct evidence for a dedicated HDGC-like Families package.

The package should preserve the central distinction:

> **HDGC-like = clinically suggestive family phenotype + no identified CDH1/CTNNA1 pathogenic variant + uncertain gastric-cancer risk.**

The key management boundary is:

> **Endoscopic surveillance may be considered, while risk-reducing total gastrectomy is not advised when endoscopies are negative because risk remains uncertain.**
