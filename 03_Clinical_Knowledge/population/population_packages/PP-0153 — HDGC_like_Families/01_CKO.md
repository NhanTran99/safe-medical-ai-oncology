# Educational Objective

Help the reader understand:

> **What is an HDGC-like family, what does it mean when no CDH1 or CTNNA1 pathogenic variant is identified, and what does this mean for gastric-cancer surveillance and risk-reducing surgery?**

This package describes a **clinically defined, genetically unresolved hereditary-risk state**. It must not be presented as equivalent to genetically confirmed CDH1-associated HDGC.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is an HDGC-like Family?

An HDGC-like family is a family with a clinical pattern suggestive of hereditary diffuse gastric cancer even though no pathogenic variant has been identified in **CDH1 or CTNNA1**.

The 2020 International Gastric Cancer Linkage Consortium (IGCLC) definition, as summarized by NCI, requires:

1. No identified CDH1 or CTNNA1 pathogenic variant.
2. At least one confirmed case of diffuse gastric cancer in the family.
3. Another gastric cancer or lobular breast cancer in first- or second-degree relatives.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does “HDGC-like” Mean?

HDGC-like describes a **clinical/family phenotype**, not a confirmed genetic diagnosis.

It means:

> The family remains clinically suggestive of HDGC, but the currently identifiable genetic cause is unresolved.

It does not mean that another specific HDGC gene has definitely been discovered.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Can Genetic Testing Be Negative?

CDH1 pathogenic variants are not found in every family with a clinically defined HDGC phenotype.

NCI reports that CDH1 pathogenic variants are found in approximately 30%–40% of clinically defined HDGC families from different ethnic backgrounds.

Therefore, a negative CDH1 result does not automatically eliminate hereditary concern.

CTNNA1 is also part of the HDGC testing pathway. A family that meets the HDGC-like definition has no identified pathogenic variant in either CDH1 or CTNNA1.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Does the Family History Matter?

The HDGC-like definition is not simply:

> “multiple gastric cancers + negative genetic testing.”

It specifically requires:

- a confirmed diffuse gastric cancer;
- an additional gastric cancer or lobular breast cancer;
- the relevant cancers occurring among first- or second-degree relatives.

Histology therefore matters.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — What Does Negative CDH1/CTNNA1 Testing Mean?

Negative testing means that no pathogenic variant was identified in the genes tested.

It does not prove:

- that the family has no hereditary susceptibility;
- that future cancer risk is absent;
- that the family history is unimportant.

In an appropriately defined HDGC-like family, the clinical phenotype remains relevant.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — How Certain Is Gastric-Cancer Risk?

The level of gastric-cancer risk in HDGC-like families is **uncertain**.

This uncertainty is clinically important.

The risk should not automatically be assumed to be:

- the same as CDH1 pathogenic-variant carriers; or
- low enough to ignore.

The evidence therefore supports a cautious, individualized approach.

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — May Endoscopic Surveillance Be Considered?

In HDGC-like families, affected individuals and their first-degree relatives may be considered for **yearly endoscopic surveillance for at least 2 years** after a negative CDH1 genetic-test result.

The decision should occur in an appropriate specialist context.

This package does not prescribe an individualized surveillance plan.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — When Does Surveillance Begin?

The NCI summary of the IGCLC framework states that surveillance begins at:

- **age 40 years**, or
- **10 years before the earliest gastric cancer in the family**,

whichever is appropriate.

HDGC surveillance can begin at, but not before, **age 18 years**.

These are source-derived thresholds and should not be generalized beyond the HDGC/HDGC-like context.

---

# Knowledge Block 9 — Can Surveillance Intervals Be Prolonged?

After an individual has participated in annual surveillance for 2 years, surveillance intervals may be prolonged at the discretion of the endoscopist.

This decision may consider:

- previous endoscopy findings;
- family history.

Therefore, the package should not be interpreted as a rigid lifelong annual-endoscopy rule.

---

# Knowledge Block 10 — Why Is Risk-Reducing Total Gastrectomy Not Automatically Advised?

In HDGC-like families, **risk-reducing total gastrectomy is not advised when endoscopies are negative** because the level of gastric-cancer risk is uncertain.

This is a critical difference from established CDH1-associated HDGC management.

HDGC-like status should therefore not automatically trigger the same surgical pathway as a confirmed pathogenic CDH1 variant.

---

# Knowledge Block 11 — What Does a Negative Endoscopy Mean?

A negative endoscopy does not remove the underlying familial concern.

Early diffuse gastric cancer and precursor lesions may be difficult to identify endoscopically, including when the gastric mucosa appears normal.

However, in an HDGC-like family, a negative endoscopic evaluation is also specifically why prophylactic total gastrectomy is not automatically advised.

Detailed endoscopic and biopsy methodology belongs to the dedicated screening/endoscopy architecture.

---

# Knowledge Block 12 — Could the Family Be Re-evaluated Later?

Because the hereditary basis remains unresolved, specialist reassessment may become relevant as:

- genetic testing evolves;
- variant interpretation changes;
- hereditary cancer knowledge develops.

The available source materials do not establish a fixed interval for repeat genetic testing.

Therefore, no arbitrary repeat-testing schedule should be created.

# Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1
**CDH1-negative means there is no hereditary gastric-cancer risk.**

**Fact:** Some clinically defined HDGC families have no identified CDH1 pathogenic variant.

### Myth 2
**Any family with several stomach cancers is HDGC-like.**

**Fact:** The formal definition requires a confirmed diffuse gastric cancer plus additional qualifying cancer history and no identified CDH1/CTNNA1 pathogenic variant.

### Myth 3
**HDGC-like means another hereditary gene has definitely been found.**

**Fact:** It describes a clinically suggestive but genetically unresolved family phenotype.

### Myth 4
**HDGC-like has exactly the same cancer risk as CDH1-positive HDGC.**

**Fact:** Gastric-cancer risk in HDGC-like families is uncertain.

### Myth 5
**A negative endoscopy proves there is no hereditary concern.**

**Fact:** Endoscopy does not eliminate the underlying familial risk.

### Myth 6
**HDGC-like automatically means prophylactic gastrectomy.**

**Fact:** Risk-reducing total gastrectomy is not advised when endoscopies are negative because the level of gastric-cancer risk is uncertain.

---

# Key Messages

- HDGC-like is a clinically defined family phenotype.
- It requires no identified CDH1 or CTNNA1 pathogenic variant.
- It also requires a confirmed diffuse gastric cancer plus an additional gastric cancer or lobular breast cancer in first- or second-degree relatives.
- Negative genetic testing does not automatically eliminate hereditary concern.
- Gastric-cancer risk in HDGC-like families remains uncertain.
- Yearly endoscopic surveillance may be considered in appropriate individuals.
- Source-supported surveillance begins at age 40 or 10 years before the earliest family gastric cancer, with no start before age 18.
- Surveillance intervals may later be prolonged based on endoscopy findings and family history.
- Negative endoscopy does not equal absence of hereditary risk.
- Risk-reducing total gastrectomy is not advised when endoscopies are negative in HDGC-like families because risk remains uncertain.
- HDGC-like should not be treated as genetically confirmed CDH1-associated HDGC.
