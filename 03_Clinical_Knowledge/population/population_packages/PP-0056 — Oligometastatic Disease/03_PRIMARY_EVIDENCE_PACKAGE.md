# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is oligometastatic disease?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **oligometastatic disease**, including what the term means, why it describes metastatic cancer involving a limited number of sites, why it is considered a clinical concept rather than a separate TNM stage, why no single universal definition exists across all cancers, and why physicians must evaluate multiple clinical factors before applying this concept.

This Population Package introduces the concept of oligometastatic disease without discussing numerical definitions, consensus criteria, local therapies, treatment strategies or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of oligometastatic disease
- Explanation of limited metastatic spread
- Oligometastatic disease as a clinical concept
- Recognition that no universal definition exists
- Importance of comprehensive medical evaluation
- Explanation that imaging findings alone are insufficient

---

## Excluded

- Numerical definitions (e.g., ≤3 or ≤5 metastases)
- ESTRO/EORTC consensus
- SABR/SBRT
- Metastasectomy
- Local ablative therapies
- Tumor burden
- RECIST
- Treatment
- Molecular mechanisms of metastasis
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Oligometastatic disease describes metastatic cancer involving a limited number of distant sites.
- It is a clinical concept used in modern oncology rather than an independent staging category.
- Definitions may vary between studies, cancer types and clinical guidelines.
- Imaging findings alone are insufficient to determine whether a patient has oligometastatic disease.
- Physicians integrate imaging findings with the overall clinical picture before using this terminology.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- recognition of oligometastatic disease as a clinical concept;
- absence of a universally accepted definition across all cancers;
- the need for individualized clinical assessment before applying the concept;
- the distinction between formal TNM staging and additional clinical terminology.

Differences between references relate mainly to operational definitions used in research or specific clinical settings.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted conceptual understanding without adopting any single research definition.
