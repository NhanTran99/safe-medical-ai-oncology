# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is tumor grade?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **tumor grade** as a pathological assessment describing how abnormal cancer cells appear under the microscope.

This Population Package explains that tumor grade reflects cellular differentiation, differs from tumor stage, and contributes to—but does not determine by itself—the overall evaluation and management of cancer.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of tumor grade
- Cellular differentiation
- General meaning of lower-grade and higher-grade tumors
- Difference between grade and stage
- Clinical role of tumor grade
- Appropriate patient understanding

---

## Excluded

- Disease-specific grading systems
- Nottingham grading
- Gleason score
- WHO grading systems
- TNM staging
- Prognostic models
- Treatment algorithms
- Biomarker interpretation

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Tumor grade evaluates microscopic cellular appearance.
- Tumor grade reflects differentiation.
- Grade and stage describe different aspects of cancer.
- Tumor grade is one part of comprehensive clinical assessment.
- Grading systems vary between cancer types.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Major oncology organizations consistently describe tumor grade as a pathological assessment of cellular differentiation and emphasize that it should be interpreted together with tumor stage and other clinical information rather than in isolation.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- pathology principles from NCI and CAP;
- patient education principles from ACS;
- multidisciplinary oncology guidance consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
