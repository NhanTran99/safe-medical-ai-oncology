## B. Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain what D2 lymphadenectomy means in gastric cancer.
2. Understand how D2 extends beyond D1.
3. Recognize the principal additional nodal territories described by the governing sources.
4. Understand the source-specific station terminology used in the supplied Vietnamese guideline.
5. Understand why D2 is used in selected curative/resectable gastric-cancer settings.
6. Understand why D2 is standard in some regional practice settings and recommended rather than universally required in others.
7. Understand the evidence comparing D2 with D1 without reducing the evidence to a simple “more is better” conclusion.
8. Understand why surgeon and hospital experience matter.
9. Understand why modern D2 should not be equated with routine splenectomy or pancreatectomy.
10. Distinguish anatomical lymphadenectomy extent from lymph-node yield and lymph-node positivity.
11. Understand how D2 interacts with pathological staging.
12. Understand why D2 can influence the context of postoperative treatment planning without becoming a treatment-selection rule.
13. Recognize the role of minimally invasive D2 in selected experienced settings.
14. Recognize the limits of D2 and the situations in which palliative surgery has different goals.
15. Ask appropriate questions about the planned or completed lymph-node dissection.

## C. Clinical Knowledge Blocks

# 1. What Is D2 Lymphadenectomy?

D2 lymphadenectomy is a defined, more extensive regional lymph-node dissection performed as part of gastric-cancer surgery.

The current NCCN source defines D2 as the D1 lymph-node dissection plus removal of lymph nodes along the:

- left gastric artery;
- common hepatic artery;
- celiac artery; and
- splenic artery.

This makes D2 an anatomical concept.

It is not simply a statement that “many lymph nodes were removed.”

### Key message

> **D2 describes a broader defined lymph-node field beyond D1.**

# 2. How Is D2 Different From D1?

D1 focuses on the regional/perigastric field.

D2 includes that D1 field and extends the dissection to additional nodal territories along major arteries associated with the celiac axis.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline expresses this through a station-based framework:

- D1 = groups 1–7;
- D1+ = D1 plus specified additional groups;
- D2 = D1 plus 8a, 9, 11d, and 12a in that source's classification.

The two sources should be preserved with their own terminology rather than converted into an invented universal station crosswalk.

# 3. Why Is D2 More Extensive?

Gastric cancer can spread through regional lymphatic pathways.

A broader lymph-node dissection can therefore:

- remove additional regional nodal tissue;
- improve the completeness of regional nodal assessment;
- provide more information for pathological staging;
- potentially reduce regional disease left behind in appropriately selected patients.

However, greater anatomical extent also creates greater technical demands.

The purpose of D2 is therefore not “remove as many nodes as possible.”

The purpose is:

> **perform an oncologically defined broader regional lymphadenectomy when the clinical setting supports it.**

# 4. Which Nodal Territories Are Added in D2?

The NCCN definition identifies the additional D2 field as lymph nodes along:

- the left gastric artery;
- the common hepatic artery;
- the celiac artery;
- the splenic artery.

These are deeper and more central regional nodal territories than the principal perigastric D1 field.

The package does not provide the operative steps required to expose or dissect these vessels.

# 5. D2 in the Vietnamese Station-Based Framework

The supplied Vietnamese guideline describes:

**D1 = groups 1–7**

and

**D2 = D1 + groups 8a, 9, 11d, and 12a.**

This terminology is useful for clinical communication and structured retrieval.

It should be presented as:

> **the station-based classification used by the supplied Vietnamese guideline.**

The package should not silently claim that every international guideline uses exactly the same station terminology.

# 6. Is D2 the Same as D2+?

No.

D2 is the defined standard extended regional lymphadenectomy discussed in this package.

D2+ refers to further extension beyond standard D2 in selected circumstances.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline discusses additional nodal extensions, including para-aortic or other special regional stations, in particular settings.

These concepts are acknowledged here only to preserve the upper boundary.

A detailed D2+ package would require its own scope decision.

# 7. Why Is D2 Clinically Important?

D2 is clinically important because the extent of lymph-node dissection can influence:

- regional disease control;
- pathological staging;
- postoperative treatment context;
- interpretation of the completeness of gastric-cancer surgery.

The importance of D2 should not be reduced to survival alone.

A more extensive operation can improve nodal assessment and regional control while also increasing technical demands and potential complications.

# 8. When Is D2 Considered?

The supplied sources place D2 primarily within the curative/resectable gastric-cancer surgical framework.

The Vietnamese guideline describes D2 for more advanced disease contexts, including T2–T4 disease and selected cT1N+ disease when curative surgery is possible.

The current NCCN source describes gastrectomy with D2 lymphadenectomy as standard treatment for curable gastric cancer in East Asia and as a recommended but not universally required procedure in Western practice.

These statements should not be converted into an individualized prescription.

### Patient-facing interpretation

> **The appropriate lymph-node extent depends on the cancer's extent, nodal status, resectability, planned gastric operation, guideline framework, and surgical expertise.**

# 9. D2 and Early Gastric Cancer

D2 is not synonymous with “early gastric cancer.”

Selected Tis/T1a lesions may be candidates for endoscopic resection when appropriate criteria are met.

For patients who require surgery, the appropriate lymphadenectomy depends on the disease context.

The package therefore avoids the rule:

> “Every early gastric cancer needs D2.”

Instead:

> **Endoscopic treatment and surgical treatment are different pathways, and the appropriate lymph-node extent is determined within the surgical pathway.**

Detailed EMR/ESD criteria belong to PP-0193–PP-0195.

# 10. D2 and Curative Intent

D2 belongs most clearly to the curative/resectable surgical framework.

The objective is to remove the primary gastric tumor together with an appropriate regional lymph-node field.

This is different from palliative surgery, where the priority may be symptom control.

The package therefore does not treat D2 as a universal requirement for every patient with gastric cancer.

# 11. D2 and Palliative Surgery

For incurable disease, the NCCN source states that gastric resection may be used for palliation of symptoms and does not need to include lymph-node dissection.

Therefore:

**curative oncologic gastrectomy**

and

**palliative gastric surgery**

have different objectives.

A patient should not assume that a palliative operation requires D2 simply because the patient has gastric cancer.

# 12. What Does the Evidence Say About D2 Versus D1?

This is one of the most important parts of the D2 package.

Historical Western randomized trials did not show a simple overall-survival advantage for D2 over D1.

In the Dutch trial summarized by NCCN, D2 was associated with higher postoperative morbidity and mortality in the historical setting.

However, long-term follow-up showed lower local recurrence, regional recurrence, and gastric-cancer-related deaths with D2, while overall survival remained similar.

The British Cooperative trial also did not demonstrate an overall-survival advantage for D2.

The correct conclusion is therefore not:

> “D2 does not work.”

Nor:

> “D2 always improves survival.”

The evidence is more nuanced.

# 13. Why Did Historical D2 Have Higher Morbidity?

Historical D2 surgery was sometimes performed with more extensive organ removal, particularly splenectomy and pancreatectomy.

These additional procedures increased postoperative morbidity and mortality.

Modern D2 practice has increasingly emphasized preservation of the spleen and pancreas unless disease involvement requires otherwise.

This distinction is essential when interpreting historical D1-versus-D2 trials.

# 14. Modern D2 Is Not the Same as Historical D2

The current NCCN evidence describes pancreas-preserving D2 surgery as having lower complication rates than historical approaches that routinely included pancreatic resection.

Therefore historical morbidity figures should not automatically be applied to every modern D2 operation.

At the same time, D2 remains technically demanding.

The appropriate message is:

> **Modern D2 can be performed safely in experienced settings, but it remains a more technically demanding operation than D1.**

# 15. Why Does Surgeon Experience Matter?

The NCCN source explicitly states that D2 requires significant training and expertise and should be performed in centers experienced with the technique.

This is a central part of the clinical meaning of D2.

A technically excellent D2 operation requires:

- appropriate patient selection;
- knowledge of gastric and regional anatomy;
- experience with lymphadenectomy;
- appropriate perioperative management;
- coordinated pathology assessment.

The package does not provide a surgical training manual.

# 16. Why Does Hospital Experience Matter?

The evidence summarized by NCCN suggests that outcomes can be better when D2 is performed in high-volume or sufficiently experienced centers.

This is particularly relevant because the historical Western experience with D2 included substantial morbidity.

Patients can therefore reasonably ask about:

- surgeon experience with gastric-cancer surgery;
- center volume;
- experience with D2 lymphadenectomy;
- postoperative support;
- pathology expertise.

# 17. Does D2 Mean Removing the Spleen?

No.

Routine splenectomy is not part of the definition of D2.

The current NCCN source states that splenectomy is appropriate when the spleen is involved or extensive hilar adenopathy is present, rather than as a routine prophylactic component.

Therefore:

> **D2 ≠ automatic splenectomy.**

# 18. Does D2 Mean Removing the Pancreas?

No.

Routine or prophylactic pancreatectomy is not recommended with D2 lymphadenectomy.

Pancreatic resection may be required when the tumor directly involves the pancreas or when a disease-specific en-bloc resection is necessary.

That is a separate surgical issue from the definition of D2.

# 19. Why Is Organ Preservation Important?

The evidence summarized by NCCN indicates that unnecessary spleen or pancreas removal can increase morbidity.

Modern D2 therefore aims to preserve these organs when they are not involved by disease.

This is an important correction to the historical image of D2 as “very extensive surgery that removes everything nearby.”

D2 is extensive in its lymph-node field.

It does not mean indiscriminate organ removal.

# 20. D2 and the ≥16-Node Examination Goal

The current NCCN surgical framework gives a goal of examining at least 16 lymph nodes.

This should not be confused with the definition of D2.

Three concepts must remain separate:

### D2

The anatomical extent of lymph-node dissection.

### Nodes examined

The number of lymph nodes assessed by pathology.

### Nodes positive

The number of examined nodes containing metastatic cancer.

Therefore:

> **D2 does not mean “16 nodes,” and a node count alone does not identify D2.**

# 21. Why Does Node Examination Matter?

Adequate lymph-node examination improves the information available for pathological staging.

A pathology report can tell the clinical team:

- how many lymph nodes were examined;
- how many contained metastatic cancer.

This information contributes to the N category.

D2 can therefore have both an oncologic and a staging role.

# 22. D2 Does Not Guarantee Negative Nodes

A patient can undergo an appropriate D2 lymphadenectomy and still have positive lymph nodes.

A positive node means that cancer was found in that examined node.

It does not automatically mean that the operation was inadequate.

Conversely, negative nodes do not prove that the cancer can never recur.

D2 is one component of comprehensive gastric-cancer treatment.

# 23. D2 and Pathological Staging

The extent of lymph-node dissection and the pathological findings are related but distinct.

The operation determines the anatomical field that was removed.

Pathology determines what was found within the specimen.

The final clinical interpretation combines:

- tumor characteristics;
- margins;
- lymph-node findings;
- overall pathological stage;
- previous treatment.

The package therefore does not attempt to replace a formal staging package.

# 24. D2 and Postoperative Treatment Context

The NCCN source states that postoperative management depends in part on the extent of lymph-node dissection.

In its treatment framework, patients with advanced resected disease who underwent D2 may follow different postoperative treatment pathways from patients who received less extensive lymphadenectomy.

This demonstrates why D2 status can matter after surgery.

However, PP-0201 does not prescribe chemotherapy or chemoradiation.

Detailed postoperative treatment belongs to the downstream treatment packages.

# 25. D2 and Minimally Invasive Surgery

D2 describes the extent of lymph-node dissection, not the surgical access route.

The supplied NCCN source summarizes randomized evidence showing that laparoscopic distal gastrectomy with D2 lymphadenectomy can have comparable oncologic outcomes to open surgery in selected patients with locally advanced gastric cancer.

NCCN therefore allows minimally invasive approaches in selected cases when the surgeon has appropriate experience with minimally invasive foregut surgery and lymphadenectomy.

This does not mean that every patient is suitable for laparoscopic or robotic D2.

# 26. D2 in T4b or Bulky N2 Disease

The NCCN source states that minimally invasive approaches are generally not recommended for T4b or N2 bulky gastric cancer.

This is an approach-selection issue, not a definition of D2.

The package should therefore avoid converting it into a general rule that D2 is contraindicated in those patients.

The key message is:

> **The extent of lymphadenectomy and the surgical access route are separate clinical decisions.**

# 27. East Asian and Western Practice

The current NCCN source identifies D2 as the standard treatment for curable gastric cancer in East Asia.

In Western countries, D2 is recommended but not universally required.

This difference reflects:

- historical surgical experience;
- center expertise;
- differences in perioperative management;
- interpretation of randomized evidence;
- evolving standards.

Patients should therefore not assume that different hospitals or countries use D2 in exactly the same way.

# 28. Does Different Practice Mean One Region Is Wrong?

No.

Differences in practice do not automatically mean that one guideline is correct and another is incorrect.

The clinical value of D2 depends on:

- appropriate patient selection;
- technical quality;
- experience;
- perioperative care;
- disease characteristics;
- the broader treatment strategy.

The package therefore presents regional differences as evidence-context differences, not as a patient-facing argument about which country provides “better” care.

# 29. D2 and Local/Regional Recurrence

The long-term Dutch trial evidence summarized by NCCN showed lower local and regional recurrence after D2 than D1.

This suggests that broader nodal dissection can influence regional disease control.

However, the same evidence did not show a simple overall-survival advantage.

This is an important example of why clinical benefit cannot be summarized using one endpoint.

# 30. D2 and Overall Survival

The evidence does not support a universal statement that D2 improves overall survival compared with D1.

Some studies show trends or benefits in particular outcomes or settings.

Other randomized studies did not show significant OS differences.

The current package therefore classifies the D1/D2 survival comparison as **context-dependent**, not as a universal superiority claim.

# 31. D2 and Gastric-Cancer-Related Death

The long-term Dutch trial follow-up showed fewer gastric-cancer-related deaths after D2 despite similar overall survival.

This is clinically relevant because overall survival includes deaths from causes other than gastric cancer.

However, this finding should still be interpreted within the trial's historical surgical context.

# 32. D2 and Postoperative Morbidity

D2 can be associated with greater postoperative morbidity when performed with unnecessary additional organ resections or in inexperienced settings.

Modern pancreas-preserving D2 has improved the safety profile.

The package therefore avoids both extremes:

> “D2 is always dangerous.”

and

> “D2 has no additional risk.”

# 33. D2 and Surgical Quality

The quality of D2 is more important than simply labeling an operation “D2.”

A patient should be able to ask:

- Was the intended D2 field completed?
- How many lymph nodes were examined?
- Were any organs removed, and why?
- What did the pathology show?
- How will the final findings affect the next treatment step?

These questions help connect the operation with the pathology and treatment plan.

# 34. D2 and the Primary Gastric Resection

D2 is not a replacement for appropriate gastric resection.

It is a lymph-node component of the operation.

The appropriate gastric resection depends on factors such as tumor location and extent and is owned by the gastrectomy packages.

PP-0201 therefore does not decide subtotal versus total gastrectomy.

# 35. D2 and Tumor Location

Tumor location can influence the type of gastric resection and the relevant lymphatic anatomy.

However, PP-0201 should not become a tumor-location surgical algorithm.

The package only needs to establish:

> **D2 is performed in association with an appropriate gastric resection and is tailored to the disease context.**

# 36. D2 and Resection Margins

D2 is not synonymous with a negative surgical margin.

Margins concern the primary tumor resection.

D2 concerns the regional lymph-node field.

Both can contribute to oncologic quality, but they answer different clinical questions.

# 37. D2 and Recurrence Risk

D2 may reduce local or regional recurrence in some evidence settings.

It does not eliminate recurrence risk.

Recurrence can arise from:

- residual microscopic disease;
- distant spread;
- peritoneal disease;
- other biological mechanisms.

D2 should therefore never be presented as a guarantee against recurrence.

# 38. D2 and Systemic Therapy

D2 does not replace systemic therapy when systemic therapy is indicated.

The extent of lymphadenectomy may influence postoperative treatment planning, but it does not independently determine the entire treatment plan.

This package therefore stops at the interface with downstream therapy.

# 39. D2 and Multidisciplinary Care

Localized gastric cancer is commonly managed through coordinated surgical, medical oncology, radiation oncology, pathology, radiology, and supportive-care input.

D2 should therefore be understood as one component of a broader treatment strategy.

The package does not simulate a multidisciplinary tumor board or generate an individualized surgical plan.

# 40. What Should a Patient Ask Before Surgery?

Useful questions include:

1. Is lymph-node dissection planned?
2. Is the planned dissection D1, D1+, D2, or another extent?
3. Why is D2 being considered for my cancer?
4. Which nodal territories are included?
5. How experienced is the surgeon with D2?
6. How experienced is the hospital with gastric-cancer surgery?
7. Will the operation be open, laparoscopic, or robotic?
8. If minimally invasive surgery is planned, does the team have experience with D2 lymphadenectomy?
9. Is spleen removal expected?
10. Is pancreas removal expected?
11. If an organ may be removed, what disease finding would make that necessary?
12. How many lymph nodes are expected to be examined?
13. How will the pathology findings affect staging?
14. Will the extent of lymphadenectomy affect postoperative treatment planning?

# 41. What Should a Patient Ask After Surgery?

After surgery, useful questions include:

1. What lymph-node dissection was actually performed?
2. Was it D1 or D2?
3. How many lymph nodes were examined?
4. How many were positive?
5. What is the final pathological stage?
6. Were the surgical margins clear?
7. Were the spleen or pancreas removed?
8. If an organ was removed, why was that necessary?
9. Does the extent of lymph-node dissection affect my next treatment?
10. What is the recommended next step?

# 42. Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1 — “D2 means twice as many nodes as D1.”

**Fact:** D2 is a defined broader anatomical field, not a numerical multiplier.

### Myth 2 — “D2 means at least 16 lymph nodes.”

**Fact:** ≥16 is an examination goal; D2 is defined anatomically.

### Myth 3 — “D2 always gives better overall survival.”

**Fact:** The evidence is mixed and context-dependent.

### Myth 4 — “D2 is always more dangerous.”

**Fact:** Modern D2 outcomes depend strongly on surgical technique, organ preservation, center experience, and perioperative care.

### Myth 5 — “D2 requires spleen removal.”

**Fact:** Routine splenectomy is not part of D2.

### Myth 6 — “D2 requires pancreas removal.”

**Fact:** Routine prophylactic pancreatectomy is not recommended.

### Myth 7 — “D2 is only used in Japan or Korea.”

**Fact:** D2 is also recommended in Western guideline frameworks, although it is not universally required in Western practice.

### Myth 8 — “If I had D2, I do not need further treatment.”

**Fact:** Postoperative treatment depends on the entire clinical and pathological context.

### Myth 9 — “A high lymph-node count proves the surgery was D2.”

**Fact:** Node count alone cannot establish the anatomical extent.

### Myth 10 — “Negative lymph nodes mean the cancer is cured.”

**Fact:** Nodal status is one component of staging and prognosis.

### Myth 11 — “D2 is the same as D2+.”

**Fact:** D2+ is an extension beyond standard D2.

### Myth 12 — “Laparoscopic D2 is a different type of lymphadenectomy.”

**Fact:** Laparoscopic or robotic describes the access approach; D2 describes the lymph-node extent.

## F. Detailed Clinical Knowledge

# 44. D2 as an Anatomical Concept

The defining idea of D2 is the extent of the regional lymph-node field.

The operation is broader than D1 because it adds nodal territories associated with major arteries around the stomach and celiac axis.

This means that D2 should be understood as an anatomical operation rather than a numerical one.

A pathology report may contain a large number of lymph nodes without documenting the complete anatomical extent.

Conversely, the anatomical intention of D2 cannot be reconstructed safely from a node count alone.

The operative report and pathology report answer different questions.

# 45. Why the Arteries Matter in the D2 Definition

The NCCN definition uses named arteries to describe the additional D2 field.

These vessels are clinically useful landmarks because regional gastric lymphatic drainage follows predictable anatomical pathways.

The package therefore uses:

- left gastric artery;
- common hepatic artery;
- celiac artery;
- splenic artery

as conceptual anatomical anchors.

This does not mean the patient needs to understand surgical vessel anatomy in detail.

The patient-facing message is simply:

> D2 removes a broader regional lymph-node field around the stomach and the major vessels supplying that region.

# 46. D2 and the Left Gastric Artery

The left gastric artery is one of the named structures in the NCCN D2 definition.

The associated nodal field is outside the most limited perigastric D1 field.

For the patient, the important implication is that D2 extends the lymph-node assessment toward more central regional drainage pathways.

The package does not describe vessel exposure or lymph-node dissection technique.

# 47. D2 and the Common Hepatic Artery

The common hepatic artery is another named anatomical boundary of the D2 field.

Its inclusion reflects the broader regional nodal territory assessed during D2.

The package does not instruct the surgeon how to expose, skeletonize, or dissect along the artery.

Those are operative-technique questions and are deliberately outside scope.

# 48. D2 and the Celiac Artery

The celiac artery is a central vascular landmark of the upper abdominal regional lymphatic field.

Its inclusion in the NCCN definition helps distinguish D2 from the more limited D1 operation.

The clinical significance is that D2 extends the nodal dissection beyond the immediate perigastric field.

# 49. D2 and the Splenic Artery

The splenic artery is also included in the NCCN D2 definition.

This does not mean that the spleen itself is routinely removed.

The lymph-node field and organ resection are separate concepts.

This distinction is especially important because historical D2 surgery sometimes included splenectomy or pancreatectomy.

Modern D2 principles emphasize avoiding unnecessary organ removal.

# 50. Why D2 Is More Technically Demanding

D2 requires dissection in a broader anatomical field.

This creates additional demands on:

- anatomical knowledge;
- operative judgment;
- technical skill;
- lymphadenectomy experience;
- management of adjacent structures;
- postoperative care.

The current NCCN source explicitly describes D2 as requiring significant training and expertise.

This is why D2 is not simply a larger version of a routine lymph-node operation.

# 51. What “Experienced Center” Means

The source materials support the concept of an experienced center.

For patients, this can mean a hospital and surgical team that regularly manages gastric cancer and performs the relevant lymphadenectomy.

The package does not define a universal numerical threshold for “high volume.”

Patients should therefore ask the team about actual experience rather than relying on a single numerical label.

# 52. D2 and High-Volume Care

The evidence summarized by NCCN suggests that D2 outcomes can be better when the procedure is performed in centers with sufficient experience.

This is especially relevant when comparing modern D2 with historical Western trial results.

A historical complication rate should not be interpreted as the inevitable outcome of every modern D2 operation.

At the same time, center experience does not eliminate all surgical risk.

# 53. D2 and Surgical Learning Curve

The concept of a learning curve is relevant because D2 is technically demanding.

However, the supplied sources do not provide a validated patient-facing numerical learning-curve threshold.

Therefore the package does not invent one.

The safe patient-facing message is:

> D2 should be performed by teams with appropriate training and experience.

# 54. D2 and Open Surgery

D2 can be performed through an open surgical approach.

The access route does not define D2.

A patient who undergoes open surgery can have D1, D1+, D2, or another extent depending on the operation.

Therefore:

**open**

does not automatically mean

**D2**.

# 55. D2 and Laparoscopic Surgery

D2 can also be performed through selected minimally invasive approaches.

The NCCN source summarizes randomized evidence for laparoscopic distal gastrectomy with D2 lymphadenectomy in locally advanced gastric cancer.

In those selected trials, oncologic outcomes were comparable to open surgery.

The package does not convert those trial results into a recommendation that every patient should receive laparoscopic D2.

# 56. D2 and Robotic Surgery

Robotic surgery is another access approach.

The D2 designation still refers to the lymph-node extent rather than the machine or access technique.

The supplied source supports selected minimally invasive approaches when the surgeon has appropriate experience.

No detailed robotic technique is included.

# 57. D2 and Patient Selection

D2 is a treatment-context concept.

Patient selection depends on:

- resectability;
- disease extent;
- suspected nodal involvement;
- planned gastric resection;
- medical fitness;
- applicable guideline;
- surgical expertise.

The package intentionally stops short of turning these factors into an individualized yes/no algorithm.

# 58. D2 and T Category

Tumor T category describes depth/local extent of the primary tumor.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline links broader lymphadenectomy with more advanced T categories.

The NCCN framework similarly places D2 within curative resection for appropriate resectable disease.

The package uses T category only to explain clinical context.

It does not replace a formal staging package.

# 59. D2 and N Category

N category describes regional lymph-node involvement.

The rationale for a thorough lymphadenectomy includes obtaining regional lymph-node tissue for pathological assessment.

However:

**N category**

and

**D2**

are not synonyms.

N category describes disease involvement.

D2 describes surgical extent.

# 60. D2 and M Category

M category concerns distant metastasis.

D2 is a regional surgical procedure.

Therefore distant metastatic disease may change the overall treatment strategy even if a patient has a technically resectable primary lesion.

The package does not provide metastatic-disease treatment algorithms.

# 61. D2 and Resectability

D2 belongs most naturally within the curative/resectable surgical pathway.

If disease is unresectable or metastatic, the treatment objective may shift toward systemic or palliative management.

The NCCN source specifically distinguishes curative resection from palliation in unresectable disease.

Therefore the package does not present D2 as an operation that should be performed whenever technically possible.

# 62. D2 and Tumor-Free Margins

A successful D2 does not by itself establish an R0 resection.

R0/R1/R2 status concerns the completeness of the primary tumor resection and margins.

D2 concerns the regional lymph-node field.

Both are important but distinct.

# 63. D2 and Lymph-Node Yield

Lymph-node yield is a pathology-related measurement.

It may be influenced by:

- the anatomical extent of surgery;
- specimen handling;
- pathology examination.

Therefore node yield is useful but cannot independently reconstruct the operation.

# 64. D2 and Positive Nodes

Positive nodes are nodes containing metastatic cancer.

A positive-node result does not mean the surgeon performed an inadequate lymphadenectomy.

It means that regional metastatic disease was identified.

The number and distribution of positive nodes contribute to staging and subsequent treatment planning.

# 65. D2 and Negative Nodes

Negative nodes are nodes in which no metastatic cancer was identified.

Negative nodes are clinically reassuring but do not prove that microscopic disease is absent elsewhere.

The package therefore avoids saying that D2 guarantees cure.

# 66. D2 and Staging Accuracy

A broader regional dissection can provide more tissue for pathological evaluation.

This can improve the confidence of nodal staging when the specimen is adequately assessed.

However, staging accuracy is not the only purpose of D2.

The operation also has an oncologic regional-control rationale.

# 67. D2 and Regional Disease Control

The long-term Dutch trial follow-up summarized by NCCN found lower local and regional recurrence with D2.

This supports the concept that broader regional lymphadenectomy may improve regional disease control.

It does not establish that every patient will experience the same benefit.

# 68. D2 and Gastric-Cancer-Specific Mortality

The same long-term evidence showed fewer gastric-cancer-related deaths after D2.

This finding is important because it indicates a possible disease-specific benefit even when overall survival was similar.

The package should preserve the distinction between:

- disease-specific mortality;
- overall mortality;
- recurrence;
- postoperative complications.

# 69. D2 and Overall Survival

Overall survival is affected by all causes of death.

The randomized Western evidence summarized by NCCN did not establish a universal OS advantage for D2.

Therefore a patient should not be told:

> “D2 has been proven to make everyone live longer.”

That is not supported by the supplied evidence.

# 70. D2 and Historical Western Trials

The Dutch and British trials are important because they shaped Western understanding of D1 versus D2.

The Dutch trial reported greater morbidity and mortality with D2 in the historical setting.

The British trial did not demonstrate a survival advantage.

These trials remain important evidence, but they must be interpreted in light of changes in surgical quality and organ-preservation practices.

# 71. D2 and the Italian Evidence

The Italian randomized phase II experience is important because it evaluated D1 versus D2 in a setting emphasizing more modern surgical practice.

The NCCN summary reports no significant OS difference between D1 and D2 in that trial.

The same evidence contributes to the understanding that pancreas-preserving D2 can have a more favorable safety profile than historical organ-resecting D2.

# 72. Why Organ Preservation Changes the Evidence Interpretation

Historical D2 may have included routine or frequent spleen and pancreas resection.

Those procedures can add substantial morbidity.

Modern D2 avoids prophylactic removal when there is no disease involvement.

Therefore:

**historical D2**

and

**modern organ-preserving D2**

should not be treated as identical operations when discussing risk.

# 73. D2 and Splenic Hilar Disease

The NCCN source allows splenectomy when the spleen is involved or extensive hilar adenopathy is present.

This is different from prophylactic splenectomy.

The package therefore teaches:

> spleen removal is a disease-driven surgical decision, not a defining feature of D2.

# 74. D2 and Pancreatic Involvement

Pancreatic resection may be required when the tumor directly involves the pancreas and an en-bloc oncologic resection is appropriate.

That is different from prophylactic pancreatectomy.

The package therefore avoids presenting pancreatic resection as part of routine D2.

# 75. D2 and Palliative Intent

Palliative surgery has a different objective.

When disease cannot be cured surgically, the operation may focus on:

- bleeding;
- obstruction;
- other symptoms.

NCCN states that palliative gastric resection does not need to include lymph-node dissection.

Therefore D2 should not be automatically applied to palliative operations.

# 76. D2 and Postoperative Treatment

The extent of lymphadenectomy can affect the postoperative treatment framework.

For example, the current NCCN source distinguishes patients who underwent less-than-D2 dissection from those who underwent D2 when discussing postoperative therapy.

This is a treatment interface, not a treatment algorithm owned by PP-0201.

The package therefore says:

> **D2 can be clinically relevant to what happens after surgery.**

It does not say:

> **D2 determines exactly which treatment you should receive.**

# 77. D2 and Multidisciplinary Review

The postoperative plan may depend on:

- pathology;
- margins;
- nodal status;
- previous systemic therapy;
- lymphadenectomy extent;
- overall patient fitness.

These factors are usually considered together.

The package therefore supports multidisciplinary communication rather than isolated surgical decision-making.

# 78. D2 and Patient Communication

A useful patient conversation should separate four questions:

1. What operation was performed?
2. What lymph-node extent was performed?
3. What did pathology find?
4. What does the final result mean for subsequent treatment?

This prevents the common error of using “D2” as if it were a complete description of the patient's cancer status.

# 79. D2 and the Meaning of “More Extensive”

“More extensive” refers primarily to the anatomical field.

It does not automatically mean:

- more cancer;
- more positive nodes;
- better outcome;
- worse outcome;
- more organs removed.

This terminology should be used carefully in patient-facing communication.

# 80. D2 and Quality Versus Quantity

A high-quality lymphadenectomy is not simply the one that produces the largest possible node count.

Quality means that the intended anatomical field is appropriately addressed, unnecessary organ removal is avoided, and the specimen is appropriately examined.

This is why D2 should not be reduced to a node-count competition.

# 81. D2 and the Pathology Report

Patients may see phrases such as:

- “lymph nodes examined”;
- “lymph nodes involved”;
- “regional lymph nodes”;
- “N category.”

These findings should be interpreted together with the operative report.

A pathology report alone may not tell the patient whether the intended operation was D1 or D2.

# 82. D2 and the Operative Report

The operative report is the most direct source for the surgeon's description of the procedure.

A patient who wants to know whether D2 was performed should ask the treating team to explain the operative documentation.

The package does not infer D2 from the pathology node count.

# 83. D2 and Follow-Up

Follow-up after gastric-cancer surgery is a separate clinical package.

D2 does not eliminate the need for follow-up.

The exact surveillance strategy depends on the patient's overall disease and treatment history.

PP-0201 does not prescribe surveillance intervals.

# 84. D2 and Recurrence

D2 may reduce regional recurrence in some evidence settings.

It cannot eliminate:

- distant recurrence;
- peritoneal recurrence;
- local recurrence;
- recurrence from residual microscopic disease.

Therefore D2 is not a guarantee against recurrence.

# 85. D2 and Patient Expectations

Patients sometimes hear “extended lymph-node dissection” and assume that more surgery must always mean more benefit.

The evidence does not justify this simplification.

The more accurate concept is:

> **D2 is a deliberately defined oncologic extent that can provide broader regional control and staging, but it also requires more technical expertise and has a context-dependent benefit-risk balance.**

# 86. D2 High-Value Summary

If a patient remembers only six points:

1. D2 is a broader lymph-node field than D1.
2. It is defined anatomically, not by a fixed node count.
3. It is most relevant to selected curative/resectable gastric-cancer surgery.
4. Its benefits and risks depend strongly on surgical quality and experience.
5. It does not automatically require spleen or pancreas removal.
6. The final meaning of D2 must be interpreted together with pathology and the overall treatment plan.

## G. Runtime Answer Patterns

### Question: “What is D2?”

Start with the anatomical definition.

### Question: “How is D2 different from D1?”

Explain that D2 includes the D1 field plus additional regional nodal territories.

### Question: “Does D2 mean 16 lymph nodes?”

Correct the misconception immediately.

### Question: “Does D2 mean my spleen will be removed?”

Explain that routine splenectomy is not part of modern D2.

### Question: “Is D2 better than D1?”

Explain that evidence is nuanced and depends on recurrence, disease-specific mortality, OS, surgical quality, and center experience.

### Question: “Why was D2 chosen for me?”

Do not infer.

Explain that the decision depends on disease extent, nodal status, resectability, guideline framework, and surgical expertise.

### Question: “I had 25 lymph nodes removed. Was it D2?”

Do not infer.

Explain that node count does not establish anatomical extent.

### Question: “Can D2 be laparoscopic?”

Explain that selected minimally invasive D2 is possible in experienced settings, but suitability is patient- and center-dependent.

### Question: “Will D2 mean I need chemotherapy?”

Do not answer from PP-0201 alone.

Explain that postoperative therapy depends on pathology, prior treatment, margins, nodal status, lymphadenectomy extent, and the applicable treatment guideline.

## I. Final CKO Key Message

> **D2 lymphadenectomy is a defined, broader regional lymph-node dissection beyond D1. Its clinical value depends on appropriate disease selection, surgical expertise, center experience, and modern organ-preserving practice. D2 is not defined by a fixed number of lymph nodes and does not automatically require removal of the spleen or pancreas.**
