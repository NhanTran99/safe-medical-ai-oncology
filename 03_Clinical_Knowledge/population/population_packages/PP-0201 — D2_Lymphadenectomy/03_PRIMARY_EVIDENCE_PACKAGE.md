# 03_PRIMARY_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE — PP-0201 D2 Lymphadenectomy

## Clinical Question

> What is D2 lymphadenectomy in gastric cancer, what additional lymph-node territory does it include beyond D1, when is it considered, why does surgical expertise matter, and what should patients understand about its benefits, risks, and limitations?

## Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded patient-facing explanation of D2 lymphadenectomy that is more specific than the general lymphadenectomy package and the D1 package, while stopping before operative technique, D2+ surgery, sentinel-node methodology, and treatment-selection algorithms.

## Primary Evidence Sources

### Source 1 — NCCN Guidelines Version 2.2026, Gastric Cancer

The current NCCN source is the primary anchor for the D2 definition and clinical evidence.

It defines D2 as D1 plus lymph nodes along:

- left gastric artery;
- common hepatic artery;
- celiac artery;
- splenic artery.

It states that D2 requires significant training and expertise and should be performed in experienced centers.

It describes D2 as standard for curable gastric cancer in East Asia and recommended but not universally required in Western practice.

It summarizes the Dutch and British randomized evidence, the Italian randomized phase II evidence, organ-preservation evidence, and minimally invasive D2 evidence.

It also separates D2 from the ≥16-node examination goal and from routine organ removal.

### Source 2 — Vietnamese gastric-cancer diagnosis and treatment guideline

The supplied Vietnamese source supports:

- D1/D1+/D2 station-based classification;
- D2 as D1 plus specified additional stations;
- clinical contexts in which D2 is considered;
- discussion of group 10/11 and splenic involvement;
- broader D2+ concepts.

The station framework is retained as source-attributed terminology.

## Detailed Evidence Notes

### 1. Anatomical definition

The current NCCN definition is the primary anchor:

**D2 = D1 + nodal dissection along left gastric, common hepatic, celiac, and splenic arteries.**

This is an anatomical definition.

### 2. Station terminology

The Vietnamese guideline provides a station-based framework.

The package retains this terminology with attribution.

It does not manufacture a cross-guideline station map.

### 3. Clinical setting

NCCN places D2 within curable gastric-cancer surgery and describes regional differences in practice.

The Vietnamese guideline provides more explicit stage-linked context.

These are presented as source-specific frameworks, not as an individualized algorithm.

### 4. Comparative D1/D2 evidence

NCCN summarizes the Dutch trial:

- higher postoperative morbidity and mortality with D2 in the historical trial;
- no significant OS difference;
- lower local and regional recurrence and fewer gastric-cancer-related deaths on long-term follow-up.

The British Cooperative trial did not demonstrate an OS benefit.

The Italian phase II trial did not show a significant OS difference and demonstrated the importance of pancreas-preserving approaches.

### 5. Organ preservation

The NCCN source reports increased morbidity/mortality associated with historical splenectomy and pancreatectomy and supports modern organ-preserving D2.

This is central to safe patient interpretation.

### 6. Surgical expertise

NCCN explicitly requires substantial training and expertise for D2.

This is treated as a core clinical characteristic, not a minor technical note.

### 7. Minimally invasive D2

NCCN summarizes randomized evidence supporting comparable outcomes of laparoscopic versus open D2 distal gastrectomy in selected locally advanced gastric-cancer patients.

The package therefore includes minimally invasive D2 as supporting context.

### 8. Node count

NCCN gives a goal of examining at least 16 lymph nodes in the localized resectable surgical framework.

This is deliberately kept separate from D2's anatomical definition.

### 9. Palliative context

NCCN states that palliative gastric resection does not need to include lymph-node dissection.

Therefore D2 is not a universal feature of all gastric-cancer operations.

## Clinical Claims Summary

### Established / guideline-supported

- D2 is a defined broader lymphadenectomy than D1.
- D2 includes the specified additional nodal territories in the NCCN definition.
- D2 requires experienced surgical teams.
- D2 is standard for curable gastric cancer in East Asian practice.
- D2 is recommended but not universally required in Western practice.
- Routine splenectomy and pancreatectomy are not part of modern D2 unless clinically indicated.
- ≥16 is a node-examination goal, not the D2 definition.

### Supported but context-dependent

- D2 may reduce regional recurrence.
- D2 may influence gastric-cancer-specific outcomes.
- D2 can be performed minimally invasively in selected experienced settings.
- D2 status can influence postoperative treatment planning.

### Not established

- D2 always improves OS.
- D2 is always superior to D1.
- D2 is always safer or always more dangerous.
- Node count alone identifies D2.
- D2 automatically requires organ removal.

## Evidence Consistency Review

The package deliberately preserves several distinctions:

### D2 anatomy versus node count

D2 = anatomical extent.

Node count = pathology quantity.

### Historical versus modern D2

Historical morbidity should not be automatically generalized to modern organ-preserving D2.

### Regional practice versus universal rule

East Asian standardization and Western recommendations are reported as source-specific practice frameworks.

### Comparative efficacy versus individualized treatment

Evidence comparing D1 and D2 does not become an individual patient's surgical prescription.

## Extended Evidence Analysis

### Source-by-Source Interpretation

#### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

NCCN is the principal source for the D2 definition.

It directly describes D2 as D1 plus nodal dissection along four named arterial territories.

It also provides the strongest evidence framework for:

- East Asian versus Western practice;
- D1/D2 randomized evidence;
- surgical expertise;
- organ preservation;
- minimally invasive D2;
- node-examination goals;
- palliative context;
- postoperative-treatment interface.

#### Vietnamese Gastric-Cancer Guideline

The Vietnamese source adds the station-based framework and stage-related clinical context.

It is particularly valuable for explaining how D1, D1+, and D2 are expressed in a Vietnamese clinical framework.

It is not used to replace the NCCN anatomical definition.

#### NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ

NCI provides independent clinical context around surgery, regional lymphadenectomy, and gastric-cancer treatment.

#### ACS Stomach Cancer

ACS is useful for patient-facing language and experience-related questions.

Its simplified descriptions should not override the more precise anatomical definition of D2 used by NCCN.

### Evidence Reconciliation

#### D2 versus ≥16 nodes

The correct interpretation is:

**D2 = anatomical extent**

**≥16 = examination goal**

This distinction is preserved across all artifacts.

#### Historical versus modern D2

Historical morbidity was influenced by more frequent spleen/pancreas resection.

Modern organ-preserving D2 is a different clinical context.

#### East versus West

The package preserves regional guideline differences without labeling one region's practice as universally correct.

### Comparative Evidence Detail

#### Dutch trial

The NCCN summary reports:

- D2 had greater postoperative morbidity in the historical trial;
- D2 had greater postoperative mortality;
- no significant overall-survival difference;
- long-term lower local recurrence;
- lower regional recurrence;
- fewer gastric-cancer-related deaths;
- similar overall survival.

Interpretation:

The operation may improve regional disease control without producing a simple OS advantage.

#### British Cooperative trial

The trial did not demonstrate a survival benefit for D2 over D1.

Interpretation:

The Western randomized evidence does not support universal OS superiority.

#### Italian randomized phase II evidence

The Italian experience did not show a significant OS difference.

Its importance for this package is also methodological:

pancreas-preserving D2 can reduce the morbidity associated with historical organ-resecting approaches.

### Organ Preservation Evidence

NCCN states that routine or prophylactic pancreatectomy is not recommended.

Splenectomy is reserved for disease-specific circumstances such as splenic involvement or extensive hilar adenopathy.

This evidence supports a core patient-facing distinction:

> D2 is a lymph-node extent, not a mandate for adjacent-organ removal.

### Minimally Invasive Evidence

The NCCN source summarizes randomized evidence from East Asian trials of laparoscopic versus open distal gastrectomy with D2 lymphadenectomy in locally advanced gastric cancer.

The evidence supports comparable oncologic outcomes in selected experienced settings.

This supports the statement:

> D2 can be performed through selected minimally invasive approaches.

It does not support:

> every patient should undergo laparoscopic or robotic D2.

### Evidence on Surgical Experience

The NCCN source explicitly links D2 with significant training and experience.

The patient-facing evidence translation is therefore:

> experience is part of the quality and safety context of D2.

No unsupported numerical threshold is introduced.

### Evidence on Node Examination

NCCN gives a goal of examining at least 16 lymph nodes in the localized resectable surgical framework.

This is used to explain staging quality.

It is not used as the definition of D2.

### Evidence on Palliative Surgery

NCCN states that gastric resection for palliation in incurable disease does not need to include lymph-node dissection.

This supports the boundary:

> D2 is primarily a curative/resectable surgical concept.

### Evidence on Postoperative Treatment

NCCN states that postoperative management depends partly on the extent of lymph-node dissection.

This establishes a clinically important interface.

However, because treatment selection is owned by downstream PPs, this package does not convert that interface into a treatment algorithm.

## Evidence-Based Patient Translation

### Question

“Why would my surgeon choose D2?”

### Safe answer model

D2 is a broader regional lymph-node dissection that may be selected when the cancer's extent and the applicable surgical framework support it. The decision also depends on the surgeon's and center's experience.

### Question

“Does D2 mean more cancer?”

### Safe answer model

No. D2 describes the extent of lymph-node surgery, not how much cancer a patient has.

### Question

“Does D2 mean the spleen is removed?”

### Safe answer model

No. Routine splenectomy is not part of modern D2.

### Question

“Is D2 better?”

### Safe answer model

The evidence is nuanced. D2 may reduce regional recurrence and disease-specific mortality in some settings, but randomized Western studies did not establish a universal overall-survival advantage.
