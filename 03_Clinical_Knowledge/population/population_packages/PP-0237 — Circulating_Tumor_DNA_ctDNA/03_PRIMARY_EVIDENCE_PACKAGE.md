# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), and what can it tell us about gastric cancer?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA)** as tumor-derived DNA that can be detected in the bloodstream and analyzed to provide molecular information about a cancer.

The package explains:

- what ctDNA is;
- how it relates to cell-free DNA and liquid biopsy;
- what genomic information it may provide;
- when blood-based ctDNA testing may be considered in gastric cancer;
- how ctDNA may provide information about tumor evolution and changing treatment-response profiles;
- why serial testing may provide longitudinal molecular information;
- why ctDNA does not universally replace tissue biopsy, pathology, imaging, or comprehensive clinical assessment;
- why a negative result must be interpreted cautiously.

The package deliberately avoids detailed assay methodology, variant interpretation, individualized treatment decisions, and disease-specific ctDNA monitoring algorithms.

# Key Evidence Details

## 1. NCCN Gastric Cancer — Direct Guideline Evidence

The NCCN Gastric Cancer guideline describes blood-based evaluation of circulating tumor DNA as a form of liquid biopsy.

The guideline states that detection of mutations, alterations, or gene fusions in DNA shed from gastric carcinomas can identify potentially targetable alterations or indicate evolution of clones with altered treatment-response profiles.

It also identifies selected clinical situations in which validated blood-based comprehensive genomic profiling may be considered, particularly when tissue is limited or when a patient with advanced/metastatic gastric cancer cannot undergo a traditional biopsy.

The guideline explicitly warns that a negative result should be interpreted with caution because it does not exclude the presence of a tumor.

This is the central evidence base for PP-0237 

---

## 2. Evidence Boundary for Clinical Utility

The evidence supports the following hierarchy:

**ctDNA detection**

→ molecular alterations

→ potential clinical relevance

→ possible information about tumor evolution

The evidence does **not** justify presenting every possible ctDNA application as a universal standard-of-care workflow.

Therefore:

### Guideline-supported / established educational content

- blood-based ctDNA genomic assessment;
- detection of genomic alterations;
- selected use when tissue is limited/unavailable;
- potential identification of targetable alterations;
- information about tumor-clone evolution;
- negative-result limitation.

### Context-dependent / emerging content

- serial molecular monitoring;
- treatment-response monitoring;
- resistance monitoring;
- MRD;
- recurrence detection.

The latter group is intentionally described conceptually and not converted into disease-specific algorithms.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- ctDNA is tumor-derived DNA that can be detected in blood.
- ctDNA is part of the broader liquid-biopsy concept.
- ctDNA can provide molecular information about a tumor.
- In gastric cancer, ctDNA-based genomic testing can detect mutations, other genomic alterations, and gene fusions.
- Such findings may identify potentially targetable alterations.
- ctDNA findings may also provide information about molecular evolution of tumor clones and altered treatment-response profiles.
- Blood-based testing may be particularly useful when adequate tissue is unavailable or traditional biopsy cannot be performed in selected advanced/metastatic cases.
- A negative ctDNA/liquid-biopsy result does not exclude the presence of a tumor.
- ctDNA does not universally replace tissue-based pathology, imaging, or clinical assessment.
- The meaning of ctDNA findings depends on the clinical context and appropriate interpretation.

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the core sources used for this package.

The strongest direct evidence is the NCCN Gastric Cancer guideline, which provides a specific clinical framework for blood-based ctDNA testing in gastric cancer.

The locked PP-0236 Discussion Batch provides the architectural boundary that:

**Liquid Biopsy → ctDNA → specialized downstream clinical applications**

The evidence supports presenting ctDNA as a clinically relevant molecular testing approach while maintaining clear boundaries around:

- diagnostic replacement;
- treatment selection;
- monitoring algorithms;
- MRD;
- recurrence surveillance.

The package therefore distinguishes **guideline-supported clinical utility** from **context-dependent/emerging applications**.

---

# Evidence Gaps

The core evidence used for this Population Package does not justify detailed educational claims regarding:

- universal ctDNA screening;
- universal postoperative MRD testing;
- universal recurrence surveillance;
- disease-specific ctDNA thresholds;
- universal treatment-response thresholds;
- universal treatment-switch criteria;
- standardized serial-sampling schedules;
- assay-specific sensitivity/specificity values;
- comprehensive technical comparison of ctDNA platforms.

These areas are intentionally outside the package.
