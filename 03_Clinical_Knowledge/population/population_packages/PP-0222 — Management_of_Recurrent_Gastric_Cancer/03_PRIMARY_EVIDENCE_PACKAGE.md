## Clinical Question

> **After gastric cancer recurrence has been established, what clinical factors determine the overall management strategy, and how are local, systemic, supportive and palliative approaches integrated without duplicating modality-specific Population Packages?**

## Educational Intent

This Evidence Package supports a patient-facing but clinically governed management framework.

It is not intended to function as a regimen compendium.

The evidence is organized around:

1. recurrence-management pathway;
2. treatment intent;
3. recurrence pattern;
4. resectability/medical fitness;
5. prior treatment;
6. performance status;
7. biomarker-informed treatment selection;
8. systemic/local treatment interface;
9. clinical trials;
10. reassessment;
11. supportive/palliative care;
12. shared decision-making;
13. downstream package ownership.

## Scope

### Included

- Management after established recurrence.

## Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

### Evidence Note 1 — Recurrence is a management state, not merely a surveillance finding

The project NCCN v2.2026 source identifies a specific recurrence pathway and separately identifies palliative management for unresectable locally advanced, locally recurrent or metastatic disease.

This supports the architectural decision that PP-0222 should begin after recurrence has been established.

### Evidence Note 2 — Isolated locoregional recurrence

The recurrence pathway distinguishes isolated locoregional recurrence from more extensive disease and provides a route in which surgery can be considered for selected medically fit patients with resectable disease.

This is the strongest direct basis for giving selected local treatment a core place in PP-0222.

The package does not infer that all locoregional recurrence is operable.

### Evidence Note 3 — Medical fitness

The guideline framework separates disease anatomy from patient suitability.

Therefore resectability alone is not enough to establish a management recommendation.

Medical fitness, performance status and other health factors remain relevant.

### Evidence Note 4 — Unresectable recurrence

NCCN provides a palliative-management pathway for unresectable locally advanced, locally recurrent or metastatic disease.

This supports a management framework in which systemic treatment, selected chemoradiation, clinical trials and supportive care can be considered according to clinical context.

### Evidence Note 5 — Subsequent treatment

The supplied NCCN systemic-therapy source explicitly states that second-line or subsequent therapy is dependent on prior therapy and performance status.

This supports the decision to make previous treatment a core management variable without importing a detailed regimen list into PP-0222.

### Evidence Note 6 — Biomarker-informed treatment

The supplied NCCN materials include biomarker-linked options in advanced/recurrent disease, including HER2, MSI-H/dMMR, PD-L1 and CLDN18.2-related pathways.

Therefore PP-0222 should explain the principle that tumor biology may change treatment options.

The package should not duplicate the detailed therapy-specific packages.

### Evidence Note 7 — Chemoradiation

The NCCN recurrence/palliative pathway allows selected chemoradiation in locally unresectable disease under appropriate circumstances, including prior-radiation considerations.

This supports strategic inclusion but not technical radiation content.

### Evidence Note 8 — Patient goals

NCI describes treatment planning as considering stage, overall health, preferences, goals, options and side effects.

This directly supports patient-facing shared decision-making content.

### Evidence Note 9 — Clinical trials

NCI identifies clinical trials among gastric-cancer treatment options.

Therefore clinical trials can be presented as a management pathway.

### Evidence Note 10 — Supportive care

The NCCN architecture includes palliative management and best supportive care.

Therefore supportive care is not an optional afterthought; it is part of the management framework.

## Evidence Limitations

### Limitation 1 — Heterogeneity of recurrence

Recurrent gastric cancer is not a single disease state.

The evidence and treatment options differ according to anatomical pattern and patient condition.

### Limitation 2 — Selected surgical evidence

The existence of a surgical pathway does not mean that surgery is appropriate for every recurrence.

### Limitation 3 — Rapidly changing systemic therapy

Specific drug options can change as guidelines are updated.

Therefore modality-specific PPs should carry regimen-level update responsibility.

### Limitation 4 — Individual treatment decisions require patient data

The project sources do not support an individualized recommendation without patient-specific imaging, pathology, biomarkers, prior therapy and health status.

## Evidence Gaps

The current package should flag rather than silently fill:

- evidence for uncommon recurrence patterns;
- rapidly evolving biomarker-directed treatment;
- novel local therapies;
- molecular monitoring strategies;
- optimal integration of emerging technologies into routine recurrence management.

These should be revisited when project source materials are updated.
