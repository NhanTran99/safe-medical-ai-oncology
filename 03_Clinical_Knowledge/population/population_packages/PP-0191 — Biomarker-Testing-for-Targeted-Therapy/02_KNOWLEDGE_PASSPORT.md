# 2. Clinical-Educational Role

PP-0191 is the **testing-strategy node** in the gastric-cancer precision-oncology knowledge graph.

Its purpose is to explain:

- why biomarker testing matters before targeted therapy is considered;
- how testing should be linked to the clinical question;
- the difference between biomarker-specific testing and broader molecular profiling;
- the role of specimen availability and assay scope;
- the high-level role of tissue and blood-based genomic testing;
- how testing results connect downstream to molecular-report interpretation and targeted-treatment selection.

It is not a treatment-selection package.

The intended conceptual chain is:

**Clinical question**

→ **Potential targeted-treatment opportunity**

→ **Relevant biomarker**

→ **Testing strategy**

→ **Test result**

→ **Molecular report**

→ **PP-0190 Biomarker-Directed Treatment Selection**

→ **Targeted-treatment Population Packages**

---

# 3. Primary Educational Outcomes

A successful reader should understand that:

1. Biomarker testing can make targeted-treatment opportunities detectable.
2. Testing should answer a clinically meaningful question.
3. A single biomarker assay and broader NGS profiling answer different questions.
4. HER2 and CLDN18.2 are important examples of targeted-treatment-relevant testing in gastric cancer.
5. Broader molecular profiling can identify selected less-common alterations.
6. NGS is not automatically required for every patient or every biomarker.
7. Tissue adequacy and assay scope can influence the usefulness of a test.
8. Blood-based genomic testing can have a role in selected circumstances.
9. Negative and inadequate results are not the same.
10. No actionable alteration is not equivalent to no molecular alteration.
11. A biomarker result does not automatically prescribe treatment.
12. Testing strategy, report literacy, and treatment selection are separate knowledge layers.

# 8. Evidence Classification

## Established / Guideline-Supported

- Biomarker testing may help predict response to selected targeted therapies.
- NCCN v2.2026 recommends HER2 assessment in the appropriate advanced/metastatic treatment context.
- NCCN v2.2026 identifies CLDN18.2 testing in appropriate advanced/metastatic disease.
- IHC/ISH/targeted gene PCR are preferred initial approaches for specified biomarkers in NCCN guidance.
- Validated NGS may be considered later in selected clinical circumstances when sufficient tumor tissue is available.
- NGS can assess multiple mutations and other molecular events simultaneously.
- Blood-based ctDNA is recognized as a form of liquid biopsy.
- Blood-based genomic testing may be considered in selected circumstances when tissue is limited or traditional biopsy is not feasible.
- Molecular testing depends on specimen and pre-analytic quality.
- Molecular findings require clinical context.
- Tumor testing may sometimes raise a question about possible inherited findings.

## Context-Dependent

- Whether broader NGS is useful for a specific patient.
- Whether a previous test already answers the current clinical question.
- Whether additional testing is useful after an inadequate or limited result.
- Whether tissue or blood-based testing is appropriate in a particular circumstance.
- Whether a molecular alteration is clinically actionable.
- Whether multidisciplinary molecular review is needed.

## Not Owned by This PP

- detailed variant interpretation;
- detailed biomarker thresholds;
- treatment algorithms;
- individualized testing;
- individualized treatment.

# 11. Patient-Facing Retrieval Safety

When retrieved for an individual patient, the package should not be interpreted as a personalized testing order.

The appropriate framing is:

> "These are the testing concepts and questions that may be relevant to discuss with the cancer care team."

The package should not generate:

- "You need this test";
- "You do not need this test";
- "You are eligible for this drug";
- "This result means you should receive treatment."
