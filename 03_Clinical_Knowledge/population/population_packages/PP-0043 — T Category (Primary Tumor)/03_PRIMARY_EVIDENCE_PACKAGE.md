# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does the T category mean in the TNM staging system?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the **T category (Primary Tumor)** within the TNM staging system, including what it represents, how it contributes to cancer staging, and why it is important for treatment planning and prognostic assessment.

This Population Package introduces the concept of the T category without discussing the detailed AJCC T classifications, stomach wall anatomy or stage-specific treatment recommendations.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of the T category
- Meaning of Primary Tumor
- Purpose of the T category
- Relationship between T category and tumor invasion
- General relationship between increasing T category and disease extent
- Contribution to TNM staging
- Contribution to treatment planning
- Contribution to prognostic assessment
- Integration with N and M categories

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The T category describes the **primary gastric tumor**.
- In gastric cancer, the T category mainly reflects **how deeply the tumor has invaded** rather than simply its size.
- Increasing T categories generally indicate more extensive local disease.
- The T category is one component of the TNM staging system.
- Doctors combine T with N and M categories to determine overall cancer stage.
- Clinical decisions are based on the complete clinical picture rather than the T category alone.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of the T category;
- its role within the TNM staging system;
- the importance of tumor invasion depth in gastric cancer;
- the contribution of the T category to prognosis and treatment planning;
- the need to interpret the T category together with N, M and other clinical information.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
