# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what the T category represents in the TNM staging system.
- Understand that T refers to the **primary tumor**.
- Understand that the T category mainly describes how deeply the tumor has grown into or beyond the stomach wall.
- Recognize that the T category is different from the N and M categories.
- Understand why the T category contributes to staging, treatment planning and prognosis.
- Understand that doctors never interpret the T category in isolation.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is the T Category?

## Patient Explanation

The **T category** is the first part of the **TNM staging system**.

The letter **T** stands for **Tumor**, meaning the **primary tumor**—the original cancer that started in the stomach.

Doctors use the T category to describe how the primary tumor has grown.

---

## Clinical Importance

The T category describes characteristics of the primary gastric tumor within the TNM staging system.

---

## Key Concepts

- T = Tumor.
- T refers to the primary tumor.
- T is one component of TNM staging.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does the T Category Describe?

## Patient Explanation

For gastric cancer, the T category mainly describes **how deeply the cancer has grown into or through the wall of the stomach**.

Although tumor size may sometimes be important, the T category is primarily based on **depth of invasion**, not simply how large the tumor appears.

This is different from some other types of cancer where size plays a larger role.

---

## Clinical Importance

In gastric cancer, the T category primarily reflects the depth of tumor invasion.

---

## Key Concepts

- T mainly reflects depth of invasion.
- T is not based only on tumor size.
- The stomach wall is an important reference for staging.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Does a Higher T Category Mean More Extensive Disease?

## Patient Explanation

In general, **higher T categories indicate that the primary tumor has grown more deeply or has spread further into nearby tissues.**

However, the T category describes **only the primary tumor**.

It does **not** tell doctors whether cancer has spread to lymph nodes or distant organs.

---

## Clinical Importance

Increasing T category generally reflects increasing local tumor extent.

---

## Key Concepts

- Higher T usually means deeper local invasion.
- T evaluates only the primary tumor.
- T does not describe lymph nodes or distant metastasis.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — How Is the T Category Used?

## Patient Explanation

Doctors combine the **T category** with:

- the **N category** (lymph nodes);
- the **M category** (distant metastasis);

to determine the overall stage of the cancer.

The T category also helps doctors discuss treatment options and estimate prognosis.

---

## Clinical Importance

The T category contributes to staging, treatment planning and prognostic assessment.

---

## Key Concepts

- T combines with N and M.
- T contributes to overall staging.
- T helps guide treatment planning.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Is the T Category Enough to Understand My Cancer?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Although the T category provides important information, doctors never rely on it alone.

They also consider:

- lymph node involvement;
- distant metastasis;
- pathology findings;
- tumor grade;
- biomarker results;
- your overall health;
- your response to treatment.

All of these factors are considered together.

---

## Clinical Importance

Modern gastric cancer management integrates T category with multiple additional clinical factors.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

A higher T category automatically tells me exactly what my prognosis will be.

**Fact**

The T category describes the primary tumor, but doctors always combine it with N category, M category, pathology findings and many other clinical factors before discussing prognosis or treatment.

---

# Key Messages

- T stands for **Primary Tumor**.
- The T category mainly describes how deeply the tumor has grown into or beyond the stomach wall.
- A higher T category generally reflects more extensive local tumor invasion.
- The T category is only one part of the TNM staging system.
- Doctors always interpret the T category together with N, M and other clinical information.
