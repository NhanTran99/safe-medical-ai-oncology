## Educational Objectives
Explain what pernicious anemia is, why it is associated with increased gastric-cancer risk, how it relates conceptually to gastric mucosal disease, and why gastric-cancer screening/follow-up may be considered in selected patients.

## Atomic Clinical Question
> What is pernicious anemia, why does it increase gastric-cancer risk, and what should a person with pernicious anemia understand about gastric-cancer risk and possible follow-up?

## Scope

- Association between pernicious anemia and increased gastric-cancer risk.

## Clinical Knowledge Blocks

### KB1 — What Is Pernicious Anemia?
Pernicious anemia is a condition associated with impaired vitamin B12 absorption because adequate intrinsic factor is unavailable.

### KB2 — Why Is Intrinsic Factor Important?
Intrinsic factor is produced by stomach cells and is needed for normal vitamin B12 absorption.

### KB3 — Gastric/Autoimmune Context
Pernicious anemia can occur in association with autoimmune conditions affecting the stomach. It can also occur after some types of stomach surgery.

### KB4 — Pernicious Anemia and Gastric Cancer
People with pernicious anemia have increased gastric-cancer risk.

### KB5 — Relationship to Gastric Mucosal Disease
Pernicious anemia may occur in the setting of chronic gastric mucosal disease, including atrophic changes. Detailed atrophic-gastritis content belongs to PP-0165.

### KB6 — Relationship to Intestinal Metaplasia
Pernicious anemia can coexist with gastric precursor changes such as intestinal metaplasia. Detailed IM content belongs to PP-0166.

### KB7 — Increased Risk Is Not Cancer
Pernicious anemia does not mean that a person has gastric cancer or will inevitably develop it.

### KB8 — Screening Implication
NCI identifies older people with pernicious anemia among higher-risk groups who may benefit from consideration of gastric-cancer screening/upper endoscopy.

### KB9 — Screening Is Not a Universal Schedule
The need and timing of screening depend on overall clinical context. This package does not establish a universal endoscopy interval.

### KB10 — Other Risk Factors
Family history, H. pylori, atrophic gastritis and other established risk factors may modify the overall gastric-cancer risk context. Detailed packages remain delegated.

### KB11 — Not Hereditary Gastric Cancer
Pernicious anemia is a medical risk condition and is not equivalent to HDGC or another germline gastric-cancer syndrome.

## Patient Explanation
> Pernicious anemia is a condition in which vitamin B12 cannot be absorbed normally because the body does not have enough intrinsic factor, a substance produced by cells in the stomach lining. It can be associated with autoimmune conditions affecting the stomach and can also occur after some stomach surgery.
>
> Pernicious anemia is important for stomach-cancer prevention because people with this condition have an increased risk of stomach cancer. This does not mean that a person has cancer or will definitely develop it.
>
> Because pernicious anemia can place a person in a higher-risk group, a clinician may consider whether gastric-cancer screening or upper endoscopy is appropriate, taking age and other risk factors into account.

## Clinical Importance
Pernicious anemia should not be treated as only a B12/hematology issue in the gastric-cancer knowledge graph; it is also a marker of increased gastric-cancer risk.

## Common Misconceptions
1. Pernicious anemia is just ordinary anemia — incorrect.
2. Pernicious anemia means gastric cancer is already present — incorrect.
3. Everyone with pernicious anemia will develop gastric cancer — incorrect.
4. Pernicious anemia is the same as atrophic gastritis — incorrect.
5. Pernicious anemia means hereditary gastric cancer — incorrect.
6. Everyone with pernicious anemia automatically needs annual endoscopy — not supported by the supplied sources.

## Key Messages
- Pernicious anemia is linked to impaired B12 absorption through loss/absence of adequate intrinsic factor.
- It is associated with increased gastric-cancer risk.
- Risk is increased, not inevitable.
- It can occur in the context of chronic/atrophic gastric disease.
- Selected patients may warrant consideration of gastric-cancer screening.
- Detailed screening, atrophic-gastritis and hereditary-risk questions belong to separate PPs.
