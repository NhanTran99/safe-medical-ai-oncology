## Clinical Question
> What is pernicious anemia, why does it increase gastric-cancer risk, and what should a person with pernicious anemia understand about gastric-cancer risk and possible follow-up?

## Educational Intent
Explain pernicious anemia as a clinically meaningful gastric-cancer risk condition without turning the package into a general B12-treatment, atrophic-gastritis, hereditary-cancer, or screening-protocol package.

## Scope

### Included
- Pernicious anemia and intrinsic factor/B12 absorption.
- Association with gastric-cancer risk.
- Autoimmune/atrophic gastric context at conceptual level.
- Relationship with intestinal metaplasia at conceptual level.
- Higher-risk screening implication.
- Upper-endoscopy concept.
- Risk versus inevitability.

### Excluded
- B12 treatment regimens.
- Detailed autoimmune-gastritis diagnosis.
- Detailed atrophic-gastritis management.
- Detailed IM surveillance.
- Universal endoscopy intervals.
- Hereditary testing.
- Gastric adenomas.
- Individual absolute-risk calculation.

## Primary Evidence Sources

### NCI — Stomach (Gastric) Cancer Prevention PDQ®
NCI identifies pernicious anemia among gastric-cancer precursor/risk conditions and describes older people with pernicious anemia as a higher-risk group.

### NCI — Stomach (Gastric) Cancer Screening PDQ®
NCI states that some higher-risk groups, including older people with chronic gastric atrophy or pernicious anemia, may benefit from screening with upper endoscopy.

### NCI — Causes of Stomach Cancer
NCI identifies pernicious anemia as a recognized gastric-cancer risk factor and explains its relationship to impaired vitamin B12 absorption.

### ACS — Stomach Cancer
ACS explains intrinsic factor, vitamin B12 absorption, autoimmune causes of pernicious anemia, and increased stomach-cancer risk.

### NCI — Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ®
NCI lists pernicious anemia among recognized gastric-cancer risk factors.

## Evidence Notes

### Risk
The evidence consistently supports **increased risk**, not inevitability.

### Mechanism
The supplied sources support the intrinsic-factor/B12 and gastric-disease relationship, but do not justify a single fully quantified causal pathway from pernicious anemia to cancer.

### Screening
The sources support considering screening in selected higher-risk patients, particularly older patients with pernicious anemia, but do not support inventing a universal interval.

## Clinical Claims Summary
1. Pernicious anemia is a B12-absorption disorder linked to intrinsic-factor deficiency/absence.
2. It is associated with increased gastric-cancer risk.
3. It can occur in autoimmune gastric disease and after some stomach surgery.
4. It can coexist with chronic gastric mucosal precursor changes.
5. Higher-risk patients may warrant consideration of gastric-cancer screening/upper endoscopy.
6. Risk is increased, not deterministic.

## Evidence Consistency Review
The NCI and ACS sources are directionally consistent: pernicious anemia is a recognized gastric-cancer risk condition. NCI screening materials provide the corresponding high-risk screening context.

No source-supported evidence was found that would justify converting this package into a universal screening schedule or individualized risk calculator.

## Evidence Gaps
- No validated individual absolute-risk calculator supplied.
- No universal endoscopy interval supplied.
- No detailed autoimmune-gastritis diagnostic algorithm supplied.
- No detailed B12 treatment protocol needed for this PP.
- No source basis for hereditary-risk interpretation from pernicious anemia alone.
