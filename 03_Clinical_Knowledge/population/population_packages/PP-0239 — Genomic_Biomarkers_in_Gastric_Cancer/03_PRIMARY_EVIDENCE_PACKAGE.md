# Clinical Question

> **What are genomic biomarkers in gastric cancer, and what can they tell us about the cancer?**

---

# Educational Intent

Explain the major clinically relevant genomic or genome-derived biomarkers in gastric cancer, what types of molecular changes they represent, why they may matter clinically, and how they differ from protein-expression biomarkers, germline hereditary-risk markers, and blood-based ctDNA.

The package is intentionally biomarker-centric rather than profiling-centric. PP-0113 explains the broader molecular tumor profiling concept; PP-0114 focuses on the genomic findings that may emerge from such testing.

# Primary Evidence Sources

## 1. NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026

NCCN provides the most direct disease-specific evidence for this package.

The guideline describes biomarker testing in gastric cancer and identifies clinically relevant biomarkers and molecular events, including HER2, MSI/MMR, PD-L1, CLDN18.2, TMB, NTRK, RET, and BRAF V600E. It distinguishes IHC, ISH, targeted PCR, and NGS approaches and describes NGS as capable of evaluating multiple genomic changes. It also identifies EBV as an emerging biomarker and does not recommend routine EBV testing for all gastric cancers.

## 2. ESMO/ASCO Global Curriculum in Medical Oncology Edition 2023

Provides broader molecular-oncology terminology and distinguishes different genomic alteration types, including mutations, copy-number abnormalities, gene fusions, and other molecular levels.

# Evidence Notes

## 1. HER2/ERBB2

HER2 is a useful boundary example because gastric-cancer HER2 status can involve both protein overexpression and gene amplification.

PP-0114 therefore treats **ERBB2 amplification** as the genomic dimension while avoiding a full HER2 testing/treatment package.

## 2. MSI/dMMR

MSI and MMR deficiency describe a molecular/genomic state, but different methods can be used to assess them. The package therefore explains MSI/dMMR at the biomarker level rather than presenting one assay as universally required.

## 3. TMB-H

TMB is a genome-derived measure of mutation burden. TMB-H can have treatment relevance in appropriate settings, but the package does not provide a technical calculation or universal threshold framework.

## 4. NTRK and RET

NTRK and RET fusions are representative gene-fusion biomarkers. They illustrate how a specific genomic event can have potential treatment relevance.

## 5. BRAF V600E

BRAF V600E illustrates a specific somatic mutation that can be clinically relevant in an appropriate context.

## 6. EBV

NCCN identifies EBV as an emerging biomarker and does not recommend routine testing for all gastric cancers. The package therefore deliberately separates EBV from the established core biomarker set.

## 7. Germline versus somatic

NCI Genetics of Gastric Cancer PDQ describes inherited susceptibility genes such as CDH1 in a hereditary cancer context. Those genes and testing approaches answer a different question from somatic tumor biomarkers and are therefore excluded from the core scope.

# Clinical Interpretation Model

A genomic biomarker should be understood as a sequence:

**Molecular finding**

↓

**Biomarker status**

↓

**Clinical interpretation**

↓

**Potential prognostic/predictive relevance**

↓

**Potential treatment relevance**

This does **not** mean:

> genomic finding → automatic treatment.

Clinical decisions also incorporate pathology, stage, prior therapy, patient factors, goals, and current evidence.

---

# Evidence on Clinical Roles

## Characterization

Genomic biomarkers can help describe molecular features of a tumor.

## Prognostic Role

Some biomarkers may provide information associated with disease outcome.

## Predictive Role

Some biomarkers may help predict benefit from particular therapies.

## Actionability

Some genomic alterations are associated with potentially targetable or biomarker-directed treatment options.

The final clinical interpretation remains context-dependent.

---

# Testing Methods — High-Level Evidence

Depending on the biomarker, molecular assessment may use:

- PCR;
- ISH;
- NGS;
- IHC for protein-expression dimensions.

The assay should be understood as a method for answering a particular biomarker question rather than as the definition of the biomarker itself.

---

# Genomic Versus Other Biomarker Classes

## Genomic / genome-derived

- ERBB2 amplification
- BRAF V600E
- NTRK fusion
- RET fusion
- MSI
- TMB

## Protein expression

- PD-L1
- CLDN18.2
- HER2 protein expression

## Germline

- inherited cancer-predisposition genes such as CDH1

## Blood-based tumor-derived DNA

- ctDNA

These categories answer different clinical questions and should not be conflated.

---

# Evidence Gaps

The current project Source Materials do not provide sufficient basis for a comprehensive catalog of all gastric-cancer genomic alterations.

Therefore, this package intentionally does **not** add research-oriented or biologically interesting genes merely for completeness.

The Source Materials also do not support detailed comparative performance claims for all molecular platforms, universal cost-effectiveness claims, or individualized molecular-report interpretation.

# Evidence Consistency Review

The primary sources are consistent in supporting the use of molecular biomarkers to characterize gastric cancer and inform selected clinical decisions.

NCCN provides disease-specific guidance and the clearest current biomarker framework. ESMO/ASCO provides complementary molecular-oncology terminology.

No major source conflict was identified that requires reconciliation within this package.
