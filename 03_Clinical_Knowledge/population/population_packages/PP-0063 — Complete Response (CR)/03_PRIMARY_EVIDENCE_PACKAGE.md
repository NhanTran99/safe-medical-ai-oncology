# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does Complete Response (CR) mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Complete Response (CR)**, including what the term means, why it is used as a standardized treatment response category, why it represents an excellent response to therapy, and why it should not be interpreted as proof that cancer has been permanently cured.

This Population Package introduces the concept of Complete Response without explaining RECIST technical response criteria, response algorithms, recurrence risk or post-treatment management.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of Complete Response
- Complete Response as a standardized RECIST response category
- Meaning of disappearance of detectable disease under standardized assessment
- Difference between Complete Response and cure
- Importance of continued follow-up
- Integration into comprehensive clinical assessment

---

## Excluded

- RECIST 1.1 technical criteria for Complete Response
- Target lesion criteria
- Non-target lesion criteria
- Lymph node criteria
- Partial Response (PR)
- Stable Disease (SD)
- Progressive Disease (PD)
- RECIST response algorithms
- Post-treatment management
- Cancer recurrence
- Prognostic significance

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Complete Response is one of the standardized RECIST response categories.
- It describes the disappearance of detectable disease according to standardized assessment methods.
- Complete Response reflects an excellent response to treatment.
- Complete Response should not be interpreted as a guarantee of permanent cure.
- Long-term follow-up remains an essential part of cancer care, even after Complete Response.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of Complete Response;
- its role as a standardized treatment response category;
- the distinction between treatment response and cure;
- the importance of continued surveillance after treatment.

Differences between references relate mainly to detailed RECIST technical response criteria and disease-specific follow-up recommendations, which are intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted RECIST 1.1 concepts without introducing technical response criteria.
