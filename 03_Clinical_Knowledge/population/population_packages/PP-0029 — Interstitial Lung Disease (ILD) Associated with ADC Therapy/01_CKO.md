# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what interstitial lung disease (ILD) means.
- Understand why ILD is an important safety concern with some antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).
- Recognize common warning symptoms.
- Understand why early reporting is essential.
- Know what to do if respiratory symptoms develop during treatment.
- Appreciate that ILD does not occur in every patient or with every ADC.
- Participate more confidently in safe cancer treatment.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)?

## Patient Explanation

Interstitial lung disease (ILD) is a term used for inflammation or injury affecting the tissues of the lungs.

Some antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) have been associated with ILD.

Although this side effect is uncommon, it can become serious if it is not recognized and treated promptly.

---

## Clinical Importance

ILD is one of the most important serious safety concerns associated with certain ADC therapies.

---

## Key Concepts

- Lung inflammation
- Important safety concern
- Early recognition matters

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Does Every ADC Cause ILD?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Not every ADC is associated with the same risk of ILD.

Likewise, most patients receiving ADC therapy do **not** develop ILD.

Your healthcare team considers many factors when selecting the most appropriate treatment.

---

## Clinical Importance

The risk varies among medicines and between patients.

---

## Key Concepts

- Different medicines
- Different risks
- Individualized treatment

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Symptoms Should Patients Watch For?

## Patient Explanation

Patients should contact their healthcare team promptly if they develop:

- a new cough;
- worsening cough;
- shortness of breath;
- chest discomfort;
- unexplained fever.

These symptoms do not always mean ILD is present, but they should never be ignored.

---

## Clinical Importance

New respiratory symptoms require timely medical assessment.

---

## Key Concepts

- New cough
- Shortness of breath
- Report symptoms early

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Is Early Detection So Important?

## Patient Explanation

ILD is much easier to evaluate and manage when symptoms are reported early.

Waiting for symptoms to become severe may increase the risk of serious complications.

For this reason, healthcare teams encourage patients to report new breathing problems immediately.

---

## Clinical Importance

Prompt recognition improves patient safety.

---

## Key Concepts

- Early recognition
- Earlier evaluation
- Safer treatment

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — What Should Patients Do?

## Patient Explanation

If you develop new or worsening breathing symptoms during ADC treatment:

- contact your healthcare team as soon as possible;
- describe your symptoms clearly;
- follow the instructions provided by your healthcare professionals.

Do not ignore persistent respiratory symptoms.

---

## Clinical Importance

Rapid communication supports timely clinical assessment.

---

## Key Concepts

- Contact the care team
- Report symptoms promptly
- Follow medical advice

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Should Patients Stop Treatment on Their Own?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Patients should **not** stop, delay or restart treatment without discussing it with their healthcare team.

Your doctors will determine the safest course of action after evaluating your symptoms.

---

## Clinical Importance

Treatment decisions should always be made by qualified healthcare professionals.

---

## Key Concepts

- Do not self-manage treatment
- Medical evaluation first
- Shared decision-making

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Common Questions About ILD

## Patient Explanation

Some patients believe every cough during ADC therapy means they have ILD.

This is not true.

Many conditions can cause cough or breathing symptoms.

However, because ILD can become serious, every new respiratory symptom should be evaluated promptly.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

Every cough means ILD.

**Fact**

Many conditions may cause respiratory symptoms, but new symptoms during ADC therapy should always be reported for medical assessment.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Should Patients Remember?

Patients should remember:

- ILD is an uncommon but potentially serious side effect of some ADCs.
- Most patients do not develop ILD.
- New breathing symptoms should never be ignored.
- Early reporting improves safety.
- Treatment decisions should always be guided by the healthcare team.

---

## Clinical Importance

Patient awareness contributes to safer cancer treatment and earlier recognition of potentially serious complications.

---

# Key Messages

- Some ADCs may be associated with interstitial lung disease (ILD).
- ILD is uncommon but may become serious if not recognized early.
- Report any new cough, shortness of breath or other respiratory symptoms promptly.
- Do not stop treatment without medical advice.
- Early communication with the healthcare team improves patient safety.
- Routine monitoring remains an important part of ADC therapy.
