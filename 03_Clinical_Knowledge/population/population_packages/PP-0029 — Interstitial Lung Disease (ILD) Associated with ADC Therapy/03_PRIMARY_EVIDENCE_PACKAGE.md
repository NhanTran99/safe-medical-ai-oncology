# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> What is interstitial lung disease (ILD), why is it an important safety concern during treatment with some antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), and what should patients do if symptoms develop?

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an evidence-based understanding of interstitial lung disease (ILD) as a potentially serious but uncommon adverse effect associated with certain antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

This Population Package explains what ILD is in plain language, why early recognition is important, which warning symptoms should never be ignored, and why prompt communication with the healthcare team is essential.

The package intentionally avoids specialist diagnostic criteria, treatment algorithms and clinical trial analyses.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- ILD is an uncommon but potentially serious adverse effect associated with some ADCs.
- Most patients do not experience ILD.
- New respiratory symptoms should always be reported promptly.
- Early recognition supports timely clinical evaluation.
- Patients should never make independent treatment decisions without medical advice.
- Ongoing communication with the healthcare team improves treatment safety.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary authoritative references.

All major references consistently emphasize that ILD is an important safety consideration during treatment with certain ADCs and that prompt recognition of respiratory symptoms is essential.

Differences among references primarily involve:

- diagnostic evaluation;
- imaging findings;
- corticosteroid treatment;
- dose modification strategies;
- drug-specific safety profiles.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- clinical recommendations from NCCN;
- evidence interpretation from JNCCN;
- patient-oriented explanations from NCI and ACS;
- internationally harmonized terminology from ESMO.
