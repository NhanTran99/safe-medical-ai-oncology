## Atomic Clinical Question

> **How is long-term follow-up organized after gastric cancer treatment, what is reassessed over time, and how does follow-up connect surveillance, recurrence evaluation, treatment-sequela monitoring and long-term survivorship care?**

## Clinical Ownership

PP-0230 owns the **longitudinal process of follow-up**.

---

# Clinical Domain

## Domain

Gastric cancer — post-treatment care.

## Population

Patients who have completed or are continuing post-treatment care for gastric cancer, including patients treated with:

- endoscopic resection;
- partial/subtotal gastrectomy;
- total gastrectomy;
- perioperative or adjuvant systemic therapy;
- other multimodality treatment pathways.

Follow-up requirements are context-dependent and must not be interpreted as one universal schedule.

# Knowledge Units — Runtime Granularity

## KU-01 — Long-term Follow-up Definition

**Purpose:** establish the package identity.

**Core statement:** Long-term follow-up is the continuing process of assessing health and cancer-related needs after treatment.

---

## KU-02 — Follow-up Versus Surveillance

**Purpose:** prevent scope confusion.

**Core distinction:**

> Surveillance is a component of follow-up; follow-up is broader.

---

## KU-03 — Temporal Follow-up

**Purpose:** explain why follow-up intensity changes.

NCCN provides time-based recommendations for relevant patient groups while allowing adaptation according to stage, treatment and clinical circumstances.

---

## KU-04 — Follow-up Assessment

**Purpose:** explain what may happen at a follow-up encounter.

Domains include symptoms, H&P, clinically indicated laboratory testing, disease-status assessment, treatment effects, nutrition and general health.

---

## KU-05 — Post-gastrectomy Follow-up

**Purpose:** recognize long-term nutritional and gastrointestinal sequelae.

Particularly relevant after total gastrectomy.

---

## KU-06 — Follow-up Beyond Five Years

**Purpose:** prevent the misconception that five years equals complete cessation of care.

Routine gastric-cancer-specific surveillance may not continue routinely beyond five years, but additional follow-up may be considered based on risk factors and comorbidities.

---

## KU-07 — Care Coordination

**Purpose:** explain oncology–PCP–specialist continuity.

NCCN encourages a therapeutic relationship with primary care throughout life and defined roles among oncology, PCP and subspecialty care.

---

## KU-08 — Patient Records and Transition

**Purpose:** support continuity when clinicians change.

Treatment and follow-up information should remain available across care transitions.

# Evidence Classification

## Established / Guideline-Supported

- Gastric cancer has an explicit NCCN follow-up/surveillance pathway.
- H&P is a core component of follow-up.
- CBC and chemistry testing may be used as clinically indicated.
- Follow-up varies according to stage/treatment pathway.
- Surveillance recommendations differ after endoscopic resection versus surgical resection and according to extent of surgery.
- Routine gastric-cancer-specific surveillance is generally not recommended beyond five years, while additional follow-up may be considered based on risk factors and comorbidities.
- Long-term nutritional monitoring is important after gastrectomy, especially total gastrectomy.
- Primary-care coordination is part of survivorship planning.
- Ongoing assessment of post-treatment needs is recommended.

## Context-Dependent

- Exact follow-up frequency.
- Exact imaging/endoscopy use.
- Timing of care transfer.
- Need for specialist follow-up.
- Follow-up beyond five years.
- Nutritional monitoring and replacement.

## Evidence-Limited / Requires Careful Framing

- Long-term surveillance evidence is not uniformly based on prospective randomized evidence.
- NCCN notes that stage-specific surveillance guidance is based on retrospective literature and expert consensus.
- Long-term follow-up recommendations should therefore not be represented as universally supported by high-level randomized evidence.

# Safety / Runtime Rules

1. Never generate a patient-specific follow-up schedule from this package alone.
2. Never interpret a symptom as recurrence without appropriate clinical assessment.
3. Never equate absence of routine surveillance with absence of ongoing healthcare needs.
4. Never use PP-0230 to replace detailed surveillance guidance.
5. Never use PP-0230 to prescribe nutritional supplementation.
6. Never infer transfer-of-care timing for an individual patient.
7. Preserve uncertainty where surveillance evidence is retrospective or consensus-based.
8. Route detailed questions to the appropriate PP.
9. Do not present five years as a universal “end of care” threshold.
10. Preserve treatment-history dependence.
