# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **How is long-term follow-up organized after gastric cancer treatment, what is reassessed over time, and how does follow-up connect surveillance, recurrence evaluation, treatment-sequela monitoring and long-term survivorship care?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a patient-facing but clinically governed framework for understanding long-term follow-up after gastric cancer treatment.

The package deliberately distinguishes:

**follow-up**

from:

**surveillance, recurrence detection, response assessment, treatment-toxicity management and survivorship.**

The goal is to explain the longitudinal process without duplicating the substantive clinical ownership of adjacent Population Packages.

# Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

## Evidence Note 1 — GAST-7 establishes follow-up as a distinct post-treatment pathway

The supplied NCCN guideline contains a dedicated:

**FOLLOW-UP/SURVEILLANCE (GAST-7)**

pathway.

This establishes that follow-up is not an incidental concept. It is a defined part of the gastric-cancer clinical pathway after treatment.

The pathway branches according to treatment and disease category, reinforcing that follow-up is context-dependent.

---

## Evidence Note 2 — Follow-up timing is structured but not universal

For the relevant patient groups, NCCN specifies:

- H&P every 3–6 months for 1–2 years;
- then every 6–12 months for 3–5 years.

Other components vary according to:

- treatment modality;
- stage;
- surgical anatomy;
- symptoms;
- clinical concern.

This supports a **framework**, not a single universal patient schedule.

---

## Evidence Note 3 — Follow-up includes more than imaging

NCCN's GAST-7 includes:

- H&P;
- CBC and chemistry profile when clinically indicated;
- EGD in specified contexts;
- CT and other imaging according to clinical circumstances;
- nutritional monitoring after surgical resection.

Therefore it is inaccurate to define follow-up as “regular scans.”

---

## Evidence Note 4 — Surveillance evidence has important limitations

NCCN explicitly states that surveillance after curative-intent R0 resection remains controversial and that prospective evidence is sparse.

The stage-specific guidance in GAST-7 is based on retrospective literature and expert consensus.

This is important for evidence maturity:

**guideline-supported ≠ uniformly high-level randomized evidence.**

The package must preserve this distinction.

---

## Evidence Note 5 — The five-year boundary is nuanced

NCCN states that the majority of gastric-cancer relapses occur within two years and approximately 90% by five years, but potentially actionable relapses have occasionally been recognized after five years.

Therefore additional follow-up after five years may be considered based on risk factors and comorbidities.

The educational message must avoid both extremes:

- “everyone needs intensive surveillance forever”;
- “everything stops at five years.”

---

## Evidence Note 6 — Post-gastrectomy follow-up can be lifelong

NCCN specifically recommends establishing follow-up with appropriate practitioners or specialists for lifelong monitoring and management of potential nutritional sequelae of gastrectomy.

Potential deficiencies include:

- vitamin B12;
- iron;
- zinc;
- calcium;
- vitamin D.

This is a particularly strong justification for a longitudinal follow-up package that is broader than cancer surveillance.

---

## Evidence Note 7 — Survivorship coordination is integrated into long-term follow-up

NCCN survivorship principles include:

- maintaining a therapeutic relationship with a PCP throughout life;
- defined oncology/PCP roles;
- follow-up and surveillance information;
- information about acute, late and long-term treatment effects;
- delineation of roles and timing of transfer of care when appropriate;
- periodic assessment of ongoing needs.

This supports PP-0230's role as the continuity-of-care bridge between disease-specific follow-up and survivorship.

---

## Evidence Note 8 — ESMO-ASCO supports the broader survivorship framework

The supplied ESMO-ASCO curriculum identifies long-term follow-up as including assessment for:

- recurrence;
- second primary cancers;
- somatic late sequelae;
- psychosocial well-being;
- functional issues;
- healthy lifestyle;
- preventive care;
- primary-care coordination.

This supports the conceptual architecture of PP-0230 but does not replace the disease-specific NCCN framework.

---

## Evidence Note 9 — ACS supports patient-facing follow-up questions

The supplied ACS material explicitly presents questions patients may ask about:

- type of follow-up;
- frequency of exams and imaging;
- blood tests;
- symptoms to watch for;
- nutrition;
- how recurrence will be assessed;
- future treatment if cancer returns.

ACS also discusses long-term nutritional monitoring after gastrectomy and the importance of maintaining medical records.

This is useful for patient-facing translation.

# Clinical Claims Summary

## High-confidence claims

1. Gastric cancer requires structured post-treatment follow-up.
2. Follow-up includes clinical assessment and may include surveillance investigations.
3. Follow-up differs according to treatment and disease context.
4. Post-gastrectomy nutritional sequelae may require long-term or lifelong monitoring.
5. Routine gastric-cancer-specific surveillance is generally not recommended beyond five years in the NCCN framework.

# Evidence Limitations

1. Surveillance evidence after curative-intent gastric-cancer treatment remains limited.
2. NCCN identifies retrospective literature and expert consensus as important bases for stage-specific surveillance recommendations.
3. Exact follow-up practices can vary by clinical setting.
4. Long-term care requirements differ according to treatment history and patient factors.
5. The supplied project materials do not establish a universal patient-specific schedule.
6. Nutritional monitoring and replacement requirements may vary by type of gastrectomy and clinical condition.
7. Evidence supporting individual survivorship interventions is not identical across all domains.

---

# Evidence Gaps

The following are deliberately not filled with unsupported assumptions:

- a universal follow-up schedule for every gastric-cancer patient;
- universal testing beyond five years;
- a single universal laboratory panel;
- a universal imaging strategy;
- a universal oncology-to-PCP transfer point;
- individualized nutritional replacement requirements;
- individualized recurrence-risk estimates.

These gaps do not block PP-0230 because the package owns the longitudinal framework rather than individualized clinical planning.

# Evidence-to-Patient Translation

### Evidence-level message

Follow-up after gastric cancer treatment is not simply a schedule of scans.

### Patient-level message

> Your follow-up may include visits, questions about symptoms, physical examinations, selected blood tests and other tests when appropriate. It may also address nutrition, treatment effects and general health.

### Evidence-level message

Follow-up becomes less intensive for many patients over time.

### Patient-level message

> Having fewer cancer-specific tests later does not necessarily mean that your healthcare has ended.

### Evidence-level message

Post-gastrectomy nutritional sequelae may require lifelong monitoring.

### Patient-level message

> If part or all of your stomach was removed, your care team may continue checking nutrition and vitamin or mineral levels over the long term.
