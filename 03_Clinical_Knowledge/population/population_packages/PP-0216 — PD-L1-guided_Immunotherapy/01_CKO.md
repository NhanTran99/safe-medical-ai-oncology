## B. Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain why PD-L1 is relevant to immunotherapy in gastric adenocarcinoma.
2. Understand the clinical meaning of the PD-L1 Combined Positive Score (CPS).
3. Understand why CPS thresholds such as ≥1 and ≥5 can have different clinical significance.
4. Understand that PD-L1 is a treatment-selection biomarker rather than a guarantee of individual response.
5. Recognize that PD-L1-guided treatment is most directly relevant in selected advanced, recurrent, unresectable, or metastatic treatment contexts, while selected perioperative contexts also exist.
6. Understand how PD-L1 interacts with other treatment-relevant biomarkers, particularly HER2 and MSI-H/dMMR.
7. Understand why MSI-H/dMMR can provide an immunotherapy-relevant pathway that is independent of PD-L1 status.
8. Distinguish PD-L1 clinical application from the technical details of PD-L1 testing.
9. Interpret a PD-L1 result without treating it as a stand-alone prescription.
10. Recognize major limitations, uncertainties, and common misconceptions surrounding PD-L1-guided immunotherapy.

# D. Clinical Knowledge Blocks

## Block 1 — What Is PD-L1 and Why Does It Matter?

PD-L1 is a protein involved in immune regulation. PD-L1 can participate in signaling that reduces T-cell activity through the PD-1 pathway.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors can interfere with this inhibitory pathway. In gastric cancer, PD-L1 therefore became clinically relevant not simply as a biological feature, but as a biomarker that can help define treatment populations for selected immunotherapy strategies.

The key clinical question for this Population Package is not:

> “Does the tumor express PD-L1?”

by itself.

It is:

> **“What does the PD-L1 result mean for the evidence and treatment options relevant to this patient's clinical setting?”**

Detailed checkpoint biology belongs to PP-0214.

---

## Block 2 — PD-L1 Is a Treatment-Selection Biomarker

PD-L1 should primarily be understood here as a **predictive/treatment-selection biomarker**.

A predictive biomarker helps identify a population in which a particular treatment is more likely to provide benefit or is supported by evidence.

This is different from saying that PD-L1 independently predicts the natural course of gastric cancer.

The supplied NCCN discussion notes that the prognostic significance of PD-L1 in gastric cancer remains unclear and may be affected by testing variability and study differences.

Therefore:

> **PP-0216 should not teach PD-L1 as a universal prognostic biomarker.**

The central role of PD-L1 in this package is treatment selection.

---

## Block 3 — What Is CPS?

The **Combined Positive Score (CPS)** is a gastric-cancer PD-L1 scoring concept.

Conceptually, CPS incorporates PD-L1-positive cells among tumor cells and tumor-associated immune cells relative to viable tumor cells in the assessed specimen.

The important patient-facing point is:

> **CPS is not simply the percentage of tumor cells that are PD-L1 positive.**

A patient's report may therefore contain a CPS value that should be interpreted using the treatment context and the specific evidence/guideline framework.

Detailed scoring methodology is delegated to PP-0183 and its downstream PD-L1 testing/scoring packages.

---

## Block 4 — Why Does the CPS Number Matter?

PD-L1 is not always used as a simple binary positive/negative variable.

Current supplied NCCN treatment material uses:

- **CPS ≥1** as a treatment-relevant threshold for selected first-line checkpoint-inhibitor regimens;
- **CPS ≥5** as the threshold at which several of these regimens carry category 1 evidence positioning.

Therefore a CPS of 1 and a CPS of 5 should not be treated as interchangeable numbers.

This does not mean that CPS behaves like a continuous personal “response score.”

Instead:

> **Clinical treatment frameworks use defined thresholds because the evidence supporting a regimen is evaluated in particular biomarker-defined populations.**

---

## Block 5 — CPS ≥1

In the current supplied NCCN Version 2.2026 framework, a checkpoint inhibitor is added to first-line chemotherapy for advanced disease when PD-L1 CPS is ≥1.

For HER2-positive advanced disease, pembrolizumab combined with trastuzumab and fluoropyrimidine/platinum chemotherapy is listed for PD-L1 CPS ≥1.

For HER2-negative advanced disease, first-line combinations involving nivolumab, pembrolizumab, or tislelizumab are listed for PD-L1 CPS ≥1, with category 1 positioning at CPS ≥5 for several of these regimens.

The practical meaning is:

> **CPS ≥1 can make a patient eligible for consideration of selected PD-L1-guided immunotherapy strategies, but the actual option depends on the full treatment context.**

---

## Block 6 — CPS ≥5

CPS ≥5 is particularly important because the current NCCN algorithm assigns category 1 status to several first-line PD-L1-guided checkpoint-inhibitor + chemotherapy regimens at this threshold.

This should not be translated into:

> “CPS below 5 means immunotherapy cannot work.”

Nor should it be translated into:

> “CPS above 5 guarantees a response.”

The correct interpretation is:

> **The threshold reflects the strength and structure of the clinical evidence for particular treatment strategies.**

---

## Block 7 — CPS ≥10: Important Trial Context, Not a Universal Threshold

CPS ≥10 appears in major trial analyses and remains useful for understanding how treatment effects can vary across PD-L1-defined populations.

KEYNOTE-062 enrolled patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1. Its final results did not show superiority of pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy over chemotherapy in the overall primary population. A prespecified CPS ≥10 subgroup showed a more favorable OS result for pembrolizumab monotherapy, but the analysis plan did not test that difference further.

KEYNOTE-859 provides a different example: in advanced HER2-negative disease, pembrolizumab + chemotherapy showed benefit in both CPS ≥10 and CPS ≥1 populations, with a larger effect estimate in the CPS ≥10 subgroup.

Therefore:

> **CPS ≥10 is an important evidence threshold in selected trials, but it is not the universal current treatment threshold for gastric-cancer immunotherapy.**

---

## Block 8 — PD-L1 and HER2 Must Be Interpreted Together

PD-L1 does not operate in isolation.

In HER2-positive advanced gastric cancer, the current treatment framework includes pembrolizumab with trastuzumab and chemotherapy for PD-L1 CPS ≥1.

KEYNOTE-811 provides an important illustration: patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 had a greater PFS benefit from pembrolizumab-containing therapy than the CPS <1 subgroup.

This package therefore teaches a broader principle:

> **A biomarker can modify treatment selection within another biomarker-defined treatment pathway.**

Detailed HER2 biology and HER2-targeted therapy remain outside PP-0216.

---

## Block 9 — PD-L1 and MSI-H/dMMR Are Parallel Biomarker Pathways

MSI-H/dMMR is not simply another way of measuring PD-L1.

The current NCCN algorithm explicitly identifies MSI-H/dMMR tumors as an immunotherapy-relevant category **independent of PD-L1 status**.

This is a critical safety concept.

A patient should not infer:

> “My PD-L1 is low, therefore I can never receive immunotherapy.”

The correct interpretation is:

> **PD-L1 is one treatment-selection pathway. MSI-H/dMMR can provide another immunotherapy-relevant pathway.**

Detailed MSI/MMR testing and treatment belong to PP-0215 and related packages.

---

## Block 10 — PD-L1 and CLDN18.2 / Other Biomarkers

Gastric-cancer treatment selection is multi-biomarker.

Other biomarkers may include:

- HER2;
- MSI/MMR;
- CLDN18.2;
- TMB and selected molecular features.

PP-0216 does not replace those packages.

Instead, it explains how PD-L1 fits into the larger decision architecture.

The patient-facing principle is:

> **A PD-L1 result is one component of treatment selection, not the complete treatment plan.**

---

## Block 11 — Advanced / Unresectable / Recurrent / Metastatic Disease

The strongest and clearest current application of PD-L1-guided immunotherapy in the supplied sources is first-line treatment of advanced, unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma where local therapy is not indicated.

The current NCCN algorithm includes:

### HER2-positive

PD-L1 CPS ≥1:
- pembrolizumab + trastuzumab + fluoropyrimidine/platinum chemotherapy.

### HER2-negative

PD-L1 CPS ≥1:
- nivolumab + chemotherapy;
- pembrolizumab + chemotherapy;
- tislelizumab + chemotherapy;

with category 1 positioning at CPS ≥5 for several regimens.

The exact regimen remains dependent on the full clinical context.

---

## Block 12 — Landmark Evidence: CheckMate-649

CheckMate-649 is an important evidence foundation for nivolumab + chemotherapy.

The supplied NCI PDQ reports:

- in all patients, median OS was 14.0 months with nivolumab + chemotherapy versus 11.3 months with chemotherapy;
- HR 0.77;
- in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥5, median OS was 14.4 versus 11.1 months;
- HR 0.71.

The package should use this evidence to teach:

> **PD-L1 can define a clinically important treatment population, while treatment effects may also be observed outside a narrowly defined biomarker subgroup depending on the regimen and evidence base.**

This prevents over-simplification.

---

## Block 13 — Landmark Evidence: KEYNOTE-859

KEYNOTE-859 evaluated pembrolizumab + chemotherapy in previously untreated advanced HER2-negative gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

The supplied NCCN source reports:

### CPS ≥10
- OS 15.7 vs 11.8 months;
- HR 0.65;
- PFS 8.1 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.62;
- ORR 61% vs 43%.

### CPS ≥1
- OS 13.0 vs 11.4 months;
- HR 0.74;
- PFS 6.9 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.72;
- ORR 52% vs 43%.

This trial is particularly useful for showing that:

> **PD-L1 thresholds can identify populations with different magnitudes of observed treatment effect, but the threshold must always be interpreted within the trial and treatment framework.**

---

## Block 14 — Landmark Evidence: KEYNOTE-811

KEYNOTE-811 studied pembrolizumab with trastuzumab + chemotherapy in previously untreated advanced HER2-positive gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

The supplied NCCN evidence reports:

- overall median PFS 10.0 vs 8.1 months;
- in PD-L1 CPS ≥1, median PFS 10.8 vs 7.2 months;
- HR 0.70;
- in CPS <1, median PFS 9.5 vs 9.6 months;
- HR 1.17.

The trial therefore provides an especially useful example of **biomarker-by-treatment context**.

The package should not interpret the subgroup HR as a guarantee for an individual patient. It is evidence at the population level.

---

## Block 15 — Landmark Evidence: KEYNOTE-062

KEYNOTE-062 demonstrates why PD-L1 interpretation requires evidence literacy.

The study enrolled previously untreated advanced gastric cancer with CPS ≥1.

The final analysis did not show superiority of pembrolizumab or pembrolizumab + chemotherapy over chemotherapy in the overall primary population.

A CPS ≥10 subgroup had median OS 17.4 versus 10.8 months for pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy, HR 0.69, but the prespecified statistical analysis plan did not test that difference further.

This should be taught as:

> **A favorable biomarker-defined subgroup result does not automatically establish a universal treatment rule.**

---

## Block 16 — Landmark Evidence: Tislelizumab / RATIONALE-305

Current NCCN Version 2.2026 includes tislelizumab + fluoropyrimidine/platinum chemotherapy for HER2-negative advanced disease with PD-L1 CPS ≥1, with category 1 positioning at CPS ≥5.

This makes tislelizumab part of the current PD-L1-guided treatment landscape rather than merely historical evidence.

The package should emphasize evidence positioning rather than dosing.

---

## Block 17 — Selected Perioperative Context

PD-L1-guided immunotherapy is not limited conceptually to metastatic disease.

The supplied NCCN material includes a perioperative durvalumab context in which subgroup analyses use PD-L1 TAP, and the panel recommends the regimen for tumors meeting PD-L1 CPS ≥1 or TAP ≥1%.

This is a selected context and should not be generalized into:

> “Every resectable PD-L1-positive gastric cancer should receive immunotherapy.”

The evidence and regimen must remain context-specific.

---

## Block 18 — PD-L1 Is Not a Guarantee

A high CPS does not guarantee response.

A low CPS does not universally exclude every immunotherapy possibility.

PD-L1 should therefore be understood as a **population-level treatment-selection biomarker**.

It helps answer:

> “Does this patient fall into a population for which a particular immunotherapy strategy has evidence?”

It does not answer:

> “Will this individual definitely respond?”

---

## Block 19 — What a Patient Should Do With a CPS Result

A patient should ask:

1. What is my exact CPS value?
2. What treatment setting are we considering?
3. Which regimen uses this CPS threshold?
4. Does my HER2 status change the interpretation?
5. Has MSI/MMR testing been performed?
6. Are there other treatment-relevant biomarkers?
7. Is the PD-L1 result technically adequate?
8. Is the treatment recommendation based on CPS ≥1, ≥5, or another threshold?
9. What is the expected benefit and what are the uncertainties?
10. Does the result need to be interpreted together with pathology, stage, prior therapy and my overall health?

# E. Clinical Importance

PD-L1-guided immunotherapy is clinically important because it connects a laboratory biomarker to a treatment decision without reducing the decision to the biomarker alone.

The clinical pathway is:

**PD-L1 testing**
→ **CPS / relevant PD-L1 result**
→ **identify applicable treatment evidence**
→ **integrate HER2 / MSI/MMR / other biomarkers**
→ **consider disease setting and prior therapy**
→ **consider expected benefit and toxicity**
→ **shared clinical decision**

The package therefore sits between testing and treatment.

# G. Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1
“PD-L1 positive means immunotherapy will definitely work.”

**Fact:** PD-L1 identifies a treatment-relevant population; it does not guarantee individual response.

### Myth 2
“CPS is just the percentage of tumor cells that stain positive.”

**Fact:** CPS incorporates tumor and selected immune cells relative to viable tumor cells.

### Myth 3
“CPS ≥1 and CPS ≥5 mean the same thing.”

**Fact:** Different thresholds can correspond to different evidence strength and treatment positioning.

### Myth 4
“CPS ≥10 is the universal threshold for immunotherapy.”

**Fact:** Current treatment frameworks use different thresholds depending on regimen and evidence.

### Myth 5
“If CPS is <1, immunotherapy is impossible.”

**Fact:** Treatment depends on regimen, clinical setting and other biomarkers, including MSI-H/dMMR.

### Myth 6
“PD-L1 is the only immunotherapy biomarker.”

**Fact:** MSI-H/dMMR and other biomarkers can independently or jointly affect treatment selection.

### Myth 7
“A higher CPS tells me exactly how much benefit I will get.”

**Fact:** CPS is not an individual response meter.

### Myth 8
“A PD-L1 test result automatically tells the doctor which drug to prescribe.”

**Fact:** Treatment selection integrates multiple clinical and biomarker factors.

### Myth 9
“PD-L1 is mainly a prognostic marker.”

**Fact:** In this package its principal role is treatment selection; its independent prognostic significance remains uncertain.

### Myth 10
“PD-L1 testing and PD-L1-guided immunotherapy are the same package.”

**Fact:** Testing determines the result; PP-0216 explains its treatment meaning.

# K. Clinical Safety Boundary

This Population Package provides general education about the clinical meaning of PD-L1 results. It is not an individualized treatment recommendation.

A PD-L1 score should not be used alone to start, stop or change cancer treatment.

Individual treatment selection requires integration of the complete pathology and biomarker record, disease setting, HER2/MSI/MMR and other relevant biomarkers, prior treatment, performance status, comorbidities, toxicity considerations, current clinical guidance and patient preferences.
