# 2. Clinical Question

> **What does a PD-L1 result mean for immunotherapy treatment in gastric cancer?**

The evidence package evaluates the clinical role of PD-L1 as a treatment-selection biomarker, with emphasis on CPS thresholds, current guideline positioning, landmark immunotherapy trials, interaction with other biomarkers, patient-facing interpretation and evidence limitations.

---

# 3. Educational Intent

This Evidence Package supports a patient-centered clinical knowledge object.

It is intended to explain:

- why PD-L1 is tested;
- what CPS means clinically;
- why CPS thresholds matter;
- how PD-L1 relates to checkpoint-inhibitor treatment;
- why the treatment meaning of PD-L1 depends on disease setting and regimen;
- why MSI-H/dMMR can provide an independent immunotherapy pathway;
- why PD-L1 does not guarantee response;
- why treatment decisions cannot be made from CPS alone.

It is not a prescribing protocol.

# 9. Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

## 9.1 PD-L1 Testing and CPS

NCCN Version 2.2026 describes PD-L1 evaluation using IHC in FFPE tumor tissue and defines CPS using PD-L1-staining tumor cells and immune cells relative to viable tumor cells.

For PP-0216, the clinically relevant output is not the laboratory procedure itself.

The package consumes the result:

> **CPS value → clinical treatment interpretation.**

Technical methodology remains delegated to PP-0183 and dedicated testing/scoring packages.

---

## 9.2 CPS and TAP

NCCN Version 2.2026 reports high concordance between CPS and TAP and states that either may be used for identifying patients likely to benefit from tislelizumab or durvalumab in selected contexts.

This is useful evidence for PP-0216 because it demonstrates that PD-L1 treatment selection can be expressed through more than one scoring framework.

However, PP-0216 must not generalize this into:

> “CPS and TAP are universally interchangeable for every regimen.”

The evidence statement is context-specific.

---

## 9.3 Current First-line Advanced Disease

NCCN Version 2.2026 states that a checkpoint inhibitor should be added to first-line chemotherapy for advanced disease with PD-L1 CPS ≥1.

The algorithm then separates HER2-positive and HER2-negative treatment pathways.

This is the strongest direct evidence that PP-0216 should center on advanced/recurrent/unresectable/metastatic treatment application.

---

## 9.4 HER2-positive Pathway

NCCN lists fluoropyrimidine + platinum + trastuzumab + pembrolizumab for PD-L1 CPS ≥1.

This establishes a key multi-biomarker principle:

> PD-L1 is interpreted inside a HER2-defined treatment pathway rather than as an isolated biomarker.

KEYNOTE-811 provides supporting evidence.

The supplied NCCN evidence reports:

- overall median PFS 10.0 vs 8.1 months;
- CPS ≥1 median PFS 10.8 vs 7.2 months;
- HR 0.70;
- CPS <1 median PFS 9.5 vs 9.6 months;
- HR 1.17.

The subgroup result supports the clinical relevance of PD-L1 in this treatment context but does not justify an individual response guarantee.

---

## 9.5 HER2-negative Pathway

NCCN lists:

- nivolumab + chemotherapy for PD-L1 CPS ≥1, category 1 at CPS ≥5;
- pembrolizumab + chemotherapy for PD-L1 CPS ≥1, category 1 at CPS ≥5;
- tislelizumab + chemotherapy for PD-L1 CPS ≥1, category 1 at CPS ≥5.

This establishes CPS threshold relevance across multiple checkpoint-inhibitor strategies.

---

## 9.6 CheckMate-649

The NCI PDQ reports:

All patients:
- median OS 14.0 vs 11.3 months;
- HR 0.77.

PD-L1 CPS ≥5:
- median OS 14.4 vs 11.1 months;
- HR 0.71.

This trial is important because it illustrates both:

1. an overall treatment effect in the studied population;
2. stronger evidence within a PD-L1-defined subgroup.

The correct educational interpretation is not that CPS is an absolute gate.

---

## 9.7 KEYNOTE-062

The NCI PDQ reports that KEYNOTE-062 enrolled previously untreated advanced gastric cancer with PD-L1 CPS ≥1.

The final results did not show superiority of pembrolizumab monotherapy or pembrolizumab + chemotherapy over chemotherapy alone in the overall primary population.

In CPS ≥10:
- median OS 17.4 vs 10.8 months;
- HR 0.69.

However, the prespecified analysis plan did not test this subgroup difference further.

This is an important evidence-quality safeguard.

The package should teach:

> A favorable subgroup estimate is not automatically equivalent to a definitive universal treatment indication.

---

## 9.8 KEYNOTE-859

NCCN reports:

CPS ≥10:
- OS 15.7 vs 11.8 months;
- HR 0.65;
- PFS 8.1 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.62;
- ORR 61% vs 43%.

CPS ≥1:
- OS 13.0 vs 11.4 months;
- HR 0.74;
- PFS 6.9 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.72;
- ORR 52% vs 43%.

The current guideline uses CPS ≥1 for the treatment option and gives category 1 positioning at CPS ≥5.

This demonstrates why the package must preserve the distinction between:

- trial subgroup thresholds;
- current guideline thresholds;
- statistical effect magnitude;
- treatment eligibility.

---

## 9.9 Tislelizumab / RATIONALE-305

Current NCCN Version 2.2026 lists tislelizumab + chemotherapy in HER2-negative advanced disease for PD-L1 CPS ≥1, with category 1 positioning at CPS ≥5.

This confirms that PD-L1-guided treatment is not limited to one checkpoint inhibitor.

The package should therefore teach the principle of biomarker-guided treatment rather than becoming a single-drug package.

---

## 9.10 MSI-H/dMMR Independence

The NCCN algorithm explicitly places MSI-H/dMMR tumors in a separate treatment category marked:

> independent of PD-L1 status.

This is a critical evidence-backed boundary.

It means:

> PD-L1 is not the universal gateway to immunotherapy.

A patient with MSI-H/dMMR disease can have an immunotherapy-relevant treatment pathway even when PD-L1 does not meet a particular threshold.

---

## 9.11 Perioperative Evidence

The supplied NCCN source includes a perioperative durvalumab context using PD-L1 TAP subgroup analysis and a panel recommendation for PD-L1 CPS ≥1 or TAP ≥1%.

This should be represented as a selected context.

The package should not extrapolate this to all localized disease.

## Claim 2

CPS is the key gastric-cancer PD-L1 scoring concept.

CPS ≥1 is used as a treatment threshold for selected current first-line regimens.

# 12. Evidence Limitations

## Limitation 1 — Biomarker heterogeneity

A single tumor sample may not perfectly represent the entire tumor.

## Limitation 2 — Assay and scoring context

PD-L1 interpretation depends on the testing framework.

## Limitation 3 — Threshold dependence

Different trials and regimens use different thresholds.

## Limitation 4 — Predictive imperfection

Not every PD-L1-positive tumor responds.

## Limitation 5 — Non-zero benefit outside a narrow threshold

Some treatment effects can occur in populations outside a selected PD-L1 subgroup, depending on the regimen and evidence.

## Limitation 6 — Changing treatment landscape

Current guideline positioning can change as new trials and regulatory decisions emerge.

## Limitation 7 — Cross-trial comparison

Hazard ratios from different trials should not be directly compared as though the studies were interchangeable.

## Limitation 8 — Subgroup inference

Subgroup results may have different statistical strength from primary trial endpoints.

---

# 13. Evidence Gaps

1. The exact individual predictive value of PD-L1 remains imperfect.
2. Borderline CPS values may not translate uniformly across regimens.
3. The optimal role of repeated PD-L1 testing is not established as a universal practice.
4. Dynamic PD-L1 changes during therapy are not equivalent to validated treatment-selection rules.
5. Perioperative PD-L1-guided immunotherapy remains more context-specific than the established advanced-disease framework.
6. Treatment after progression on an ICI cannot be determined from PD-L1 alone.
7. PD-L1 prognostic significance remains uncertain.

# 15. Evidence-to-Patient Translation

## Patient statement

“My PD-L1 is positive.”

### Correct translation

The tumor meets a defined PD-L1 criterion, but the exact clinical meaning depends on the CPS value and treatment context.

---

## Patient statement

“My CPS is 5.”

### Correct translation

CPS ≥5 is clinically important because several current first-line checkpoint-inhibitor regimens have stronger category 1 evidence at this threshold.

---

## Patient statement

“My CPS is 0.”

### Correct translation

This result does not automatically mean that every immunotherapy pathway is unavailable. Other biomarkers and the treatment setting matter.

---

## Patient statement

“My CPS is 10, so immunotherapy should definitely work.”

### Correct translation

A higher CPS can be associated with stronger treatment evidence in selected populations, but it does not guarantee individual response.
