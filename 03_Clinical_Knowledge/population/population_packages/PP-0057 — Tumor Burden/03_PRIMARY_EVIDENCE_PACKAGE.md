# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does tumor burden mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **tumor burden**, including what the term means, why it describes the overall amount of cancer present in the body, why it is considered a clinical concept rather than a laboratory test or TNM stage, why there is no single universal method for measuring it, and why physicians integrate multiple sources of clinical information when evaluating disease burden.

This Population Package introduces the concept of tumor burden without discussing RECIST measurements, volumetric assessment, imaging protocols, biomarkers, treatment response or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of tumor burden
- Explanation of the overall amount of cancer present in the body
- General contributors to tumor burden
- Tumor burden as a clinical concept
- Importance of comprehensive clinical evaluation
- Recognition that no universal measurement exists

---

## Excluded

- RECIST
- Target lesion measurements
- Sum of target lesion diameters
- Volumetric tumor assessment
- PET quantitative measurements
- Metabolic tumor volume
- Total lesion glycolysis
- ctDNA
- Tumor markers
- Treatment response assessment
- Treatment
- Molecular mechanisms
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Tumor burden refers to the overall amount of cancer present in the body.
- It reflects the extent of disease rather than a single tumor or lesion.
- Tumor burden is a clinical concept and not an independent staging system or laboratory test.
- Physicians evaluate tumor burden by integrating imaging findings, clinical examination, laboratory results, pathology and other relevant information.
- The assessment of tumor burden varies according to the clinical situation and should always be interpreted within the broader context of patient care.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- tumor burden as a description of overall disease extent;
- use of tumor burden as a clinical concept rather than a formal staging category;
- the need for comprehensive clinical assessment instead of reliance on a single measurement;
- individualized interpretation according to each patient's disease.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and the degree of technical detail provided.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
