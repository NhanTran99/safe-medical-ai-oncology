# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what peritoneal metastasis means.
- Understand what the peritoneum is in simple language.
- Understand that gastric cancer can spread to the peritoneum.
- Recognize that peritoneal metastasis is still gastric cancer, not a new cancer.
- Understand that not every patient with gastric cancer develops peritoneal metastasis.
- Understand that doctors use appropriate medical evaluation to determine whether peritoneal metastasis is present.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is the Peritoneum?

## Patient Explanation

The **peritoneum** is a thin layer of tissue that lines the inside of the abdomen and covers many of the organs inside it.

It helps protect the organs and allows them to move smoothly against one another.

---

## Clinical Importance

The peritoneum is an important anatomical surface that can be affected by the spread of gastric cancer.

---

## Key Concepts

- The peritoneum lines the abdominal cavity.
- It covers many abdominal organs.
- It is different from the stomach itself.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Is Peritoneal Metastasis?

## Patient Explanation

**Peritoneal metastasis** means that stomach cancer has spread from the original tumor to the peritoneum.

This is one form of metastatic gastric cancer.

---

## Clinical Importance

Peritoneal metastasis represents dissemination of gastric cancer to the peritoneal lining.

---

## Key Concepts

- Gastric cancer can spread to the peritoneum.
- This is a form of metastatic disease.
- The original cancer remains gastric cancer.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Is It a New Cancer?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Even after gastric cancer spreads to the peritoneum, it is still called **gastric cancer with peritoneal metastasis**.

It is **not** considered a new cancer that started in the peritoneum.

---

## Clinical Importance

Metastatic tumors retain the identity of their primary cancer.

---

## Key Concepts

- Metastatic tumors keep the identity of the original cancer.
- Peritoneal metastasis is not primary peritoneal cancer.
- Treatment decisions are based on the original cancer.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does Every Patient Develop Peritoneal Metastasis?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Many patients never develop peritoneal metastasis.

Whether gastric cancer has spread to the peritoneum varies from person to person.

---

## Clinical Importance

Peritoneal metastasis occurs in only a proportion of patients with gastric cancer.

---

## Key Concepts

- Not every patient develops peritoneal metastasis.
- Disease patterns differ between individuals.
- Medical evaluation is required.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — How Do Doctors Determine Whether Peritoneal Metastasis Is Present?

## Patient Explanation

Doctors do not determine peritoneal metastasis based on symptoms alone.

They combine information from:

- imaging studies;
- surgery (when appropriate);
- pathology findings;
- other clinical information.

Only after reviewing all available information can doctors determine whether peritoneal metastasis is present.

---

## Clinical Importance

Diagnosis requires comprehensive clinical assessment using appropriate investigations.

---

## Key Concepts

- Diagnosis requires medical evaluation.
- Multiple sources of information are considered.
- Symptoms alone are not enough.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Should I Assume My Situation Is the Same as Someone Else's?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Every patient's cancer is different.

Even when two patients both have peritoneal metastasis, their disease, treatment options and overall clinical situation may not be the same.

---

## Clinical Importance

Clinical management is individualized and based on the complete clinical picture.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

Every patient with peritoneal metastasis has the same disease and treatment.

**Fact**

Peritoneal metastasis can vary greatly between patients. Doctors consider many clinical factors before discussing treatment or prognosis.

---

# Key Messages

- The peritoneum is a thin membrane inside the abdomen.
- Peritoneal metastasis means gastric cancer has spread to the peritoneum.
- This is still gastric cancer, not a new cancer.
- Not every patient develops peritoneal metastasis.
- Doctors diagnose peritoneal metastasis using appropriate medical evaluation.
