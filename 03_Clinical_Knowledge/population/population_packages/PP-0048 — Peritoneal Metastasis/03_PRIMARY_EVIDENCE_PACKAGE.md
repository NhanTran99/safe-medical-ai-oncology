# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is peritoneal metastasis?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **peritoneal metastasis**, including what the peritoneum is, how gastric cancer can spread to the peritoneum, why peritoneal metastasis is still gastric cancer, and why appropriate medical evaluation is required to establish the diagnosis.

This Population Package introduces the concept of peritoneal metastasis without discussing diagnostic procedures, disease burden scoring systems, treatment strategies or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of the peritoneum
- Definition of peritoneal metastasis
- Relationship between gastric cancer and the peritoneum
- Difference between primary gastric cancer and peritoneal metastasis
- Relationship between peritoneal metastasis and metastatic disease
- Importance of medical evaluation

---

## Excluded

- Peritoneal Carcinomatosis Index (PCI)
- Staging laparoscopy
- Peritoneal cytology
- Ascites
- Cytoreductive surgery (CRS)
- Hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC)
- Molecular mechanisms of peritoneal dissemination
- M1 classification
- Treatment recommendations
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The peritoneum is a thin membrane within the abdomen.
- Gastric cancer can spread to the peritoneum.
- Peritoneal metastasis is still gastric cancer because it originates from the stomach.
- Not every patient develops peritoneal metastasis.
- Doctors determine the presence of peritoneal metastasis using appropriate clinical assessment rather than symptoms alone.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of the peritoneum;
- the concept of peritoneal metastatic spread;
- the distinction between metastatic gastric cancer and a new primary cancer;
- the importance of appropriate clinical evaluation before confirming peritoneal metastasis.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
