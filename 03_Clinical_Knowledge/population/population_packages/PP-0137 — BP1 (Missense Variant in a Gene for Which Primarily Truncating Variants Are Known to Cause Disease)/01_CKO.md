# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is BP1?

## Patient Explanation

**BP1** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant is likely to be **benign (not disease-causing).**

BP1 represents one type of **Supporting Benign Evidence**.

It focuses on **missense variants** occurring in certain genes.

---

## Clinical Importance

BP1 provides supporting benign evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- BP1
- Supporting benign evidence
- ACMG Evidence Codes

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Is a Missense Variant?

## Patient Explanation

A **missense variant** is a genetic change that replaces one amino acid in a protein with another.

Many missense variants do **not** cause disease, while some may affect how a protein functions.

---

## Clinical Importance

Not every missense variant has the same clinical significance.

---

## Key Concepts

- Missense variant
- Amino acid
- Protein

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Is BP1 Important?

## Patient Explanation

Different genes can cause disease in different ways.

For some genes, disease is usually caused by **truncating variants**, which shorten or greatly disrupt the protein.

In these genes, a **missense variant** is often **less likely** to be the cause of disease.

Because of this, a missense variant may provide **supporting evidence** that it is benign.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding how a gene usually causes disease helps genetics professionals interpret variants more accurately.

---

## Key Concepts

- Gene disease mechanism
- Truncating variants
- Supporting benign evidence

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does BP1 Mean the Variant Is Definitely Benign?

## Patient Explanation

No.

BP1 **does not prove** that a variant is benign.

It provides one piece of supporting evidence that must always be considered together with many other types of scientific and clinical information.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification depends on integrating multiple evidence categories.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Professional interpretation
- Benign classification

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Does the ACMG Use BP1?

## Patient Explanation

Genes behave differently.

Using BP1 helps genetics professionals consider whether a missense variant occurs in a gene where this type of change is usually **not** the main cause of disease.

This supports a more consistent and evidence-based interpretation.

---

## Clinical Importance

Standardized evidence criteria improve consistency and reproducibility in clinical genomics.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Every missense variant has the same chance of causing disease.

**Fact**

The significance of a missense variant depends on the biology of the affected gene.

In some genes, missense variants are much less likely to be disease-causing, which is why BP1 may provide supporting benign evidence.

---

# Key Messages

- BP1 is one Supporting Benign Evidence criterion.
- BP1 applies to missense variants in certain genes.
- Different genes cause disease through different mechanisms.
- BP1 provides supporting—not definitive—evidence for benign classification.
- Final variant classification always requires integration of multiple evidence categories.
