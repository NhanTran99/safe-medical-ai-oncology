# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is BP1 (Missense Variant in a Gene for Which Primarily Truncating Variants Are Known to Cause Disease)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **BP1 (Missense Variant in a Gene for Which Primarily Truncating Variants Are Known to Cause Disease)** as one of the standardized **Supporting Benign Evidence** criteria within the **ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework**.

This Population Package explains that **BP1 provides supporting benign evidence when a missense variant occurs in a gene in which disease is primarily caused by truncating (loss-of-function) variants rather than missense variants.** In this biological context, a missense variant is generally less likely to be disease-causing. However, **BP1 represents supporting evidence only** and must always be interpreted together with clinical findings, population data, laboratory evidence, and other ACMG evidence categories before a final variant classification is made.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- BP1 is one individual ACMG evidence criterion.
- BP1 considers the biological mechanism by which a gene typically causes disease.
- Missense variants may be less concerning in genes where truncating variants are the primary disease mechanism.
- BP1 contributes supporting benign evidence.
- Final variant classification always requires comprehensive professional evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **BP1** as one of the **Supporting Benign Evidence** criteria within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that when a gene is known to cause disease primarily through truncating variants, a missense variant in that gene may provide supporting evidence toward a benign interpretation. ClinGen SVI provides additional recommendations regarding gene-specific implementation, but these technical refinements intentionally remain outside the scope of this introductory educational package.
