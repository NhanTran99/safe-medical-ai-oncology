## Intended Runtime Usage

Use when a patient asks:
- what EBV-associated gastric cancer means;
- whether previous EBV infection means their tumor is EBV-positive;
- how common EBV-associated gastric cancer is;
- why EBV testing may be requested;
- whether EBV testing is routine;
- what prominent lymphoid stroma has to do with EBV testing;
- whether EBV status determines immunotherapy;
- whether EBV-associated gastric cancer is hereditary.

Do not use as:
- an EBV infection-management guide;
- a laboratory-methodology guide;
- a PD-L1/MSI/HER2/CLDN18.2 testing guide;
- an immunotherapy treatment-selection algorithm;
- a hereditary cancer package.

## Evidence Character

### Established / source-supported
- EBV infection is strongly associated with gastric cancer.
- Approximately 8%–10% of gastric cancers are associated with EBV in NCCN.
- EBV-positive tumors preferentially occur in the proximal stomach.
- EBV-positive tumors are associated with diffuse-type histology and early onset.
- EBV testing is not routinely recommended.
- Testing should be performed when tumor morphology contains prominent lymphoid stroma.

### Context-dependent / emerging
- Prognostic significance.
- PD-L1 elevation.
- Interferon-gamma gene signature.
- Potential sensitivity to PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapies.

### Not established in supplied sources
- Universal treatment selection based on EBV status.
- Individual absolute prognosis based on EBV status.
- EBV blood testing as a diagnostic substitute for tumor testing.
- Detailed assay-specific interpretation thresholds.
