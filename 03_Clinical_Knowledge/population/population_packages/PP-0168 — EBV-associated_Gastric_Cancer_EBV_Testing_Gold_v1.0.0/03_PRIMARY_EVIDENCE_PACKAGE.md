## Clinical Question

> What is EBV-associated gastric cancer, why is EBV relevant to gastric cancer, and when should EBV status be tested in a patient with gastric cancer?

## Evidence Scope

This package addresses EBV as a tumor-associated gastric-cancer biomarker and the current clinical role of tumor EBV testing. It deliberately separates common EBV exposure from tumor EBV status and does not convert emerging biomarker evidence into a treatment algorithm.

## Primary Evidence Sources

### NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026

The NCCN source identifies tumor EBV status as an emerging potential biomarker for personalized treatment strategies.

It estimates that approximately 8%–10% of gastric cancers are associated with EBV infection. EBV-positive tumors are reported to occur preferentially in the proximal stomach and are associated with diffuse-type histology and early onset.

NCCN states that the prognostic value of EBV status remains debated. Some studies suggest better overall survival compared with other molecular genotypes.

NCCN also notes elevated PD-L1 expression and enrichment of an interferon-gamma-driven gene signature in EBV-positive tumors, suggesting potential sensitivity to PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapies. However, NCCN states that more data are needed.

Most importantly for this PP, NCCN states that because of lack of prospective trials and limited understanding of the exact association between EBV and gastric cancer, EBV testing is not currently recommended for routine clinical care. It should be performed if the tumor morphology contains prominent lymphoid stroma.

### NCI — Stomach (Gastric) Cancer Prevention PDQ®

NCI reports consistent and solid evidence that EBV infection is strongly associated with gastric cancer.

The cited systematic review/meta-analysis included 71 articles and 20,361 patients with gastric cancer. Pooled EBV prevalence was 8.77% (95% CI 7.73%–9.92%). Results were consistent across studies although heterogeneous.

The pooled odds ratio for matched-pair studies was 18.56 (95% CI 15.68–21.97). EBV prevalence was higher in the cardia and body than in the antrum.

This evidence supports EBV as a meaningful gastric-cancer-associated factor but does not establish an individual patient-level causal risk calculation.

### ACS — Stomach Cancer

ACS states that most people are infected with EBV at some point in life, usually during childhood or adolescence. EBV is found in the cancer cells of approximately 5%–10% of people with stomach cancer, although it is not yet clear how the virus might cause stomach cancer.

ACS also states that stomach cancers linked to EBV tend to grow more slowly and have less tendency to spread.

### NCI — Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ®

NCI includes EBV infection among recognized gastric-cancer risk factors.

## Evidence-Supported Clinical Model

### Step 1 — Recognize EBV as a tumor-associated feature

A subset of gastric cancers is EBV-associated.

### Step 2 — Understand the clinicopathologic context

EBV-positive tumors are associated with:
- proximal stomach;
- diffuse-type histology;
- early onset.

These are associations, not diagnostic criteria.

### Step 3 — Look for a morphology-based reason to test

Prominent lymphoid stroma should raise the possibility of EBV-associated gastric cancer.

### Step 4 — Perform selective tumor EBV testing

NCCN recommends testing in this morphology context, while not recommending routine testing for all gastric-cancer patients.

### Step 5 — Interpret within broader biomarker context

EBV status is only one tumor-biologic feature. It does not replace HER2, MSI/MMR, PD-L1, CLDN18.2, or broader molecular profiling when those are clinically indicated.

### Step 6 — Avoid treatment overinterpretation

EBV may be relevant to immune biology, but current evidence does not justify an EBV-only treatment-selection rule.

## Critical Distinction: EBV Exposure vs Tumor EBV Status

Most people are infected with EBV at some point.

Therefore:

**Prior EBV infection**
≠
**EBV-associated gastric cancer**

The clinically relevant biomarker question is whether the gastric tumor is EBV-associated.

## Testing Decision

### Routine testing

**Not currently recommended.**

### Morphology-triggered testing

**Perform EBV testing when tumor morphology contains prominent lymphoid stroma.**

This is the key source-supported clinical testing rule.

## Prognostic Interpretation

The evidence is mixed/uncertain.

NCCN reports studies suggesting better overall survival among EBV-positive tumors but explicitly states that prognostic value remains a subject of debate.

Therefore:

> EBV status should not be presented as a definitive favorable-prognosis biomarker.

## Immune Biology

NCCN reports:
- elevated PD-L1 expression in EBV-positive gastric cancers;
- an interferon-gamma-driven gene signature;
- a hypothesis of increased sensitivity to PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapies.

However, more evidence is needed.

Therefore this package treats immune biology as **supporting/emerging context**, not treatment guidance.

## Clinical Interpretation

### A patient with gastric cancer and prominent lymphoid stroma

Appropriate conceptual pathway:

**Gastric cancer diagnosed**
↓
**Pathology identifies prominent lymphoid stroma**
↓
**EBV testing should be performed**
↓
**Interpret tumor EBV status**
↓
**Integrate with other clinically indicated biomarkers**

### A patient with ordinary gastric cancer morphology

Routine EBV testing is not currently recommended solely because gastric cancer is present.

## What EBV Status Does Not Tell You

EBV status alone does not establish:
- treatment choice;
- immunotherapy eligibility;
- hereditary risk;
- family testing;
- exact individual prognosis;
- recent EBV infection.

## Evidence Gaps

1. The supplied sources do not provide a validated EBV-based individual prognosis calculator.
2. The supplied NCCN material does not establish EBV as a routine biomarker for every gastric-cancer patient.
3. The supplied sources do not justify a universal EBV-based treatment algorithm.
4. Detailed laboratory assay methodology is not developed sufficiently for this package.
5. The causal mechanism linking EBV to gastric cancer remains incompletely understood in the patient-facing ACS material.
