# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What is total gastrectomy, when and why might the entire stomach need to be removed for gastric cancer, and what should a patient expect after living without a stomach?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded explanation of total gastrectomy that integrates oncologic selection, surgical meaning, reconstruction concept and long-term consequences while avoiding individualized treatment recommendations or technical operative instruction.

---

# Scope

## Included
- Definition and patient-facing meaning of total gastrectomy.
- Why total removal may be necessary when adequate cancer resection cannot be achieved while preserving a useful gastric remnant.
- Location and extent considerations, including diffuse gastric involvement and selected cardia/proximal disease.
- Adequate gastric resection and negative microscopic margin as the central oncologic principle.
- Conceptual distinction between total and subtotal gastrectomy.
- Conceptual description of what is removed and what may be removed only when directly involved.
- Regional lymph-node management as an integrated component of curative gastric resection.
- Routine spleen preservation and circumstances in which spleen involvement may alter the operation.
- Conceptual reconstruction after total gastrectomy.
- Open, laparoscopic and robotic approaches at a non-technical level.
- Curative-intent total gastrectomy.
- Selected palliative surgical context.
- Potential need for feeding support in selected patients.
- Life without a gastric reservoir.
- Small and more frequent eating patterns.
- Postprandial fullness and eating dysfunction.
- Weight loss and nutritional consequences.
- Vitamin B12, iron, vitamin D, calcium and zinc deficiencies.
- Dumping syndrome.
- Small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth as a long-term issue.
- Diarrhea, reflux, dysphagia and eating restrictions as possible quality-of-life issues.
- Long-term nutritional monitoring.
- Patient-facing questions and common misconceptions.

## Excluded
- Detailed operative steps or surgical technique.
- Detailed gastric, esophageal or vascular anatomy.
- Incision, trocar/port placement, vessel ligation or robotic-console technique.
- Detailed reconstruction or anastomotic construction.
- Detailed D1/D2 lymph-node station anatomy or dissection technique.
- Sentinel lymph-node methodology.
- Detailed Siewert classification or full EGJ/esophageal-cancer treatment algorithm.
- Esophagectomy technique.
- Specific chemotherapy, FLOT, immunotherapy, targeted therapy or chemoradiation regimens.
- Individualized nutritional prescriptions, doses, laboratory schedules or supplementation plans.
- Detailed management of postoperative complications.
- Individualized prognosis or recurrence prediction.
- Detailed surveillance or recurrence-management algorithms.
- Metastatic gastric-cancer treatment.
- HIPEC/PIPAC protocols.
- Hereditary genetic-testing algorithms or individualized risk-reducing gastrectomy decisions.

# Detailed Evidence Interpretation

## Total gastrectomy is an established surgical option

NCCN lists total gastrectomy among adequate gastric resection options for T1b–T3 resectable disease when the goal is negative microscopic margins. This does not mean every T1b–T3 tumor requires total gastrectomy; it means total gastrectomy is one of the available resection forms.

## Diffuse disease

NCI specifically identifies diffuse gastric involvement as a setting in which total gastrectomy may be required. This is stronger than a generic statement that total gastrectomy is sometimes used and therefore belongs in the core scope.

## Cardia disease

NCI describes proximal subtotal gastrectomy or total gastrectomy for cardia lesions with curative intent. NCCN separately requires Siewert assessment for EGJ adenocarcinomas and notes that additional esophageal resection may sometimes be required for adequate margins. PP-0198 therefore uses a conceptual cardia/EGJ boundary rather than owning the full EGJ algorithm.

## Margin principle

NCCN states that adequate gastric resection to achieve negative microscopic margins is central for resectable disease. The PP treats this as the reason preservation of stomach is subordinate to oncologic adequacy.

## Subtotal versus total

NCI reports equivalent survival and decreased morbidity for subtotal gastrectomy compared with total gastrectomy in the cited stage I setting when the lesion is not at the cardioesophageal junction and does not diffusely involve the stomach. This prevents the PP from presenting total gastrectomy as universally superior.

## Lymph-node management

NCCN and NCI integrate regional lymphadenectomy into gastric resection. The PP acknowledges this but delegates detailed D1/D2 knowledge to the dedicated lymphadenectomy packages.

## Spleen

NCCN explicitly states routine splenectomy is not indicated unless the spleen is involved or there is extensive hilar adenopathy. This supports a patient-facing misconception correction.

## Minimally invasive approach

NCCN cites randomized evidence for laparoscopic versus open total gastrectomy in selected early-stage patients and allows minimally invasive approaches in selected cases when surgeon expertise is appropriate. The PP therefore uses conditional wording and excludes a universal superiority claim.

## Long-term consequences

NCCN describes lifetime issues after gastrectomy including postprandial fullness, nutritional deficiencies, dumping syndrome and bacterial overgrowth, with quality-of-life effects including diarrhea, dysphagia, reflux, eating restrictions and fatigue. This supports the unusually deep patient-facing recovery/survivorship portion of PP-0198.

## Feeding support

NCCN says to consider a feeding tube in selected total-gastrectomy patients, especially when postoperative chemoradiation is likely. The PP presents this as selective supportive care rather than a routine requirement.

# Evidence Conflict / Calibration

## No material conflict on the definition

NCI and ACS both support the basic definition of total gastrectomy as removal of the entire stomach.

## No material conflict on the margin principle

NCCN and NCI both place complete/adequate resection and negative margins at the center of curative surgery.

## Potentially different levels of detail on lymphadenectomy

NCCN provides more detailed D1/D2 definitions; NCI gives a minimum regional-node concept. PP-0198 deliberately does not adjudicate the technical branch.

## Potentially different contexts for minimally invasive surgery

NCCN's current guidance is used for the runtime statement: selected cases and experienced surgeons, with restrictions for T4b/bulky N2 disease.

## Survivorship consequences are intentionally prominent

NCCN's current survivorship section specifically identifies lifelong nutritional and functional issues after gastrectomy, justifying their inclusion as core patient education.

# Evidence Gaps

- The supplied sources do not support a universal numeric surgical-margin rule for every gastric-cancer location and histology.
- They do not support a single universal reconstruction method for every total gastrectomy.
- They do not support an individualized nutrition prescription without patient-specific assessment.
- They do not support using total gastrectomy alone to predict prognosis.
- They do not support a universal claim that minimally invasive surgery is superior to open surgery in every disease context.
- They do not support turning PP-0198 into a detailed EGJ/esophageal-cancer treatment algorithm.

# Evidence Package Conclusion

The supplied Source Materials are sufficient to support PP-0198 as a distinct, atomic total-gastrectomy package. The evidence supports a patient-facing explanation of why the entire stomach may need to be removed, how this differs from subtotal gastrectomy, what the operation means conceptually, and why lifelong nutritional and functional follow-up matters. The evidence does not support expanding this PP into detailed operative technique, individualized treatment, or adjacent technical packages.

# Extended Evidence Limitations

## Limitation 1 — Selection Is Not a Single Variable

The supplied sources support a multidimensional selection model involving tumor location, extent, resectability and adequacy of resection. Therefore the Evidence Package intentionally avoids a one-variable indication rule.

## Limitation 2 — Outcome Comparisons Are Context-Specific

The NCI comparison between subtotal and total gastrectomy is retained in its cited stage I context. It is not generalized to every stage, location, histology or patient.

## Limitation 3 — Surgical Approach Evidence Is Selection-Dependent

NCCN's minimally invasive evidence includes selected patients and experienced surgeons. The Evidence Package therefore avoids claiming equivalence in every disease setting.

## Limitation 4 — Survivorship Evidence Does Not Equal Individual Prescription

NCCN identifies deficiencies and long-term issues but individual monitoring and replacement remain clinical decisions. The PP uses the evidence to educate, not prescribe.

## Limitation 5 — EGJ Evidence Has Separate Ownership

Although NCCN requires attention to Siewert classification for EGJ adenocarcinoma, PP-0198 does not own the complete EGJ treatment pathway. The evidence is therefore used only to define a safe boundary.

## Limitation 6 — Hereditary Total Gastrectomy Is a Distinct Context

NCI hereditary diffuse gastric cancer material demonstrates that total gastrectomy can also be risk-reducing in CDH1-related disease. That context is intentionally not merged with treatment of established gastric cancer in PP-0198.
