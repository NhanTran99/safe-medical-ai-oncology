# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is the ACMG Variant Classification Framework?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the **ACMG Variant Classification Framework** as the internationally recognized system used to classify genetic variants according to standardized scientific evidence.

This Population Package explains why standardized classification is essential for consistent genomic reporting, emphasizes that the framework supports expert interpretation rather than replacing clinical judgment, and introduces the framework as the foundation underlying modern clinical genomic reporting.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The ACMG framework standardizes genetic variant classification.
- Standardization improves consistency across laboratories and healthcare systems.
- Classification is based on structured evaluation of scientific evidence.
- The framework supports—but does not replace—clinical interpretation.
- Professional standards evolve as genomic knowledge advances.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize the **ACMG Variant Classification Framework** as the international standard for evidence-based genetic variant classification. Guidance consistently explains that the framework was developed to improve consistency, transparency, and reproducibility in genomic interpretation across laboratories. Recommendations also emphasize that variant classification represents one component of comprehensive clinical assessment and should always be interpreted alongside the patient's history, clinical findings, and other diagnostic information.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- classification standards jointly developed by ACMG and AMP;
- implementation guidance from ClinGen;
- molecular pathology standards from CAP;
- oncology implementation guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- international consistency with ESMO.
