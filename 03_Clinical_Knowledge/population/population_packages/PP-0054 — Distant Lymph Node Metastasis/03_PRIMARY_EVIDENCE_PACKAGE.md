# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is distant lymph node metastasis?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **distant lymph node metastasis**, including the difference between regional and distant lymph nodes, why not every lymph node belongs to the regional lymph node group, why spread to distant lymph nodes is considered distant metastatic disease, and why physicians use established staging systems to classify lymph node involvement.

This Population Package introduces the concept of distant lymph node metastasis without discussing lymph node maps, staging classifications, imaging, biopsy, treatment or prognosis.

---

## Included

- Definition of lymph nodes

- Relationship between distant lymph node metastasis and metastatic gastric cancer
- Importance of established staging systems

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Lymph nodes are distributed throughout the body.
- Some lymph nodes are classified as regional and others as distant.
- Gastric cancer can spread beyond regional lymph nodes to distant lymph nodes.
- Spread to distant lymph nodes is considered a form of distant metastasis.
- Not all lymph node involvement has the same meaning in cancer staging.
- Doctors determine lymph node classification using internationally accepted staging systems together with clinical assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the distinction between regional and distant lymph node involvement;
- classification of distant lymph node involvement as distant metastatic disease;
- the importance of established staging systems for lymph node classification;
- the need for comprehensive clinical assessment before assigning stage.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and presentation of staging systems.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
