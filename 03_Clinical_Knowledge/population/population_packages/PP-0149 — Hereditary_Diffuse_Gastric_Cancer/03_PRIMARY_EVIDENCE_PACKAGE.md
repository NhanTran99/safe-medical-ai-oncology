## Primary Clinical Question
> **What is hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (HDGC), who may be at risk, and what does an HDGC diagnosis mean for gastric-cancer and family-risk management?**

## Evidence Hierarchy

## Core Evidence Statements

### HDGC Definition
HDGC is an inherited cancer syndrome associated primarily with diffuse gastric cancer and lobular breast cancer. It is most strongly associated with germline pathogenic variants in CDH1; CTNNA1 accounts for a smaller subset.

### CDH1
CDH1 encodes E-cadherin, a cell-adhesion protein. Loss of E-cadherin function is associated with the HDGC phenotype. Detailed molecular biology is outside scope.

### Cancer Risk
CDH1 pathogenic-variant carriers have substantial lifetime diffuse gastric-cancer risk, with estimates varying by sex and study. Female carriers also have substantial lifetime invasive lobular breast-cancer risk. Population estimates must not be converted into an individual's exact risk.

### Testing Criteria
HDGC evaluation may be prompted by young-onset diffuse gastric cancer, diffuse gastric cancer with lobular breast cancer, multiple gastric cancers including diffuse gastric cancer, selected early lobular breast-cancer patterns, gastric signet-ring cell carcinoma in situ/pagetoid spread, and other guideline-defined contexts.

### CDH1 → CTNNA1
When testing criteria are met but CDH1 testing does not identify a pathogenic variant, CTNNA1 testing may be appropriate. CTNNA1 has a smaller evidence base and frequency than CDH1.

### HDGC-Like Families
Some families have a strong HDGC phenotype without an identifiable CDH1/CTNNA1 pathogenic variant. This prevents a negative genetic result from being treated as absolute reassurance when clinical suspicion remains high.

## Gastric Risk Management
### Risk-Reducing Total Gastrectomy
NCI describes risk-reducing total gastrectomy as the standard risk-reducing strategy for CDH1 pathogenic-variant carriers. The rationale includes the presence of microscopic, multifocal signet-ring-cell lesions that may not be reliably excluded by endoscopy.

### Endoscopic Surveillance
Surveillance may be considered in selected circumstances, including when surgery is deferred/declined and in some HDGC-like families. Its limitation is that microscopic lesions may be missed, even with careful examination.

## Breast Risk Management
Female CDH1 pathogenic-variant carriers may require enhanced breast surveillance, including consideration of MRI and other risk-management strategies according to age, family history, additional risk factors and patient preference. Detailed breast-cancer management is delegated.

## Family and Genetic Counseling Evidence
Because HDGC is inherited in an autosomal-dominant pattern, a pathogenic germline variant can have implications for relatives. Genetic counseling supports risk assessment, familial testing, result interpretation and communication.

## Variant Interpretation Evidence
CDH1 variants range in clinical significance. The package distinguishes pathogenic/likely pathogenic, benign/likely benign and VUS at a patient level. A VUS should not be treated as proof of HDGC. Detailed classification is delegated.

## Negative Testing Evidence
A negative CDH1 result does not necessarily eliminate hereditary concern when the phenotype remains strongly suggestive; HDGC-like families provide the key boundary.

## Important Uncertainties
Evidence does not support reliable individualized prediction of exactly who will develop cancer, when it will occur, or which CDH1 variant will produce a particular phenotype. Management therefore requires specialist multidisciplinary assessment.

## Clinical Knowledge Flow
**Personal/family history → HDGC clinical assessment → genetic testing → CDH1/CTNNA1 result → risk interpretation → gastric + breast risk management → family/cascade testing.**

This is an educational pathway, not an individualized clinical algorithm.

## Evidence Gaps
The source set does not support a single universal individual risk estimate, reliable genotype–phenotype prediction for every CDH1 variant, a complete catalog of hereditary gastric-cancer genes, detailed surgical technique, or detailed laboratory methodology. These gaps are intentionally preserved.

## Primary Evidence Conclusion
The source set provides a strong, direct evidence base for the locked PP-0149 scope. The NCI HDGC PDQ is sufficiently detailed to support a dedicated syndrome-specific package without importing unsupported general knowledge.
