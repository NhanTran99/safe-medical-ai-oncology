# Educational Objective

> **What is hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (HDGC), who may be at risk, and what does an HDGC diagnosis mean for gastric-cancer and family-risk management?**

The package explains HDGC as a hereditary cancer syndrome rather than simply as a gene mutation.

# Knowledge Blocks

## 1. What Is HDGC?
HDGC is a hereditary cancer syndrome associated primarily with increased risk of diffuse gastric cancer and lobular breast cancer. It is most strongly associated with inherited pathogenic variants in **CDH1**; pathogenic variants in **CTNNA1** account for a smaller subset of families.

## 2. What Causes HDGC?
CDH1 encodes E-cadherin, a protein involved in cell-to-cell adhesion. Loss of normal E-cadherin function contributes to the diffuse gastric and lobular breast phenotype. At patient level: **inherited pathogenic variant → increased cancer susceptibility → HDGC-associated risk**.

## 3. What Cancers Are Associated With HDGC?
Diffuse gastric cancer and lobular breast cancer are the major associated cancers. Early gastric lesions can be multifocal, microscopic, and composed of signet-ring cells.

## 4. Who May Need Genetic Testing?
Evaluation may be prompted by young-onset diffuse gastric cancer, diffuse gastric cancer with lobular breast cancer, multiple gastric cancers including diffuse gastric cancer, selected early lobular breast-cancer patterns, gastric signet-ring cell carcinoma in situ/pagetoid spread, and other guideline-defined contexts.

## 5. What Does a Positive CDH1/CTNNA1 Result Mean?
A pathogenic germline variant indicates inherited susceptibility. It does not by itself prove that cancer is already present. It may affect gastric and breast risk management and relatives' testing/counseling.

## 6. What If Testing Is Negative?
A negative CDH1 test does not always eliminate hereditary concern. Some clinically suggestive families remain **HDGC-like** without an identifiable CDH1/CTNNA1 pathogenic variant.

## 7. How Is Gastric-Cancer Risk Managed?
Risk-reducing total gastrectomy remains the standard risk-reducing strategy for CDH1 pathogenic-variant carriers. Endoscopic surveillance may be considered in selected circumstances, but microscopic lesions may be missed; a normal endoscopy does not necessarily exclude early HDGC.

## 8. How Is Lobular Breast-Cancer Risk Managed?
Female CDH1 pathogenic-variant carriers may need enhanced breast surveillance, including consideration of MRI and other risk-management approaches according to age, family history, other risk factors, and patient preference.

## 9. What Does HDGC Mean for Family Members?
HDGC is inherited in an autosomal-dominant pattern. A pathogenic germline variant may therefore affect relatives' risk and may lead to targeted familial testing and genetic counseling.

## 10. What Are the Important Uncertainties?
Individual risk cannot be predicted precisely from population estimates; genotype–phenotype relationships are imperfect; not every CDH1 variant is pathogenic; a VUS is not proof of HDGC; negative testing does not always remove clinical suspicion; and surveillance cannot guarantee detection of every early lesion.

# Common Misconceptions
- **HDGC means cancer is already present.** No; it is an inherited cancer-risk syndrome.
- **HDGC is only about the stomach.** No; lobular breast cancer is also important.
- **CDH1 is the only gene involved.** No; CTNNA1 explains a smaller subset.
- **A negative CDH1 test rules out hereditary risk.** No; HDGC-like families exist.
- **Normal endoscopy rules out HDGC.** No; early lesions can be microscopic.
- **Every CDH1 variant is pathogenic.** No; variants differ in clinical significance.
- **Every carrier must immediately undergo surgery.** No; management requires individualized multidisciplinary discussion.

# Key Messages
- HDGC is a hereditary cancer syndrome, not simply a gene result.
- CDH1 is central; CTNNA1 accounts for a smaller subset.
- HDGC is associated with diffuse gastric and lobular breast cancer.
- Testing is guided by personal/family history and established criteria.
- A pathogenic germline result indicates inherited susceptibility.
- Negative testing does not always eliminate hereditary concern.
- Risk-reducing total gastrectomy remains the standard risk-reducing strategy for CDH1 pathogenic-variant carriers.
- Endoscopic surveillance has important limitations.
- HDGC has implications for relatives and may require genetic counseling/cascade testing.
- Individual management requires specialist multidisciplinary assessment.
