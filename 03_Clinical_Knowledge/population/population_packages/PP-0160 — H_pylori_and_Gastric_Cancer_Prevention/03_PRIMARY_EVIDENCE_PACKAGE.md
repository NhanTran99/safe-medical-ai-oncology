## Clinical Question

> **How does H. pylori contribute to gastric cancer risk, and what does H. pylori-related gastric cancer prevention mean?**

## Educational Intent

Explain the causal relationship between chronic H. pylori infection and gastric adenocarcinoma and summarize the evidence that eradication can reduce gastric-cancer risk, while preserving uncertainty around generalizability, residual risk, and population-level testing/treatment.

## Evidence Notes

### H. pylori as a carcinogenic infection

NCI states that H. pylori infection is an accepted cause of gastric adenocarcinoma and that IARC classifies it as a Group 1 human carcinogen. The strongest established causal relationship is with noncardia gastric carcinoma.

### Mechanistic prevention pathway

NCI Genetics of Gastric Cancer describes progression from chronic active gastritis to gastric atrophy, intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia, and eventually gastric cancer. This supports the conceptual rationale for preventing persistent infection.

### Randomized evidence

The NCI Prevention PDQ summarizes a meta-analysis of seven randomized studies showing a reduction in gastric-cancer risk from 1.7% to 1.1% (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.43–0.98). The studies were predominantly conducted in high-risk Asian populations.

The Shandong randomized trial of 3,365 participants found a 39% reduction in gastric-cancer incidence during 15 years of follow-up after short-term H. pylori treatment. Longer follow-up to 22 years showed reductions in both incidence and mortality.

### Family-history evidence

A randomized trial involving 1,676 people with a first-degree relative with gastric cancer found gastric-cancer incidence of 2.7% in placebo versus 1.2% after H. pylori treatment (HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.21–0.94).

This supports prevention relevance in a population with elevated baseline risk.

### Asymptomatic infection

NCI summarizes evidence that eradication lowers gastric-cancer incidence in healthy asymptomatic infected individuals.

A pooled analysis cited by NCI reported lower incidence after eradication among asymptomatic individuals (pooled incidence rate ratio 0.62, 95% CI 0.49–0.79).

### Secondary prevention

NCI reports that eradication after endoscopic treatment of gastric cancer was associated with lower incidence of metachronous gastric cancer. The cited pooled incidence rate ratio was 0.46 (95% CI 0.35–0.60).

### Population generalizability

NCI explicitly notes that much of the randomized evidence was generated in high-risk Asian populations and that the magnitude of benefit across populations with different baseline gastric-cancer incidence remains uncertain.

Therefore the package must not convert trial estimates into universal individual risk reductions.

### Antimicrobial stewardship

NCI states that treating all chronically infected people may be impractical and could trigger antimicrobial resistance. NCI's patient-facing source also states that most experts do not support widespread testing and eradication based solely on the available evidence.

## Clinical Claims Summary

### Established / guideline-supported

- Chronic H. pylori infection is a cause of gastric adenocarcinoma.
- H. pylori is a Group 1 human carcinogen.
- Eradication can reduce gastric-cancer incidence.
- Randomized evidence supports a preventive effect.
- Benefit is observed in asymptomatic infected populations.
- Higher-risk populations, including people with family history, may derive meaningful benefit.
- Secondary prevention after early gastric-cancer treatment is supported.
- Antimicrobial stewardship is important.

### Context-dependent

- Absolute benefit depends on baseline gastric-cancer risk.
- Magnitude of benefit across populations varies.
- Benefit after established advanced precursor changes is less straightforward.
- Reinfection and household transmission vary by context.

### Not established

- Universal test-and-treat for everyone.
- Complete reversal of established gastric mucosal damage.
- Elimination of all future gastric-cancer risk.
- One universal prevention policy across all populations.

## Evidence Consistency Review

The NCI sources are internally consistent:

1. H. pylori is causally related to gastric adenocarcinoma.
2. Eradication can reduce future gastric-cancer incidence.
3. Benefit is strongest/easiest to demonstrate in higher-risk populations.
4. Residual risk can remain after eradication.
5. Indiscriminate treatment creates antimicrobial-resistance concerns.

## Evidence Gaps

1. Generalizability of Asian randomized-trial estimates to lower-incidence populations remains uncertain.
2. The magnitude of benefit after advanced atrophic gastritis or intestinal metaplasia is less certain.
3. Reinfection/recurrence patterns vary by population.
4. The optimal population-level testing strategy remains context-dependent.
5. The current source set does not establish a single universal “test-and-treat everyone” policy.
