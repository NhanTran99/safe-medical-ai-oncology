# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> What is immunotherapy and what is its role in the treatment of gastric adenocarcinoma?

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based introduction to immunotherapy as a modern treatment option for selected patients with gastric adenocarcinoma.

This Population Package establishes the foundational knowledge required before introducing biomarker testing, immune checkpoint inhibitors, immune-related adverse events and other immunotherapy-specific Population Packages.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of immunotherapy
- Role of the immune system in cancer treatment
- Difference between immunotherapy, chemotherapy and targeted therapy
- Importance of biomarker testing
- Common biomarker examples (overview only)
- Clinical situations where immunotherapy may be used
- Expected benefits
- General limitations
- Immune-related side effects (overview)
- General patient expectations

---

## Excluded

- Individual immune checkpoint inhibitors
- PD-L1 CPS methodology
- MSI-H testing
- dMMR testing
- TMB testing
- Drug dosing
- Treatment sequencing
- Combination regimens
- Immune-related adverse event grading
- Corticosteroid management
- Resistance mechanisms

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Immunotherapy strengthens the body's immune response against cancer.
- Only selected patients are appropriate candidates.
- Biomarker testing is an essential component of treatment selection.
- Immunotherapy may be combined with chemotherapy in appropriate situations.
- Immune-related side effects differ from those of chemotherapy.
- Early recognition and management of side effects are important.
- Treatment decisions should always be individualized.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary authoritative references.

The major references consistently describe immunotherapy as an important component of biomarker-guided multidisciplinary management for selected patients with gastric adenocarcinoma.

Differences among references primarily involve:

- eligible patient populations;
- evolving biomarker recommendations;
- approved medicines;
- educational wording.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- treatment principles from NCCN;
- evidence interpretation from JNCCN;
- patient-oriented explanations from NCI and ACS;
- internationally harmonized terminology from ESMO.
