# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Immunotherapy?

## Patient Explanation

Immunotherapy is a type of cancer treatment that helps the body's own immune system recognize and attack cancer cells.

Normally, the immune system can identify and destroy abnormal cells. However, cancer cells sometimes develop ways to avoid being recognized.

Immunotherapy helps restore or strengthen the immune system's ability to fight cancer.

---

## Clinical Importance

Immunotherapy has become an important treatment option for selected patients with gastric adenocarcinoma.

---

## Key Concepts

- Immunotherapy works through the immune system.
- It differs from chemotherapy and targeted therapy.
- Not every patient is eligible.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — How Is Immunotherapy Different From Other Treatments?

## Patient Explanation

Chemotherapy attacks rapidly growing cells throughout the body.

Targeted therapy acts on specific biological targets found in some cancers.

Immunotherapy works differently. Instead of attacking cancer cells directly, it helps the immune system respond more effectively against the cancer.

Because these treatments work differently, they may also have different benefits and side effects.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding the differences among treatment types helps patients understand why more than one treatment may be recommended.

---

## Key Concepts

- Different mechanisms
- Different treatment strategies
- Treatments may be combined

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Is Biomarker Testing Needed?

## Patient Explanation

Before immunotherapy is recommended, doctors may perform biomarker testing on the tumor.

These tests help determine whether immunotherapy is likely to benefit a particular patient.

Examples of biomarkers include:

- PD-L1
- MSI-H/dMMR
- Other biomarkers recommended in current clinical guidelines

Not every patient has the same biomarker results.

---

## Clinical Importance

Biomarker testing supports individualized treatment selection.

---

## Key Concepts

- Testing guides treatment.
- Different biomarkers identify different groups of patients.
- Treatment decisions are personalized.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — When May Immunotherapy Be Used?

## Patient Explanation

Depending on the cancer and the biomarker results, immunotherapy may be used:

- together with chemotherapy;
- by itself in selected situations;
- according to current clinical guidelines.

Not every patient with gastric adenocarcinoma requires immunotherapy.

---

## Clinical Importance

Treatment recommendations depend on disease stage, biomarker results and the overall treatment strategy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Selected patients only.
- Biomarker-guided treatment.
- Individualized care.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — What Are the Benefits?

## Patient Explanation

For appropriate patients, immunotherapy may:

- improve treatment effectiveness;
- help control cancer for longer;
- prolong survival;
- work together with other cancer treatments.

The amount of benefit differs among individuals.

---

## Clinical Importance

Appropriate patient selection is essential for maximizing clinical benefit.

---

## Key Concepts

- Benefits vary.
- Patient selection is important.
- Biomarker testing helps identify likely responders.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — What Are the Limitations?

## Patient Explanation

Immunotherapy is not suitable for everyone.

Some tumors do not have the characteristics needed for these medicines to work well.

Even when treatment is effective at first, cancer may eventually stop responding.

Treatment decisions are therefore reviewed regularly throughout care.

---

## Clinical Importance

Continuous assessment is important because treatment response may change over time.

---

## Key Concepts

- Not all patients benefit.
- Ongoing monitoring is necessary.
- Treatment plans may change.

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Possible Side Effects

## Patient Explanation

Unlike chemotherapy, many immunotherapy side effects occur because the immune system becomes more active.

Sometimes the immune system may attack healthy organs as well as cancer cells.

Possible symptoms may involve:

- skin;
- intestines;
- lungs;
- liver;
- hormone-producing glands.

Many immune-related side effects can be treated successfully if they are recognized early.

Patients should report new symptoms promptly.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

Immunotherapy has no side effects because it uses the immune system.

**Fact**

Immunotherapy can cause important immune-related side effects. Early recognition and treatment are essential.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Should Patients Expect?

Patients receiving immunotherapy are monitored throughout treatment.

Doctors evaluate:

- treatment response;
- possible side effects;
- laboratory results when appropriate;
- overall health.

Treatment may continue, be adjusted or be stopped depending on how the patient responds.

---

## Clinical Importance

Regular follow-up helps maximize treatment effectiveness while maintaining patient safety.

---

# Key Messages

- Immunotherapy helps the immune system fight cancer.
- It works differently from chemotherapy and targeted therapy.
- Biomarker testing often guides treatment decisions.
- Not every patient is eligible.
- Benefits vary among individuals.
- Immune-related side effects require prompt medical attention.
- Regular follow-up is an important part of treatment.
