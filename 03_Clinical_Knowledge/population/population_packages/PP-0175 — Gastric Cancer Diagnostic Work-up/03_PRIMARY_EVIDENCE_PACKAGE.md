# Clinical Question

> **How is gastric cancer evaluated after it is suspected or detected, and how are clinical assessment, endoscopy, tissue confirmation, imaging, selected staging procedures and molecular testing combined to establish the diagnosis and determine the extent of disease?**

# Educational Intent

Explain the integrated diagnostic pathway in patient-centered language while preserving the distinction between:

- suspicion;
- definitive diagnosis;
- disease characterization;
- staging;
- treatment-planning readiness.

The package is designed to explain why multiple tests may be required without converting the work-up into a rigid universal algorithm.

# Evidence Notes

## 1. Clinical suspicion is the entry point

ACS organizes the diagnostic pathway around the situation in which stomach cancer is suspected, followed by tests to determine whether cancer is present and, if found, additional tests to learn more about it. fileciteturn20file15

## 2. Endoscopy and biopsy are central to definitive diagnosis

The supplied Vietnam guideline explicitly describes flexible endoscopy combined with biopsy as an important method for gastric-cancer diagnosis and states that histopathology is the gold standard for definitive diagnosis. fileciteturn20file9turn20file3

## 3. Imaging answers a different question from pathology

The Vietnam guideline places CT, MRI, PET/CT and EUS within the imaging/staging framework, while separating histopathology as definitive diagnosis. fileciteturn20file3

ACS similarly explains that CT can show the stomach and possible spread to organs such as the liver and nearby lymph nodes, helping assess extent/stage. fileciteturn20file0

## 4. EUS is a local-staging tool

ACS describes EUS as a way to assess spread into the stomach wall, nearby areas and lymph nodes. fileciteturn20file0

## 5. PET/CT is not universally required

ACS explains that PET can help identify spread but is not always useful for every type of stomach cancer because some tumors have limited tracer uptake. The NCCN pathway uses PET/CT selectively according to clinical context.

## 6. Laparoscopy is selective

ACS notes that staging laparoscopy may be performed along with CT/PET and EUS when accurate staging is important before potential resection. fileciteturn20file11

## 7. Early gastric cancer requires special attention

NCCN v2.2026 recognizes the role of endoscopic resection in accurate staging of selected early-stage cancers. The detailed procedure is downstream from PP-0175.

## 8. Biomarker testing is an interface, not the core diagnostic confirmation

NCCN integrates biomarker testing into the gastric-cancer work-up, while the supplied Vietnam guideline also discusses molecular biomarkers. The detailed biomarker packages are separately owned.

## 9. Laboratory assessment is supportive

ACS describes blood tests for blood counts and liver/kidney function, with additional cardiac assessment in selected treatment-readiness contexts. fileciteturn20file7

## 10. Work-up is not a rigid checklist

Because the guideline pathway contains selective tests such as PET/CT, EUS and staging laparoscopy, the package should not state that every patient receives every test.

# Evidence Consistency Review

The supplied NCCN, Vietnam MOH and ACS sources are concordant on the broad architecture:

**clinical suspicion → endoscopic assessment → tissue confirmation → additional imaging/staging → disease characterization.**

They differ in level of detail and clinical context, which is expected. The package therefore preserves context rather than attempting to force every source into one rigid algorithm.

The Vietnam guideline explicitly separates definitive diagnosis from staging, while ACS emphasizes the patient-facing sequence from suspicion to confirmation and then further tests. NCCN provides the current guideline-level structure for selected imaging, staging and biomarker components.

# Evidence Gaps

The supplied sources do not support:

- one universal diagnostic sequence for every patient;
- one universal set of imaging tests;
- universal PET/CT use;
- universal EUS use;
- universal staging-laparoscopy use;
- individualized diagnostic conclusions without patient-specific data;
- treatment selection from this package alone.
