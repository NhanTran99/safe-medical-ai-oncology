# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is PVS1 (Very Strong Pathogenic Evidence)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **PVS1 (Very Strong Pathogenic Evidence)** as one of the standardized ACMG/AMP evidence codes used during genetic variant interpretation.

This Population Package explains that PVS1 represents **very strong evidence supporting pathogenicity under specific biological circumstances**, while emphasizing that it is **one component of the overall evidence assessment** and not a final variant classification or diagnosis.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- PVS1 is one standardized evidence code within the ACMG/AMP framework.
- PVS1 represents very strong evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- PVS1 alone does not establish the final classification of a variant.
- PVS1 is applied only under defined biological circumstances.
- Professional expertise is required for appropriate application of PVS1.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently describe **PVS1** as one of the highest-strength pathogenic evidence codes within the ACMG/AMP framework. Guidance consistently explains that PVS1 may be applied when predicted loss of gene function is an established disease mechanism and predefined criteria are satisfied. ClinGen SVI recommendations further refine appropriate application of PVS1 while emphasizing that it should always be integrated with additional evidence before assigning a final variant classification.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- evidence standards jointly developed by ACMG and AMP;
- PVS1 implementation guidance from ClinGen SVI;
- molecular pathology standards from CAP;
- oncology implementation guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- international consistency with ESMO.
