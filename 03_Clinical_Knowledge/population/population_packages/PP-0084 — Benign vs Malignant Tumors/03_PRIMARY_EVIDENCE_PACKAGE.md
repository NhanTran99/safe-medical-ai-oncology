# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is the difference between benign and malignant tumors?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based comparison between **benign tumors** and **malignant tumors**, emphasizing that the distinction is based on their biological behavior rather than simply their appearance.

This Population Package helps patients understand that **not every tumor is cancer**, while also reinforcing that determining whether a tumor is benign or malignant requires appropriate medical evaluation.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Comparison between benign and malignant tumors
- General biological behavior
- High-level clinical differences
- Importance of diagnosis
- Appropriate patient interpretation

---

## Excluded

- Tumor pathology
- Biopsy interpretation
- Molecular biology
- TNM staging
- Tumor grading
- Detailed metastasis
- Treatment strategies
- Prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Tumors are not all cancers.
- Benign tumors are non-cancerous.
- Malignant tumors are cancers.
- Biological behavior differs between benign and malignant tumors.
- Accurate diagnosis requires integration of clinical evaluation, imaging and, when appropriate, pathology.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence consistently distinguishes benign and malignant tumors according to their biological behavior rather than imaging appearance alone. All major oncology organizations emphasize that diagnosis requires comprehensive medical evaluation.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI and ACS;
- oncology concepts consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
