## B. Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

1.  Explain what D1 lymphadenectomy means in gastric cancer.
2.  Understand that D1 describes an anatomical extent of regional
    lymph-node removal rather than a simple fixed node count.
3.  Recognize the regional/perigastric nodal territory included in D1
    according to the supplied sources.
4.  Understand the supplied station-based description of D1 as groups
    1--7.
5.  Distinguish D1 from D1+ and D2.
6.  Understand why lymphadenectomy is performed with curative gastric
    resection.
7.  Understand the relationship between D1 and early gastric cancer.
8.  Understand why selected early gastric cancers may instead be treated
    endoscopically.
9.  Understand why examining an adequate number of lymph nodes matters
    for staging.
10. Distinguish lymph nodes removed, lymph nodes examined, and lymph
    nodes found to contain cancer.
11. Understand that D1 selection is clinical-context dependent rather
    than a universal rule for every patient.
12. Recognize the limits of patient-facing information and the need to
    discuss the actual operation with the treating team.

## C. Clinical Knowledge Blocks

# 1. What Is D1 Lymphadenectomy?

D1 lymphadenectomy is a defined extent of regional lymph-node removal
performed as part of gastric-cancer surgery.

The current NCCN source defines D1 in anatomical terms. It describes D1
as gastrectomy with removal of the greater and lesser omenta, including
regional lymph nodes in the right and left cardiac regions, along the
lesser and greater curvature, in the suprapyloric area along the right
gastric artery, and in the infrapyloric area.

This definition is important because it shows that D1 is not simply a
label for a certain number of lymph nodes.

A lymph-node count is a separate measurement obtained from the surgical
specimen and pathology examination.

### Key patient message

> **D1 describes where the lymph-node dissection extends. It is not
> simply a statement that a certain number of nodes were removed.**

# 2. Why Are Lymph Nodes Removed During Gastric-Cancer Surgery?

Gastric cancer can spread from the stomach to nearby regional lymph
nodes.

For a patient undergoing curative gastric resection, removing regional
lymph nodes has two closely related purposes:

-   to remove regional tissue that may contain cancer; and
-   to provide lymph-node tissue for pathological examination and
    staging.

The supplied NCI source describes regional lymphadenectomy as part of
surgical treatment for stage I and stages II--III gastric cancer.

The NCCN source similarly states that gastric resection should include
removal of regional lymph nodes.

This means lymphadenectomy is not an optional add-on simply because the
surgeon wants to remove more tissue. It is part of the oncologic
framework of gastric resection when curative surgery is intended.

# 3. What Does "D1" Describe?

The word D1 refers to an extent of lymph-node dissection.

The supplied NCCN definition emphasizes regional/perigastric lymph
nodes.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline uses a station-based framework in
which D1 corresponds to groups 1--7, with D1+ and D2 representing
progressively broader nodal fields.

These two descriptions should not be treated as competing definitions.
They describe the same clinical concept at different levels of
granularity:

-   NCCN provides an anatomical/regional description.
-   The Vietnamese guideline provides a station-based classification.

For patient education, the important point is that D1 is a defined
regional field.

# 4. Which Areas Are Included in D1?

The NCCN source describes D1 as including lymph nodes associated with:

-   the right and left cardiac regions;
-   the lesser curvature;
-   the greater curvature;
-   the suprapyloric region along the right gastric artery; and
-   the infrapyloric region.

The Vietnamese source describes D1 as groups 1--7.

The package should preserve both terminologies because they are useful
for different audiences.

For patients, "nearby regional lymph nodes around the stomach" is
usually easier to understand.

For clinicians and structured knowledge retrieval, the station-based
description provides a more precise classification.

# 5. D1 Is Not the Same as "16 Nodes"

One of the most important safeguards in this package is the distinction
between:

**anatomical extent**

and

**number of lymph nodes examined**.

The NCCN source states that gastric resection should include regional
lymphatics and gives a goal of examining at least 16 lymph nodes.

Therefore:

**D1 ≠ 16 nodes.**

The number 16 is a node-examination goal, not a universal anatomical
definition of D1.

The ACS patient-facing material uses a simplified description in which
at least 16 lymph nodes are described as a D1 lymphadenectomy.

For the governed knowledge system, the more precise current NCCN
formulation should anchor the package: D1 is defined by anatomical
extent, while node count is a separate quality/staging dimension.

# 6. Removed, Examined, and Positive Nodes Are Different Concepts

Three numbers may appear in a patient's surgical and pathology
documentation:

### Nodes removed

How many lymph nodes were actually retrieved with the surgical specimen.

### Nodes examined

How many retrieved nodes were assessed by the pathologist.

### Nodes positive

How many examined nodes contained metastatic cancer.

These numbers should not be automatically treated as interchangeable.

For example, a pathology report may say that 20 lymph nodes were
examined and 3 contained cancer.

That does not mean that D1 "means 20 nodes" or that D1 "means 3 positive
nodes."

The operation's anatomical extent and the pathology's node count answer
different questions.

# 7. D1, D1+, and D2

The supplied Vietnamese guideline describes a progression:

**D1 → D1+ → D2**

D1 corresponds to groups 1--7.

D1+ extends beyond D1 by adding specified additional nodal groups.

D2 extends the dissection further.

NCCN similarly defines D2 as D1 plus nodes along the left gastric
artery, common hepatic artery, celiac artery, and splenic artery.

This package uses D2 only to establish the boundary of D1.

Detailed D2 anatomy, indications, evidence, and operative considerations
belong to PP-0201.

# 8. When May D1 Be Used?

The appropriate lymph-node dissection depends on the disease context.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline describes D1 for selected early
disease, including particular T1N0 contexts.

It describes broader dissection for more advanced or node-positive
disease.

This should not be converted into a universal rule for every patient.

A patient's treatment pathway depends on factors such as tumor depth,
suspected nodal involvement, tumor location, endoscopic eligibility,
resectability, overall treatment strategy, and the guideline framework
being followed.

The patient-facing message is:

> **The appropriate lymph-node extent is chosen according to the
> cancer's characteristics and the planned surgical treatment.**

# 9. D1 and Early Gastric Cancer

Early gastric cancer does not automatically mean that a patient must
undergo gastrectomy with D1.

The current NCCN source states that selected Tis/T1a mucosal lesions may
be candidates for EMR or ESD when appropriate criteria are met and the
procedure is performed in an experienced center.

When surgery is required, lymphadenectomy becomes part of the surgical
framework.

Therefore the relationship is:

**selected early lesion**

→ possible endoscopic pathway

or

**lesion requiring surgical resection**

→ gastrectomy + appropriate lymphadenectomy.

The detailed endoscopic pathway belongs to PP-0193--PP-0195.

# 10. D1 and Pathological Staging

Regional lymph nodes provide important staging information.

The NCI source describes gastric-cancer N categories according to the
number of regional lymph nodes containing metastases.

This is why an adequate lymph-node assessment matters even when the
surgeon cannot see cancer in the nodes during the operation.

Microscopic examination can identify metastatic disease that is not
visible to the naked eye.

D1 therefore has a staging role as well as an oncologic surgical role.

# 11. Does D1 Guarantee That No Cancer Remains?

No.

A lymphadenectomy is designed to remove a defined regional nodal field
and provide tissue for pathological assessment.

It cannot guarantee that:

-   every microscopic tumor cell has been removed;
-   the cancer has not spread outside the dissected field;
-   no distant disease exists; or
-   recurrence cannot occur.

The operation must therefore be understood within the complete
gastric-cancer treatment pathway.

# 12. Does D1 Mean the Spleen or Pancreas Is Removed?

No.

D1 lymphadenectomy does not mean that the spleen or pancreas is
routinely removed.

The current NCCN source specifically states that routine splenectomy is
not indicated unless there is a relevant reason such as splenic
involvement or extensive hilar adenopathy.

The absence of routine splenectomy is important because patients
sometimes equate "more lymph-node surgery" with "removal of nearby
organs."

These are different decisions.

# 13. D1 in Curative and Palliative Surgery

The purpose of surgery matters.

The NCCN source states that in incurable disease, gastric resection
performed for palliation does not need to include lymph-node dissection.

Therefore D1 belongs primarily to the framework of oncologic gastric
resection with curative intent or appropriate disease-control intent,
not to every operation performed on a patient with gastric cancer.

Palliative surgery may instead focus on relieving obstruction, bleeding,
or other symptoms.

# 14. D1 Versus D2: Why Is There More Than One Extent?

D1 and D2 represent different anatomical extents of lymph-node removal.

D2 extends beyond the regional/perigastric field and includes nodes
along named vessels of the celiac axis according to NCCN.

The choice between them is influenced by disease characteristics,
regional practice, guideline recommendations, and surgical expertise.

The current NCCN discussion notes that D2 has been standard in East
Asian practice for curable gastric cancer, while historical Western
randomized trials did not show a simple overall-survival advantage for
D2 over D1 and reported greater morbidity/mortality in the historical
trial setting.

This does not mean that D2 is "bad" or that D1 is "better."

It means the comparative evidence is context-dependent and belongs in
the dedicated D2 package.

# 15. Surgical Experience Matters

Gastric-cancer surgery is complex.

The ACS source emphasizes the importance of surgeon and hospital
experience in stomach-cancer surgery.

NCCN specifically emphasizes experience for D2, but the broader
principle also matters for D1 because D1 is part of an oncologic
gastrectomy.

Patients can reasonably ask:

-   How often does the surgeon perform gastric-cancer surgery?
-   How often does the center treat gastric cancer?
-   What lymphadenectomy approach is standard at the center?
-   How will the lymph nodes be evaluated by pathology?

# 16. Open and Minimally Invasive Surgery

D1 describes the extent of lymphadenectomy, not the incision or surgical
access route.

The supplied NCCN source allows minimally invasive approaches for
selected cases when the surgeon has appropriate experience with foregut
surgery and lymphadenectomy.

Therefore:

**open surgery vs laparoscopic/robotic surgery**

and

**D1 vs D2**

are different dimensions.

A patient should not assume that a minimally invasive operation means a
smaller oncologic lymph-node operation.

# 17. What Does D1 Mean for the Patient?

For a patient, D1 usually means that the planned gastric operation
includes removal of a defined group of nearby regional lymph nodes.

Those nodes are then examined by pathology.

The final pathology report may provide:

-   the number of nodes examined;
-   the number containing cancer; and
-   other information needed for staging.

The exact meaning of an individual patient's operation should be
confirmed from the operative report and pathology report.

# 18. Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1: "D1 means exactly 16 lymph nodes."

**Fact:** D1 is an anatomical extent. The ≥16-node goal is a separate
node-examination concept in the current NCCN framework.

### Myth 2: "If 16 nodes were examined, the operation must have been D1."

**Fact:** Node count alone does not establish the anatomical extent of
lymphadenectomy.

### Myth 3: "D1 means only a few nodes are removed."

**Fact:** D1 is a defined regional/perigastric field.

### Myth 4: "D2 is always better than D1."

**Fact:** Comparative outcomes depend on disease setting, surgical
expertise, center experience, and the evidence framework.

### Myth 5: "D1 is always safer than D2."

**Fact:** No universal safety claim should be made from the label alone.

### Myth 6: "Every early gastric cancer needs D1."

**Fact:** Selected early cancers may be eligible for endoscopic
treatment instead of gastrectomy.

### Myth 7: "Lymphadenectomy means the spleen must be removed."

**Fact:** Routine splenectomy is not part of standard D1
lymphadenectomy.

### Myth 8: "Negative lymph nodes mean the cancer is cured."

**Fact:** Nodal status is one component of staging and does not by
itself determine cure.

### Myth 9: "D1 tells me how many positive lymph nodes I have."

**Fact:** D1 describes surgical extent; positive-node count comes from
pathology.

### Myth 10: "Palliative surgery always includes lymph-node dissection."

**Fact:** Palliative surgery may be performed without lymphadenectomy
when the goal is symptom relief.

# 19. Patient Questions to Ask

A patient may ask:

1.  What type of gastrectomy am I having?
2.  Is lymphadenectomy planned?
3.  Is the planned lymphadenectomy D1, D1+, D2, or another extent?
4.  Why was this extent chosen for my cancer?
5.  Which regional lymph-node areas will be included?
6.  How many lymph nodes were examined in my pathology specimen?
7.  How many contained cancer?
8.  Does the node count meet the center's and guideline's quality
    expectations?
9.  Does my lymph-node result change my stage?
10. Could my cancer have been treated endoscopically instead?
11. Does the operation require removal of my spleen or another nearby
    organ?
12. How experienced is the surgical team with gastric-cancer surgery?
13. Will my pathology report separately describe lymph-node findings?
14. What parts of my treatment depend on the final pathology result?

# 20. Key Messages

1.  **D1 is an anatomical extent of regional lymph-node dissection.**
2.  **D1 is not simply a fixed number of lymph nodes.**
3.  **The current NCCN source describes D1 as regional/perigastric
    lymphadenectomy performed with gastrectomy.**
4.  **The supplied Vietnamese guideline describes D1 as groups 1--7.**
5.  **D1+ and D2 extend beyond the D1 field.**
6.  **The ≥16-node goal is a separate lymph-node examination concept.**
7.  **The number of nodes examined and the number of positive nodes are
    different.**
8.  **Selected early gastric cancers may follow an endoscopic pathway
    instead of gastrectomy.**
9.  **D1 does not automatically mean splenectomy or pancreatectomy.**
10. **The correct extent of lymphadenectomy depends on the cancer and
    the clinical setting.**
11. **D1 is primarily part of the oncologic gastric-resection
    framework.**
12. **The actual operation should be interpreted together with the
    operative and pathology reports.**

# F. Detailed Clinical Knowledge

## 21. D1 as an Anatomical Concept

The central concept for this package is that a lymphadenectomy is
described by the anatomical field that is intentionally removed.

D1 therefore belongs to the same conceptual family as D2:

**D1 = one defined extent**

**D2 = a broader defined extent**

The difference is not simply the number of nodes found after surgery.

A patient can therefore have a pathology report with a high node count
without that number alone proving that the operation was D2.

Conversely, a lower-than-expected node count does not automatically
prove that the surgeon intended a different anatomical operation.

The operative report, surgical specimen, and pathology report need to be
interpreted together.

## 22. Why the Omenta Appear in the D1 Definition

The NCCN definition links D1 with removal of the greater and lesser
omenta.

For patient education, the important point is that the omenta are part
of the surgical anatomy associated with the regional nodal field.

This should not be interpreted as meaning that every visible lymph node
in the abdomen is removed.

The D1 field is defined by the intended regional anatomy.

## 23. Cardiac and Curvature-Related Regions

The NCCN description identifies right and left cardiac regions and nodal
areas along the lesser and greater curvature.

These regions are anatomically close to the stomach and represent part
of the regional/perigastric lymphatic drainage considered in the D1
field.

The package does not provide operative instructions for reaching these
areas.

## 24. Suprapyloric and Infrapyloric Regions

The NCCN definition also includes:

-   suprapyloric nodes along the right gastric artery;
-   infrapyloric nodes.

These terms describe regional nodal territories around the distal
stomach.

The purpose of including them in this package is conceptual orientation.

Detailed vessel dissection and surgical exposure are outside scope.

## 25. Station-Based Versus Anatomical Terminology

Different guidelines may describe lymph-node fields using different but
related conventions.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline uses numbered groups.

The current NCCN source describes the D1 field anatomically.

The governed package therefore uses the source terminology without
inventing a new unified station map.

This protects the knowledge system from creating false equivalence
between terminology that may be used differently across guideline
systems.

## 26. What Is D1+?

D1+ is an extension of the D1 field.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline describes D1+ as D1 plus specified
additional nodal groups.

The important educational point is that D1+ is not simply a larger
number of lymph nodes.

It is an extension of the anatomical field.

Detailed D1+ clinical selection is not independently owned by this
package unless supported by the applicable guideline context.

## 27. Why Is D2 Kept as a Separate Package?

D2 is clinically important because it expands beyond the D1
regional/perigastric field.

However, giving D2 a full discussion inside PP-0200 would blur package
ownership.

Therefore PP-0200 uses D2 only to answer:

> "How is D1 different from a broader lymphadenectomy?"

PP-0201 owns the detailed D2 question.

## 28. D1 and Surgical Margins

D1 is not the same thing as obtaining a negative gastric resection
margin.

A gastrectomy has several oncologic components.

These can include:

-   removal of the primary tumor;
-   appropriate gastric resection;
-   adequate margins;
-   regional lymphadenectomy;
-   pathological assessment.

D1 specifically addresses the lymph-node component.

The package therefore does not equate:

**D1 = complete gastric cancer surgery**

or

**D1 = negative margin.**

## 29. D1 and Tumor Location

The appropriate operation is influenced by tumor location.

Subtotal and total gastrectomy are separate package topics.

The lymph-node extent is also influenced by the disease context.

PP-0200 therefore does not independently choose subtotal versus total
gastrectomy.

Instead, it explains that D1 is an extent of lymphadenectomy performed
in association with the appropriate gastric resection.

## 30. D1 and T Category

Tumor depth is one of the factors used to describe gastric cancer.

The supplied NCCN source distinguishes T1a disease, for which selected
endoscopic treatment may be appropriate, from disease requiring surgical
resection.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline also links lymphadenectomy extent to T
and N context.

PP-0200 may explain this relationship conceptually.

It does not replace a formal staging package.

## 31. D1 and N Category

The N category is based on regional lymph-node involvement.

The NCI source provides examples in which N categories correspond to
increasing numbers of metastatic regional lymph nodes.

This is one reason an adequate lymph-node assessment matters.

However, N category is not the same as D1.

D1 describes the surgical field.

N category describes the pathological burden of regional nodal
metastasis.

## 32. Why Can More Nodes Be Helpful?

Examining an adequate number of lymph nodes can improve the reliability
of nodal staging.

A low node count can make the absence of detected nodal disease harder
to interpret.

The package therefore teaches the ≥16-node goal as part of staging
quality.

It does not teach that every patient must have exactly 16 nodes.

It also does not use node count alone to reconstruct the operation.

## 33. What Does a Positive Node Mean?

A positive lymph node means that cancer was identified in that examined
node.

This information contributes to N classification.

It does not mean that the lymphadenectomy itself failed.

A patient can have a technically appropriate lymphadenectomy and still
have positive lymph nodes.

The positive-node finding is an important part of the disease
assessment.

## 34. What Does a Negative Node Mean?

A negative lymph node means that no cancer was identified in that
particular examined node.

It does not prove that cancer could not be present elsewhere.

Nodal status is one component of the overall staging system.

The patient should therefore not interpret "negative nodes" as an
automatic guarantee of cure.

## 35. Why the Pathology Report Matters

The pathology report may contain:

-   number of lymph nodes examined;
-   number of lymph nodes involved;
-   other tumor characteristics;
-   margin information;
-   pathological stage-related findings.

These findings are interpreted together.

The package therefore encourages patients to ask the care team how the
lymph-node findings affect the overall stage.

## 36. D1 and Endoscopic Treatment

Some early gastric cancers may be managed endoscopically.

When a lesion is treated successfully through an endoscopic pathway, a
gastrectomy with D1 lymphadenectomy may not be necessary.

This is why D1 should never be described as mandatory for every early
gastric cancer.

The exact criteria for EMR or ESD are owned by PP-0194 and PP-0195.

## 37. D1 and Surgical Treatment

When surgical resection is required, regional lymphadenectomy becomes
part of the oncologic operation.

The exact extent depends on the clinical context.

The package therefore teaches a decision concept rather than a universal
prescription:

**Disease assessment**

→ **determine appropriate treatment pathway**

→ **if surgery is required, determine appropriate gastrectomy and
lymphadenectomy extent**

## 38. D1 and Minimally Invasive Surgery

D1 is not synonymous with open surgery.

The supplied NCCN source states that minimally invasive approaches may
be considered in selected cases when the surgeon has appropriate
experience.

Therefore:

**surgical access**

and

**lymphadenectomy extent**

are separate dimensions.

A laparoscopic or robotic operation can still include an oncologically
defined lymphadenectomy.

## 39. D1 and Surgical Expertise

The quality of gastric-cancer surgery depends on the team's experience.

The ACS source specifically advises patients to ask about surgeon and
hospital experience.

This is especially important because gastric surgery requires
coordinated management of:

-   the primary tumor;
-   margins;
-   lymph nodes;
-   reconstruction;
-   pathology;
-   postoperative care.

PP-0200 focuses only on the lymph-node component.

## 40. D1 and Organ Preservation

A lymphadenectomy does not automatically require removal of adjacent
organs.

Routine splenectomy is specifically not recommended by NCCN without a
relevant indication.

This illustrates a broader principle:

> **Oncologic surgery should remove the tissue required by the disease
> and planned operation, not automatically every nearby organ.**

## 41. D1 in Incurable Disease

The purpose of an operation can change when disease is incurable.

NCCN describes palliative gastric procedures that focus on symptom
control and states that lymph-node dissection is not necessarily
required.

Therefore PP-0200 should not be used to tell a patient with advanced
incurable disease that D1 is automatically required.

## 42. D1 and Patient Expectations

Patients may hear:

> "We removed the lymph nodes."

This statement does not by itself tell them:

-   which nodal extent was used;
-   how many nodes were examined;
-   how many were positive;
-   what the final stage is.

Those are separate questions.

A useful patient conversation therefore includes both the operative
extent and the pathology findings.

## 43. D1 and the Meaning of "Adequate"

The word "adequate" can refer to different things.

It may refer to:

-   adequate gastric resection;
-   adequate margins;
-   adequate lymph-node field;
-   adequate node examination.

The package avoids using "adequate" as a vague synonym for "many nodes."

## 44. D1 and Quality of Pathological Assessment

The number of nodes examined is influenced not only by the operation but
also by specimen handling and pathology assessment.

The current package does not provide technical pathology instructions.

The patient-facing implication is simply:

> The final pathology report is essential for understanding how many
> nodes were assessed and what they showed.

## 45. D1 and Recurrence

Lymphadenectomy is part of oncologic surgery, but it does not eliminate
the possibility of recurrence.

Recurrence can occur through different biological pathways and at
different anatomical sites.

Therefore D1 should not be described as a recurrence-prevention
guarantee.

Detailed recurrence surveillance belongs elsewhere in the knowledge
graph.

## 46. D1 and Systemic Treatment

Surgery and lymphadenectomy are only one component of gastric-cancer
care for many patients.

The final pathological findings may influence subsequent treatment
planning.

However, PP-0200 does not select chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted
therapy, or radiotherapy.

Those are downstream treatment packages.

## 47. D1 and Multidisciplinary Care

The appropriate surgical approach may be discussed within a
multidisciplinary team.

The package can support patient questions about:

-   surgery;
-   pathology;
-   staging;
-   subsequent treatment.

It should not simulate a multidisciplinary tumor board or provide an
individualized treatment plan.

## 48. What the Patient's Operative Report Can Tell Them

The operative report is the most direct source for what the surgeon
intended and performed.

Patients can ask the team:

> "What lymph-node dissection was performed?"

> "Was it D1, D1+, D2, or another extent?"

The pathology report then provides the node examination findings.

This is the safest way to interpret an individual patient's surgery.

## 49. What the Pathology Report Can Tell Them

The pathology report may answer:

-   How many nodes were examined?
-   How many were positive?
-   What was the tumor stage-related finding?
-   Were the margins clear?

It does not necessarily, by itself, fully describe the intended
anatomical dissection.

## 50. High-Value Patient Summary

If a patient remembers only five things, they should remember:

1.  D1 is an anatomical lymph-node dissection extent.
2.  D1 is not simply "16 nodes."
3.  Node count and positive-node count come from pathology.
4.  D1 is one possible extent and is not automatically appropriate for
    every gastric cancer.
5.  The operative and pathology reports together provide the most
    accurate description of an individual operation.

PP-0196 owns principles of gastrectomy.

### Boundary with PP-0197 and PP-0198

Those packages own subtotal and total gastrectomy respectively.

PP-0200 does not decide which gastrectomy is appropriate.

### Boundary with PP-0193--0195

Those packages own endoscopic resection.

PP-0200 only explains why endoscopic treatment can sometimes avoid
surgical lymphadenectomy.

### Boundary with PP-0201

PP-0201 owns D2.

PP-0200 mentions D2 only to establish the D1 boundary.

### Boundary with PP-0202

PP-0202 owns sentinel lymph-node strategy.

PP-0200 does not provide sentinel-node methodology.

### Boundary with pathology packages

PP-0200 explains why lymph nodes matter for staging.

It does not teach complete pathology-report interpretation.

## H. Runtime Answer Patterns

### If asked "What is D1?"

Answer with the anatomical concept first.

### If asked "Does D1 mean 16 nodes?"

Correct the misconception immediately.

### If asked "Is D1 enough for my cancer?"

Do not answer individually.

Explain that the appropriate extent depends on tumor and nodal context
and that the treating team should interpret the patient's case.

### If asked "Why did I have D1 instead of D2?"

Explain that different extents are used in different clinical contexts
and that the patient's operative indication and guideline framework
determine the choice.

### If asked "I had 20 nodes removed; was it D2?"

Do not infer.

Explain that node count does not establish the anatomical extent.

### If asked "Do I need my spleen removed because I need lymphadenectomy?"

Explain that routine splenectomy is not part of standard D1.

### If asked "Can I have D1 without a gastrectomy?"

Do not imply that D1 is a standalone treatment.

The NCCN definition places D1 within gastrectomy.

## I. Final CKO Key Message

> **D1 lymphadenectomy is a defined regional/perigastric lymph-node
> dissection performed with gastric resection. It should be understood
> by its anatomical extent, not simply by the number of lymph nodes
> found in the pathology report. The ≥16-node examination goal is a
> separate staging-quality concept.**
