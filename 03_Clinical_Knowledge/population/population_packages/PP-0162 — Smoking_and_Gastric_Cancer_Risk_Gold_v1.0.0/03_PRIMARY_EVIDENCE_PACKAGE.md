## Clinical Question
How does cigarette smoking affect gastric-cancer risk, and what does stopping smoking mean for reducing that risk?

## Educational Intent
Provide a source-grounded explanation of smoking as a modifiable gastric-cancer risk factor, with emphasis on current versus former smoking and the prevention benefit of cessation.

## Scope

### Included
- Risk association and evidence strength.
- Magnitude of risk.
- Current versus former smoking.
- Exposure concept.
- Cessation and time since cessation.
- Prevention implications.
- Environmental-risk context.

### Excluded
- Generic tobacco education.
- Secondhand smoke/vaping/smokeless tobacco.
- Detailed cessation pharmacotherapy.
- Individual risk calculators.
- Detailed molecular carcinogenesis.
- Alcohol/diet/obesity.
- H. pylori management.
- Precursor lesions.
- Screening/prognosis/recurrence.

## Primary Evidence Sources

### NCI — Stomach (Gastric) Cancer Prevention PDQ
Directly supports:
- smoking as a key gastric-cancer risk factor;
- solid evidence;
- approximately 60% increased risk in male smokers and 20% in female smokers versus nonsmokers;
- average RR 1.6 in current smokers and 1.2 in former smokers;
- declining risk with increasing time since cessation;
- inference that smoking prevention or cessation reduces gastric-cancer risk.

### NCI — Causes of Stomach Cancer
Supports:
- smokers have higher stomach-cancer risk than nonsmokers;
- people who stop smoking lower their risk over time;
- smoking can make H. pylori treatment less effective.

The last point is contextual and does not make PP-0162 an H. pylori-treatment package.

### NCCN Gastric Cancer Version 2.2026
Supports:
- smoking as a gastric-cancer risk factor;
- environmental association, particularly in intestinal-type gastric cancer;
- smoking-cessation advice, counseling, and pharmacotherapy as indicated in gastric-cancer care.

### NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment / Genetics PDQ
Supports smoking as an acknowledged gastric-cancer risk factor and its place among environmental and multifactorial risk factors.

## Evidence Notes

### Magnitude
NCI summarizes a systematic review/meta-analysis showing a 60% increase in gastric-cancer risk in male smokers and 20% in female smokers compared with nonsmokers.

### Current Versus Former Smoking
NCI reports average RR values of approximately 1.6 for current smokers and 1.2 for former smokers, supporting the distinction between continued and former smoking.

### Cessation
NCI reports that risk decreases among former smokers compared with persistent smokers with increasing time since cessation. This supports cessation as a modifiable prevention measure.

### Exposure
The source set recognizes different levels of smoking exposure but does not provide a validated individual absolute-risk equation. Therefore exposure-response is described conceptually only.

## Clinical Claims Summary

### Established / Guideline-supported
- Cigarette smoking increases gastric-cancer risk.
- Smoking is a major modifiable behavioral risk factor.
- Current smokers have greater risk than former smokers.
- Risk decreases after cessation.
- NCCN recommends smoking-cessation support in gastric-cancer care.

### Context-dependent
- Sex-specific magnitude.
- Exposure-response.
- Coexistence with H. pylori, diet, obesity, family history, and precursor conditions.

### Not established as precise clinical outputs
- Individual absolute gastric-cancer risk from smoking alone.
- A universal safe exposure threshold.
- A universal time point at which risk becomes identical to never-smokers.

## Evidence Consistency Review
The supplied sources are consistent:
- NCI Prevention PDQ provides the strongest direct prevention evidence.
- NCI Causes of Stomach Cancer provides consistent patient-facing framing.
- NCCN identifies smoking as a risk factor and supports cessation in gastric-cancer care.

No material contradiction requiring reconciliation was identified.

## Evidence Gaps
1. No validated individual pack-year risk calculator in the supplied sources.
2. No universal time-to-baseline-risk threshold.
3. Insufficient source support for vaping, smokeless tobacco, or secondhand smoke as gastric-cancer-specific exposures.
4. Insufficient source support for a detailed smoking/H. pylori interaction model.

## Future Update Triggers
Update if authoritative gastric-cancer prevention evidence provides:
- validated absolute-risk models;
- more precise exposure-response estimates;
- new evidence for specific tobacco products;
- refined cessation-related gastric-cancer risk trajectories.
