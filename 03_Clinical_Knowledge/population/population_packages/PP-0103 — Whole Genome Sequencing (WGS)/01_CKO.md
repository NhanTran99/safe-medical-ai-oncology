# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS) is.
- Recognize how WGS differs from gene panel testing.
- Understand how WGS differs from NGS.
- Recognize why WGS may be used in selected situations.
- Understand that WGS is not routinely recommended for every patient.
- Feel more confident discussing WGS with the healthcare team.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS)?

## Patient Explanation

**Whole Genome Sequencing (WGS)** is a laboratory test that analyzes **nearly all of the DNA** in a biological sample.

Because it examines almost the entire genome, WGS can identify many different types of genetic changes.

---

## Clinical Importance

WGS provides comprehensive genomic information.

---

## Key Concepts

- Whole genome.
- DNA.
- Comprehensive sequencing.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Is WGS the Same as NGS?

## Patient Explanation

No.

**NGS (Next-Generation Sequencing)** is the **technology** used to read DNA.

**WGS** is one **application** of that technology.

In simple terms:

- **NGS = how DNA is analyzed**
- **WGS = what is analyzed (almost the entire genome)**

---

## Clinical Importance

Technology and testing application are different concepts.

---

## Key Concepts

- NGS.
- WGS.
- Sequencing technology.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — How Is WGS Different from Gene Panel Testing?

## Patient Explanation

Gene panel testing examines **selected genes** that are already known to be clinically important.

WGS examines **nearly the entire genome**, including many regions that are not included in a typical gene panel.

Because of this, WGS can provide much broader genomic information.

---

## Clinical Importance

WGS has a broader scope than gene panel testing.

---

## Key Concepts

- Gene panel.
- Whole genome.
- Genomic scope.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — When Might WGS Be Used?

## Patient Explanation

Depending on the clinical situation, WGS may help doctors:

- better understand the genetic characteristics of a cancer;
- support precision medicine;
- investigate complex or uncommon situations;
- contribute to specialized clinical care or research.

The decision to perform WGS depends on the individual patient and current medical guidelines.

---

## Clinical Importance

WGS is used selectively according to clinical indications.

---

## Key Concepts

- Precision medicine.
- Clinical indications.
- Specialized testing.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does Everyone Need WGS?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Most patients do **not** routinely require WGS.

Doctors recommend WGS only when it is appropriate for the specific cancer type and clinical situation.

Many patients receive excellent care without undergoing WGS.

---

## Clinical Importance

Routine WGS is not indicated for every patient.

---

## Key Concepts

- Appropriate testing.
- Individualized care.
- Evidence-based practice.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Everyone with cancer should have whole genome sequencing.

**Fact**

WGS is a specialized genomic test. Whether it is appropriate depends on the cancer type, the clinical question, available resources, and current medical guidelines.

---

# Key Messages

- WGS analyzes nearly the entire genome.
- WGS is an application of NGS technology.
- WGS provides broader genomic information than gene panel testing.
- WGS is not routinely needed for every patient.
- WGS supports precision medicine in selected clinical situations.
