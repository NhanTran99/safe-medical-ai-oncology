# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is PM6 (Assumed De Novo, Without Confirmation of Parentage)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **PM6 (Assumed De Novo, Without Confirmation of Parentage)** as one of the standardized **ACMG/AMP Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes**.

This Population Package explains that **PM6 represents moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity when a genetic variant is considered likely to have arisen de novo, but parentage has not been formally confirmed, according to accepted professional standards.** It emphasizes that the absence of parentage confirmation reduces the certainty of this evidence compared with a fully confirmed de novo observation, and that PM6 contributes **one category of scientific evidence** rather than determining the final variant classification.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- PM6 is one standardized ACMG/AMP evidence code.
- Some variants appear to have arisen newly in an individual.
- Lack of parentage confirmation reduces the strength of this evidence.
- PM6 provides moderate supporting evidence but does not prove a variant is de novo.
- Final variant classification always requires comprehensive professional evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **PM6** as one of the standardized **Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes** within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that PM6 may be applied when a variant is **assumed to have arisen de novo**, but **formal confirmation of parentage is unavailable**, according to accepted professional standards. ClinGen SVI recommendations further refine the application of PM6 while emphasizing that **assumed de novo status represents one component of structured evidence-based interpretation rather than an independent diagnostic conclusion**, and that evidence strength depends on the quality and completeness of supporting information.
