# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what PM6 is.
- Understand the basic concept of an assumed de novo variant.
- Understand why confirmation of parentage affects the strength of evidence.
- Recognize that PM6 is one individual ACMG Evidence Code within the PM evidence-code family.
- Understand why an assumed de novo variant may provide moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- Recognize that PM6 contributes one category of evidence rather than determining the final variant classification.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is PM6?

## Patient Explanation

**PM6** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant may cause disease.

It belongs to the **PM (Moderate Pathogenic Evidence)** family and represents one specific type of **moderate scientific evidence**.

---

## Clinical Importance

PM6 contributes one category of moderate evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- PM6
- Moderate evidence
- ACMG Evidence Code

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does "Assumed De Novo" Mean?

## Patient Explanation

Sometimes a genetic variant appears to be **new** in an individual because it is **not detected in the tested parents**.

When available information suggests this possibility, but additional confirmation is not available, the variant may be described as **assumed de novo**.

---

## Clinical Importance

A variant that appears to have arisen newly may contribute useful evidence during variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Assumed de novo
- Newly observed variant
- Genetic evidence

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Does Parentage Confirmation Matter?

## Patient Explanation

Healthcare professionals try to use the strongest possible evidence when interpreting genetic variants.

If **parentage has not been formally confirmed**, there is a little more uncertainty about whether the variant truly appeared for the first time in the individual.

Because of this uncertainty, PM6 represents **moderate evidence** rather than stronger evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

The quality and completeness of available evidence influence variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Parentage confirmation
- Evidence strength
- Scientific confidence

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does PM6 Mean the Variant Definitely Causes Disease?

## Patient Explanation

No.

PM6 is **not the final classification**.

It does **not** prove that a variant is truly new, and it does **not** prove that the variant causes disease.

Healthcare professionals always evaluate PM6 together with many other categories of scientific evidence before determining whether a variant should be classified as pathogenic.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification depends on the complete body of available evidence.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Professional interpretation
- Final classification

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Is PM6 Important?

## Patient Explanation

PM6 allows genetics professionals to consider situations where a variant is **likely to have appeared newly**, even when all possible confirmation information is not available.

Using standardized evidence codes helps laboratories interpret genetic findings more consistently and transparently.

---

## Clinical Importance

Standardized evaluation of assumed de novo observations improves consistency in clinical genomics.

---

## Key Concepts

- Assumed de novo evidence
- Standardization
- Clinical genomics

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

PM6 proves that a variant definitely appeared for the first time in the individual.

**Fact**

PM6 reflects an **assumed de novo** observation **without formal confirmation of parentage**.

It provides **moderate supporting evidence** only and must always be interpreted together with many other categories of scientific evidence.

---

# Key Messages

- PM6 is one ACMG Evidence Code.
- PM6 belongs to the PM (Moderate Pathogenic Evidence) family.
- Some variants appear to be newly observed in an individual.
- Lack of parentage confirmation reduces the certainty of this evidence.
- PM6 contributes one category of evidence but is not the final variant classification.
