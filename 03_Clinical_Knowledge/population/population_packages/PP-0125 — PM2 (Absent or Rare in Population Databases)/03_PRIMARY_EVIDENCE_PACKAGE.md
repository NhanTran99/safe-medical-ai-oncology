# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is PM2 (Absent or Rare in Population Databases)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **PM2 (Absent or Rare in Population Databases)** as one of the standardized **ACMG/AMP Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes**.

This Population Package explains that **PM2 represents moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity when a genetic variant is absent from, or observed at a very low frequency in, appropriate large population databases according to accepted professional standards.** It emphasizes that rarity is **one category of scientific evidence**, not proof that a variant causes disease, and that PM2 always contributes to a broader evidence-based interpretation.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- PM2 is one standardized ACMG/AMP evidence code.
- Large population databases help scientists evaluate variant frequency.
- Very rare variants may provide moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- Rarity alone never proves that a variant causes disease.
- Final variant classification always requires comprehensive professional evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **PM2** as one of the standardized **Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes** within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that PM2 may be applied when a variant is absent from, or sufficiently rare within, appropriate population reference datasets according to accepted professional standards. ClinGen SVI recommendations further refine the application of PM2 while emphasizing that **variant rarity represents one component of structured evidence-based interpretation rather than an independent diagnostic conclusion.**
