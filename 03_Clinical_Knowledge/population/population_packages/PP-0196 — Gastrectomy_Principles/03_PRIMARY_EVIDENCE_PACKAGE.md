# 1. Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What is gastrectomy for gastric cancer, when is surgical resection considered, what principles determine how much of the stomach is removed, why are lymph nodes removed, what does an adequate cancer operation aim to achieve, and what should patients understand about surgical approaches, reconstruction, risks, and life after gastrectomy?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

The Evidence Package supports a patient-facing explanation of gastrectomy as an oncologic surgical strategy.

The package must explain the clinical reasoning chain:

**Diagnosis/staging**

→ **resectability**

→ **surgical intent**

→ **extent of gastric resection**

→ **lymphadenectomy**

→ **oncologic completeness**

→ **pathology**

→ **postoperative treatment**

→ **recovery/nutrition**

The evidence must not be used to create an individualized surgical recommendation.

# 6. Evidence Matrix

## Claim 1 — Surgery is a primary treatment option for localized gastric cancer.

**Evidence:** NCCN Principles of Surgery.

**Source role:** Guideline.

**Interpretation:** Surgery is a central treatment option when disease is localized and adequately resectable.

**Patient wording:** “For localized gastric cancer that can be removed safely and adequately, surgery is an important treatment option.”

---

## Claim 2 — The primary surgical goal is R0 resection.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Definition:** Complete resection with negative microscopic margins.

**Interpretation:** The operation should aim to leave no residual microscopic cancer.

**Patient wording:** “The surgical team aims to remove the cancer completely with clear microscopic margins.”

---

## Claim 3 — R1 means microscopic residual disease.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Cancer cells remain at the microscopic level after resection.

---

## Claim 4 — R2 means macroscopic residual disease.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Visible residual disease remains in the absence of distant metastasis.

---

## Claim 5 — T1b–T3 resectable tumors generally require adequate gastric resection and lymphadenectomy in the cited NCCN framework.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Surgery is not limited to removing the visible primary tumor.

---

## Claim 6 — T4b tumors may require en-bloc resection.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Directly involved adjacent structures may need to be removed together with the primary tumor when required for oncologic resection.

---

## Claim 7 — Tumor location influences the type and extent of gastrectomy.

**Evidence:** NCCN and NCI.

**Interpretation:** Distal, proximal, cardia, and diffuse tumors may require different surgical extents.

---

## Claim 8 — Subtotal gastrectomy can have similar outcomes to total gastrectomy for appropriate distal gastric cancers.

**Evidence:** NCCN and NCI.

**Interpretation:** More extensive surgery is not automatically superior.

**Safety:** Do not generalize to proximal, diffuse, or otherwise different disease.

---

## Claim 9 — Regional lymphadenectomy is part of gastric resection.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Lymph-node removal is an oncologic and staging component of surgery.

---

## Claim 10 — D1 and D2 represent different extents of lymph-node dissection.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** D2 is more extensive than D1.

---

## Claim 11 — D2 requires significant training and expertise.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** D2 should be performed in experienced centers.

---

## Claim 12 — A goal of at least 16 examined lymph nodes is included in the NCCN framework.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Adequate lymph-node assessment supports staging.

**Safety:** Do not present 16 as a universal guarantee of surgical quality.

---

## Claim 13 — Routine splenectomy is not indicated.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Spleen removal is reserved for specific oncologic circumstances.

---

## Claim 14 — Minimally invasive surgery may be considered in selected cases.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Conditions:** Appropriate surgeon experience and lymphadenectomy expertise.

---

## Claim 15 — Minimally invasive surgery is generally not recommended for T4b or bulky N2 disease in the cited NCCN framework.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Surgical approach depends on disease complexity.

---

## Claim 16 — Laparoscopic and open approaches can have comparable oncologic outcomes in selected settings.

**Evidence:** NCCN summaries of CLASS and KLASS randomized trials.

**Interpretation:** Minimally invasive surgery can be appropriate without sacrificing oncologic outcomes in selected experienced settings.

---

## Claim 17 — Palliative gastric resection may be used for selected symptoms.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Examples:** obstruction or uncontrollable bleeding when nonsurgical options are not feasible.

---

## Claim 18 — Gastrojejunostomy or endoluminal stenting may be options for selected obstruction.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Not every symptomatic patient requires gastrectomy.

---

## Claim 19 — Staging laparoscopy may detect occult metastatic disease.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Surgical plans may change when radiographically occult peritoneal disease is identified.

---

## Claim 20 — Positive peritoneal cytology is metastatic disease in the cited NCCN framework.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Cytology can materially change treatment intent.

---

## Claim 21 — Gastrectomy changes nutrition and eating.

**Evidence:** NCCN and ACS.

**Interpretation:** Postoperative care extends beyond wound healing.

---

## Claim 22 — Dumping syndrome can occur after gastrectomy.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Early and late dumping represent different postprandial symptom patterns.

---

## Claim 23 — Vitamin and mineral deficiencies can occur.

**Evidence:** NCCN and ACS.

**Examples:** B12, iron, zinc, vitamin D, calcium.

---

## Claim 24 — Feeding tubes may be considered selectively.

**Evidence:** NCCN.

**Interpretation:** Particularly relevant in selected total-gastrectomy patients and specific postoperative treatment contexts.

# 7. Evidence Notes — Surgical Extent

## 7.1 Distal/Subtotal Principle

NCI describes distal subtotal gastrectomy when the lesion is not in the fundus or cardioesophageal junction.

NCI also describes subtotal gastrectomy as the preferred procedure for appropriate distal gastric lesions because survival can be equivalent to total gastrectomy with lower morbidity.

### Interpretation

The evidence supports a preservation-oriented principle:

> If adequate cancer removal can be achieved without removing the entire stomach, total gastrectomy is not automatically necessary.

---

## 7.2 Proximal/Cardia Principle

NCI describes proximal subtotal or total gastrectomy for lesions involving the cardia.

NCCN further requires attention to EGJ/Siewert classification.

### Interpretation

Proximal disease requires different planning from distal disease.

---

## 7.3 Diffuse Disease

NCI states that diffuse stomach involvement may require total gastrectomy.

### Interpretation

This is a disease-distribution principle, not a universal rule for every gastric cancer.

---

# 8. Evidence Notes — Lymphadenectomy

NCCN describes:

### D1

Perigastric/regional nodal dissection.

### D2

D1 plus nodes along:

- left gastric artery;
- common hepatic artery;
- celiac artery;
- splenic artery.

The technical complexity of D2 is emphasized.

### Evidence interpretation

The package should explain:

> Lymphadenectomy is both a treatment and staging component.

Detailed station-by-station teaching belongs to downstream packages.

---

# 9. Evidence Notes — Spleen Preservation

NCCN cites randomized and meta-analytic evidence showing increased morbidity with routine splenectomy without survival benefit in appropriate proximal gastric cancer.

### Clinical interpretation

Routine spleen removal is not part of standard gastrectomy solely because the tumor is proximal or the stomach is being removed completely.

---

# 10. Evidence Notes — Minimally Invasive Surgery

NCCN summarizes randomized trials:

- CLASS-01;
- CLASS-02;
- KLASS-01;
- KLASS-02.

These studies support comparable oncologic outcomes in selected patients.

### Interpretation

The evidence supports **selected use**, not universal replacement of open surgery.

---

# 11. Evidence Notes — Curative Versus Palliative

NCCN distinguishes:

### Curative surgery

Adequate resection with the goal of complete cancer removal.

### Palliative surgery

Symptom relief when disease is incurable and less invasive alternatives are not feasible.

### Clinical safety point

A patient with metastatic disease should not automatically be told that gastrectomy is inappropriate or appropriate.

The decision depends on:

- symptoms;
- resectability;
- alternative interventions;
- overall prognosis;
- fitness;
- multidisciplinary assessment.

---

# 12. Evidence Notes — Nutrition

NCCN and ACS identify:

- eating dysfunction;
- dumping syndrome;
- B12 deficiency;
- iron deficiency;
- vitamin D/calcium issues;
- weight and nutritional problems.

### Interpretation

Nutrition is a core consequence of gastrectomy rather than an optional survivorship detail.

---

# 13. Evidence Notes — Postoperative Pathology

The surgical specimen provides information on:

This package should explain the interface, not duplicate pathology-report interpretation.

# 16. Claim Calibration

## Strong language permitted

- “NCCN recommends…”
- “NCCN states…”
- “The goal is…”
- “Routine splenectomy is not indicated…”
- “T4b tumors require en-bloc resection…”

when the source directly supports the statement.

## Calibrated language required

- “may be considered”;
- “selected patients”;
- “depending on tumor location and extent”;
- “in experienced centers”;
- “can be appropriate.”

These terms should be preserved when the source is conditional.

# 17. Patient-Facing Evidence Translation

## Technical statement

“Complete resection with negative microscopic margins.”

### Patient translation

“The aim is to remove the cancer completely and leave no cancer cells at the microscopic edge of the removed tissue.”

---

## Technical statement

“Regional lymphadenectomy.”

### Patient translation

“Nearby regional lymph nodes are removed because stomach cancer can spread through these lymphatic pathways and because examining them helps determine the stage.”

---

## Technical statement

“R0.”

### Patient translation

“No residual microscopic cancer was identified at the resection margins.”

---

## Technical statement

“En-bloc resection.”

### Patient translation

“If the cancer directly involves a nearby structure, that structure may need to be removed together with the stomach tumor to achieve an adequate cancer operation.”

# 23. Final Evidence Statement

The evidence base supports PP-0196 as a **principles-level gastrectomy package**.

The strongest evidence is for:

- complete oncologic resection;
- negative margins;
- appropriate gastric resection extent;
- regional lymphadenectomy;
- selected minimally invasive approaches;
- spleen preservation;
- staging before surgery;
- post-gastrectomy nutritional consequences.

The package deliberately stops before detailed operative technique and downstream procedure-specific knowledge.

---

# 24. Evidence Integrity Rule

No claim in the CKO should be stronger than its source.

Where a source says:

- “may be considered” → preserve conditional language;
- “selected cases” → preserve selected-case framing;
- “generally not recommended” → do not convert to absolute prohibition;
- “goal” → do not convert to guarantee;
- “associated with” → do not convert to causation.
