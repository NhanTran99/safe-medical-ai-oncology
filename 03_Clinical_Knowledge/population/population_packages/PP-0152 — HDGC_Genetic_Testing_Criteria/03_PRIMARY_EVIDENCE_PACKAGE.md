# Core Evidence: Personal-History Criteria

The NCI summary of IGCLC 2020 criteria includes:

### Criterion 1
Diffuse gastric cancer diagnosed at age **≤49 years**.

### Criterion 2
Diffuse gastric cancer at any age in an individual of **Māori descent**.

### Criterion 3
Diffuse gastric cancer plus cleft lip or cleft palate in the same individual.

### Criterion 4
Diffuse gastric cancer plus a first-degree relative with cleft lip or cleft palate.

### Criterion 5
Diffuse gastric cancer plus lobular breast cancer in the same individual, with both diagnosed at age **≤69 years**.

### Criterion 6
Bilateral lobular breast cancer diagnosed at age **≤69 years**.

### Criterion 7
Gastric signet-ring cell carcinoma in situ or pagetoid spread of gastric signet-ring cells diagnosed at age **≤49 years**.

---

# Core Evidence: Family-History Criteria

### Criterion 8
At least **2 cases of gastric cancer** in the same family, with at least **1 being diffuse gastric cancer**, regardless of age.

### Criterion 9
At least **1 diffuse gastric cancer at any age** plus at least **1 lobular breast cancer diagnosed at age ≤69 years** in another family member.

### Criterion 10
At least **2 cases of lobular breast cancer** in family members diagnosed at age **≤49 years**.

For the relevant family criteria, family members should be first- or second-degree relatives of each other.

---

# Histology Evidence Boundary

The criteria depend on cancer histology.

### Relevant

- diffuse gastric cancer;
- gastric signet-ring cell carcinoma;
- lobular breast cancer.

### Not sufficient for the HDGC criteria

- intestinal-type gastric cancer;
- non-lobular breast cancer.

Therefore a family-history statement such as “my relative had stomach cancer” is not sufficient by itself to establish that a criterion is met.

---

# Verification Evidence

NCI recommends validation of cancer diagnoses and histologies with appropriate medical records.

Relevant evidence can include:

- pathology reports;
- medical records;
- cancer registry records;
- death records where appropriate.

This is especially important because family history may be incomplete or inaccurate.

---

# Testing Strategy Evidence

When HDGC testing criteria are met:

**CDH1 germline testing**

↓

### CDH1 pathogenic variant identified

→ CDH1-associated hereditary cancer pathway

### No CDH1 pathogenic variant identified

→ **CTNNA1 testing**

The package does not interpret the resulting variant.

---

# Affected-Relative-First Evidence

When possible, an affected family member should be tested first.

If no living affected individual is available, appropriate testing of a deceased affected relative may be considered.

If affected-relative testing is not feasible, testing an unaffected family member may be considered in the appropriate clinical context.

This is a testing-strategy principle, not a laboratory protocol.

---

# Multigene Testing Evidence

NCI describes testing for both CDH1 and CTNNA1 when HDGC criteria are met.

Broader multigene testing may also be appropriate when the differential diagnosis includes other hereditary cancer syndromes.

The general design and interpretation of multigene testing belong to PP-0016 and related genetic-testing architecture.

---

# Criteria Versus Diagnosis

Meeting HDGC testing criteria means:

> **the personal/family history warrants hereditary cancer evaluation/testing.**

It does not establish:

- a current cancer diagnosis;
- genetically confirmed HDGC;
- the presence of a pathogenic variant.

Conversely, a pathogenic variant found outside a classic phenotype can still have important clinical implications and should be interpreted appropriately.

---

# Negative Testing / HDGC-Like Evidence

A family may remain clinically HDGC-like after no pathogenic variant is identified in CDH1 or CTNNA1.

Therefore:

**criteria met**
→ **CDH1 negative**
→ **CTNNA1 negative**
→ **clinical HDGC-like phenotype may remain**

This prevents false reassurance from negative genetic testing.

# Evidence Gaps

The Source Materials do not justify:

- modifying the canonical age thresholds;
- broadening qualifying histologies;
- treating all gastric or breast cancers as equivalent;
- creating additional ethnicity-based criteria beyond those stated;
- using testing criteria as a cancer-risk calculator;
- using criteria alone to diagnose HDGC.

These boundaries are intentionally preserved.
