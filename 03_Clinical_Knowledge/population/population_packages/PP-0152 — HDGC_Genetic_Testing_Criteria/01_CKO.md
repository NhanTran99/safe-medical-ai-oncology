# Knowledge Block 1 — What Are HDGC Genetic Testing Criteria?

HDGC genetic-testing criteria identify people whose personal or family cancer history is sufficiently suggestive of hereditary diffuse gastric cancer to warrant genetic evaluation.

Meeting testing criteria means:

> **genetic evaluation/testing should be considered**

It does **not** by itself mean:

> **the person has HDGC.**

The NCI source summarizes the International Gastric Cancer Linkage Consortium (IGCLC) 2020 HDGC genetic-testing guidelines.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Personal-History Criteria

An individual meets criteria through relevant personal history when one of the following applies:

1. Diffuse gastric cancer diagnosed at age **≤49 years**.
2. Diffuse gastric cancer at any age in an individual of **Māori descent**.
3. Diffuse gastric cancer plus cleft lip or cleft palate in the same individual.
4. Diffuse gastric cancer plus a first-degree relative with cleft lip or cleft palate.
5. Diffuse gastric cancer plus lobular breast cancer in the same individual, with both cancers diagnosed at age **≤69 years**.
6. Bilateral lobular breast cancer diagnosed at age **≤69 years**.
7. Gastric signet-ring cell carcinoma in situ or pagetoid spread of gastric signet-ring cells diagnosed at age **≤49 years**.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Family-History Criteria

Family-history criteria include:

1. At least **2 cases of gastric cancer** in the same family, with at least **1 being diffuse gastric cancer**, regardless of age.
2. At least **1 diffuse gastric cancer at any age** plus at least **1 lobular breast cancer diagnosed at age ≤69 years** in another family member.
3. At least **2 cases of lobular breast cancer** in family members diagnosed at age **≤49 years**.

For the relevant family criteria, family members should be first- or second-degree relatives of each other.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Which Histologies Count?

Histology matters.

### Relevant

- Diffuse gastric cancer
- Gastric signet-ring cell carcinoma
- Lobular breast cancer

### Not sufficient for these HDGC criteria

- Intestinal-type gastric cancer
- Non-lobular breast cancer

A family history reported simply as “stomach cancer” or “breast cancer” may therefore require medical-record/pathology verification.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Medical Records Matter

Cancer diagnoses and histology should be validated with appropriate medical records whenever possible.

Examples include:

- pathology reports;
- medical records;
- cancer registry or death records when appropriate.

This is particularly important because family history may be incompletely or inaccurately recalled.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Who Should Ideally Be Tested?

When possible, an affected family member should undergo genetic testing first.

If no living affected individual is available, testing of appropriate material from a deceased affected relative may sometimes be considered.

If affected-relative testing is not feasible, testing an unaffected family member may be considered in the appropriate clinical context.

Detailed testing methodology and counseling are outside this package.

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — What Happens After Criteria Are Met?

The HDGC testing pathway is:

**Meets HDGC testing criteria**

↓

**CDH1 germline testing**

↓

### If a CDH1 pathogenic variant is identified

→ CDH1-associated hereditary cancer pathway

### If no CDH1 pathogenic variant is identified

→ **CTNNA1 testing**

The package does not interpret the individual CDH1 or CTNNA1 result.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What If Both CDH1 and CTNNA1 Are Negative?

A negative result does not necessarily eliminate a clinically suggestive hereditary phenotype.

Some families remain **HDGC-like** despite no identifiable CDH1 or CTNNA1 pathogenic variant.

Therefore:

> **Negative genetic testing does not automatically equal “no hereditary risk.”**

Detailed HDGC-like management belongs to PP-0149.

---

# Knowledge Block 9 — What Does Meeting Criteria Mean?

Meeting criteria means the personal/family history is sufficiently suggestive to warrant hereditary cancer evaluation.

It does not mean:

- cancer is currently present;
- HDGC has been genetically confirmed;
- a pathogenic variant will necessarily be found.

# Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1
**Any stomach cancer in a family qualifies for HDGC testing.**

**Fact:** The criteria specifically emphasize diffuse gastric cancer and relevant histology.

### Myth 2
**Any breast cancer can satisfy the family-history criteria.**

**Fact:** The relevant breast cancer is lobular breast cancer.

### Myth 3
**Meeting testing criteria means the person has HDGC.**

**Fact:** It means genetic evaluation/testing should be considered.

### Myth 4
**A negative CDH1 test ends the HDGC genetic work-up.**

**Fact:** CTNNA1 testing may be indicated when HDGC criteria are met and CDH1 is negative.

### Myth 5
**Negative CDH1 and CTNNA1 testing proves that hereditary risk is absent.**

**Fact:** Some families remain clinically HDGC-like.

### Myth 6
**A family member's reported “stomach cancer” is enough to establish the criterion.**

**Fact:** Histology should be verified whenever possible.

---

# Key Messages

- HDGC testing criteria identify people who warrant hereditary evaluation.
- The criteria are based on specific personal and family cancer patterns.
- Exact age thresholds matter.
- Histology matters: diffuse gastric and lobular breast cancers are specifically relevant.
- Medical-record/pathology verification is important.
- When possible, testing an affected family member first is preferred.
- CDH1 testing is central to the pathway.
- CTNNA1 testing may follow when CDH1 is negative and HDGC criteria remain applicable.
- Meeting criteria is not the same as having genetically confirmed HDGC.
- Negative testing does not necessarily eliminate an HDGC-like hereditary phenotype.
