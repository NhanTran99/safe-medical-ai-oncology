# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a benign tumor?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **benign tumors**, emphasizing that they are **non-cancerous growths** that generally remain localized and do not spread to distant organs.

This Population Package corrects the common misconception that every tumor is cancer while explaining that some benign tumors may still require medical monitoring or treatment depending on their location, size, and associated symptoms.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of benign tumor
- General biological behavior
- Difference from cancer at a high level
- Potential symptoms
- Importance of medical evaluation
- Appropriate patient interpretation

---

## Excluded

- Malignant tumors
- Benign versus malignant comparison
- Tumor pathology
- Biopsy
- Tumor grading
- Tumor staging
- Treatment strategies
- Prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Benign tumors are not cancer.
- Benign tumors usually remain localized.
- Benign tumors generally do not spread to distant organs.
- Some benign tumors may still require treatment because of symptoms or location.
- Appropriate diagnosis requires professional medical evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence consistently describes benign tumors as non-cancerous growths with generally localized behavior while acknowledging that clinical management depends on the individual patient's situation.
