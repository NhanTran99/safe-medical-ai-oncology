# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What are ACMG Evidence Codes?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **ACMG Evidence Codes** as the standardized labels used by genetics professionals to organize, document, and combine scientific evidence during genetic variant interpretation.

This Population Package explains that evidence codes are **professional tools within the ACMG/AMP Variant Classification Framework**, helping laboratories apply scientific evidence consistently and transparently. It emphasizes that evidence codes themselves are **not genetic test results** and are not intended for patients to interpret independently.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- ACMG Evidence Codes are standardized professional tools.
- They organize scientific evidence used during variant interpretation.
- Multiple evidence sources are evaluated together before assigning a classification.
- Evidence codes improve consistency across laboratories.
- Patients benefit from understanding the purpose of evidence codes but are not expected to interpret them individually.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently describe **ACMG Evidence Codes** as standardized components of the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that these codes provide a structured method for documenting different categories of scientific evidence and support transparent, reproducible variant interpretation across laboratories. Recommendations also emphasize that evidence codes are intended for professional genomic evaluation rather than direct patient interpretation.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- evidence standards jointly developed by ACMG and AMP;
- implementation guidance from ClinGen;
- molecular pathology standards from CAP;
- oncology implementation guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- international consistency with ESMO.
