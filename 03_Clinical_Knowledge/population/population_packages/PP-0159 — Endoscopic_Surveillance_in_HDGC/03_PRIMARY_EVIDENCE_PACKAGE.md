## Clinical Question

> **How is endoscopic surveillance used in hereditary diffuse gastric cancer, who may undergo surveillance, how should surveillance be performed conceptually, what are its limitations, and what happens when suspicious or signet-ring-cell findings are detected?**

## Educational Intent

Provide patient-centered, evidence-grounded information about the HDGC-specific endoscopic surveillance pathway without presenting surveillance as universally equivalent to risk-reducing gastrectomy or providing individualized surveillance prescriptions.

## Evidence Notes

### Surveillance cohort

NCI summarizes a prospective cohort of 270 asymptomatic CDH1 carriers who declined RRTG. Participants underwent surveillance with the Cambridge or Bethesda protocol, with recommended intervals of 6–12 months.

The cohort detected SRCC in 63% of patients with available relevant biopsy data. Among patients with malignancy despite visibly normal mucosa, 97% were detected through random biopsies. Among patients who subsequently underwent RRTG, 93% had multifocal stage IA gastric carcinoma and no stage III/IV cancers were detected.

These findings support surveillance as a structured alternative for selected patients but do not establish equivalence to RRTG.

### Visual limitations

NCI states that no definitive endoscopic features reliably identify early diffuse gastric cancer or its precursors. Targeted biopsies of ulcers, erythema, nodularity/nodules, polyps, erosions and scars detected SRCC in fewer than 10% of samples, while pale spots had the highest yield.

### Random/systematic biopsy

Because the epithelium overlying SRCC may appear normal, NCI describes liberal random biopsy sampling as an important component of surveillance and notes that random biopsies improved detection compared with targeted biopsies.

### Surveillance interval

NCI recommends annual surveillance when an endoscopic approach is taken, unless signet-ring-cell features are found. The cited prospective cohort used 6–12-month intervals.

### HDGC-like families

NCI describes yearly surveillance for at least 2 years in selected HDGC-like families, with later interval individualization according to prior findings and family history.

### Enhanced imaging

NCI describes historical chromoendoscopy experience in CDH1 carriers and illustrates small pale lesions detectable with narrow-band imaging. Congo red–methylene blue chromoendoscopy increased biopsy yield in one historical study but did not persist because of concerns regarding Congo red.

These findings support enhanced imaging as an adjunct rather than a universal replacement for systematic biopsy.

## Clinical Claims Summary

### Established / guideline-supported

- Endoscopic surveillance has a role in HDGC when RRTG is declined or deferred.
- HDGC may be microscopic and visually occult.
- Systematic/random biopsy is important.
- Annual surveillance is recommended when an endoscopic HDGC approach is used unless signet-ring-cell features are found.
- Signet-ring-cell findings warrant multidisciplinary discussion.
- Surveillance has important limitations.

### Supported / context-dependent

- 6–12-month surveillance intervals.
- Cambridge/Bethesda protocol use.
- Surveillance in selected HDGC-like families.
- Enhanced imaging as an adjunct.

### Not established by the current source set

- Surveillance as universal equivalent to RRTG.
- One universally superior biopsy map.
- One universal surveillance interval for all hereditary-risk groups.
- Direct extrapolation of CDH1 surveillance recommendations to CTNNA1.

## Evidence Consistency Review

The NCI HDGC source consistently presents:

1. RRTG as the gold-standard risk-reduction strategy.
2. Surveillance as an alternative for selected patients.
3. The importance of systematic/random biopsy because microscopic disease may be invisible.
4. The need for multidisciplinary discussion when signet-ring-cell lesions are found.

This provides a coherent evidence hierarchy for PP-0159.

## Evidence Gaps

1. Long-term comparative outcomes of surveillance versus RRTG remain evolving.
2. Progression of intramucosal signet-ring lesions to advanced disease is unpredictable.
3. Optimal surveillance interval may vary by patient and risk context.
4. The best biopsy strategy and topographic sampling approach continue to evolve.
5. CTNNA1-specific surveillance evidence is not equivalent to the CDH1 evidence base.
6. Enhanced-imaging approaches have not established one universal standard in the current source set.
