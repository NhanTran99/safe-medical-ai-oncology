# Core Evidence 1 — Purpose of Genetic Counseling

NCI defines genetic counseling as a process intended to help people understand and adapt to the medical, psychological and familial implications of genetic contributions to disease.

Therefore PP-0156 treats genetic counseling as:

> **education + risk communication + informed decision support + psychosocial/family support**

rather than merely explaining a laboratory test.

---

# Core Evidence 2 — Pretest Counseling

NCI identifies pretest counseling as a process in which patients can understand:

- why testing is being considered;
- what testing options exist;
- possible results;
- benefits and limitations;
- psychosocial implications;
- family implications;
- confidentiality/privacy;
- potential medical consequences.

The package therefore places pretest counseling before the decision to test.

---

# Core Evidence 3 — Informed Consent

NCI emphasizes that informed consent is an integral part of genetic counseling.

Patients should understand:

- what is being tested;
- why it is being tested;
- potential outcomes;
- potential benefits;
- limitations;
- psychosocial implications;
- medical and family implications.

The purpose is to support a voluntary and informed decision.

---

# Core Evidence 4 — Genetic Testing Does Not Have to Be Accepted

Genetic counseling should not be presented as an instruction to undergo testing.

A patient may:

- accept testing;
- defer testing;
- request additional information;
- decline testing.

The counseling process supports understanding and autonomous choice.

---

# Core Evidence 5 — Possible Test Results

The NCI counseling framework recognizes multiple clinically meaningful result categories.

### Pathogenic / likely pathogenic

May establish a clinically relevant hereditary susceptibility.

### Negative

No relevant pathogenic variant was identified by the test performed.

The result may not completely eliminate hereditary risk.

### VUS

A variant is identified but evidence is insufficient to determine its clinical significance.

A VUS is not equivalent to a pathogenic variant.

### Limited / discordant findings

Some findings require additional evidence or interpretation because the result does not clearly resolve the clinical question.

---

# Core Evidence 6 — Posttest Counseling

Posttest counseling helps patients understand:

- the meaning of the result;
- limitations of the result;
- implications for personal risk;
- implications for relatives;
- whether further evaluation is needed;
- what downstream management pathways may be relevant.

PP-0156 owns the communication/decision-support layer.

Detailed variant interpretation is delegated.

---

# Core Evidence 7 — Psychosocial Assessment

NCI identifies psychosocial assessment as part of comprehensive cancer genetic counseling.

Relevant domains can include:

- motivation;
- beliefs;
- fears;
- coping;
- support systems;
- family communication;
- cultural considerations;
- cognitive limitations;
- socioeconomic factors;
- psychological vulnerability.

This supports inclusion of psychosocial care as a core component of PP-0156.

---

# Core Evidence 8 — Family Communication

Genetic information may affect biological relatives.

NCI discusses the importance of sharing relevant genetic-risk information with relatives and the role of counseling in helping patients communicate this information.

PP-0156 therefore includes:

- preparing patients for family communication;
- considering which relatives may need information;
- explaining that family implications can exist.

Detailed cascade testing is delegated to PP-0157.

---

# Core Evidence 9 — Privacy and Confidentiality

Genetic information is sensitive.

Counseling can address:

- confidentiality;
- information sharing;
- potential insurance/employment implications;
- jurisdiction-specific protections.

Because laws differ between jurisdictions, PP-0156 uses general principles rather than country-specific legal advice.

---

# Core Evidence 10 — Multigene Testing

NCI notes that multigene testing changes the counseling landscape because testing multiple genes can increase the number and complexity of findings.

Counseling should prepare patients for:

- pathogenic variants;
- negative results;
- VUS;
- findings in genes with limited evidence;
- uncertainty about clinical utility.

Detailed laboratory methodology is outside this package.

---

# Core Evidence 11 — Vulnerable Patients

NCI emphasizes additional care when factors may affect comprehension, autonomy or coping.

Relevant circumstances include:

- communication barriers;
- cognitive impairment;
- psychiatric vulnerability;
- acute illness;
- financial/social vulnerability;
- coercion.

Counseling should support an informed and voluntary decision.

---

# Core Evidence 12 — Remote Counseling

NCI describes telephone and video-based genetic counseling as established service-delivery approaches in selected settings.

Remote counseling can improve access while retaining the fundamental counseling goals:

- informed decision-making;
- comprehension;
- psychosocial assessment;
- support.

---

# Core Evidence 13 — Gastric-Cancer-Specific Context

NCI gastric-genetics materials identify hereditary gastric-cancer pathways including:

- CDH1;
- CTNNA1;
- HDGC;
- other hereditary syndromes involving gastric cancer.

PP-0156 does not interpret these genes.

Instead, it provides the counseling framework for patients being evaluated for hereditary gastric cancer.

---

# Evidence Boundary

## What the evidence supports strongly

- Genetic counseling is a clinical process.
- Pretest and posttest counseling are distinct but connected.
- Informed consent is central.
- Psychosocial and family implications matter.
- Results can be pathogenic, negative, uncertain or otherwise difficult to interpret.
- Counseling should support voluntary informed decisions.
- Multigene testing increases counseling complexity.

## What the current source set does not support as a universal rule

- One gastric-cancer-specific counseling protocol.
- One universal counseling duration.
- One universal professional-delivery model.
- One universal threshold for testing.
- One universal legal/privacy framework across countries.

These gaps are preserved rather than filled by unsupported assumptions.

# Evidence Consistency Review

The primary NCI cancer-genetics counseling source is internally consistent with the package scope.

Gastric-cancer-specific sources provide the disease context but do not justify inventing a separate gastric-specific counseling algorithm.

Therefore the package deliberately uses:

> **general evidence-based genetic-counseling principles applied to hereditary gastric-cancer context**

rather than presenting unsupported gastric-specific counseling rules.
