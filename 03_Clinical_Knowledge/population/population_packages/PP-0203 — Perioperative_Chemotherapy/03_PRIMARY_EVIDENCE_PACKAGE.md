# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What is perioperative chemotherapy in gastric cancer, why is chemotherapy given before and after surgery, who may be considered for this strategy, how does the treatment sequence work, what evidence supports it, and how should patients understand its benefits, limitations, and relationship to surgery and other treatment strategies?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with a source-grounded framework for understanding perioperative chemotherapy as a **strategy around curative-intent surgery**.

The package deliberately distinguishes:

- strategy from regimen;
- perioperative from adjuvant;
- perioperative from neoadjuvant;
- chemotherapy from chemoradiation;
- curative-intent treatment from palliative systemic therapy.

It is not intended to prescribe an individualized treatment plan.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Current Disease-Specific Guideline

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

The NCCN guideline is the highest-priority current disease-specific source for PP-0203.

It supports the following framework:

- perioperative systemic therapy for appropriately selected localized/resectable gastric cancer;
- FLOT as a preferred category-1 regimen in the relevant perioperative setting;
- postoperative treatment after preoperative systemic therapy in appropriate patients;
- treatment decisions informed by surgical and pathologic findings;
- selected biomarker-defined systemic-treatment contexts.

NCCN therefore establishes the **current clinical architecture**.

---

# Level I — Randomized Clinical Evidence

## AIO-FLOT4

The NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ reports:

- randomized phase III design;
- 716 patients;
- stage IB–III;
- resectable gastric or gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma;
- FLOT versus ECF/ECX.

Reported results:

| Outcome | FLOT | ECF/ECX |
|---|---:|---:|
| Median OS | 50 months | 35 months |
| Margin-free resection | 85% | 78% |

Reported HR for death:

**0.77 (95% CI 0.63–0.94; P = .012).**

Toxicity rates were similar in terms of hospitalization, but adverse-event patterns differed.

Source:

**NCI — Gastric Cancer Treatment (PDQ), Perioperative Chemotherapy section.**

---

# Level I — Randomized Clinical Evidence

## MAGIC

The NCI PDQ reports:

- randomized phase III trial;
- stage II or higher gastric/lower-third esophageal adenocarcinoma;
- perioperative ECF versus surgery alone.

Reported:

| Outcome | Perioperative chemotherapy | Surgery alone |
|---|---:|---:|
| 5-year OS | 36.3% | 23.0% |

Reported hazard ratios:

- PFS HR 0.66;
- OS HR 0.75.

The educational role of MAGIC is different from FLOT4.

### MAGIC establishes:

> **The broader strategy of perioperative chemotherapy can improve outcomes compared with surgery alone.**

### FLOT4 establishes:

> **FLOT can improve outcomes compared with the historical ECF/ECX perioperative regimen.**

This distinction is retained throughout the package.

# Detailed Evidence Notes

## Evidence Note 1 — Why MAGIC belongs in PP-0203

The package is not merely a definition.

It needs to explain why perioperative chemotherapy exists as a clinical strategy.

MAGIC provides the most direct randomized evidence anchor in the supplied NCI material for:

**perioperative chemotherapy**

versus

**surgery alone**.

The reported improvement in PFS and OS supports the principle that multimodality treatment can improve outcomes compared with surgery alone in the studied population.

The package uses this evidence to establish the rationale for the strategy.

It does not reproduce the complete MAGIC trial methodology.

---

# Evidence Note 2 — Why FLOT4 belongs in PP-0203

FLOT4 is necessary because PP-0203 is not a historical package.

Current treatment strategy has evolved.

FLOT4 connects:

**perioperative chemotherapy strategy**

to

**contemporary preferred regimen selection**.

The evidence reported in the NCI PDQ shows improved median OS and margin-free resection with FLOT compared with ECF/ECX.

The detailed regimen remains delegated to PP-0204.

---

# Evidence Note 3 — Stage and Population

FLOT4 included:

- stage IB–III;
- resectable;
- gastric or gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma.

MAGIC included:

- stage II or higher;
- stomach or lower third of esophagus.

These populations are not identical.

Therefore the package does not collapse the trials into one universal stage statement.

Instead it teaches:

> The evidence base comes from selected patients with localized/resectable disease, and the exact trial eligibility criteria differed.

---

# Evidence Note 4 — Current Guideline Context

The NCCN v2.2026 source provides the current framework.

This is important because the evidence base includes historical regimens such as ECF/ECX, while contemporary practice has evolved.

The package therefore distinguishes:

**historical evidence**

from

**current guideline positioning**.

This prevents presenting ECF as equivalent to the current preferred FLOT framework.

---

# Evidence Note 5 — Surgery Interface

NCI describes surgical resection with regional lymphadenectomy as a treatment of choice for appropriate stage II/III disease and notes that perioperative and postoperative therapy should be considered in addition to surgery.

The package therefore positions chemotherapy as a component of multimodality treatment.

It does not consume the surgical packages.

---

# Evidence Note 6 — Postoperative Decision Context

NCCN describes postoperative management in relation to:

- pathologic stage;
- nodal status;
- margins;
- extent of lymph-node dissection;
- previous treatment.

This is why PP-0203 must explain that the postoperative component is influenced by what happened during surgery and what pathology shows.

The package does not convert these factors into an individualized treatment algorithm.

---

# Evidence Note 7 — D2 / Less-than-D2 Interface

The NCCN source distinguishes postoperative treatment pathways in relation to the extent of lymphadenectomy in selected contexts.

PP-0203 therefore includes the concept:

> **The extent of lymph-node dissection can affect postoperative treatment planning.**

But it delegates:

- D1 definition;
- D2 definition;
- operative technique;
- lymph-node stations;
- surgical morbidity

to PP-0199–PP-0201.

---

# Evidence Note 8 — FLOT4 Toxicity

NCI reports that hospitalization rates were similar between groups in FLOT4, while the types of side effects differed.

Higher rates in the ECF/ECX group included:

- nausea;
- thromboembolic events;
- anemia.

Higher rates in the FLOT group included:

- grade 3/4 infections;
- neutropenia;
- diarrhea;
- neuropathy.

This evidence supports a patient-facing statement:

> Different chemotherapy regimens can have different toxicity profiles.

It does not support individualized prediction of toxicity.

---

# Evidence Note 9 — Margin-Free Resection

FLOT4 reported:

**85%**

margin-free resection with FLOT versus

**78%**

with ECF/ECX.

This is included as a selected evidence outcome because it connects systemic treatment with surgical outcome.

The package does not claim that perioperative chemotherapy guarantees R0 resection.

---

# Evidence Note 10 — Treatment Response

Response can be evaluated before and after surgery using different forms of clinical information.

The package includes the concept of:

- radiologic response;
- pathologic response;
- stable disease;
- progression.

No universal response threshold is created.

No RECIST algorithm is included.

---

# Evidence Note 11 — Treatment Completion

The supplied clinical framework supports the importance of treatment tolerability and sequencing.

The package therefore distinguishes:

**planned treatment**

from

**delivered treatment**.

A patient may not complete every planned treatment component because of:

- toxicity;
- complications;
- recovery;
- disease progression;
- other medical factors.

This is presented as a clinical reality rather than as an efficacy endpoint.

---

# Evidence Note 12 — Biomarker Context

Current gastric-cancer treatment may incorporate biomarker information.

The supplied NCCN material includes:

- HER2;
- PD-L1;
- MSI/MMR;
- CLDN18.2;
- other molecular features

in different treatment contexts.

PP-0203 only explains:

> biomarkers may influence treatment selection.

Detailed testing and interpretation are delegated to the biomarker-specific PPs.

---

# Evidence Note 13 — Perioperative Immunotherapy

The current NCCN material reflects an evolving treatment landscape that includes selected perioperative systemic approaches involving immunotherapy.

This is important context but not core ownership.

PP-0203 therefore states:

> perioperative systemic treatment is evolving and may include immunotherapy in selected contexts.

It does not describe:

- checkpoint-inhibitor regimens;
- biomarker thresholds;
- immunotherapy eligibility algorithms;
- immunotherapy toxicity management.

Those belong to PP-0213–PP-0216.

---

# Evidence Note 14 — Evidence Limitations

The Source Materials do not establish:

- one universal perioperative regimen for every patient;
- one universal stage cutoff independent of other factors;
- one universal number of cycles;
- one universal toxicity profile across all regimens;
- one universal postoperative plan.

Therefore the package deliberately avoids those claims.

---

# Evidence Gaps

The following gaps are explicitly acknowledged:

1. Trial populations differ.
2. Historical and current treatment regimens are not identical.
3. Current treatment continues to evolve.
4. Exact regimen selection is individualized.
5. Postoperative treatment depends on pathology and surgery.
6. Treatment completion may vary.
7. Biomarker-defined systemic therapy is evolving.
8. Detailed regimen and toxicity information belongs to downstream packages.

These gaps do not block PP-0203 because its scope is strategic and educational.

# Evidence Safety Principles

The package intentionally avoids these unsafe shortcuts:

1. **Perioperative chemotherapy → guaranteed cure.**
2. **FLOT → appropriate for every patient.**
3. **Preoperative response → automatic surgery decision.**
4. **All planned cycles → must always be completed.**
5. **Stage alone → automatic treatment selection.**
6. **Biomarker finding → automatic treatment.**
7. **Postoperative plan → predetermined before pathology.**
