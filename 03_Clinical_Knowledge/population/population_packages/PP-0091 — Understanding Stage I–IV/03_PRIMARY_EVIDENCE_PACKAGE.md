# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What do Stage I, II, III and IV mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Stage I–IV** as broad categories describing the anatomical extent of cancer.

This Population Package explains that Stage I–IV summarizes disease extent, introduces the general meaning of each stage, emphasizes that staging differs among cancer types, and reinforces that stage is only one component of comprehensive cancer evaluation.

---

## Included

- General meaning of each stage

- Clinical purpose of stage grouping
- Appropriate patient understanding

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Stage I–IV summarize how far cancer has spread.
- Higher stages generally indicate more extensive disease.
- Stage definitions differ between cancer types.
- Stage grouping supports communication and treatment planning.
- Stage is only one component of comprehensive cancer assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

AJCC and UICC consistently define Stage I–IV as standardized stage groupings summarizing disease extent. Major oncology organizations also consistently emphasize that staging must be interpreted within the broader clinical context and should not be viewed as the sole determinant of prognosis or treatment.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- international staging standards from AJCC and UICC;
- educational principles from NCI and ACS;
- multidisciplinary oncology guidance consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

---

# Evidence Gaps
