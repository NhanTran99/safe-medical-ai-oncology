# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is liver metastasis?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **liver metastasis**, including what the liver is, how gastric cancer can spread to the liver, why liver metastasis remains gastric cancer rather than primary liver cancer, and why appropriate medical evaluation is required before confirming liver metastasis.

This Population Package introduces the concept of liver metastasis without discussing imaging techniques, biopsy, local therapies, systemic treatment or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of the liver
- Definition of liver metastasis
- Relationship between gastric cancer and the liver
- Difference between primary liver cancer and liver metastasis
- Relationship between liver metastasis and metastatic gastric cancer
- Importance of appropriate medical evaluation

---

## Excluded

- Hepatocellular carcinoma
- Cholangiocarcinoma
- M1 classification
- CT
- MRI
- PET-CT
- Liver biopsy
- Liver resection
- Ablation
- Embolization
- Systemic therapy
- Molecular mechanisms of liver metastasis
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The liver is a major organ that may become involved when gastric cancer spreads.
- Liver metastasis means gastric cancer has spread to the liver.
- Liver metastasis remains gastric cancer because it originated in the stomach.
- Liver metastasis should not be confused with primary liver cancer.
- Not every patient develops liver metastasis.
- Doctors determine whether liver metastasis is present through appropriate clinical assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- liver metastasis as a form of distant metastatic disease;
- the distinction between metastatic gastric cancer and primary liver cancer;
- the role of liver metastasis in gastric cancer staging;
- the importance of confirming metastatic disease through appropriate clinical evaluation.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
