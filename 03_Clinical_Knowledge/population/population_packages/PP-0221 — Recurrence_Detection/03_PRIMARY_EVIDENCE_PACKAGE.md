# Clinical Question

> **How is recurrent gastric cancer recognized, evaluated, and distinguished from other causes of a new abnormal finding after previous treatment?**

---

# Clinical-Educational Intent

The package defines the evidence-grounded transition from:

**routine surveillance / new clinical concern**

to:

**recurrence evaluation**

to:

**established and characterized recurrence**

while stopping before recurrent-disease treatment.

# Detailed Evidence Notes

## Evidence Note 1 — GAST-8 is the direct owner of recurrence

The NCCN v2.2026 document explicitly labels the pathway:

**GAST-8 — RECURRENCE**.

It then separates:

**Locoregional recurrence**

from

**Metastatic disease**.

For locoregional recurrence, it distinguishes:

- resectable and medically operable;
- unresectable or medically inoperable.

This is the strongest source for PP-0221 ownership. fileciteturn26file12

---

## Evidence Note 2 — GAST-7 and GAST-8 create a clean surveillance-to-recurrence transition

GAST-7 is the surveillance pathway.

GAST-8 is the recurrence pathway.

Therefore the package boundary should be:

**planned surveillance**

→ **new concern**

→ **recurrence evaluation**.

This prevents PP-0221 from duplicating PP-0220.

---

## Evidence Note 3 — GAST-8 and GAST-9 create a clean detection-to-management transition

GAST-8 identifies recurrence.

GAST-9 provides palliative management for unresectable locally advanced, recurrent or metastatic disease.

Therefore PP-0221 should stop before treatment selection.

This preserves PP-0222 and downstream treatment ownership. fileciteturn26file12

---

## Evidence Note 4 — Peritoneal disease has a dedicated pathway

GAST-10 identifies a separate work-up for peritoneal carcinoma as only disease.

The supplied NCCN material lists:

- CT;
- diagnostic laparoscopy;
- washings;
- PCI;
- possible biopsy;
- FDG-PET/CT consideration;
- EGD consideration. fileciteturn26file0

This supports including peritoneal recurrence as a diagnostic pattern while delegating detailed disease management.

---

## Evidence Note 5 — Endoscopy has a diagnostic role

NCCN GAST-A states that diagnostic and surveillance endoscopies are performed to determine the presence and location of neoplastic disease and to biopsy suspicious lesions. fileciteturn26file0

This supports:

**endoscopy = recurrence-evaluation tool**

rather than:

**endoscopy = PP-0221 procedure package**.

---

## Evidence Note 6 — Imaging should remain conceptual

The recurrence/peritoneal pathways support CT and clinically indicated FDG-PET/CT.

However, the project has separate imaging and response-assessment PPs.

Therefore PP-0221 should explain:

> **why imaging can raise or characterize suspicion for recurrence**

without reproducing:

- image acquisition;
- formal response criteria;
- modality-specific interpretation;
- RECIST.

---

## Evidence Note 7 — Recurrence timing

NCCN GAST-H states:

- 70–80% of gastric-cancer relapses occur within two years;
- approximately 90% occur by five years;
- potentially actionable relapses can occasionally be recognized after five years. fileciteturn26file8

These are population-level observations.

They must not become an individual risk calculator.

---

## Evidence Note 8 — Evidence uncertainty

NCCN explicitly states that surveillance strategies after curative-intent R0 resection remain controversial and that evidence is sparse, with recommendations based on retrospectively analyzed literature and expert consensus. fileciteturn26file8

This matters because recurrence detection is often downstream from surveillance.

The package should therefore avoid implying that surveillance or recurrence detection is perfectly sensitive.

# Clinical Claims Summary

## High-confidence

1. Recurrence is a distinct clinical pathway.
2. Locoregional and metastatic recurrence are distinct.
3. Disease extent matters.
4. Resectability/medical operability matter for downstream management.
5. Imaging contributes to recurrence evaluation.
6. Endoscopy can evaluate accessible suspicious disease.
7. Pathology can contribute to establishing disease.
8. Peritoneal recurrence can require laparoscopy/washings and other targeted evaluation.
9. Most relapse occurs early, but late recurrence can occur.
10. Detection and treatment are separate steps.

## Context-dependent

- Biopsy.
- Imaging modality.
- Endoscopy.
- Laparoscopy.
- Laboratory testing.
- Resectability.
- Multidisciplinary review.

## Not established in the supplied source set

- universal tumor-marker-only recurrence detection;
- universal ctDNA/MRD recurrence detection;
- universal biopsy;
- one diagnostic algorithm for every recurrence pattern.

---

# Evidence Gaps

## Gap 1 — No universal recurrence-detection algorithm

The supplied materials do not establish one algorithm for every patient.

## Gap 2 — No universal biopsy rule

The need for biopsy is context-dependent.

## Gap 3 — No universal tumor-marker rule

No fixed tumor-marker threshold or tumor-marker-only diagnosis should be invented.

## Gap 4 — Molecular recurrence detection

The source set does not support making ctDNA/MRD a universal standard in PP-0221.

## Gap 5 — Late recurrence

Late relapse is recognized, but the evidence does not justify a universal intensive diagnostic program beyond five years.

# Evidence-to-Patient Translation

## Technical

Recurrence may be locoregional or metastatic.

## Patient translation

> **If cancer returns, it may come back near the original area or appear in distant parts of the body. The evaluation depends on where the doctors think it may be.**

---

## Technical

Imaging contributes to recurrence evaluation.

## Patient translation

> **A scan may show an area that needs further investigation, but a suspicious scan does not automatically prove that the cancer has returned.**

---

## Technical

Diagnostic endoscopy can identify and biopsy suspicious lesions.

## Patient translation

> **If the suspected problem is in an area that can be reached with an endoscope, an endoscopy may help doctors look directly at the area and take a tissue sample if appropriate.**

---

## Technical

Peritoneal disease may require laparoscopy and washings.

## Patient translation

> **If doctors are concerned that cancer may have spread to the lining of the abdomen, a different type of evaluation may sometimes be needed.**

---

## Technical

Recurrence management depends on resectability and medical fitness.

## Patient translation

> **Once recurrence is confirmed, doctors need to understand how much disease is present and whether it can potentially be removed or needs another form of treatment.**

# Boundary Verification

## Core

Recurrence detection after prior gastric-cancer treatment; definition of recurrence; distinction from persistent disease and progression during active treatment; transition from surveillance/new concern to recurrence evaluation; symptoms and clinical triggers; clinical reassessment; conceptual roles of laboratory testing, CT, FDG-PET/CT, MRI where appropriate, EGD, biopsy/pathology and diagnostic laparoscopy; characterization of local/locoregional, metastatic and peritoneal recurrence; disease distribution and extent; conceptual resectability/medical operability; distinction between suspected and established recurrence; recurrence after different treatment pathways; late recurrence; patient-facing interpretation; diagnostic limitations and uncertainty; handoff to recurrent-disease management.

## Supporting

Treatment-context interface; postoperative and treatment-related alternative explanations for new findings; multidisciplinary review; high-level staging/restaging concepts; patient questions and misconceptions; high-level relationship to biomarkers and molecular testing without creating a molecular recurrence-detection algorithm.

## Explicitly Excluded

Routine surveillance schedules; detailed post-treatment imaging methodology; RECIST/iRECIST; detailed CT/MRI/PET methodology; detailed endoscopy or biopsy technique; detailed pathology; detailed TNM staging methodology; recurrent-disease treatment; systemic therapy; immunotherapy; targeted therapy; recurrent surgery algorithms; detailed peritoneal cytoreduction/HIPEC/PIPAC; palliative care; individualized recurrence diagnosis; individualized recurrence probability; individualized treatment recommendations; universal tumor-marker-only recurrence detection; universal ctDNA/MRD recurrence algorithm.

## Delegated-to PP

PP-0217 Response Assessment; PP-0218 RECIST-based Assessment; PP-0219 Post-treatment Imaging; PP-0220 Surveillance After Gastric Cancer Treatment; PP-0222 Management of Recurrent Gastric Cancer; PP-0223 Metastatic Gastric Cancer; PP-0224 Peritoneal Carcinomatosis; PP-0225 Peritoneal Carcinoma as Only Disease; PP-0226 HIPEC; PP-0227 Palliative Care; PP-0228 Best Supportive Care; PP-0229 Gastric Cancer Survivorship; PP-0230 Long-term Follow-up; dedicated pathology, biopsy, endoscopy, imaging, molecular/biomarker and multidisciplinary-management packages.
