# Clinical-Educational Intent

PP-0221 explains the clinical reasoning pathway that begins when a previously treated gastric-cancer patient develops a finding that raises concern for recurrence.

The package is not a list of recurrence symptoms and is not a treatment package.

It answers:

> **What happens when clinicians need to determine whether gastric cancer has returned?**

# Knowledge Units

## KU-01 — Recurrence

Return of previously treated gastric cancer.

## KU-02 — Recurrence vs Progression

Recurrence is distinguished from progression during active treatment by clinical context.

## KU-03 — Trigger

New symptom, examination finding or abnormal test that raises concern.

## KU-04 — Clinical Reassessment

Determine whether the finding plausibly represents recurrence and what further evaluation is appropriate.

## KU-05 — Imaging

CT and selected other imaging modalities may identify suspicious disease.

## KU-06 — Endoscopy

Useful for suspected disease in endoscopically accessible locations.

## KU-07 — Pathology

Can provide tissue confirmation when clinically appropriate.

## KU-08 — Local/Locoregional Recurrence

Recurrence confined to local/regional disease territory.

## KU-09 — Metastatic Recurrence

Recurrence with distant disease.

## KU-10 — Peritoneal Recurrence

Peritoneal dissemination requiring specialized evaluation in selected cases.

## KU-11 — Disease Extent

Characterization of where and how extensively disease is present.

## KU-12 — Resectability

Conceptual assessment of whether disease may be amenable to surgical removal.

## KU-13 — Medical Operability

Whether the patient is medically fit for a proposed surgical approach.

## KU-14 — Established Recurrence

Recurrence sufficiently characterized to enter management.

## KU-15 — Late Recurrence

Recurrence can occasionally occur after five years.

# Evidence Classification

## Established / Guideline-Supported

- Dedicated NCCN GAST-8 recurrence pathway.
- Locoregional versus metastatic distinction.
- Surgery consideration for isolated resectable locoregional recurrence in medically fit patients.
- Palliative-management pathway for unresectable/medically inoperable or metastatic disease.
- CT and FDG-PET/CT roles in relevant recurrent/metastatic work-up.
- Endoscopy and biopsy of suspicious lesions.
- Peritoneal-only disease has a dedicated diagnostic pathway.
- Most relapse occurs within two years; approximately 90% occurs by five years, with occasional actionable relapse beyond five years.

## Context-Dependent

- Biopsy.
- Imaging modality.
- Endoscopy.
- Diagnostic laparoscopy.
- Laboratory testing.
- Resectability.
- Multidisciplinary review.

## Emerging / Uncertain

- Universal ctDNA recurrence detection.
- Universal MRD monitoring.
- Universal tumor-marker-only recurrence detection.
- Universal biopsy requirement.
- One diagnostic algorithm for all recurrence patterns.

## Not Established / Excluded

- One test that always proves recurrence.
- Universal tumor-marker threshold.
- Universal ctDNA threshold.
- Individual recurrence probability from population timing data.

# Safety Rules

1. Never diagnose recurrence from symptoms alone.
2. Never diagnose recurrence from a single imaging finding alone.
3. Never imply that every suspected recurrence requires biopsy.
4. Never create a universal tumor-marker recurrence algorithm.
5. Never create a universal ctDNA/MRD recurrence algorithm.
6. Never convert population recurrence timing into individual recurrence probability.
7. Never prescribe individualized imaging or biopsy.
8. Never prescribe recurrence treatment.
9. Never reproduce RECIST methodology.
10. Preserve the distinction between recurrence and progression during active treatment.
11. Preserve the distinction between suspected and established recurrence.
12. Preserve the handoff to PP-0222 once recurrence is established/characterized.

# Boundary

Core = Recurrence detection after prior gastric-cancer treatment; definition of recurrence; distinction from persistent disease and progression during active treatment; transition from surveillance/new concern to recurrence evaluation; symptoms and clinical triggers; clinical reassessment; conceptual roles of laboratory testing, CT, FDG-PET/CT, MRI where appropriate, EGD, biopsy/pathology and diagnostic laparoscopy; characterization of local/locoregional, metastatic and peritoneal recurrence; disease distribution and extent; conceptual resectability/medical operability; distinction between suspected and established recurrence; recurrence after different treatment pathways; late recurrence; patient-facing interpretation; diagnostic limitations and uncertainty; handoff to recurrent-disease management.

Supporting = Treatment-context interface; postoperative and treatment-related alternative explanations for new findings; multidisciplinary review; high-level staging/restaging concepts; patient questions and misconceptions; high-level relationship to biomarkers and molecular testing without creating a molecular recurrence-detection algorithm.

Explicitly Excluded = Routine surveillance schedules; detailed post-treatment imaging methodology; RECIST/iRECIST; detailed CT/MRI/PET methodology; detailed endoscopy or biopsy technique; detailed pathology; detailed TNM staging methodology; recurrent-disease treatment; systemic therapy; immunotherapy; targeted therapy; recurrent surgery algorithms; detailed peritoneal cytoreduction/HIPEC/PIPAC; palliative care; individualized recurrence diagnosis; individualized recurrence probability; individualized treatment recommendations; universal tumor-marker-only recurrence detection; universal ctDNA/MRD recurrence algorithm.
