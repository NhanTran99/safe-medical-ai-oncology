# 1. Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

**What is targeted therapy for gastric cancer, how is it different from chemotherapy and immunotherapy, when can it be used, and why do biomarker results matter?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Build the evidence foundation for the modality-level targeted-therapy concept without duplicating:

- detailed biomarker testing;
- biomarker-directed treatment selection;
- individual targeted-treatment packages;
- individualized prescribing.

The evidence architecture is:

**target biology**

→ **biomarker evidence**

→ **treatment relevance**

→ **clinical treatment context**

→ **benefit / burden**

→ **limitations**.

# 2. Evidence Scope

## Included

- Targeted therapy definition.
- Targeted therapy as a gastric-cancer systemic-treatment modality.
- Biomarker-treatment relationship.
- HER2-directed treatment as a major paradigm.
- CLDN18.2-directed treatment as a major paradigm.
- VEGFR-2-directed treatment as a major paradigm.
- Selected NTRK, BRAF V600E and RET alterations.
- First-line and subsequent-line context.
- Combination therapy and selected single-agent targeted therapy.
- Selected representative efficacy evidence.
- General safety principles.
- Resistance and tumor-evolution concepts.
- Biomarker reassessment concept.
- Patient-facing interpretation.

## Excluded

- Detailed assay methodology.
- Detailed dosing.
- Detailed administration.
- Detailed treatment sequencing.
- Individualized treatment recommendations.
- Therapy-specific toxicity algorithms.
- Therapy-specific resistance algorithms.

# 6. Evidence Matrix — HER2

## Core Evidence

The supplied NCCN v2.2026 source identifies trastuzumab-containing combinations for advanced HER2-positive gastric cancer.

The guideline also identifies fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan as a preferred subsequent-treatment option for HER2 overexpression-positive adenocarcinoma.

## Representative Trial Evidence

The NCCN source describes the ToGA evidence supporting addition of trastuzumab to chemotherapy in HER2 overexpression-positive advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma.

The NCI PDQ describes DESTINY-Gastric01, in which trastuzumab deruxtecan produced a higher objective response rate and longer median overall survival than physician-choice chemotherapy in the specified previously treated HER2-positive population.

The NCI PDQ also reports clinically important adverse events, including severe neutropenia, anemia, and drug-related interstitial lung disease.

## Interpretation for PP-0209

The evidence establishes a general principle:

> **A validated treatment-relevant target can create a clinically meaningful treatment branch.**

PP-0209 does not reproduce the complete HER2 treatment algorithm.

# 7. Evidence Matrix — CLDN18.2

## Core Evidence

The supplied NCCN v2.2026 source identifies fluoropyrimidine/oxaliplatin plus zolbetuximab as a category 1 first-line option for appropriate CLDN18.2-positive disease.

## Representative Trial Evidence

NCCN summarizes SPOTLIGHT and GLOW, both of which demonstrated improvement in progression-free and overall survival measures with zolbetuximab-containing therapy in the specified populations.

NCI PDQ likewise summarizes the evidence supporting zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy in CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced gastroesophageal/gastric adenocarcinoma.

## Interpretation for PP-0209

CLDN18.2 demonstrates:

- protein-targeted treatment;
- biomarker-selected therapy;
- combination therapy;
- first-line targeted treatment.

Detailed CLDN18.2 testing and zolbetuximab management remain downstream.

# 8. Evidence Matrix — VEGFR-2 / Ramucirumab

The supplied NCCN and NCI sources support ramucirumab-containing treatment in selected previously treated advanced gastric cancer.

The evidence includes:

- REGARD: ramucirumab monotherapy versus placebo;
- RAINBOW: ramucirumab plus paclitaxel versus paclitaxel.

The NCI PDQ and NCCN framework use these studies to support the role of ramucirumab in later-line treatment.

## Interpretation

Ramucirumab is important for PP-0209 because it demonstrates that targeted therapy can focus on a receptor/pathway involved in tumor biology rather than on a tumor-specific genomic mutation.

Detailed anti-angiogenic treatment is delegated to PP-0212.

# 9. Evidence Matrix — NTRK, BRAF V600E, RET

## NTRK

NCCN identifies entrectinib, larotrectinib, or repotrectinib for NTRK fusion-positive tumors in selected circumstances.

ACS similarly describes selected advanced cancers with NTRK changes as candidates for TRK-directed therapy.

## BRAF V600E

NCCN identifies dabrafenib plus trametinib for selected BRAF V600E-mutated tumors.

ACS describes BRAF-directed treatment as an option for selected cancers with the relevant alteration.

## RET

NCCN identifies selpercatinib for selected RET fusion-positive tumors.

ACS describes RET-directed treatment as a potential option in selected advanced disease.

## Interpretation

These examples establish that targeted treatment can be driven by rare genomic alterations.

They should be treated as **supporting examples**, not as the dominant gastric-cancer targeted-therapy pathway.

# 11. Evidence Matrix — Combination Treatment

The supplied ACS and NCCN materials support the concept that targeted therapy may be combined with chemotherapy.

Representative examples include:

- trastuzumab + chemotherapy;
- zolbetuximab + chemotherapy.

The ACS patient-facing source also describes selected circumstances in which chemotherapy, immunotherapy and targeted therapy can be combined when the relevant biomarkers are present.

The clinical lesson is:

> **Treatment modalities are not mutually exclusive categories.**

The exact combination, however, depends on the target, evidence, treatment line, patient factors and current guideline.

# 12. Evidence Matrix — Treatment-Line Dependence

The supplied NCCN treatment framework demonstrates that targeted therapies occupy different positions in the treatment pathway.

### First-line examples

- trastuzumab-containing therapy for appropriate HER2-positive disease;
- zolbetuximab-containing therapy for appropriate CLDN18.2-positive/HER2-negative disease.

### Subsequent-line examples

- ramucirumab-containing therapy;
- fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan for appropriate HER2-positive disease.

### Selected molecular-alteration pathways

- NTRK;
- BRAF V600E;
- RET.

The treatment line must therefore be preserved whenever a targeted therapy is discussed.

# 13. Evidence Matrix — Treatment Benefit and Burden

Targeted therapy can provide meaningful clinical benefit in selected biomarker-defined populations.

However, evidence must be interpreted together with treatment burden.

Relevant burden may include:

- infusion or administration burden;
- monitoring;
- laboratory surveillance;
- adverse effects;
- cumulative treatment burden;
- interactions with previous therapies;
- quality-of-life effects.

The NCI PDQ example of trastuzumab deruxtecan demonstrates why this matters: clinically meaningful efficacy was accompanied by important hematologic toxicity and interstitial lung disease risk.

Therefore:

**Benefit**

must be considered together with

**risk + burden + patient goals**.

# 14. Evidence Matrix — Resistance and Tumor Evolution

The project sources support a broader molecular-treatment principle in which cancer biology can evolve over time.

For PP-0209, resistance is therefore treated as a conceptual treatment limitation.

The evidence does not support a single universal resistance mechanism for all targeted therapies.

Instead:

- resistance may be target-specific;
- resistance may arise through alternative pathways;
- tumor clones may evolve;
- treatment exposure can change selective pressure;
- the clinically relevant target may differ at progression.

Detailed resistance mechanisms are delegated to therapy-specific packages.

# 15. Evidence Matrix — Biomarker Reassessment

The supplied NCCN v2.2026 material includes a framework in which repeat biomarker assessment may be considered at progression in selected advanced/metastatic settings.

This supports a cautious educational statement:

> **The biomarker information used at the beginning of treatment may not always be the only relevant information later in the disease course.**

It does not support:

- automatic retesting for every patient;
- one universal specimen;
- one universal assay;
- automatic treatment change after a new result.

Those decisions remain clinical-context dependent.

# 16. Evidence Gaps and Uncertainties

## Gap 1 — Rare actionable alterations

Some targets are uncommon in gastric cancer, so evidence may be based on smaller populations or broader tumor-agnostic evidence.

## Gap 2 — Evolving treatment pathways

Targeted-treatment recommendations can change as new therapies and trial results emerge.

## Gap 3 — Biomarker heterogeneity

Expression or alteration status can differ between tumor regions or disease sites.

## Gap 4 — Assay limitations

A negative result does not necessarily prove that no biological alteration exists outside the scope or sensitivity of the test.

## Gap 5 — Response heterogeneity

Patients with the same biomarker may respond differently.

## Gap 6 — Resistance

The molecular basis of resistance differs by target and drug.

## Gap 7 — Combination strategies

The optimal combination of targeted therapy with chemotherapy and/or immunotherapy remains context-specific and can evolve.

## Gap 8 — Real-world applicability

Clinical trials enroll selected populations and may not fully represent every patient encountered in routine practice.

# 17. Evidence Interpretation Rules

### Rule 1 — Target presence is not treatment certainty

A target may create an option; it does not independently determine the final treatment.

### Rule 2 — Biomarker evidence must be clinically contextualized

The same result can have different implications depending on disease setting and treatment line.

### Rule 3 — Guideline recommendation is not individualized prescribing

Guidelines describe population-level recommendations.

### Rule 4 — Trial benefit is not guaranteed individual benefit

Trial outcomes are estimates for study populations.

### Rule 5 — Targeted does not mean non-toxic

Targeted drugs have specific and sometimes serious adverse effects.

### Rule 6 — Targeted does not mean mutation-only

Targets may be proteins, receptors, pathways, fusions, amplifications, or mutations.

### Rule 7 — Modality and drug package are different ownership layers

PP-0209 explains the modality; downstream packages explain individual therapies.

# 25. Expanded Evidence Notes — HER2

## 25.1 Why HER2 Is a Core Paradigm

HER2 demonstrates nearly the complete targeted-treatment chain:

**biomarker** → **target** → **targeted drug** → **combination therapy** → **subsequent-line therapy** → **clinical benefit** → **toxicity** → **possible resistance**.

This makes HER2 the strongest teaching example for PP-0209.

## 25.2 What the Evidence Establishes

The supplied NCCN v2.2026 source supports trastuzumab-containing first-line systemic therapy for appropriate HER2-positive advanced disease.

The source also supports fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan as a subsequent-treatment option for HER2 overexpression-positive adenocarcinoma.

NCI independently summarizes later-line trastuzumab deruxtecan evidence.

## 25.3 What the Evidence Does Not Establish

It does not establish that every HER2-positive patient should receive the same regimen, that trastuzumab is always first-line in every clinical setting, that T-DXd is always the next treatment after progression, or that a HER2-positive result guarantees response.

# 26. Expanded Evidence Notes — CLDN18.2

CLDN18.2 demonstrates the modern expansion of gastric-cancer targeted therapy beyond HER2.

It shows:

- protein-based target;
- biomarker-selected population;
- first-line systemic treatment;
- combination with chemotherapy;
- randomized clinical evidence.

The supplied NCCN and NCI materials support zolbetuximab-containing therapy in the appropriate CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced setting.

The evidence should not be generalized to CLDN18.2-negative disease, unrelated tumor types, all treatment lines, or all clinical settings.

---

# 27. Expanded Evidence Notes — VEGFR-2

Ramucirumab broadens the definition of targeted therapy.

It does not require a tumor-specific mutation.

Instead, it targets a receptor involved in angiogenic signaling.

REGARD and RAINBOW provide evidence for selected previously treated advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma populations.

The educational conclusion is:

> Targeted therapy can act on a biological pathway that supports tumor growth, not only on a mutation unique to the tumor.

# 28. Expanded Evidence Notes — Rare Alterations

## NTRK

The treatment concept is driven by a gene fusion. The alteration is uncommon, so the evidence base differs from common gastric-cancer biomarkers.

## BRAF V600E

The treatment concept is driven by a specific mutation. The clinical pathway is highly selected.

## RET

The treatment concept is driven by a gene fusion. The evidence is relevant to a selected molecularly defined population.

### Interpretation Rule

Rare-target evidence should be described as **selected and context-dependent**, not as routine treatment for gastric cancer.

# 30. Why PP-0209 Does Not List Every Targeted Drug

A complete drug catalog would:

1. duplicate downstream therapy packages;
2. become difficult to maintain;
3. blur common versus rare targets;
4. encourage drug-specific retrieval from a modality-level package;
5. increase the risk of outdated treatment status.

The package therefore uses a target-class architecture and routes detailed drug information downstream.

# 37. Evidence Matrix — What Remains Context-Dependent

The following should never be represented as universal rules:

- which target to test first;
- which assay to use for every patient;
- which drug to choose;
- whether treatment should be monotherapy or combination;
- treatment duration;
- treatment after progression;
- when to repeat testing;
- whether a rare alteration should change treatment;
- whether a patient should receive a clinical trial.

These questions require the relevant package and clinical context.

# 40. Evidence Update Boundary

A future source should not automatically be added merely because it discusses targeted therapy.

Before inclusion, ask:

1. Does it address gastric cancer?
2. Does it address targeted therapy?
3. Does it change the current treatment architecture?
4. Does it introduce a new target?
5. Does it change evidence strength?
6. Does it change safety?
7. Does it belong to PP-0209 or a downstream therapy package?

This prevents evidence drift and duplicate ownership.

# 42. Evidence Package Closure Statement

The evidence package is therefore suitable as the durable evidence layer for PP-0209.

Its purpose is not to replace the therapy-specific evidence packages.

Its purpose is to preserve the **modality-level evidence architecture** that explains why targeted therapy exists, how biomarker information connects to it, and how treatment context modifies its use.


# 43. Evidence Maintenance Principle

The evidence layer must remain aligned with the locked clinical boundary.

When evidence expands, the preferred action is to update the relevant downstream package rather than inflate PP-0209 into a drug-specific repository.

This preserves:

- atomicity;
- traceability;
- maintainability;
- retrieval precision;
- non-duplication.

The modality-level evidence architecture therefore remains the stable anchor for future targeted-therapy updates.
