# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a pathology report and why is it important?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the pathology report, including why it is produced, who prepares it, how it contributes to diagnosis, and why it should always be interpreted together with other clinical information by the treating healthcare team.

This Population Package establishes the foundational educational concept of pathology reporting before introducing pathology interpretation, histologic classification, biomarker testing and treatment planning.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of pathology report
- Relationship between biopsy and pathology report
- Role of the pathologist
- Purpose of microscopic tissue examination
- Types of findings that may be reported
- Importance of pathology in confirming diagnosis
- Integration of pathology with other clinical information
- Importance of physician explanation

---

## Excluded

- Interpretation of individual pathology report sections
- Histologic subtype
- Tumor grade
- Lauren classification
- WHO classification
- TNM staging
- HER2 interpretation
- MSI interpretation
- PD-L1 interpretation
- Molecular pathology
- Treatment planning

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- A pathology report is based on microscopic examination of tissue.
- The report is prepared by a pathologist after biopsy.
- Pathology plays a central role in establishing many diagnoses, including gastric cancer.
- Not every pathology report identifies cancer.
- Clinical decisions require integration of pathology findings with endoscopy, imaging, symptoms and other investigations.
- Patients benefit from discussing pathology findings with their treating physician.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the purpose of pathology reports;
- the role of pathologists;
- the importance of microscopic tissue examination;
- the relationship between biopsy and diagnosis;
- the need for clinical interpretation of pathology findings.

Differences between references are limited to educational wording and explanatory depth.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-friendly educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- detailed pathology report interpretation;
- histologic subtype;
- tumor grading;
- staging;
- biomarker testing;
- molecular pathology;
- treatment decisions based on pathology findings.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
