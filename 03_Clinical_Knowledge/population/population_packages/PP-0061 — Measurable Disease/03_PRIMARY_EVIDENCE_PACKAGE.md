# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does measurable disease mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **measurable disease**, including what the term means, why only certain lesions can be measured using standardized methods, how measurable disease supports assessment of treatment response, and why lesions that are not considered measurable remain clinically important.

This Population Package introduces the concept of measurable disease without explaining RECIST size thresholds, lymph node measurement criteria, lesion selection rules or treatment response categories.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of measurable disease
- Purpose of standardized measurement
- Importance for monitoring disease over time
- Explanation that not every lesion is measurable
- Continued clinical importance of non-measurable lesions
- Professional determination of measurability

---

## Excluded

- RECIST size thresholds
- Lymph node measurement criteria
- Target lesions
- Non-target lesions
- Complete Response (CR)
- Partial Response (PR)
- Stable Disease (SD)
- Progressive Disease (PD)
- RECIST calculations
- CT protocols
- MRI protocols
- Treatment decisions
- Prognostic significance

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Measurable disease describes lesions that can be evaluated reliably using standardized measurement methods.
- Reliable measurements make it possible to compare imaging findings across multiple time points.
- Not every cancer lesion satisfies standardized measurement criteria.
- Lesions that are not measurable continue to contribute to the overall assessment of disease.
- Measurability is a technical classification used for response assessment rather than a measure of clinical importance.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of measurable disease;
- its role in standardized response assessment;
- the distinction between measurability and clinical importance;
- the need for comprehensive evaluation of all disease sites.

Differences between references relate mainly to detailed technical measurement criteria, which are intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted RECIST 1.1 concepts without introducing technical measurement thresholds.
