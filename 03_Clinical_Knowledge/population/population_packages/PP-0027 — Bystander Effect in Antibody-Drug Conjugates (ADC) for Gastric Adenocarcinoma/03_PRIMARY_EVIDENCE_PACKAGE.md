# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> What is the bystander effect in antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), and why is it important in the treatment of gastric adenocarcinoma?

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an evidence-based understanding of the bystander effect associated with some antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

This Population Package explains, in plain language, what the bystander effect means, why it may occur with certain ADCs, its potential clinical significance, and why it is considered one of the concepts underlying modern precision oncology.

The package intentionally avoids molecular pharmacology, intracellular drug release mechanisms, payload chemistry and drug-specific comparisons.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The bystander effect is a characteristic of some—but not all—ADCs.
- The bystander effect may allow treatment to affect nearby cancer cells under certain circumstances.
- Different ADCs have different biological properties.
- Treatment selection depends on the complete clinical context rather than the bystander effect alone.
- Patients should understand the bystander effect as one component of modern precision oncology.
- Individualized treatment decisions remain essential.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary authoritative references.

All major references consistently describe the bystander effect as a treatment characteristic that varies among ADC platforms and contributes to the evolving understanding of targeted drug delivery.

Differences among references primarily involve:

- molecular mechanisms;
- payload chemistry;
- linker technologies;
- pharmacokinetics;
- clinical trial interpretation.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- clinical recommendations from NCCN;
- evidence interpretation from JNCCN;
- patient-oriented explanations from NCI and ACS;
- internationally harmonized terminology from ESMO.
