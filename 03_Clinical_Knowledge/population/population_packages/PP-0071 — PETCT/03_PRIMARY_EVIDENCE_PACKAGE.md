# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a PET/CT scan and why is it used during cancer care?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **PET/CT**, including what PET/CT is, how PET and CT complement one another, why PET/CT is recommended in selected clinical situations, the basic concept of radiotracers, and why PET/CT findings are interpreted together with other clinical information.

This Population Package introduces the purpose of PET/CT without discussing PET physics, FDG metabolism, SUV measurements, imaging protocols, radiation dose, or disease-specific indications.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of PET/CT
- PET and CT as complementary imaging techniques
- Functional and anatomical information
- Role of PET/CT during cancer care
- Basic concept of radiotracers
- PET/CT as one component of comprehensive clinical assessment

---

## Excluded

- PET physics
- FDG metabolism
- SUV values
- Radiotracer pharmacology
- Radiation dose
- PET preparation
- Blood glucose management
- PET/CT procedure
- PET/CT interpretation
- PET-specific indications by cancer type
- Treatment decisions based on PET/CT

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- PET/CT combines metabolic and anatomical imaging.
- PET/CT helps answer specific clinical questions during cancer care.
- PET/CT is recommended only when clinically appropriate.
- PET imaging usually requires a small amount of radiotracer.
- PET/CT findings contribute to, but do not independently determine, clinical decision-making.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the complementary roles of PET and CT;
- PET/CT as a problem-solving imaging modality;
- individualized clinical indications for PET/CT;
- integration of PET/CT findings into comprehensive patient assessment.

Differences between references primarily concern disease-specific indications, radiotracer selection and imaging protocols, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- oncology recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- nuclear medicine education principles from SNMMI and RSNA.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- FDG metabolism;
- SUV measurements;
- PET preparation;
- blood glucose management;
- radiation exposure;
- PET/CT interpretation;
- PET-specific indications by cancer type;
- comparison with CT or MRI.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
