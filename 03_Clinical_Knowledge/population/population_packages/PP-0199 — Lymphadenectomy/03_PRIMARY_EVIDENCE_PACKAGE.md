**EP ID:** EP-PP-0199

**Primary Educational Question:** What is lymphadenectomy in gastric cancer, why is it performed, how is its extent described, and why do extent, node yield, nodal positivity and surgical expertise all matter?

# 1. Clinical Question

For patients with gastric adenocarcinoma undergoing surgical evaluation, what does regional lymphadenectomy mean, why is it integrated into gastric-cancer surgery, how are D0/D1/D1+/D2/D2+ concepts distinguished, how does lymph-node examination contribute to staging, and what evidence supports the balance between adequate oncologic surgery and surgical morbidity?

# 5. Core Evidence Findings

## Finding 1 — Regional lymphadenectomy is part of gastric oncologic surgery

NCI states that surgical resection with regional lymphadenectomy is the treatment of choice for appropriate stage II and III gastric cancer.

NCI also states that regional lymphadenectomy is recommended with the relevant gastrectomy procedures in stage I disease.

### Interpretation

Lymphadenectomy is not an optional educational add-on to gastrectomy. It is part of the oncologic surgical framework for appropriate resectable gastric cancer.

---

# 6. Core Evidence Finding 2 — Lymphadenectomy has an oncologic and staging role

The supplied Vietnamese guideline explicitly gives four reasons for lymphadenectomy:

1. lymphatic spread is a major route of gastric-cancer metastasis;
2. lymphadenectomy contributes to oncologic completeness;
3. lymphadenectomy improves assessment of stage and prognosis;
4. skip metastasis can occur.

### Interpretation

The evidence supports a dual explanation:

**regional disease treatment**

and

**regional disease assessment.**

Neither should be presented as the only purpose.

---

# 7. Core Evidence Finding 3 — D0/D1/D2 are extent concepts

NCCN describes D0, D1 and D2 as different levels of lymph-node dissection.

The supplied Vietnamese guideline additionally describes D1+, D2+ and more extended nodal territories.

### Interpretation

The project should treat:

**D0 → D1 → D1+ → D2 → D2+**

as an extent hierarchy.

However, detailed definitions are delegated to dedicated downstream PPs.

---

# 8. Core Evidence Finding 4 — D2 is anatomically more extensive

NCCN describes D2 as D1 plus additional nodal groups along major arterial territories, including left gastric, common hepatic, celiac and splenic arterial regions.

### Interpretation

The patient-facing statement:

> D2 is more extensive than D1.

is evidence-supported.

The detailed station-level anatomy is not owned by PP-0199.

---

# 9. Core Evidence Finding 5 — Node yield is a separate dimension

NCCN recommends a goal of examining at least 16 lymph nodes in localized resectable gastric cancer.

### Interpretation

The number examined is a separate quality/pathology concept from the anatomical extent of dissection.

Therefore:

**D2 ≠ 16 nodes.**

This is a mandatory evidence-calibration rule.

---

# 10. Core Evidence Finding 6 — Positive-node count affects N category

The supplied NCI staging material describes:

- N1: 1–2 positive regional nodes;
- N2: 3–6;
- N3a: 7–15;
- N3b: ≥16.

### Interpretation

The number of positive nodes is a staging variable.

It must not be confused with the number of nodes examined.

---

# 11. Core Evidence Finding 7 — D2 evidence is context-dependent

NCI describes uncertainty regarding extended D2 dissection and notes increased morbidity in some series.

NCCN reviews historical randomized evidence and modern evidence and supports D2 in appropriately selected settings with appropriate expertise.

### Interpretation

The package must not say:

> D2 is always better.

The correct evidence-calibrated statement is:

> **D2 may be appropriate and beneficial in selected patients when performed appropriately, but greater extent does not automatically produce better outcomes for every patient.**

---

# 12. Core Evidence Finding 8 — Expertise matters

NCCN states that D2 requires substantial training and expertise and should be performed in experienced centers.

ACS also emphasizes the importance of surgeon and hospital experience.

### Interpretation

Expertise is not a cosmetic qualification.

It is part of the safety and quality context for extended gastric lymphadenectomy.

---

# 13. Core Evidence Finding 9 — D2 does not equal routine splenectomy

NCCN states that splenectomy is not routinely performed.

The Vietnamese guideline discusses selected situations involving splenic hilar nodes and tumor involvement.

### Interpretation

The package should actively correct the misconception that D2 automatically requires spleen removal.

---

# 14. Core Evidence Finding 10 — D2 does not equal routine pancreatectomy

NCCN does not recommend routine/prophylactic pancreatectomy.

### Interpretation

The package should distinguish nodal dissection from adjacent-organ resection.

---

# 15. Core Evidence Finding 11 — Palliative surgery is different

NCCN states that palliative gastric resection in incurable disease does not need to include lymph-node dissection.

### Interpretation

The package must preserve the distinction between:

- curative oncologic surgery;
- selected palliative surgery.

# 26. Evidence Decomposition — Regional Practice

## East Asia

D2 has historically been standard in curable gastric cancer.

## Western settings

Historical evidence was less supportive of routine extended dissection.

## Modern synthesis

Appropriately performed D2 in experienced centers is supported by contemporary guideline frameworks.

# 29. Evidence Limitations

## Limitation 1 — Different guidelines use different nomenclature

The supplied Vietnamese guideline provides detailed D1/D1+/D2/D2+ station descriptions that differ in granularity from the NCCN presentation.

### Control

Preserve the existence of the classifications while avoiding unsupported crosswalks between every station definition.

---

## Limitation 2 — Historical evidence may not reflect modern D2 quality

Older Western D2 trials may have involved surgical techniques and perioperative management that differ from modern high-volume centers.

### Control

Present historical morbidity findings as historical/contextual evidence.

---

## Limitation 3 — Node count cannot define anatomical extent

### Control

Repeat the distinction:

**extent ≠ yield ≠ positivity.**

---

## Limitation 4 — Positive-node count cannot define the quality of surgery alone

### Control

Interpret positive nodes with the complete pathological and clinical context.

---

## Limitation 5 — D2 is not a universal requirement

### Control

Use guideline-dependent, context-dependent wording.

# 30. Evidence-Supported Clinical Model

The most useful evidence architecture is:

**Gastric cancer**

↓

**Risk of regional lymphatic spread**

↓

**Gastrectomy + appropriate regional lymphadenectomy**

↓

**Nodes retrieved and examined**

↓

**Number examined**

+

**Number positive**

↓

**Pathological N category**

↓

**Overall stage**

↓

**Treatment planning**

The anatomical extent of lymphadenectomy runs in parallel:

**D0 → D1 → D1+ → D2 → D2+**

These two pathways intersect but are not identical.

# 31. Evidence-Supported Benefit–Risk Model

## Potential benefit

- removal of regional disease;
- improved pathological staging;
- more complete oncologic regional assessment.

## Potential cost

- greater operative complexity;
- greater potential morbidity;
- requirement for appropriate expertise.

## Evidence synthesis

The optimal operation is not the maximum operation.

It is the operation that provides appropriate oncologic treatment with acceptable risk in the relevant clinical context.

# 32. Source-Supported Patient Explanation

A patient asking “Why are they taking out lymph nodes?” should receive:

> Gastric cancer can spread through nearby lymphatic pathways. During an appropriate cancer operation, the surgeon may remove regional lymph nodes so that cancer in those nodes can be treated as part of the operation and so that the nodes can be examined under the microscope to determine whether cancer has spread.

A patient asking “Why D2?” should receive:

> D2 is a more extensive regional lymph-node dissection than D1. Whether that extent is appropriate depends on the cancer and the surgical setting, and NCCN emphasizes experienced surgeons and centers for D2.

# 37. Final Evidence Conclusion

The supplied project materials support PP-0199 as a distinct, clinically meaningful parent package.

The evidence establishes that:

1. regional lymphadenectomy is integrated into appropriate gastric-cancer surgery;
2. lymphadenectomy has both oncologic and staging functions;
3. D0/D1/D1+/D2/D2+ represent different extents;
4. D1/D2 are anatomical concepts rather than node counts;
5. NCCN uses ≥16 examined nodes as a goal in localized resectable disease;
6. positive-node number contributes to N staging;
7. D2 evidence is context-dependent;
8. D2 requires appropriate expertise and experienced centers;
9. routine splenectomy and prophylactic pancreatectomy are not required simply because lymphadenectomy is extensive;
10. palliative surgery has different lymphadenectomy objectives.

This evidence base is sufficient for Gold production without importing unsupported external claims.
