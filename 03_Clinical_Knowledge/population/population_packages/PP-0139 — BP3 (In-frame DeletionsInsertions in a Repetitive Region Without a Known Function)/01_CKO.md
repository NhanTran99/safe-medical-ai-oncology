# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what BP3 is.
- Understand the basic meaning of **in-frame deletions** and **in-frame insertions**.
- Understand what a repetitive region is.
- Recognize why the location of a variant within a protein may influence interpretation.
- Understand why BP3 may provide supporting benign evidence.
- Recognize that BP3 alone cannot determine that a variant is benign.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is BP3?

## Patient Explanation

**BP3** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant is likely to be **benign (not disease-causing).**

BP3 represents one type of **Supporting Benign Evidence**.

It applies to certain **in-frame insertions** or **in-frame deletions** occurring in specific regions of a protein.

---

## Clinical Importance

BP3 provides supporting benign evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Supporting benign evidence

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Are In-frame Deletions and Insertions?

## Patient Explanation

An **in-frame deletion** removes one or more amino acids from a protein **without changing the overall reading frame**.

An **in-frame insertion** adds one or more amino acids **without changing the reading frame**.

These changes are different from variants that disrupt how the rest of the protein is read.

---

## Clinical Importance

Not all genetic changes affect proteins in the same way.

---

## Key Concepts

- In-frame deletion
- In-frame insertion
- Reading frame

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Is a Repetitive Region?

## Patient Explanation

Some parts of proteins contain **repeated sequences**.

In certain repetitive regions, small insertions or deletions may occur without affecting an important known function.

When this situation matches professional ACMG guidance, it may contribute **supporting benign evidence**.

---

## Clinical Importance

The biological location of a variant helps genetics professionals interpret its significance.

---

## Key Concepts

- Repetitive region
- Protein sequence
- Functional importance

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does BP3 Mean the Variant Is Definitely Benign?

## Patient Explanation

No.

BP3 **does not prove** that a variant is benign.

It provides one piece of supporting evidence that must always be interpreted together with many other clinical and scientific findings.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification depends on integrating multiple evidence categories.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Professional interpretation
- Benign classification

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Does the ACMG Use BP3?

## Patient Explanation

Not every part of a protein has the same importance.

Considering **where** a genetic change occurs helps genetics professionals make more accurate and consistent interpretations.

BP3 allows this information to contribute to evidence-based variant classification.

---

## Clinical Importance

Variant location is an important component of standardized genomic interpretation.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Every insertion or deletion has the same chance of causing disease.

**Fact**

The effect of a genetic change depends on many factors, including **where it occurs**.

Some in-frame changes in repetitive regions without a known function may provide **supporting benign evidence**, but BP3 alone never determines the final classification.

---

# Key Messages

- BP3 is one Supporting Benign Evidence criterion.
- BP3 applies to certain in-frame insertions and deletions.
- The location of a variant within a protein matters.
- BP3 provides supporting—not definitive—benign evidence.
- Final variant classification always requires integration of multiple evidence categories.
