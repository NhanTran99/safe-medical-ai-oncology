# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is BP3 (In-frame Deletions/Insertions in a Repetitive Region Without a Known Function)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **BP3 (In-frame Deletions/Insertions in a Repetitive Region Without a Known Function)** as one of the standardized **Supporting Benign Evidence** criteria within the **ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework**.

This Population Package explains that **BP3 applies to certain in-frame insertions or deletions occurring within repetitive regions that have no known important biological function.** Because these regions may tolerate some sequence changes without affecting protein function, these variants may contribute **supporting benign evidence** in specific ACMG-defined situations. However, **BP3 represents supporting evidence only** and must always be interpreted together with clinical findings, laboratory evidence, population data, and other ACMG evidence categories before a final variant classification is made.

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **BP3** as one of the **Supporting Benign Evidence** criteria within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that certain **in-frame insertions or deletions** occurring in **repetitive regions without known functional importance** may contribute supporting benign evidence because these regions may better tolerate sequence variation. ClinGen SVI provides additional implementation recommendations for BP3, but these technical refinements intentionally remain outside the scope of this introductory educational package.
