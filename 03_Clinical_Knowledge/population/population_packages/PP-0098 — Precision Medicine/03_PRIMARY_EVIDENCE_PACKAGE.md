# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is precision medicine?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **precision medicine** as an approach that uses biological, clinical, and individual patient characteristics to guide healthcare decisions.

This Population Package introduces precision medicine as a modern approach to cancer care while emphasizing that it complements, rather than replaces, established clinical assessment and evidence-based decision-making.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Precision medicine individualizes healthcare using patient and disease characteristics.
- Biomarker testing is an important component of precision oncology.
- Different patients may benefit from different testing strategies.
- Precision medicine complements comprehensive clinical assessment.
- Precision medicine improves treatment selection but cannot eliminate uncertainty.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Major oncology organizations consistently describe precision medicine as an evidence-based approach that integrates biological and clinical information to improve patient care. They emphasize that precision medicine should be incorporated into comprehensive clinical decision-making rather than viewed as an independent or replacement strategy.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- terminology from NCI and NIH;
- conceptual framework from NHGRI;
- oncology guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from ACS;
- international guidance consistent with ESMO and JNCCN.

Major organizations including the NIH and NHGRI note that personalized medicine remains a commonly used public-facing term, although precision medicine is generally preferred in contemporary scientific and clinical communication.
