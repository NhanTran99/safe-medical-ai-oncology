# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> What is adjuvant therapy after definitive gastric-cancer surgery, why may it be recommended, how do pathology, lymphadenectomy extent and previous treatment influence postoperative decisions, and what evidence supports postoperative chemotherapy and related modalities?

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-traceable evidence base for a strategy-level, patient-facing package.

The evidence package distinguishes:

- current guideline positioning;
- randomized clinical evidence;
- regional clinical-guideline context;
- patient-facing definitions;
- regimen-level and modality-level boundaries;
- evidence limitations.

# Evidence Note 1 — Definition of Adjuvant Therapy

NCI patient-facing material defines treatment after surgery that lowers the risk of recurrence as adjuvant therapy.

The source explains that after visible cancer has been removed, some patients may receive chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or both to address remaining cancer cells that cannot be seen.

### Interpretation

This is the conceptual foundation of PP-0205.

The package should use:

> **risk reduction**

rather than:

> **known residual cancer**.

# Evidence Note 2 — Postoperative Decision Inputs

The current NCCN source set identifies the following as important postoperative determinants:

- pathologic tumor stage;
- nodal status;
- surgical margins;
- extent of lymph-node dissection;
- previous treatment.

### Interpretation

This makes PP-0205 a **decision-framework package**, not simply a list of postoperative drugs.

# Evidence Note 3 — Primary D2 Dissection

NCCN states:

> postoperative chemotherapy is recommended following primary D2 lymph-node dissection in the appropriate clinical setting.

This aligns with the randomized evidence from CLASSIC and ACTS-GC.

### Interpretation

D2 is therefore a major context variable for adjuvant treatment.

The package must not teach D2 surgical technique.

# Evidence Note 4 — CLASSIC Trial Design

The NCI PDQ reports:

- 1,035 patients;
- stage IIA, IIB, IIIA, or IIIB gastric cancer;
- curative D2 gastrectomy;
- postoperative capecitabine plus oxaliplatin versus follow-up alone.

### Interpretation

CLASSIC directly supports postoperative chemotherapy after D2 surgery in a population with stage II/III disease.

# Evidence Note 5 — CLASSIC Disease-Free Survival

Reported 3-year DFS:

- 74% with postoperative capecitabine/oxaliplatin;
- 59% with surgery/follow-up.

HR:

**0.56**

95% CI:

**0.44–0.72**

P < .0001.

### Interpretation

This is strong randomized evidence for improved disease-free survival.

# Evidence Note 6 — CLASSIC Overall Survival

Reported 3-year OS:

- 83% with postoperative chemotherapy;
- 78% with surgery alone.

HR:

**0.72**

95% CI:

**0.52–1.00**

P = .0493.

### Interpretation

The survival result supports a clinically meaningful benefit in the studied population.

It should not be transformed into an individual survival prediction.

# Evidence Note 7 — ACTS-GC

NCI reports:

- 1,059 stage II/III patients;
- D2 gastrectomy;
- S-1 for one year versus surgery alone.

Three-year OS:

- 80.1%;
- 70.1%.

HR:

**0.68**

95% CI:

**0.52–0.87**

P = .003.

### Interpretation

ACTS-GC independently supports postoperative systemic therapy in an Asian D2-resected population.

# Evidence Note 8 — Why Two Trials Matter

CLASSIC and ACTS-GC used different postoperative systemic strategies.

This demonstrates:

> the principle of postoperative systemic therapy is broader than one drug combination.

The package should therefore avoid:

> “Adjuvant therapy = CAPOX.”

# Evidence Note 9 — INT-0116 / SWOG-9008

NCI reports:

- 559 patients with completely resected stage IB–IV M0 gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma;
- postoperative chemotherapy and concurrent radiation versus surgery alone;
- median OS 35 vs 27 months;
- median relapse-free survival 27 vs 19 months.

### Interpretation

This supports postoperative chemoradiation as an adjuvant modality in the studied setting.

# Evidence Note 10 — Why INT-0116 Does Not Automatically Mean Everyone Should Receive Chemoradiation

The interpretation of postoperative chemoradiation depends on:

- extent of lymphadenectomy;
- prior treatment;
- disease risk;
- current guideline context.

The current NCCN framework does not treat postoperative chemoradiation as universally interchangeable with postoperative chemotherapy after adequate D2 surgery.

# Evidence Note 11 — CRITICS

NCI reports that 788 patients received preoperative chemotherapy and surgery, then postoperative chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy.

Median OS:

- 43 months chemotherapy;
- 37 months chemoradiotherapy.

HR:

**1.01**

P = .90.

### Interpretation

Postoperative chemoradiation did not improve survival over postoperative chemotherapy after preoperative chemotherapy.

This is a key reason why previous treatment must be included in PP-0205.

# Evidence Note 12 — ARTIST

NCCN summarizes evidence that adding postoperative chemoradiation after D2 surgery did not establish a routine survival advantage over postoperative chemotherapy.

### Interpretation

The package should avoid simplistic:

> “Radiation is always better after surgery.”

# Evidence Note 13 — ARTIST II

NCCN further summarizes ARTIST II as not showing a survival advantage from adding radiation to postoperative chemotherapy in node-positive patients after D2 resection.

### Interpretation

The current role of postoperative chemoradiation is context-specific.

# Evidence Note 14 — R0, R1, R2

R0:

- no residual tumor at the surgical margin.

R1/R2:

- residual disease remains at microscopic or macroscopic level.

### Interpretation

Routine adjuvant therapy after R0 is conceptually different from management of known residual disease.

# Evidence Note 15 — Early-Stage Observation

The supplied NCCN framework includes observation for selected very early/node-negative situations.

### Interpretation

Adjuvant therapy should not be represented as mandatory after every gastrectomy.

# Evidence Note 16 — Previous Perioperative Treatment

When systemic therapy was given before surgery, postoperative treatment may be continuation of the perioperative plan.

### Interpretation

This is why:

**perioperative chemotherapy**

and

**adjuvant therapy**

must remain distinct PP concepts.

# Evidence Note 17 — FLOT Interface

FLOT is a specific chemotherapy regimen.

PP-0204 owns:

- composition;
- regimen structure;
- FLOT4;
- FLOT-specific toxicity.

PP-0205 owns:

- postoperative/adjuvant strategy;
- how postoperative treatment fits into the broader pathway.

# Evidence Note 18 — Postoperative Readiness

The supplied Vietnamese guideline and ESMO-ASCO curriculum support consideration of:

- functional status;
- multidisciplinary sequencing;
- nutritional recovery;
- overall treatment context.

### Interpretation

This supports conceptual readiness language without creating universal numerical thresholds.

# Evidence Note 19 — Treatment Burden

Adjuvant therapy can create:

- toxicity;
- additional treatment burden;
- recovery challenges.

Therefore evidence interpretation should retain the benefit-risk framework.

# Evidence Note 20 — Population Evidence Is Not Individual Prediction

A trial result such as:

> 3-year DFS 74% vs 59%

does not mean:

> “Your chance of being disease-free is 74%.”

Individual prognosis depends on factors not represented by the trial summary alone.

# Evidence Note 21 — Patient-Facing Translation

Recommended translation:

> “Clinical trials show that some patients benefit from additional treatment after surgery. The benefit depends on the cancer's stage and other features, the type of surgery, and whether treatment was already given before surgery.”

This is preferable to:

> “Everyone needs chemotherapy after surgery.”

# Evidence Note 22 — Treatment Completion

Planned treatment is not necessarily delivered exactly as planned.

Postoperative complications, nutritional problems, toxicity, and functional decline may require modification.

The package should preserve this distinction.

# Evidence Note 23 — Nutritional Recovery

The Vietnamese guideline emphasizes nutritional support around surgery and during adjuvant treatment.

### Interpretation

Nutrition is an important supportive determinant of treatment readiness but is not itself an anticancer substitute.

# Evidence Note 24 — Biomarker Interface

Biomarkers can influence treatment selection in the broader gastric-cancer pathway.

However:

- HER2;
- PD-L1;
- MSI-H/dMMR;
- CLDN18.2;

have dedicated PPs.

PP-0205 should mention them only as interfaces.

# Evidence Note 25 — Recurrence Is a Different Treatment State

Adjuvant therapy is delivered before recurrence is established.

Once recurrence is documented, the clinical question changes.

This prevents PP-0205 from absorbing metastatic/recurrent therapy.

# Evidence Limitations

## Population Limitation

CLASSIC and ACTS-GC describe selected trial populations.

Their outcomes should not be generalized without regard to:

- stage;
- surgical quality;
- geography;
- regimen;
- patient fitness.

## Surgical Limitation

The strongest postoperative chemotherapy evidence is closely linked to adequate D2 surgery.

## Treatment-Sequence Limitation

CRITICS demonstrates that previous preoperative treatment changes the interpretation of postoperative chemoradiation.

## Regimen Limitation

This package establishes the strategy, not a universal regimen.

## Individualization Limitation

The evidence does not determine a patient's exact postoperative treatment without patient-specific clinical data.

# Evidence-to-Knowledge Translation Rules

1. Use current NCCN for present positioning.
2. Use NCI PDQ for randomized trial details.
3. Use Vietnamese guideline for regional clinical context.
4. Preserve trial population qualifiers.
5. Never convert trial averages into individual predictions.
6. Never convert adjuvant strategy into a regimen.
7. Never convert chemoradiation evidence into universal radiation recommendation.
8. Never use PP-0205 to prescribe a patient-specific dose.
9. Keep surveillance separate from adjuvant therapy.
10. Keep recurrence treatment separate from adjuvant therapy.

# Extended Evidence Synthesis

# Evidence Note 26 — Current Guideline Versus Historical Trial

The current guideline provides the framework for present-day decision-making.

Historical randomized trials provide the evidence that established benefit.

The correct evidence chain is:

**trial evidence**

→ **evidence synthesis**

→ **current guideline interpretation**

→ **patient-specific clinical decision**

The package should not skip the middle steps.

# Evidence Note 27 — Why CLASSIC Is Not a Universal Chemotherapy Prescription

CLASSIC studied:

- stage IIA–IIIB;
- curative D2 surgery;
- capecitabine plus oxaliplatin.

Therefore the evidence directly answers:

> Does postoperative CAPOX improve outcomes in this studied population?

It does not answer:

> Should every patient after every gastrectomy receive CAPOX?

# Evidence Note 28 — Why ACTS-GC Does Not Mean S-1 Is Universal

ACTS-GC provides strong evidence in an Asian D2-resected population.

It does not establish:

- universal use across all countries;
- universal use across all patient populations;
- universal use after different surgical approaches.

The package therefore presents S-1 as an evidence-supported postoperative strategy, not as a universal prescription.

# Evidence Note 29 — Why Geography Matters

Gastric-cancer treatment evidence has substantial regional variation.

Differences can include:

- surgical quality;
- D2 adoption;
- regimen availability;
- patient characteristics;
- supportive care;
- healthcare systems.

The source set includes both international and Vietnamese materials.

Therefore regional context is preserved rather than erased.

# Evidence Note 30 — Why Surgical Quality Is an Evidence Variable

A postoperative regimen cannot be interpreted independently of the surgery performed.

In CLASSIC and ACTS-GC, D2 surgery is part of the evidence context.

Therefore:

**surgery + systemic treatment**

is the relevant evidence architecture.

# Evidence Note 31 — Why Less-than-D2 Is Not Simply “Worse D2”

A less extensive lymphadenectomy represents a different surgical context.

The current guideline framework gives postoperative chemoradiation a different role in selected patients after less-than-D2 surgery.

The package therefore avoids value-laden language and instead describes the evidence context.

# Evidence Note 32 — Why R1/R2 Is Not Routine Adjuvant Therapy

Routine adjuvant therapy assumes definitive local treatment has been completed.

R1/R2 indicates residual disease.

Therefore treatment intent and management can differ.

# Evidence Note 33 — Why CRITICS Limits Radiation Expansion

CRITICS demonstrates that postoperative chemoradiation did not improve survival compared with postoperative chemotherapy in patients who had already received preoperative chemotherapy.

This is important negative evidence.

It prevents the knowledge system from learning:

> “If postoperative chemotherapy is possible, adding radiation is always better.”

# Evidence Note 34 — Why INT-0116 Remains Relevant

INT-0116 establishes that postoperative chemoradiation can improve outcomes in an appropriate historical/clinical context.

It remains important for understanding:

- why postoperative chemoradiation exists;
- why regional-control benefit matters;
- why surgery quality changes interpretation.

# Evidence Note 35 — Why ARTIST/ARTIST II Refine the Modern Context

These studies help define the limits of routine postoperative radiation after adequate D2 surgery.

They support a more selective rather than universal radiation approach.

# Evidence Note 36 — Why FLOT4 Belongs in the Boundary Rather Than the Core Evidence Set

FLOT4 is a major gastric-cancer perioperative trial.

But its clinical question differs from the primary PP-0205 question.

Therefore FLOT4 is included as a boundary/interface evidence node rather than as the central adjuvant evidence anchor.

# Evidence Note 37 — Why “Adjuvant Therapy” Is a Strategy-Level Concept

The source set contains evidence for multiple postoperative approaches.

Therefore the title “Adjuvant Therapy” is appropriately broader than any individual regimen.

The package preserves this hierarchy.

# Evidence Note 38 — Evidence Language Rules

Use:

- “showed” for randomized trial results;
- “supports” for evidence-supported strategies;
- “recommended” only when the current guideline supports the recommendation;
- “may be considered” when the source frames the option conditionally;
- “not established” when evidence does not demonstrate a routine benefit.

Avoid:

- “always”;
- “never”;
- “guarantees”;
- “cures”;
- “everyone.”

# Evidence Note 39 — What the Package Can Say About Survival

It can report trial outcomes.

It cannot predict:

- a patient's exact survival;
- individual recurrence probability;
- individual benefit magnitude.

# Evidence Note 40 — What the Package Can Say About Recurrence

It can explain:

> adjuvant treatment aims to reduce recurrence risk.

It cannot claim:

> adjuvant treatment prevents recurrence in an individual.

# Evidence Note 41 — What the Package Can Say About Cure

The package may explain curative-intent treatment.

It should not equate:

> curative intent

with:

> guaranteed cure.

# Evidence Note 42 — Why Patient Preference Matters

Even when evidence supports postoperative treatment, the patient may have:

- competing health problems;
- recovery limitations;
- treatment priorities;
- quality-of-life concerns.

The final clinical decision is individualized.

# Evidence Note 43 — Why Source Gaps Must Remain Visible

The supplied sources support the strategy and major evidence.

They do not establish one universal:

- dose;
- duration;
- toxicity algorithm;
- individualized regimen choice.

The package therefore deliberately leaves those areas delegated.

# Evidence Note 44 — Evidence Traceability Philosophy

Each major clinical claim is assigned to:

- guideline;
- randomized evidence;
- patient-facing source;
- professional curriculum.

This prevents unsupported generalization.

# Evidence Note 45 — Source Conflict Handling

If a historical trial and current guideline appear different:

1. preserve the trial result;
2. identify the historical context;
3. use the current guideline for current positioning;
4. do not silently overwrite the trial.

# Evidence Note 46 — Current Versus Legacy Terminology

“Adjuvant chemotherapy” may appear in older literature where treatment pathways differed from contemporary perioperative strategies.

The package should preserve the source terminology while explaining the current conceptual distinction.

# Evidence Note 47 — Evidence-to-Patient Translation

A technically accurate explanation can still mislead a patient if context is omitted.

For example:

> “Chemotherapy improves survival.”

is incomplete.

A safer translation is:

> “In selected clinical trials, postoperative chemotherapy improved disease-free survival and, in some studies, overall survival compared with surgery alone.”

# Evidence Note 48 — Why Negative Evidence Is a Knowledge Asset

CRITICS and ARTIST/ARTIST II are not failures of the evidence package.

They help define:

- where radiation does not add established survival benefit;
- why previous treatment matters;
- why a treatment cannot be universally expanded.

# Evidence Note 49 — Why the Vietnamese Guideline Is Supporting Rather Than Sole Authority

The supplied Vietnamese guideline provides important regional context.

However, current NCCN and randomized evidence are stronger anchors for contemporary international postoperative decision logic.

The hierarchy is therefore preserved.

# Evidence Note 50 — ESMO-ASCO Role

ESMO-ASCO is useful for the broader principle that gastric-cancer treatment should be guided by:

- stage;
- functional status;
- biomarkers;
- multidisciplinary sequencing.

It is not used to invent regimen-specific postoperative recommendations absent from the disease-specific sources.
