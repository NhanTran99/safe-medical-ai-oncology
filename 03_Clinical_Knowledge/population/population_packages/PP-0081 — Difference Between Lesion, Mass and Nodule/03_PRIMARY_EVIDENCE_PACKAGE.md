# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is the difference between a lesion, a mass, and a nodule?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based comparison of the three commonly used radiology terms **lesion**, **mass**, and **nodule**.

This Population Package explains that these are **descriptive imaging terms rather than diagnoses**, helping patients understand why different words may appear in CT reports without assuming that one term automatically indicates a more serious condition than another.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Lesion, mass and nodule are descriptive radiology terms.
- None of these terms is a diagnosis.
- Lesion is the broadest concept among the three.
- Mass generally refers to a visible tissue growth.
- Nodule generally refers to a relatively small focal abnormality.
- None of these terms alone determines whether cancer is present.
- Accurate interpretation requires comprehensive clinical evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- standardized descriptive radiology terminology;
- conceptual distinctions among lesion, mass and nodule;
- multidisciplinary interpretation of imaging findings;
- avoidance of diagnostic conclusions based solely on report terminology.

Differences among references primarily involve specialty-specific definitions, organ-specific imaging criteria and reporting conventions, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- multidisciplinary oncology recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- standardized radiology terminology principles from ACR and RSNA.
