# 9. Evidence Notes

## Note 1 — INT-0116 is landmark, not universal

SWOG-9008/INT-0116 is the key randomized evidence supporting postoperative chemoradiation in the studied surgery-first context.

The evidence should be presented together with the modern treatment-sequence context rather than as a blanket rule.

## Note 2 — CRITICS changes the interpretation after preoperative chemotherapy

CRITICS demonstrates that postoperative chemoradiation cannot simply be added to all patients who have already received preoperative chemotherapy and surgery.

The absence of a survival benefit is central to current treatment interpretation.

## Note 3 — TOPGEAR separates response from survival

TOPGEAR is an especially useful patient-facing evidence-literacy example because the pathologic complete response improved while survival did not.

## Note 4 — R1/R2 after prior systemic therapy

NCCN considers chemoradiation reasonable in selected patients with R1/R2 resection after prior systemic therapy, but the guideline discussion explicitly recognizes the lack of prospective evaluation for this exact setting.

This should be labeled as guideline-supported/context-dependent rather than described as equivalent to category-1 randomized evidence.

## Note 5 — D2 is a treatment-interface variable

The extent of lymph-node dissection changes postoperative treatment selection and therefore must be preserved in the package without owning the surgical technique itself.

## Note 6 — Unresectable disease is a different treatment intent

Chemoradiation in unresectable local/regional disease is not the same clinical question as postoperative adjuvant chemoradiation after complete resection.

## Note 7 — Palliative radiation is not curative chemoradiation

The package must distinguish symptom control from curative-intent treatment.

# 10. Landmark Trial Evidence

## 10.1 SWOG-9008 / INT-0116

**Question:** Does postoperative combined chemoradiation improve outcomes compared with surgery alone in completely resected gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma?

**Population:** 559 patients in the NCI PDQ summary, stage IB–IV M0, completely resected.

**Key result:**

- median OS: 35 vs 27 months;
- median relapse-free survival: 27 vs 19 months;
- locoregional recurrence was reduced.

**Interpretation:** landmark evidence supporting postoperative combined-modality treatment in the studied context.

**Limitation for current application:** modern treatment sequences and surgical pathways differ; current selection incorporates prior systemic therapy, margin status and lymph-node dissection.

---

## 10.2 CALGB-80101

**Question:** Does changing the postoperative chemotherapy backbone improve outcomes when used with radiation?

**Population:** 546 patients after curative resection of stage IB–IV M0 gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

**Key result:** 5-year OS was 44% in both evaluated treatment arms.

**Interpretation:** intensification or substitution of chemotherapy within combined-modality therapy did not automatically improve survival.

**PP-0207 role:** evidence principle, not regimen education.

---

## 10.3 CRITICS

**Question:** After preoperative chemotherapy and surgery, is postoperative chemoradiation better than postoperative chemotherapy?

**Population:** 788 patients with stage IB–IVA gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

**Key result:**

- median OS 43 months with postoperative chemotherapy;
- median OS 37 months with postoperative chemoradiation;
- HR 1.01; 95% CI 0.84–1.22; P=.90 in the NCCN summary.

Longer follow-up also did not establish a survival advantage for the chemoradiation strategy.

**Interpretation:** routine addition of postoperative radiation after adequate preoperative chemotherapy and surgery is not supported by the trial.

---

## 10.4 TOPGEAR

**Question:** Does adding preoperative chemoradiation to perioperative chemotherapy improve outcomes in resectable gastric/EGJ cancer?

**Population:** 574 patients.

**Key result:** pathologic complete response 17% vs 8%.

**Survival:**

- median OS 46 vs 49 months;
- HR for death 1.05;
- median PFS 31 vs 32 months;
- HR for progression/death 0.98.

**Interpretation:** higher pathologic response without survival benefit; routine preoperative chemoradiation is not recommended for resectable gastric cancer by current NCCN guidance.

---

## 10.5 RTOG 9904

**Question:** Can preoperative chemoradiation be delivered effectively in localized gastric adenocarcinoma?

**Study role:** phase II evidence.

**PP-0207 interpretation:** historical evidence of feasibility and investigation, not current evidence establishing routine preoperative chemoradiation.

# 12. Evidence Consistency Review

## Consistency Finding 1

NCCN and NCI are consistent that INT-0116 provides landmark evidence for postoperative chemoradiation.

## Consistency Finding 2

NCCN and NCI are consistent that CRITICS did not establish a survival benefit for postoperative chemoradiation after preoperative chemotherapy and surgery.

## Consistency Finding 3

NCCN states that TOPGEAR does not support preoperative chemoradiation for resectable gastric cancer because survival was not improved despite higher pathologic response.

## Consistency Finding 4

ACS and NCCN are consistent in describing chemoradiation as chemotherapy combined with radiation and in identifying selected unresectable local/regional and palliative roles.

## Consistency Finding 5

ESMO-ASCO provides broader radiation-oncology curriculum support for multidisciplinary treatment planning and treatment timing; it is not used as the primary disease-specific source for gastric-cancer indications.

# 13. Evidence Gaps

1. The exact role of postoperative chemoradiation after modern perioperative systemic therapy remains context-dependent.
2. R1/R2 management after prior systemic therapy is guideline-supported but not supported by the same prospective evidence base as INT-0116.
3. CRITICS II is ongoing and may clarify preoperative strategies.
4. Radiation technology continues to evolve, potentially affecting toxicity and feasibility.
5. EGJ overlap may require different disease-specific frameworks depending on tumor location/classification.
6. Evidence from historical trials may not perfectly represent current staging, surgery and systemic therapy.

# 21. Detailed Guideline Evidence — Current Postoperative Pathway

The current NCCN evidence establishes that postoperative management is not determined by stage alone.

The guideline explicitly incorporates:

- pathologic tumor stage;
- nodal status;
- surgical margins;
- extent of lymph-node dissection;
- previous treatment.

For patients who have not received systemic therapy before surgery, the current pathway distinguishes R0, R1 and R2 outcomes and distinguishes less-than-D2 from primary D2 surgery.

This is the evidence foundation for the package's central boundary principle:

> **Chemoradiation ownership is inseparable from the treatment sequence, but PP-0207 does not own the other treatment modalities themselves.**

---

# 22. Detailed Guideline Evidence — Patients Without Previous Systemic Therapy

Current NCCN guidance identifies postoperative chemoradiation for:

- all patients following R1 or R2 resection in appropriate nonmetastatic settings;
- selected R0 pT2 N0 patients with high-risk features;
- pT3–pT4 any N or any pT N+ patients who received less than D2 dissection.

For pT3–pT4 any N or any pT N+ patients after primary D2 dissection, postoperative chemotherapy is recommended rather than automatically adding chemoradiation.

For pT2 N0 without high-risk features, observation is appropriate.

For pTis or pT1 N0 after R0 resection, the current guideline does not support routine postoperative chemoradiation because randomized survival benefit has not been demonstrated for that group.

This hierarchy prevents PP-0207 from being interpreted as a general “radiation after surgery” package.

---

# 23. Detailed Guideline Evidence — Patients With Previous Systemic Therapy

For patients who received preoperative systemic therapy and then underwent R0 resection, current NCCN guidance continues postoperative systemic therapy as the perioperative treatment pathway.

For R1/R2 resection after prior systemic therapy, chemoradiation is considered a reasonable option in the absence of distant metastases.

NCCN also notes that this exact approach has not been evaluated prospectively to the same extent as other treatment settings.

Therefore the evidence label should be:

**Guideline-supported / context-dependent**

rather than:

**equivalent to category-1 randomized evidence.**

This distinction is mandatory for accurate evidence communication.

---

# 24. Detailed Guideline Evidence — Radiation Principles

NCCN's radiation section establishes several principles relevant to PP-0207.

First, radiation alone has limited value in unresectable gastric cancer.

Second, concurrent chemotherapy and radiation produced better outcomes than radiation alone in historical locally advanced studies.

Third, radiation has been evaluated in both preoperative and postoperative settings.

Fourth, IMRT may reduce radiation-related toxicity through improved organ sparing compared with older conformal approaches.

Fifth, radiation recommendations should be developed through multidisciplinary consultation.

These principles support the package without requiring the package to become a technical radiation manual.

---

# 25. Evidence Interpretation — Why Historical Trials Remain Relevant

Historical trials remain important when they established a treatment principle that continues to inform current guidance.

INT-0116 is the clearest example.

However, historical evidence should not be treated as a complete description of contemporary care.

Changes in:

- staging;
- surgery;
- lymph-node dissection;
- systemic therapy;
- radiation technology;
- supportive care;

can alter the balance of benefit and harm.

Therefore historical trial evidence should be paired with current guideline interpretation.

---

# 26. Evidence Interpretation — Why Negative Trials Matter

Negative randomized trials are clinically valuable because they prevent treatment escalation without demonstrated benefit.

CRITICS is important because it shows that a strategy that appears biologically attractive—adding radiation to postoperative treatment—did not improve survival after preoperative chemotherapy and surgery.

TOPGEAR is important because it shows that increased pathologic complete response does not guarantee improved survival.

The evidence package therefore intentionally gives substantial space to negative or neutral evidence rather than presenting only positive trials.

---

# 27. Evidence Interpretation — Treatment Burden

Chemoradiation exposes patients to the burden of two modalities.

The ACS source states that radiation side effects can include gastrointestinal symptoms, fatigue and blood-count changes and that side effects may be worse when radiation is given with chemotherapy.

This makes toxicity part of treatment value.

The evidence question is not simply:

> “Can radiation shrink or control cancer?”

It is also:

> “Does adding radiation provide enough meaningful benefit in this clinical setting to justify its burden?”

This is a central clinical reasoning principle.

---

# 28. Evidence Interpretation — Unresectable Local/Regional Disease

For medically fit patients whose tumor is surgically unresectable but remains local/regional, NCCN allows chemoradiation or systemic therapy as treatment options.

ACS similarly explains that chemoradiation may be used when the cancer cannot be removed by surgery but has not spread distantly and that treatment can sometimes shrink the cancer sufficiently for surgery to become an option.

The wording “may” and “sometimes” is deliberate.

The evidence does not justify promising conversion to resectability for every patient.

---

# 29. Evidence Interpretation — Palliative Radiation

The palliative role of radiation is evidence-supported but has a different objective.

Symptoms such as:

- bleeding;
- pain;
- eating difficulty;

can sometimes be addressed with local radiation.

This is not the same endpoint as improving disease-free survival after curative surgery.

Therefore the package must always identify treatment intent when discussing radiation.

# 32. Evidence Limitations

The following limitations must remain visible:

1. Historical trials may not reflect current perioperative therapy.
2. Surgery and lymphadenectomy patterns differ across studies.
3. Gastric and EGJ populations may be mixed in some trials.
4. Treatment adherence can affect trial interpretation.
5. R1/R2 after prior systemic therapy has less prospective evidence.
6. Radiation technology has evolved.
7. Patient selection in routine practice may differ from trial populations.
