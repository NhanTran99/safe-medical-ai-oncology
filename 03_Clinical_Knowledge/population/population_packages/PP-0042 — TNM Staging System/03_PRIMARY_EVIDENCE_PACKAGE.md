# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is the TNM staging system and what should patients understand about it?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the TNM staging system, including its purpose, the general meaning of T, N and M, why it is used worldwide, and how it contributes to staging, treatment planning and prognostic assessment.

This Population Package introduces the TNM framework without discussing detailed TNM categories, AJCC stage groups or stage-specific treatment recommendations.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of the TNM staging system
- Purpose of TNM staging
- General meaning of T (Tumor)
- General meaning of N (Regional Lymph Nodes)
- General meaning of M (Distant Metastasis)
- Role of TNM in determining cancer stage
- Contribution to treatment planning
- Contribution to prognostic assessment
- International standardization

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- TNM is the international standard system for describing the anatomical extent of gastric cancer.
- T refers to the primary tumor.
- N refers to regional lymph node involvement.
- M refers to distant metastasis.
- The TNM categories are combined to determine the overall stage of cancer.
- TNM staging supports treatment planning, prognostic assessment and multidisciplinary communication.
- TNM is an essential part of clinical assessment but does not represent the entire clinical picture.

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the purpose of the TNM staging system;
- the meaning of the T, N and M components;
- the international role of TNM staging;
- the contribution of TNM to prognosis and treatment planning;
- the need to integrate TNM with additional pathological and clinical information.

Differences between references are limited to educational emphasis and implementation details.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
