# Evidence Extraction Summary

## Core Concepts

- Early gastric adenocarcinoma may produce no symptoms.
- Symptoms are frequently nonspecific.
- Symptoms alone cannot diagnose gastric cancer.

---

## Common Symptoms

- Persistent indigestion.
- Epigastric discomfort or pain.
- Early satiety.
- Postprandial fullness.
- Loss of appetite.
- Unintentional weight loss.
- Nausea.
- Vomiting.
- Fatigue secondary to anemia.
- Gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Melena.
- Dysphagia (selected patients depending on tumor location).

---

## Important Clinical Messages

Many symptoms overlap with benign gastrointestinal disorders.

Persistent symptoms require medical evaluation.

Early diagnosis cannot rely on symptoms alone.

Definitive diagnosis requires appropriate investigations.

---

## Key Patient Messages

Symptoms

≠

Cancer.

No symptoms

≠

No cancer.

Persistent symptoms should not be ignored.

Patients should seek professional medical assessment rather than self-diagnose.
