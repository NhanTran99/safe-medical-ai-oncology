# Knowledge Classification

## Knowledge Type

Patient-facing clinical education / early gastric cancer treatment literacy / specialized endoscopic-resection modality.

## Atomic Clinical Question

> **What is endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), when can it be used for early gastric cancer, why can it provide more extensive/en-bloc resection than EMR, how is the specimen assessed for curative treatment, and what are the main benefits, risks, limitations, and downstream implications?**

## Primary Function

This PP is a **modality-specific treatment node** within the early-gastric-cancer endoscopic-resection pathway.

It explains:

- what ESD is;
- why it is used;
- which lesion characteristics may support its use;
- how ESD differs from EMR;
- why pathology determines whether treatment was curative;
- why additional treatment may sometimes be needed.

It does not own detailed procedural technique, detailed pathology methodology, or detailed surgery.

# Evidence Classification

## Established / Guideline-Supported

- ESD is a recognized endoscopic treatment for selected early gastric cancer.
- ESD can extend more deeply into the stomach wall than EMR.
- ESD can facilitate more extensive/en-bloc resection.
- ESD has classic and expanded indication frameworks.
- Histology, size, ulceration, invasion depth, LVI, margins, and expected nodal risk affect suitability/curative assessment.
- EUS before treatment is important when patients are being considered for ER.
- Pathologic assessment of the resection specimen is essential.
- Deep submucosal invasion, LVI, positive margins, and other high-risk findings can make endoscopic treatment incomplete.
- ESD is technically more complex than EMR.
- Bleeding and perforation are important ESD risks.
- Experienced/high-volume centers are recommended.
- ESD has a strong evidence base in East Asian practice.
- Surveillance remains necessary after endoscopic resection.

## Context-Dependent

- Whether a particular patient qualifies for ESD.
- Whether ESD or EMR is preferable for an individual lesion.
- Whether an expanded indication should be used in an individual case.
- Whether additional gastrectomy is needed after non-curative ESD.
- Whether historical Asian outcomes apply directly to another healthcare system.
- Individual recurrence risk.
- Individual likelihood of avoiding surgery.

## Not Owned by This PP

- Detailed ESD technique.
- Detailed pathology classification.
- Individualized pathology interpretation.
- Detailed EUS methodology.
- Detailed surgery.
- Detailed systemic treatment.
- Detailed surveillance scheduling.

# Clinical Reasoning Notes

## Why ESD Is a Distinct Knowledge Node

ESD is not merely another name for endoscopic resection.

The endoscopic-resection umbrella asks whether local endoscopic treatment is oncologically reasonable.

EMR and ESD then represent different technical/clinical modalities within that strategy.

This package therefore belongs downstream of the umbrella concept and alongside the dedicated EMR package.

---

## Why the Package Must Teach Curative Assessment

A patient can understandably interpret “the tumor was removed” as “the cancer is cured.”

The clinical evidence requires a more careful distinction.

The resection specimen determines whether:

- the lesion was completely removed;
- the depth remains within an acceptable range;
- the histology is compatible with the relevant indication;
- LVI is absent;
- margins are appropriate.

This makes pathology an essential part of the ESD knowledge pathway.

---

## Why the Package Must Teach Non-Curative ESD

A non-curative result is not merely a procedural complication.

It is a **clinical-pathologic treatment assessment**.

The result may redirect the patient from:

**ESD → surveillance**

to:

**ESD → additional treatment consideration**

This is why the package must explain downstream surgery without owning the surgical package itself.

# Clinical Context Interpretation

## If the user asks about “2 cm”

Do not answer:

> “Below 2 cm means ESD is appropriate.”

Instead explain that size is one element of a larger selection framework.

---

## If the user asks about “3 cm”

Explain that selected lesions up to the relevant size can appear within expanded ESD indications, but histology, ulceration, depth, LVI, and overall clinical context remain important.

---

## If the user asks about “500 µm”

Explain that this refers to a superficial submucosal invasion threshold used in the guideline framework.

Do not turn the value into a self-measured rule.

---

## If the user asks about “poorly differentiated”

Explain that selected poorly differentiated lesions can appear within expanded ESD indications, but this is a specialized context requiring complete clinical-pathologic assessment.

---

## If the user asks about “ulceration”

Explain that ulceration changes the indication framework but does not automatically exclude ESD in every circumstance.

# Evidence Communication Rules

## Rule 1 — Guideline versus study

Guideline-supported indications should be described as clinical framework.

Individual study outcomes should be labeled as study-level evidence.

---

## Rule 2 — “Preferred” versus “always required”

When NCCN says ESD is preferred in the current endoscopic-resection framework, do not convert this into:

> “Every patient should have ESD.”

---

## Rule 3 — “Potentially curative” versus “guaranteed cure”

Use:

> potentially curative / meets curative criteria

rather than:

> guaranteed cure.

---

## Rule 4 — “More extensive” versus “appropriate for deep cancer”

ESD's deeper resection capability should never be interpreted as permission to treat deep gastric-wall invasion endoscopically.

---

## Rule 5 — “Stomach-preserving” versus “surgery-free”

ESD can preserve the stomach in selected patients.

It does not guarantee avoidance of later surgery.

---

# Patient Communication Guardrails

When a patient asks:

> “Can I have ESD?”

the package should orient the patient to the factors clinicians evaluate rather than issue a yes/no decision.

Recommended framing:

> “ESD is considered for selected early gastric cancers. Your team usually looks at the tumor’s depth, size, histologic type, ulceration, risk of lymph-node spread, and whether the lesion can be completely removed. The final pathology after ESD also determines whether additional treatment is needed.”

---

When a patient asks:

> “Does ESD cure cancer?”

recommended framing:

> “For selected early gastric cancers, ESD can be definitive treatment when the final specimen meets the relevant curative criteria. If pathology shows deeper invasion, lymphovascular invasion, positive margins, or another high-risk feature, additional treatment may still be needed.”

---

When a patient asks:

> “Why not just have surgery?”

recommended framing:

> “Surgery can treat the primary tumor and remove regional lymph nodes, while ESD is a less invasive local treatment for selected cancers with a sufficiently low expected risk of lymph-node spread. The appropriate choice depends on the cancer’s characteristics and staging.”

# Boundary Runtime Rule

The package must preserve this ownership hierarchy:

**Endoscopic resection strategy**
→ asks whether local endoscopic treatment is appropriate.

**EMR**
→ explains mucosal resection.

**ESD**
→ explains submucosal dissection and broader/en-bloc endoscopic resection.

**Pathology**
→ determines whether the resection meets curative criteria.

**Surgery**
→ addresses disease for which endoscopic treatment is inadequate.

This hierarchy should remain stable across retrieval contexts.
