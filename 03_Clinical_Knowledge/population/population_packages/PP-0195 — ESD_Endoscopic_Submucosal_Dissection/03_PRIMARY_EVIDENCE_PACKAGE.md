# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What is endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), when can it be used for early gastric cancer, why can it provide more extensive/en-bloc resection than EMR, how is the ESD specimen assessed for curative treatment, and what are the main benefits, risks, limitations, and downstream implications?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded, patient-facing explanation of ESD as a specialized treatment modality for selected early gastric cancer.

The package is deliberately positioned between the broader endoscopic-resection strategy and downstream pathology/surgical management.

It teaches the clinical logic behind ESD without becoming:

- a procedural manual;
- a pathology textbook;
- a staging manual;
- a surgery manual;
- an individualized treatment calculator.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Direct Gastric-Cancer Guideline

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

Primary source for PP-0195.

The project source identifies ESD as endoscopic submucosal dissection and places EMR/ESD within endoscopic staging and therapy.

The NCCN source states that:

- EMR/ESD can be used for small focal lesions in early-stage disease;
- these procedures can provide a larger specimen;
- the specimen provides information on differentiation, LVI, and depth of invasion;
- EUS before treatment is particularly important for patients being considered for ER;
- ESD has greater en-bloc capability than EMR in cited evidence;
- ESD is preferred in the current NCCN endoscopic-resection framework;
- selected expanded ESD indications exist;
- pathology determines curative status;
- high-volume experienced centers are recommended;
- surveillance remains necessary.

**Source traceability:** `1. Gastric Cancer_v.2.2026_NCCN-3-109.pdf`.

---

## Level I — Regional Gastric-Cancer Guideline

### Vietnamese Guideline

The source identifies:

- cTis/cT1a disease as the setting for endoscopic resection;
- EMR and ESD as endoscopic-resection modalities;
- the principle that lesions should have very low likelihood of lymph-node metastasis and be suitable for en-bloc removal;
- absolute indication involving differentiated, non-ulcerated, clinically T1a tumors under 2 cm;
- expanded indications including selected larger, ulcerated, and poorly differentiated lesions;
- ESD as more commonly used than EMR when broader submucosal resection is required;
- detailed pathology assessment after resection;
- curative potential based on complete removal and low nodal-metastatic risk.

**Source traceability:** `3. Huong dan chan doan va dieu tri ung thu DA DAY.pdf`.

## Level I — Patient-Facing Cancer Education

### American Cancer Society — Stomach Cancer

The source explains that:

- EMR and ESD can treat some very early-stage stomach cancers;
- these procedures are performed through an endoscope without a skin incision;
- ESD goes deeper than EMR;
- ESD is more complex;
- not all doctors are trained in ESD;
- high-volume centers are important;
- these techniques are used more often in East Asian countries.

# Detailed Evidence Notes

## Evidence Note 1 — ESD as a specialized endoscopic treatment

The sources consistently position ESD within the endoscopic treatment of selected early gastric cancer.

This supports treating ESD as a dedicated modality rather than a generic synonym for endoscopic resection.

---

## Evidence Note 2 — Why the specimen matters

NCCN specifically describes EMR/ESD as providing a larger specimen that can improve assessment of:

- differentiation;
- LVI;
- depth of infiltration.

This supports a major educational principle:

> **The ESD specimen is part of the treatment assessment, not merely a diagnostic sample.**

---

## Evidence Note 3 — Depth and nodal risk

The Vietnamese guideline states that endoscopic treatment should be considered for lesions with very low likelihood of lymph-node metastasis and appropriate for en-bloc removal.

This provides the conceptual bridge between:

**local resection**

and

**oncologic adequacy**.

---

## Evidence Note 4 — Expanded indications

The NCCN and Vietnamese sources both support expanded indications.

These should be represented as **selected clinical contexts**, not automatic treatment rules.

This is particularly important for:

- ulcerated lesions;
- lesions >2 cm;
- selected poorly differentiated lesions;
- superficial submucosal invasion.

---

## Evidence Note 5 — ESD versus EMR

The NCCN source reports greater en-bloc efficacy for ESD in several studies and identifies ESD as preferred in its current framework.

ACS explains the patient-facing difference:

> ESD goes deeper than EMR and is more complex.

The final package should combine these two levels:

**clinical rationale + patient understanding**.

---

## Evidence Note 6 — Safety and expertise

Both NCCN and ACS emphasize technical complexity and the importance of experienced centers.

Therefore center expertise is not an optional contextual detail; it is a meaningful part of the package's safety education.

---

## Evidence Note 7 — Non-curative resection

The source framework makes clear that final pathology can identify:

- deeper invasion;
- LVI;
- positive margins;
- other unfavorable findings.

These findings can change the pathway toward additional surgery.

This supports the patient-facing statement:

> **ESD can be successful technically but still not be considered curative oncologically.**

# Evidence Consistency Review

## NCCN versus Vietnamese Guidance

The two sources are broadly concordant on the central architecture:

**early gastric cancer**

→ **selected low nodal-risk lesions**

→ **endoscopic resection**

→ **EMR/ESD**

→ **pathology**

→ **curative/non-curative assessment**

The Vietnamese source gives a regional formulation of absolute and expanded indications and explicitly states that ESD is more often used when broader submucosal resection is required.

NCCN provides the current U.S.-based guideline framework and detailed evidence on ESD preference, expanded indications, pathology, safety, and surveillance.

No material contradiction affecting the locked PP-0195 scope was identified.

---

## NCCN versus ACS

ACS is consistent with the guideline-level concept that:

- ESD is for selected very early disease;
- ESD is deeper and more complex than EMR;
- experienced centers matter.

ACS is used primarily for patient-facing explanation and not to replace the disease-specific guideline framework.

---

# Evidence Gaps

The project sources do not provide sufficient depth for a source-grounded technical ESD manual covering:

1. Exact procedural sequence.
2. Marking technique.
3. Injection technique.
4. Electrosurgical settings.
5. Specific dissection knives.
6. Traction systems.
7. Detailed hemostatic technique.
8. Perforation closure.
9. Rescue-surgery technique.
10. Detailed anesthesia management.

These gaps are intentionally preserved rather than filled from general medical knowledge.

Additional evidence gaps include:

- exact individualized selection thresholds outside the stated guideline frameworks;
- universal numerical complication rates;
- universal recurrence-risk estimates;
- individualized probability of avoiding surgery.
