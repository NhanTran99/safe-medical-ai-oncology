# Knowledge Block 1 — What Are BS Evidence Codes?

## Patient Explanation

**BS Evidence Codes** are a group of standardized evidence categories used in the ACMG/AMP framework to describe **Strong Benign Evidence**.

They are used when scientific or clinical evidence provides a strong reason to believe that a genetic variant is **unlikely to cause disease**.

---

## Clinical Importance

BS evidence represents a stronger level of benign evidence than **Supporting Benign Evidence (BP)**.

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does "Strong Benign Evidence" Mean?

## Patient Explanation

"Strong benign evidence" means that the available evidence provides a **strong reason to think that a genetic variant is not disease-causing**.

The word **strong** describes the strength of that particular evidence category.

It does not mean that the variant's final classification can always be determined from that evidence alone.

---

## Clinical Importance

Variant interpretation considers evidence from multiple sources and categories before reaching an overall classification.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — How Does BS Compare With BP?

## Patient Explanation

The ACMG/AMP framework uses different levels of evidence strength.

For benign evidence, two important levels are:

- **BP — Supporting Benign Evidence**
- **BS — Strong Benign Evidence**

BS represents a **stronger level of benign evidence** than BP.

This distinction helps genetics professionals describe how much weight a particular piece of evidence should carry during variant interpretation.

---

## Clinical Importance

The distinction between supporting and strong evidence prevents different types of evidence from being treated as if they all had the same strength.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does Strong Benign Evidence Mean the Variant Is Definitely Benign?

## Patient Explanation

Not necessarily.

BS evidence is strong evidence that supports a benign interpretation, but the final classification of a genetic variant depends on the **overall evidence** available for that variant.

Other evidence may also need to be considered.

---

## Clinical Importance

A single evidence category should not automatically be presented to a patient as the final answer unless the applicable classification framework and evidence combination support that conclusion.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Are Different Evidence Strengths Used?

## Patient Explanation

Genetic variants can be evaluated using many different types of information.

Some evidence is more convincing than other evidence.

The ACMG/AMP framework therefore uses different evidence-strength categories to help genetics professionals distinguish between:

- supporting evidence;
- strong evidence;
- and other levels of evidence.

This creates a structured approach to interpreting genetic variants.

---

## Clinical Importance

Evidence-strength categories help make variant interpretation more systematic and transparent.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

If a variant has strong benign evidence, there is no need to consider anything else.

**Fact**

Strong benign evidence is an important part of variant interpretation, but the overall classification depends on the applicable evidence framework and the total relevant evidence.

---

# Key Messages

- **BS Evidence Codes** represent **Strong Benign Evidence**.
- BS is stronger than **BP (Supporting Benign Evidence)**.
- BS evidence provides a strong reason to consider a variant unlikely to cause disease.
- Evidence strength helps genetics professionals weigh different types of information systematically.
- BS evidence is part of the overall variant interpretation process.
- The individual BS criteria and evidence-combination rules are addressed separately.
