# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What are BS Evidence Codes (Strong Benign Evidence)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **BS Evidence Codes** as the **Strong Benign Evidence** category within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework.

BS evidence represents a stronger level of benign evidence than **BP (Supporting Benign Evidence)**. It helps communicate that a particular body of evidence provides a strong reason to consider a genetic variant unlikely to cause disease.

However, the presence of BS evidence should not be presented as automatically equivalent to a final benign classification. Variant interpretation requires consideration of the applicable evidence framework and the total relevant evidence.

This Population Package introduces the **BS evidence family only**. Individual criteria such as **BS1–BS4**, **BA1**, and evidence-combination rules are intentionally deferred to separate Population Packages.

# Evidence Hierarchy Context

The ACMG/AMP framework organizes evidence according to different strengths.

For benign evidence, the relevant conceptual categories include:

| Category | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **BA** | Stand-alone Benign Evidence |
| **BS** | Strong Benign Evidence |
| **BP** | Supporting Benign Evidence |

This Population Package focuses exclusively on **BS**.

The individual criteria within these categories are addressed separately.

---

# BS Versus BP

The distinction between BP and BS is important for patient education.

### BP — Supporting Benign Evidence

Provides **supporting-level evidence** that a variant may be benign.

### BS — Strong Benign Evidence

Provides **stronger evidence** supporting the interpretation that a variant is benign.

Therefore:

> **BS carries greater evidence strength than BP.**

This difference does not mean that every BS criterion automatically determines the final classification. The applicable classification framework and total evidence remain important.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence supports the following educational messages:

- BS Evidence Codes represent **Strong Benign Evidence**.
- BS is stronger than Supporting Benign Evidence (BP).
- Evidence strength is used to distinguish how convincing different evidence categories are.
- BS evidence can provide an important reason to consider a variant unlikely to cause disease.
- Final variant interpretation requires integration of the relevant evidence under the applicable framework.
- Individual BS criteria require separate interpretation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

The conceptual distinction between **Supporting Benign Evidence (BP)** and **Strong Benign Evidence (BS)** is consistent with the ACMG/AMP evidence-strength framework.

This package deliberately does not assign specific evidentiary meaning to individual BS criteria. That content will be developed separately so that each criterion can be presented according to its own clinical and technical requirements.

The package also avoids presenting **"strong evidence"** as synonymous with **"final diagnosis"**. Evidence strength describes the contribution of a particular evidence category to variant interpretation; it does not replace the overall classification process.
