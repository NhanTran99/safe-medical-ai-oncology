# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What does the "Final Diagnosis" section of my pathology report mean?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the **Final Diagnosis** section as the principal diagnostic summary within a pathology report.

This Population Package explains that the Final Diagnosis summarizes the pathologist's diagnostic conclusions, serves as an important starting point for clinical discussion, and must always be interpreted together with the patient's complete clinical information.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of Final Diagnosis
- Purpose of the Final Diagnosis section
- Typical information contained
- Relationship to the remainder of the pathology report
- Importance of clinical interpretation

---

## Excluded

- Interpretation of specific diagnoses
- Tumor grade
- TNM stage
- Margin status
- Lymphovascular invasion
- Perineural invasion
- Biomarker testing
- Molecular testing
- Prognosis
- Treatment planning

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Final Diagnosis summarizes the pathologist's principal conclusions.
- It is usually the primary section clinicians review.
- Supporting sections remain important.
- Clinical interpretation requires integration with other medical information.
- Patients benefit from discussing the report with their healthcare team.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

CAP reporting standards and major oncology organizations consistently recognize the Final Diagnosis as the official diagnostic summary within a pathology report while emphasizing that interpretation depends on complete clinical correlation.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- pathology reporting principles from CAP;
- educational principles from NCI and ACS;
- multidisciplinary oncology guidance consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
