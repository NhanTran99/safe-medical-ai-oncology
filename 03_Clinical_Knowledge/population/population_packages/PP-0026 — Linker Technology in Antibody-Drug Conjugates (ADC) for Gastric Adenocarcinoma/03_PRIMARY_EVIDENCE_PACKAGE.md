# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> What is the linker in an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), and why is it important in the treatment of gastric adenocarcinoma?

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an evidence-based understanding of the linker component of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

This Population Package explains, in plain language, what the linker is, why it connects the antibody and payload, how it supports targeted drug delivery, and why it is an essential structural component of ADC therapy.

The package intentionally avoids detailed chemistry, intracellular biology and drug-specific linker technologies.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The linker is the structural connection between the antibody and the payload.
- The linker enables all components of an ADC to function together.
- Linker stability supports targeted drug delivery.
- The linker contributes to the precision of ADC therapy.
- The linker itself is not responsible for the anti-cancer effect.
- Patients benefit from understanding how all three ADC components work together.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary authoritative references.

All major references consistently describe the linker as an essential structural component that enables coordinated antibody-guided drug delivery within ADC technology.

Differences among references primarily involve:

- linker chemistry;
- intracellular drug release;
- payload activation;
- toxicity mechanisms;
- individual ADC platform design.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- clinical recommendations from NCCN;
- evidence interpretation from JNCCN;
- patient-oriented explanations from NCI and ACS;
- internationally harmonized terminology from ESMO.
