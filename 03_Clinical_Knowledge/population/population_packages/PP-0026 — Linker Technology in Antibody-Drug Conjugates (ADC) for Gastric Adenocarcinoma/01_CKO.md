# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is the Linker?

## Patient Explanation

The **linker** is the connector that joins the antibody and the cancer-fighting medicine (payload) together in an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC).

You can think of it as the connection that allows both parts to work as one treatment.

Without the linker, the antibody and the payload could not function together as an ADC.

---

## Clinical Importance

The linker is an essential structural component of every ADC.

---

## Key Concepts

- Connects antibody and payload
- Essential component
- Part of every ADC

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Is the Linker Important?

## Patient Explanation

The linker helps keep the ADC together while it travels through the body.

This allows the antibody to guide the treatment toward cancer cells before the payload performs its intended role.

---

## Clinical Importance

A properly functioning linker supports accurate targeted drug delivery.

---

## Key Concepts

- Holds treatment together
- Supports targeted delivery
- Works with the antibody

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Must the Linker Be Stable?

## Patient Explanation

The linker is designed to help keep the antibody and payload connected until the treatment reaches its intended target.

This helps the ADC function as a coordinated treatment system.

The detailed chemistry behind this process is discussed in later Population Packages.

---

## Clinical Importance

Linker stability contributes to the overall effectiveness of ADC therapy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Stability
- Coordinated treatment
- Target-directed delivery

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — How Does the Linker Support Precision Oncology?

## Patient Explanation

Because the linker helps the antibody carry the payload toward selected cancer cells, it contributes to the precision of ADC therapy.

Together, the antibody, linker and payload form a treatment designed to deliver therapy more selectively.

---

## Clinical Importance

The linker is one of the components that enables precision-guided therapy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Precision medicine
- Coordinated delivery
- Target-guided treatment

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does Every ADC Use a Linker?

## Patient Explanation

Yes.

Every antibody-drug conjugate includes a linker because the antibody and payload must be connected to function together.

Different ADC medicines may use different linker technologies, but all ADCs require this basic component.

---

## Clinical Importance

The linker is a universal feature of ADC technology.

---

## Key Concepts

- Universal component
- Different designs
- Same overall purpose

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Benefits and Limitations

## Patient Explanation

The linker contributes to the ability of ADCs to deliver treatment in a more targeted way.

However, the linker is only one part of the complete treatment.

Successful therapy also depends on:

- the appropriate biomarker;
- the antibody;
- the payload;
- the patient's overall clinical situation.

---

## Clinical Importance

The linker supports—but does not determine—the success of treatment.

---

## Key Concepts

- One component of ADC
- Supports treatment precision
- Individualized care

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Common Questions About the Linker

## Patient Explanation

Some patients think the linker is the medicine itself.

This is incorrect.

The linker does not provide the anti-cancer effect.

Instead, it helps connect the antibody and the payload so they can function together.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

The linker is the cancer medicine.

**Fact**

The payload is the cancer-fighting medicine. The linker is the connector that joins the payload to the antibody.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Should Patients Remember?

Patients should understand that:

- every ADC contains a linker;
- the linker connects the antibody and payload;
- the linker supports targeted drug delivery;
- all three components work together as one treatment.

Questions about treatment options should always be discussed with the healthcare team.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding the linker helps patients understand the overall design of ADC therapy.

---

# Key Messages

- The linker connects the antibody and the payload.
- Every ADC contains a linker.
- The linker helps keep the treatment functioning as one system.
- The linker contributes to targeted drug delivery.
- The linker itself is not the cancer-fighting medicine.
- ADC treatment remains individualized and requires clinical supervision.
