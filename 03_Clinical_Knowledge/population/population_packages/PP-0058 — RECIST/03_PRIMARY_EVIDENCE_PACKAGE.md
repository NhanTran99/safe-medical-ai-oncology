# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is RECIST?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **RECIST (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors)**, including what the acronym means, why RECIST was developed, how it provides a standardized approach for evaluating changes in solid tumors over time, and why it is widely used in oncology practice and research.

This Population Package introduces RECIST as a standardized assessment framework without discussing RECIST 1.1 technical criteria, lesion measurements, response categories or treatment decisions.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of RECIST
- Meaning of the acronym
- RECIST as a standardized response assessment system
- Purpose of evaluating changes in solid tumors over time
- General role of imaging studies
- Difference between RECIST and cancer staging
- Explanation that RECIST is not a treatment

---

- Target lesions
- Non-target lesions

- Stable Disease (SD)

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- RECIST is a standardized framework for evaluating changes in solid tumors.
- The acronym stands for **Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors**.
- RECIST improves consistency when describing treatment response across clinical practice and research.
- Imaging studies commonly provide the information used for RECIST assessment.
- RECIST is an assessment tool and should not be confused with cancer staging, treatment or prognosis.
- Whether RECIST is appropriate depends on the individual clinical situation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the purpose of RECIST as a standardized response assessment system;
- its widespread use for evaluating solid tumors;
- its role in improving consistency of communication;
- the distinction between RECIST and TNM staging.

Differences between references relate mainly to technical implementation, which is intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted RECIST terminology without introducing technical criteria.
