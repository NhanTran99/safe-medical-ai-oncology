# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is metastasis?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **metastasis**, including what the term means, how metastatic cancer differs from the primary tumor, why metastatic tumors retain the identity of the original cancer, and how the concept of metastasis relates to—but is different from—the M category in the TNM staging system.

This Population Package introduces the concept of metastasis without discussing TNM classifications, metastatic mechanisms, organ-specific metastases, diagnostic investigations or treatment recommendations.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of metastasis
- Definition of the primary tumor
- Relationship between primary and metastatic tumors
- Simple explanation of cancer spread
- Difference between metastasis and the M category
- Relationship between metastasis and cancer staging
- Importance of confirming metastasis through appropriate medical evaluation

---

## Excluded

- M0
- M1
- Peritoneal metastasis
- Liver metastasis
- Lung metastasis
- Bone metastasis
- Brain metastasis
- Biological mechanisms of metastasis
- Routes of tumor spread
- Imaging investigations
- AJCC Stage Groups
- Stage-specific prognosis
- Treatment recommendations for metastatic disease

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Metastasis means cancer has spread from its original location.
- The first tumor is called the primary tumor.
- Metastatic tumors remain the same cancer type as the original tumor.
- Metastasis is different from the M category used in TNM staging.
- The presence of metastasis is determined through appropriate medical evaluation.
- Doctors integrate metastatic status with many additional clinical findings before making treatment decisions.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of metastasis;
- the distinction between primary and metastatic tumors;
- the relationship between metastasis and TNM staging;
- the importance of confirming metastatic disease before assigning stage;
- the role of metastatic disease in treatment planning and prognosis.

Differences between references are limited to wording and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific terminology from NCI;
- patient-friendly explanations from ACS;
- clinical terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
