# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What does survivorship care mean after gastric cancer treatment, and what long-term health, functional, nutritional, psychosocial, and preventive needs may remain?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded, patient-facing framework for understanding survivorship after gastric cancer treatment.

The package is designed to bridge:

**cancer treatment**

↓

**transition**

↓

**long-term health and survivorship needs**

without duplicating:

- surveillance;
- recurrence detection;
- recurrent/metastatic treatment;
- long-term follow-up operations;
- acute toxicity management.

# Detailed Evidence Notes

## Note 1 — Survivorship and surveillance

NCCN places surveillance within survivorship care but also states that routine gastric-cancer-specific surveillance is not recommended beyond 5 years.

This supports a critical distinction:

**Surveillance is a component of survivorship, not the definition of survivorship.**

The package therefore does not reproduce PP-0220.

## Note 2 — Long-term sequelae

The NCCN survivorship section specifically identifies:

- weight loss;
- diarrhea;
- chemotherapy-induced neuropathy;
- fatigue;
- bone-health problems.

These are treated as core gastric-cancer survivorship issues.

## Note 3 — Post-gastrectomy sequelae

NCCN identifies long-term, often lifelong issues after gastrectomy, including:

- postprandial fullness;
- nutritional deficiencies;
- dumping syndrome;
- small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth.

The guideline specifically highlights vitamin B12, vitamin D, calcium, iron and zinc.

This makes post-gastrectomy survivorship one of the most gastric-cancer-specific elements of PP-0229.

## Note 4 — Quality of life

The supplied NCCN discussion cites prospective follow-up evidence showing that symptoms including diarrhea, dysphagia, reflux, eating restrictions, physical functioning, cognitive functioning and fatigue can negatively affect long-term quality of life.

The package therefore treats quality of life as a clinical outcome of survivorship, not an optional psychosocial add-on.

## Note 5 — Healthy behaviors

NCCN recommends:

- healthy body weight;
- physical activity;
- healthy diet;
- limiting alcohol;
- smoking cessation;
- preventive care and immunization.

The package preserves these as general health-promotion recommendations and does not overclaim that they guarantee prevention of recurrence.

## Note 6 — Primary-care coordination

NCCN explicitly recommends maintaining a therapeutic relationship with a PCP throughout life, defining roles between oncology and primary care, communicating those roles to the patient, and considering transfer of care when appropriate.

This supports primary-care coordination as a core survivorship domain.

## Note 7 — Evidence maturity

The supplied NCCN guideline notes that gastric-cancer survivorship recommendations are supported partly by smaller studies, clinical experience, consensus recommendations, and guidelines.

Therefore the package distinguishes:

- established guideline-supported principles;
- context-dependent management;
- areas where detailed evidence remains limited.

# Patient-Facing Interpretation

The evidence supports the following messages:

1. Treatment completion does not necessarily end healthcare needs.
2. Some gastric-cancer treatment effects can persist long term.
3. Gastrectomy can create lifelong nutritional and gastrointestinal issues.
4. Survivorship includes prevention and general health maintenance.
5. Primary care remains important.
6. Surveillance and survivorship are not the same.
7. Symptoms should be assessed rather than self-attributed.
8. Healthy behaviors support health but do not guarantee recurrence prevention.
9. Survivorship plans should be individualized.

| Misconception | Corrective Message | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

| Survivor means cured | Survivorship does not establish cancer status | ESMO-ASCO / ACS |
| Survivorship equals surveillance | Surveillance is one component | NCCN |
| Nutritional issues are temporary | Some deficiencies can require lifelong monitoring | NCCN |
| Fatigue proves recurrence | Fatigue has multiple possible causes | NCCN survivorship framework |
| Neuropathy always resolves | Persistent neuropathy can affect function | NCCN |
| Healthy lifestyle guarantees no recurrence | Supports health; does not guarantee recurrence prevention | NCCN/ESMO-ASCO |

---

# Evidence Gaps

1. There is no single survivorship pathway that applies identically to every gastric-cancer survivor.
2. Exact follow-up and transition-of-care schedules vary.
3. Nutritional monitoring and replacement must be individualized.
4. The optimal frequency of every survivorship assessment is not universally established.
5. Psychosocial needs vary substantially among individuals.
6. The supplied sources do not justify a universal lifestyle intervention as a recurrence-prevention guarantee.
7. Detailed rehabilitation and nutrition algorithms are not within this package.
8. Detailed disease-specific surveillance is delegated to PP-0220.
9. Detailed recurrence detection is delegated to PP-0221.
10. Long-term follow-up operations are delegated to PP-0230.
11. Detailed treatment-toxicity management is delegated to PP-0231.

These gaps do not block the package because its educational purpose is framework-level survivorship, not individualized management.

# Final Evidence Decision

The evidence base is sufficient to support PP-0229 as a distinct, clinically governed survivorship package.

The strongest architectural conclusion is:

**Survivorship ≠ Surveillance ≠ Recurrence Detection ≠ Long-term Follow-up ≠ Toxicity Management.**

PP-0229 therefore remains a distinct knowledge node rather than a duplicate follow-up package.
