# Intended Runtime Usage

PP-0229 is intended to support patient-facing and clinician-supported educational interactions concerning life after gastric cancer treatment.

It may be retrieved when a user asks:

- What happens after gastric cancer treatment?
- What is cancer survivorship?
- What long-term problems can happen after gastrectomy?
- Why do I need nutrition monitoring after gastrectomy?
- Can fatigue or neuropathy continue after treatment?
- What should be included in a survivorship care plan?
- Who should look after me after cancer treatment?
- What preventive care do I still need?
- Why is survivorship different from surveillance?
- What can I do to support my health after treatment?

It should not be used as a substitute for:

- recurrence assessment;
- individualized surveillance planning;
- treatment selection;
- individualized toxicity management;
- individualized nutritional prescriptions;
- individualized prognosis.

# Evidence Classification

## Established / Guideline-Supported

- Survivorship includes routine medical care, preventive care, and cancer screening.
- Gastric-cancer-specific surveillance is not recommended beyond 5 years in the supplied NCCN v2.2026 framework.
- Weight loss, diarrhea, chemotherapy-induced neuropathy, and fatigue are recognized survivorship issues.
- Bone health is a long-term concern.
- Gastrectomy can create long-term or lifelong nutritional and gastrointestinal issues.
- Vitamin B12, vitamin D, calcium, iron, and zinc deficiencies may require monitoring/management.
- Healthy body weight, physical activity, healthy diet, alcohol limitation, smoking cessation, preventive care, and immunization are survivorship principles.
- Primary-care coordination and defined roles among oncology, PCPs, and subspecialists are recommended.
- Survivorship care planning includes treatment history, follow-up, surveillance, screening, post-treatment needs, long-term effects, health risks, role delineation, healthy behaviors, and periodic needs assessment.

## Context-Dependent

- Exact duration and schedule of follow-up.
- Timing of transfer of care.
- Frequency of nutritional monitoring.
- Need and interval for bone-density assessment.
- Rehabilitation referral.
- Psychosocial referral.
- Cancer-screening strategy based on individual risk.
- Nutritional replacement and monitoring after subtotal versus total gastrectomy.

## Evidence-Limited / Consensus-Heavy

The supplied NCCN discussion explicitly notes that gastric-cancer survivorship recommendations are supported in part by smaller studies, clinical experience, consensus recommendations, and guidelines rather than a large randomized-trial evidence base.

This limitation must be preserved rather than converted into false certainty.

# Knowledge Units — Runtime Definitions

## KU-01 — Survivorship

Survivorship is the continuing health and care framework for people living after or through cancer treatment. It does not itself establish cancer status.

## KU-02 — Survivorship Care Plan

A structured record connecting treatment history to follow-up, surveillance, screening, long-term effects, health risks, roles of clinicians, healthy behaviors, ongoing needs, and resources.

## KU-03 — Long-term Sequelae

Persistent or late effects of disease or treatment that can influence physical, nutritional, functional, psychological, or social health.

## KU-04 — Post-gastrectomy Survivorship

Long-term adaptation and monitoring after partial or total gastrectomy, including nutrition, GI function, weight, and quality of life.

## KU-05 — Health Promotion

Healthy body weight, physical activity, healthy diet, smoking cessation, alcohol limitation, preventive care, and immunization.

## KU-06 — Coordination

Long-term survivorship may involve oncology, primary care, nutrition, rehabilitation, and other specialists.

## KU-07 — Surveillance Interface

Surveillance is part of survivorship planning but is separately owned by PP-0220.

## KU-08 — Recurrence Interface

Fear/awareness of recurrence may be discussed in survivorship, while detection is owned by PP-0221.

## KU-09 — Long-term Follow-up Interface

Survivorship defines needs and content; PP-0230 defines operational follow-up over time.

## KU-10 — Toxicity Interface

Long-term consequences may be discussed in survivorship; detailed treatment-toxicity management is owned by PP-0231.

# Safety / Runtime Rules

1. Do not tell a patient that survivorship means cure.
2. Do not interpret a symptom as recurrence or non-recurrence.
3. Do not create an individualized surveillance schedule.
4. Do not prescribe individualized supplements, diets, exercise programs, or medications.
5. Do not replace the patient's oncology, primary-care, nutrition, rehabilitation, or specialist team.
6. Explain that long-term effects can have multiple causes.
7. Encourage clinical assessment of new, worsening, persistent, or concerning symptoms.
8. Preserve uncertainty where evidence is consensus-based.
9. Do not imply healthy behavior guarantees recurrence prevention.
10. Keep detailed ownership with the designated PP.
