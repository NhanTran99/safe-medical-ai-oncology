## B. Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain what CLDN18.2 is and why it can become clinically relevant in gastric adenocarcinoma.
2. Understand that CLDN18.2 testing is a tissue-based biomarker assessment rather than a routine blood test or a test for a CLDN18 gene mutation.
3. Understand the current NCCN-supported clinical setting in which CLDN18.2 testing is recommended: untreated patients with inoperable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma for whom zolbetuximab is being considered.
4. Recognize the main specimen sources used for assessment: biopsy or surgical tumor specimen.
5. Understand that the clinical assessment is performed by immunohistochemistry (IHC).
6. Understand the current NCCN positivity criterion: at least 75% of viable tumor cells showing moderate-to-strong membranous CLDN18.2 staining at 2+ or 3+ intensity.
7. Distinguish a CLDN18.2-negative result from proof that no CLDN18.2 protein exists anywhere in the tumor.
8. Understand why CLDN18.2 status is considered alongside other biomarkers such as HER2, PD-L1, and MSI/MMR rather than replacing them.
9. Understand that a positive CLDN18.2 result can make CLDN18.2-directed treatment relevant but does not by itself determine an individualized treatment plan.
10. Recognize the major limitations of CLDN18.2 testing, including assay variation, sampling, tumor heterogeneity, and the need to interpret results in clinical context.

# D. Clinical Knowledge Blocks

## Block 1 — What Is CLDN18.2?

CLDN18.2 stands for **claudin-18 isoform 2**. It is a transmembrane protein associated with tight junctions in epithelial cells.

NCCN describes CLDN18.2 as a tight-junction transmembrane protein that is typically expressed in normal gastric epithelium and can be retained during malignant transformation.

The clinical importance of CLDN18.2 in gastric cancer is not simply that the protein exists. Its importance comes from the fact that, in some gastric cancers, the protein becomes sufficiently exposed and expressed on tumor cells to serve as a target for treatment.

The current clinical package therefore treats CLDN18.2 primarily as an **actionable tissue biomarker**.

---

## Block 2 — Why Can CLDN18.2 Become a Cancer Target?

In normal gastric epithelium, CLDN18.2 can be relatively inaccessible to therapeutic targeting because of its normal cellular localization.

NCCN describes changes in tumor-cell adhesion and polarity that can expose the normally less accessible CLDN18.2 protein. This exposure creates a biologically plausible and clinically useful target for a monoclonal antibody such as zolbetuximab.

This does not mean that every gastric cancer expresses CLDN18.2 at a clinically actionable level.

The purpose of testing is therefore to determine whether the patient's tumor meets the predefined clinical criterion for CLDN18.2 positivity.

---

## Block 3 — Why Is CLDN18.2 Testing Performed?

The primary clinical purpose of testing is **treatment selection**.

CLDN18.2 testing identifies whether the tumor meets the biomarker definition that can make CLDN18.2-directed therapy relevant.

Under the current NCCN framework, testing is specifically connected to consideration of zolbetuximab in patients with untreated inoperable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma.

Thus, CLDN18.2 testing is not simply a descriptive pathology exercise. It is part of precision treatment planning.

---

## Block 4 — Who Should Be Tested?

The current NCCN v2.2026 biomarker-testing table identifies testing for patients with:

- untreated;
- inoperable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic;
- gastric adenocarcinoma;

when zolbetuximab therapy is being considered.

NCCN's gastric-cancer treatment algorithm also places CLDN18.2 testing within the advanced/metastatic work-up when it has not previously been performed.

This is important because CLDN18.2 testing should not be described as a universal test for every gastric cancer regardless of clinical setting.

The indication is linked to the clinical situation in which CLDN18.2-directed treatment may be relevant.

---

## Block 5 — When Is Testing Performed?

Testing is performed early enough to inform treatment selection when a CLDN18.2-directed treatment pathway is being considered.

The current guideline wording specifically identifies **untreated** patients in the relevant advanced clinical setting.

The exact timing within an individual's diagnostic and treatment workflow can depend on specimen availability and the overall clinical plan.

The package should therefore teach the principle:

> **The biomarker should be known before the treatment decision for which the biomarker is relevant.**

It should not invent a universal number of days or a rigid testing schedule.

---

## Block 6 — What Sample Is Used?

CLDN18.2 assessment is performed on **tumor tissue**.

NCCN specifies:

- biopsy specimen; or
- surgical specimen.

The 2026 guideline describes the assessment as a qualitative IHC assay for **FFPE tumor tissue**.

This makes CLDN18.2 testing fundamentally different from a routine blood-based test.

A blood test should not be assumed to substitute for the guideline-defined CLDN18.2 tissue assessment.

---

## Block 7 — What Is IHC?

**Immunohistochemistry (IHC)** is a pathology technique used to detect a specific protein in tissue using an antibody.

For CLDN18.2 testing, IHC is used to determine whether CLDN18.2 protein is expressed on tumor cells and whether the pattern and extent of staining meet the clinical positivity criterion.

The patient-facing concept is:

> **The laboratory stains the tumor tissue to see whether CLDN18.2 protein is present on the tumor-cell membranes at the required level.**

Detailed laboratory staining protocols and analytical validation are outside this package.

---

## Block 8 — What Does “Membranous” Staining Mean?

The NCCN criterion is based on **membrane CLDN18.2 staining**.

The cell membrane is the boundary surrounding a cell. For this biomarker, the location of staining matters.

Therefore, the test is not simply asking:

> “Is there any brown staining somewhere on the slide?”

It is asking whether the relevant tumor cells demonstrate the specified **membranous CLDN18.2 staining**.

This distinction is important for understanding why the test has a defined interpretation rather than an informal “high versus low” visual judgment.

---

## Block 9 — What Does 2+ or 3+ Mean?

IHC staining intensity is commonly described using ordinal categories.

For CLDN18.2, the current NCCN positivity criterion requires:

- moderate-to-strong membrane staining;
- corresponding to **2+ or 3+ intensity**.

The intensity requirement is only one component of the result.

A tumor does not become CLDN18.2-positive simply because some cells show 2+ or 3+ staining.

The proportion of viable tumor cells must also reach the required threshold.

---

## Block 10 — Why Does the 75% Threshold Matter?

The current NCCN criterion defines CLDN18.2 positivity as:

> **at least 75% of viable tumor cells demonstrating moderate-to-strong membranous CLDN18.2 staining (2+ or 3+ intensity).**

The threshold therefore combines:

1. the **proportion of viable tumor cells**;
2. the **location of staining** — membrane;
3. the **intensity of staining** — 2+ or 3+.

All three components matter.

The 75% figure refers to **viable tumor cells**, not 75% of the entire pathology slide.

---

## Block 11 — What Does CLDN18.2-Positive Mean?

A CLDN18.2-positive result means that the tested tumor specimen meets the current guideline-defined IHC criterion.

In practical terms, this means:

- sufficient viable tumor cells show the required membranous staining;
- the staining is at the required moderate-to-strong intensity;
- the result reaches the ≥75% threshold.

A positive result therefore identifies a tumor with a biomarker profile that may make CLDN18.2-directed therapy clinically relevant.

It does not by itself determine that treatment must be given.

---

## Block 12 — What Does CLDN18.2-Negative Mean?

Under the current NCCN table, a negative result is defined as fewer than 75% of viable tumor cells demonstrating moderate-to-strong membrane CLDN18.2 staining.

A negative result should not be interpreted as proof that the tumor contains absolutely no CLDN18.2 protein.

It means that the tested specimen **does not meet the current clinical threshold for CLDN18.2 positivity**.

This distinction matters because biomarkers are interpreted using predefined clinical thresholds rather than an absolute biological yes/no concept.

---

## Block 13 — Negative Is Not the Same as “No Protein Exists”

A tumor can have some CLDN18.2 expression without meeting the threshold for a CLDN18.2-positive classification.

Therefore:

**Biological expression**

and

**clinical biomarker positivity**

are not always identical concepts.

The clinical classification is based on the validated/guideline-defined threshold.

---

## Block 14 — What If the Tissue Is Inadequate?

A technically inadequate specimen should not automatically be treated as a biological negative.

The ability to classify a biomarker depends on having suitable tumor tissue and an interpretable assay.

If the specimen is insufficient or otherwise not adequately evaluable, the appropriate clinical response is to consider pathology review and, when clinically appropriate, whether additional material is available.

This package does not prescribe a universal re-biopsy rule because the decision depends on the clinical situation.

---

## Block 15 — Can CLDN18.2 Expression Be Heterogeneous?

Tumor biomarkers can vary across different areas of a tumor.

NCCN also notes that reported CLDN18.2 prevalence varies according to detection methods, assays, and definitions of positivity.

Therefore a test result represents the tissue that was sampled and assessed.

This is one reason why a pathology result must be interpreted in the context of:

- specimen type;
- tumor adequacy;
- staining pattern;
- clinical setting.

Detailed heterogeneity and scoring methodology are delegated to the dedicated CLDN18.2 IHC and scoring packages.

---

## Block 16 — How Common Is CLDN18.2 Positivity?

NCCN reports CLDN18.2-positive tumors in approximately **24%–38% of advanced gastric and EGJ adenocarcinomas**.

However, this range should not be treated as a universal fixed prevalence.

NCCN explicitly notes that prevalence varies across studies because of differences in:

- detection methods;
- assays;
- definitions of positivity.

Therefore the patient-facing message is:

> **CLDN18.2 positivity occurs in a substantial minority of advanced gastric/EGJ adenocarcinomas, but the exact percentage depends on how the biomarker is measured and defined.**

---

## Block 17 — Does Histology Predict CLDN18.2 Status?

NCCN reports that CLDN18.2 expression has been observed in intestinal-type gastric tumors and is more commonly seen in diffuse-subtype tumors.

This is a **clinicopathologic association**, not a substitute for testing.

A patient should not be considered CLDN18.2-positive merely because the tumor has diffuse histology.

Direct biomarker assessment remains the basis for clinical classification.

---

## Block 18 — Is CLDN18.2 the Same as HER2?

No.

CLDN18.2 and HER2 are different biomarkers.

Both may be evaluated in gastric adenocarcinoma and both can influence targeted-treatment decisions, but they measure different biological features and have different testing criteria.

NCCN describes CLDN18.2 positivity as independent of HER2 status.

Therefore:

- HER2-positive does not automatically mean CLDN18.2-positive;
- HER2-negative does not automatically mean CLDN18.2-negative.

HER2 testing belongs to PP-0181.

---

## Block 19 — Is CLDN18.2 the Same as PD-L1?

No.

PD-L1 is an immune-related biomarker used in immunotherapy selection, whereas CLDN18.2 is a tumor-cell surface biomarker used in the context of CLDN18.2-directed targeted therapy.

NCCN describes CLDN18.2 positivity as independent of PD-L1.

Therefore CLDN18.2 testing does not replace PD-L1 testing.

Detailed PD-L1 assessment belongs to PP-0183.

---

## Block 20 — Is CLDN18.2 the Same as MSI/MMR?

No.

MSI/MMR evaluates a different biological pathway involving DNA mismatch repair and microsatellite instability.

NCCN describes CLDN18.2 positivity as independent of dMMR.

Therefore CLDN18.2 status cannot be inferred from MSI/MMR status.

MSI/MMR testing belongs to PP-0182.

---

## Block 21 — Is CLDN18.2 a Gene Mutation Test?

No.

The clinically relevant CLDN18.2 assessment in the current NCCN pathway is a **protein-expression test by IHC**.

It should not be described as a routine test for:

- CLDN18 gene mutation;
- CLDN18 gene amplification;
- a generic genomic alteration.

The package therefore distinguishes:

> **CLDN18.2 protein expression**

from

> **CLDN18 genomic alterations**.

Detailed molecular biology is delegated to the CLDN18.2 Biology package.

---

## Block 22 — Why Does CLDN18.2 Matter for Treatment?

The clinical importance of CLDN18.2 testing comes from its relationship to targeted therapy.

NCCN describes zolbetuximab as a monoclonal antibody targeting CLDN18.2 and identifies zolbetuximab plus fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy and oxaliplatin as a preferred first-line option for appropriate patients with unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric cancer whose tumors are HER2-negative and CLDN18.2-positive.

The test therefore connects:

**Tumor tissue**

→ **CLDN18.2 IHC**

→ **CLDN18.2 status**

→ **treatment-selection information**

This is the central precision-oncology role of the package.

---

## Block 23 — What Is Zolbetuximab?

Zolbetuximab is a monoclonal antibody that targets CLDN18.2.

The detailed drug package is separate.

Within this PP, zolbetuximab is included only to explain **why knowing CLDN18.2 status can matter**.

This package does not teach:

- dosing;
- administration;
- infusion schedule;
- adverse-event management;
- detailed treatment sequencing;
- individualized eligibility decisions.

Those topics belong to the relevant treatment packages.

---

## Block 24 — What Does a Positive Result Do to Treatment Planning?

A positive result can identify a patient whose tumor meets the biomarker criterion for a CLDN18.2-directed treatment pathway.

However, treatment decisions are not made from CLDN18.2 status alone.

The clinical team must integrate:

- disease extent;
- resectability;
- treatment setting;
- HER2 status;
- other biomarker results;
- performance status and overall clinical condition;
- prior treatment where relevant;
- patient preferences and clinical judgment.

Therefore:

> **A positive biomarker result opens a treatment option; it does not automatically prescribe the treatment.**

---

## Block 25 — Why Is HER2 Status Still Important?

Current NCCN treatment recommendations for zolbetuximab are framed for **HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive** unresectable locally advanced, recurrent or metastatic gastric cancer.

This demonstrates why biomarker testing is integrated rather than performed as isolated tests.

CLDN18.2 testing answers one question.

HER2 testing answers another.

The results can then be interpreted together within the treatment-selection pathway.

---

## Block 26 — Why Are Multiple Biomarkers Tested?

Gastric adenocarcinoma is biologically heterogeneous.

Different biomarkers identify different clinically relevant features.

Depending on the clinical setting, the work-up can include:

- HER2;
- PD-L1;
- MSI/MMR;
- CLDN18.2;
- other molecular testing where appropriate.

A patient may therefore have more than one biomarker result.

One positive or negative biomarker does not erase the relevance of the others.

---

## Block 27 — What About EBV?

The relationship between CLDN18.2 and Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) status is not settled.

NCCN notes that some studies have reported a significant association, while another study did not find a significant association.

Therefore EBV status should not be used as a substitute for direct CLDN18.2 testing.

This is an example of evidence that is useful for biological context but not sufficiently consistent to create a clinical rule within this package.

---

## Block 28 — Is CLDN18.2 a Prognostic Test?

The primary clinical role established for this package is **treatment selection**, not standalone prognosis.

The existence of clinicopathologic associations does not justify telling patients that CLDN18.2-positive disease necessarily has a better or worse prognosis.

The package therefore avoids converting expression prevalence or histologic association into a prognostic prediction.

---

## Block 29 — How Should a Patient Read the Pathology Result?

A patient-facing interpretation should ask:

1. Was the specimen adequate for assessment?
2. Was CLDN18.2 assessed by the expected IHC method?
3. What was the reported CLDN18.2 classification?
4. If a percentage was reported, how does it relate to the guideline threshold?
5. Was the staining membranous?
6. What intensity was reported?
7. Does the clinical setting make CLDN18.2-directed treatment relevant?
8. How does the result fit with HER2, PD-L1, MSI/MMR, staging, and the overall treatment plan?

A pathology result should not be interpreted in isolation from the clinical context.

# E. Patient Explanation

## In simple terms

CLDN18.2 is a protein that can be present on gastric cancer cells.

Some gastric cancers have enough CLDN18.2 on their tumor-cell membranes to meet the definition of **CLDN18.2-positive** disease.

Doctors can test for this protein using a laboratory method called **immunohistochemistry (IHC)** on a tumor tissue sample, such as a biopsy or surgical specimen.

Under the current NCCN definition, a tumor is CLDN18.2-positive when at least **75% of viable tumor cells** show **moderate-to-strong membranous staining** at **2+ or 3+ intensity**.

The reason this test matters is that CLDN18.2-positive status can make a targeted treatment called **zolbetuximab** relevant in the appropriate advanced gastric-cancer setting.

A positive result does not mean that the treatment is automatically required. It is one piece of information used with other biomarkers and clinical factors to choose treatment.

---

# F. Clinical Importance

## 1. It is a treatment-selection biomarker

The main reason to test CLDN18.2 is to identify patients whose tumors may qualify for CLDN18.2-directed treatment.

## 2. It is tissue-based

The current guideline assessment uses tumor tissue rather than a routine blood test.

## 3. It has a defined threshold

The result is not based on vague “high expression.” It uses:

- ≥75% viable tumor cells;
- moderate-to-strong membrane staining;
- 2+ or 3+ intensity.

## 4. It must be interpreted with other biomarkers

CLDN18.2 does not replace:

- HER2;
- PD-L1;
- MSI/MMR;

when those tests are clinically indicated.

## 5. It is clinically contextual

The relevance of a positive result depends on disease setting and whether a CLDN18.2-directed treatment is being considered.

# H. Common Misconceptions

## Myth 1 — “CLDN18.2 is a gene mutation.”

**Fact:** The clinical test described by NCCN evaluates CLDN18.2 protein expression by IHC.

## Myth 2 — “CLDN18.2 testing is a blood test.”

**Fact:** The guideline-defined assessment uses tumor tissue from a biopsy or surgical specimen.

## Myth 3 — “Any CLDN18.2 staining means positive.”

**Fact:** The clinical definition requires the specified proportion of viable tumor cells and the required membranous staining intensity.

## Myth 4 — “75% means 75% of the slide.”

**Fact:** The denominator is viable tumor cells, not the whole tissue slide.

## Myth 5 — “If the stain is 3+, the tumor is automatically positive.”

**Fact:** Strong intensity alone is not sufficient. The proportion of viable tumor cells must also meet the threshold.

## Myth 6 — “A negative result means the tumor has no CLDN18.2.”

**Fact:** It means the specimen does not meet the clinical positivity threshold.

## Myth 7 — “CLDN18.2-positive means the patient must receive zolbetuximab.”

**Fact:** The result identifies a treatment-relevant biomarker; the actual treatment decision requires the full clinical context.

## Myth 8 — “CLDN18.2-positive means HER2-negative.”

**Fact:** CLDN18.2 status and HER2 status are separate biomarkers.

## Myth 9 — “CLDN18.2 testing replaces PD-L1 testing.”

**Fact:** The two biomarkers serve different clinical purposes.

## Myth 10 — “Diffuse-type gastric cancer does not need CLDN18.2 testing.”

**Fact:** Histologic association does not replace direct biomarker assessment.

## Myth 11 — “CLDN18.2 is a prognostic test.”

**Fact:** Its major established clinical role in this package is treatment selection.

## Myth 12 — “CLDN18.2 testing is required for every gastric-cancer patient.”

**Fact:** Current NCCN testing language is linked to the relevant advanced clinical setting and consideration of zolbetuximab.

# J. Evidence Classification

## Established / guideline-supported

- CLDN18.2 is a tight-junction transmembrane protein associated with gastric epithelium.
- CLDN18.2 can be retained during malignant transformation.
- CLDN18.2 assessment is clinically relevant in advanced gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.
- NCCN recommends testing in untreated patients with inoperable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma when zolbetuximab is being considered.
- Biopsy or surgical specimen can be used.
- IHC is the assessment method.
- The current positive criterion is ≥75% viable tumor cells with moderate-to-strong membranous staining at 2+ or 3+ intensity.
- CLDN18.2 status can guide consideration of CLDN18.2-directed targeted therapy.
- Current NCCN treatment recommendations identify zolbetuximab plus fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy and oxaliplatin as a preferred first-line option for appropriate HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive advanced disease.

## Supported but context-dependent

- CLDN18.2 positivity occurs in approximately 24%–38% of advanced gastric and EGJ adenocarcinomas.
- Expression is reported in intestinal-type tumors and more commonly in diffuse-subtype tumors.
- CLDN18.2 positivity can occur independently of HER2, dMMR, and PD-L1.
- CLDN18.2 expression may be heterogeneous.

## Uncertain / inconsistent

- The relationship between CLDN18.2 and EBV status is not consistent across studies.
- Prevalence varies with assay, detection method, and positivity definition.
- The package does not treat CLDN18.2 expression as an independently established prognostic classifier.
