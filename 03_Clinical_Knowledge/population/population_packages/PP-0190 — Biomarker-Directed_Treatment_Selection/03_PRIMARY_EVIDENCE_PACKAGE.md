# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **How do biomarker results help determine which targeted treatment options may be considered for gastric adenocarcinoma?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with a source-grounded framework for understanding how biomarker information moves from a laboratory result into targeted-treatment consideration.

The package is intentionally positioned:

**after biomarker testing and molecular-report literacy**

and

**before detailed targeted-therapy treatment packages**.

The package explains clinical actionability, treatment-setting dependence, treatment-line dependence, representative biomarker-directed treatment pathways, and the need for multidisciplinary/shared decision-making.

It does not provide individualized treatment recommendations.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Direct Disease-Specific Guideline

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

This is the highest-priority source for PP-0190.

The current project copy identifies:

- universal MSI testing by PCR/NGS or MMR testing by IHC;
- universal PD-L1 testing;
- HER2 testing when advanced/metastatic disease is documented or suspected;
- CLDN18.2 testing when advanced/metastatic disease is documented or suspected;
- NGS consideration;
- biomarker-specific treatment pathways.

The NCCN biomarker-testing section states that IHC/ISH/targeted gene PCR is preferred initially for specified biomarkers and that validated NGS may be considered later in selected patients with sufficient tumor tissue.

The same section lists clinically relevant targeted biomarkers including:

- HER2;
- BRAF V600E;
- NTRK 1/2/3;
- RET;
- CLDN18.2;
- and other molecular events that may be assessed by NGS.

Source location: NCCN v2.2026, GAST-B 3–7.

The systemic-therapy section places biomarker status directly within treatment pathways.

Source location: NCCN v2.2026, GAST-F 4–5 and targeted-therapy discussion.

---

## Level I — NCI Professional Treatment Evidence

### Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ

The NCI professional treatment source describes stage IV, inoperable, and recurrent gastric-cancer treatment as incorporating combinations of cytotoxic therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and palliative approaches.

It specifically identifies:

- HER2 testing;
- dMMR/MSI testing;
- PD-L1 testing;
- CLDN18.2-positive treatment with zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy.

This supports the principle that biomarker information is integrated into treatment selection rather than existing as an isolated laboratory result.

---

## Level I — NCI Patient-Facing Treatment Evidence

### Treatment of Stomach Cancer

NCI explains that targeted therapy uses drugs or other substances to identify and attack specific cancer cells and that biomarker tests may help predict response to certain targeted therapy drugs.

The patient-facing targeted-treatment list includes:

- ramucirumab;
- regorafenib;
- trastuzumab;
- trastuzumab deruxtecan;
- zolbetuximab.

This source supports the educational bridge between biomarker testing and treatment consideration.

# Evidence Notes

## Note 1 — Biomarker testing and treatment selection are distinct layers

The Source Materials support a clear architecture:

**Testing**

→ **Result**

→ **Clinical interpretation**

→ **Treatment relevance**

→ **Treatment selection**

PP-0190 owns the fourth layer and the beginning of the fifth.

Detailed testing remains upstream.

Detailed treatment remains downstream.

---

## Note 2 — HER2 is a primary example

NCCN v2.2026 recommends HER2 assessment when advanced/metastatic disease is documented or suspected.

The guideline places trastuzumab-containing therapy in first-line treatment for advanced HER2 overexpression-positive adenocarcinoma and fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan in later-line treatment.

The evidence therefore supports HER2 as a central example of biomarker-directed targeted treatment.

---

## Note 3 — CLDN18.2 is a primary example

NCCN v2.2026 defines CLDN18.2 positivity in the setting where zolbetuximab is being considered and places zolbetuximab-containing treatment in the first-line pathway for appropriate HER2-negative advanced disease.

This provides a strong example of why:

**biomarker result + clinical setting**

must be interpreted together.

---

## Note 4 — Rare molecular alterations

NTRK, BRAF V600E, and RET are included because the current NCCN source identifies specific targeted options for these alterations in selected circumstances.

They are not presented as common gastric-cancer biomarkers.

They are presented as examples of the broader precision-oncology principle that a molecularly defined tumor subset can create a targeted-treatment opportunity.

---

## Note 5 — Immunotherapy boundary

PD-L1, MSI-H/dMMR, and TMB are treatment-relevant biomarkers but are not made into substantive targeted-therapy content in PP-0190.

NCCN places them within immunotherapy pathways.

PP-0191 owns the biomarker-directed immunotherapy-selection layer.

This avoids duplication.

---

## Note 6 — Treatment line

NCCN explicitly states that second-line or subsequent therapy is dependent on prior therapy and performance status.

This is clinically important because a biomarker result is not interpreted in a vacuum.

The same molecular finding can have different practical treatment implications depending on whether the patient is:

- untreated;
- receiving first-line treatment;
- progressing after first-line treatment;
- or receiving later-line treatment.

---

## Note 7 — Combination treatment

ACS explains that targeted therapy may be used alone or with chemotherapy depending on the situation.

NCCN identifies combination regimens such as:

- fluoropyrimidine + platinum + trastuzumab;
- fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + zolbetuximab.

PP-0190 uses these only to teach the concept that a biomarker may select one component of a broader treatment strategy.

---

## Note 8 — Actionability

“Actionable” is intentionally defined as a contextual clinical concept.

The package does not claim that every detected molecular alteration is actionable.

It also does not claim that actionability automatically means treatment eligibility.

This distinction is essential for safe patient-facing precision oncology.

---

## Note 9 — NGS and ctDNA

NCCN v2.2026 recognizes NGS as a method capable of evaluating multiple molecular events and describes ctDNA as a blood-based source of genomic information that may identify targetable alterations.

PP-0190 therefore acknowledges both as possible sources of treatment-relevant biomarker information while preserving detailed ownership in PP-0187 and dedicated ctDNA packages.

# Evidence Consistency Review

## NCCN versus NCI

No material conflict identified for the locked scope.

NCCN provides the disease-specific treatment-selection framework.

NCI provides a patient-facing and professional treatment framework in which biomarker testing contributes to targeted-treatment decisions.

The two source sets are complementary.

---

## NCCN versus ACS

ACS provides patient-facing explanations of the same major targeted-treatment relationships:

- HER2;
- CLDN18.2;
- NTRK/TRK;
- RET;
- BRAF.

The ACS framing is less algorithmic and is therefore useful for patient-facing explanation, while NCCN remains the primary source for clinical pathway ownership.

---

## NCI/ACS versus Project Architecture

NCI and ACS discuss both targeted therapy and immunotherapy.

The project architecture intentionally separates them:

**PP-0190 = targeted-treatment selection**

**PP-0191 = immunotherapy selection**

This is an architecture decision, not a claim that the biological domains never overlap.

# Evidence Gaps

The following gaps are intentionally not filled with unsupported general knowledge:

1. No universal algorithm exists in the Source Materials for every possible molecular alteration.
2. Rare or emerging alterations may have treatment relevance that depends on evolving evidence and regulatory/guideline context.
3. The exact treatment applicability of an actionable finding requires the patient's complete clinical context.
4. Treatment availability and regulatory access can vary by jurisdiction and time.
5. Detailed companion-diagnostic requirements are distributed across biomarker-specific and treatment-specific materials.
6. The Source Materials do not justify a universal priority order when several biomarkers are simultaneously positive.
7. The Source Materials do not justify converting an individual biomarker result into an individualized treatment recommendation without the patient's complete clinical record.

These gaps do not block PP-0190 because the package is intentionally a conceptual treatment-selection bridge.

# Established / Guideline-Supported

- Biomarker testing can contribute directly to treatment planning.
- HER2 is a treatment-directing biomarker for selected advanced gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma settings.
- CLDN18.2 is a treatment-directing biomarker for selected advanced gastric adenocarcinoma settings.
- NTRK fusions can identify selected TRK-directed treatment opportunities.
- BRAF V600E can identify selected BRAF/MEK-directed treatment opportunities.
- RET fusions can identify selected RET-directed treatment opportunities.
- Treatment line and previous therapy influence the relevance of treatment options.
- Targeted treatment can be part of combination systemic therapy.
- PD-L1/MSI-H/dMMR/TMB belong to a separate immunotherapy-selection branch in the project architecture.

---

# Context-Dependent / Emerging

- Treatment of uncommon actionable alterations outside the core examples.
- Use of broader NGS findings in selected clinical circumstances.
- Tumor-agnostic interpretation of selected molecular alterations.
- Clinical-trial use of emerging targeted agents.
- Treatment selection when multiple biomarkers coexist.
- Treatment selection based on rare molecular findings not represented in the current core treatment pathways.

# Boundary Verification

## Core

The package owns the **clinical bridge from validated biomarker result to targeted-treatment relevance**, including actionability, treatment-setting dependence, treatment-line dependence, multiple-biomarker integration, positive/negative result implications, and representative HER2, CLDN18.2, NTRK, BRAF V600E, and RET pathways.

## Supporting

The package may introduce companion-diagnostic concepts, molecular tumor board/multidisciplinary review, clinical-trial relevance, ctDNA-derived actionable findings, and the distinction between targeted and immunotherapy biomarker branches.

## Explicitly Excluded

Detailed biomarker testing/scoring, NGS methodology, variant interpretation/classification, molecular-report literacy, detailed ctDNA biology, general treatment-by-stage algorithms, non-biomarker chemotherapy/surgery/radiotherapy selection, detailed immunotherapy selection, drug dosing/administration/toxicity management, and individualized treatment recommendations.

## Delegated-to PP

PP-0181, PP-0182, PP-0183, PP-0184, PP-0185, PP-0186, PP-0187, PP-0189, PP-0191, PP-0208, PP-0209, PP-0210, PP-0211, dedicated ctDNA/liquid-biopsy packages, and subsequent therapy-specific PPs according to the Project Coordinator's authoritative package list.

# Final Evidence Conclusion

The supplied Source Materials provide sufficient, direct evidence to support PP-0190 as a distinct Population Package.

The strongest architecture is:

**Biomarker Testing**

↓

**Biomarker Result**

↓

**Molecular Report Literacy**

↓

# **Biomarker-Directed Treatment Selection**

↓

**Targeted Therapy**

↓

**Therapy-Specific Treatment**

The evidence is strongest for HER2 and CLDN18.2 as core gastric-cancer targeted-treatment examples, with NTRK, BRAF V600E, and RET representing selected molecularly directed options.

The package deliberately avoids a universal molecular-treatment algorithm and preserves the principle that:

> **A biomarker result informs treatment selection; it does not independently prescribe treatment.**
