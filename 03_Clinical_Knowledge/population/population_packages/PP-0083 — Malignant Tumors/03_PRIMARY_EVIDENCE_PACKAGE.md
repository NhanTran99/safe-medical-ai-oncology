# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a malignant tumor?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **malignant tumors**, emphasizing that they are **cancerous growths** capable of invading nearby tissues and, in some cases, spreading to distant parts of the body.

This Population Package also helps reduce unnecessary fear by explaining that malignant tumors differ greatly in their behavior and that many cancers today can be treated effectively.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of malignant tumor
- General biological behavior
- High-level explanation of invasion
- High-level explanation of metastasis
- Variation between cancers
- Importance of medical evaluation and treatment

---

## Excluded

- Benign tumors
- Benign versus malignant comparison
- Molecular mechanisms of cancer
- TNM staging
- Tumor grading
- Detailed metastasis
- Cancer treatment strategies
- Prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Malignant tumors are cancers.
- Malignant tumors may invade surrounding tissues.
- Some malignant tumors can metastasize.
- Different cancers vary greatly in biological behavior.
- Modern cancer treatment has improved outcomes for many patients.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence consistently describes malignant tumors as cancerous growths capable of tissue invasion and potential spread while emphasizing that biological behavior, treatment options, and outcomes vary substantially between cancer types.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI and ACS;
- oncology concepts consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

---
