# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what PS1 is.
- Understand that PS1 is one individual ACMG Evidence Code within the PS evidence-code family.
- Recognize that different DNA changes can sometimes produce the same amino acid (protein) change.
- Understand why this situation may provide strong evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- Recognize that PS1 is one piece of evidence rather than the final variant classification.
- Feel more confident understanding discussions about genetic variant interpretation.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is PS1?

## Patient Explanation

**PS1** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant may cause disease.

It belongs to the **PS (Strong Pathogenic Evidence)** family and represents one specific type of strong scientific evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

PS1 provides one category of strong evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- PS1
- Strong evidence
- ACMG Evidence Code

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does "Same Amino Acid Change" Mean?

## Patient Explanation

Sometimes two different DNA changes can produce the **same change in a protein**.

If that protein change has already been shown to be associated with disease, a new DNA change that produces the **same protein change** may provide important supporting evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

The biological effect on the protein may be more important than the exact DNA change itself.

---

## Key Concepts

- Protein change
- Amino acid change
- Scientific evidence

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Does PS1 Mean the Variant Is Definitely Pathogenic?

## Patient Explanation

No.

PS1 is **one strong piece of evidence**, but it is **not the final classification**.

Healthcare professionals combine PS1 with many other types of evidence before determining whether a variant should be classified as pathogenic.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification always depends on the complete body of available evidence.

---

## Key Concepts

- Multiple evidence sources
- Final classification
- Professional interpretation

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Is PS1 Useful?

## Patient Explanation

PS1 allows experts to use existing scientific knowledge about previously studied variants.

If two different DNA changes lead to the same protein change, previously established evidence may help interpret the newer variant.

---

## Clinical Importance

PS1 promotes consistent and evidence-based genomic interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Existing scientific knowledge
- Evidence reuse
- Consistency

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Do Patients Need to Understand PS1?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Patients do not need to memorize individual ACMG evidence codes.

The important point is that laboratories use internationally accepted scientific standards when interpreting genetic variants.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding the interpretation process is generally more valuable than learning individual evidence-code terminology.

---

## Key Concepts

- Patient education
- Standardization
- Clinical genomics

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

PS1 means that two DNA variants must be exactly the same.

**Fact**

PS1 applies when **different DNA changes produce the same amino acid (protein) change**. The emphasis is on the shared biological effect rather than identical DNA sequences.

---

# Key Messages

- PS1 is one ACMG Evidence Code.
- PS1 belongs to the PS (Strong Pathogenic Evidence) family.
- Different DNA variants may produce the same protein change.
- PS1 contributes strong evidence but is not the final classification.
- Professional interpretation always combines PS1 with additional evidence.
