# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What are prognostic factors?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **prognostic factors** as characteristics associated with the likely course of cancer.

This Population Package explains the purpose of prognostic factors, introduces common examples, emphasizes that they are interpreted together rather than individually, and reinforces that they estimate probabilities rather than predict individual outcomes with certainty.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Prognostic factors help estimate the likely course of cancer.
- Prognostic factors differ among cancer types.
- Multiple prognostic factors are interpreted together.
- Prognostic information estimates probability rather than certainty.
- Prognostic factors support individualized clinical assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Major oncology organizations consistently describe prognostic factors as characteristics associated with disease outcome that assist clinicians in estimating prognosis and supporting patient care. They also emphasize that prognosis should never be based on a single factor and that population-level estimates cannot predict an individual patient's future with certainty.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI and ACS;
- clinical oncology guidance from NCCN, ESMO and JNCCN;
- staging relationships consistent with AJCC.
