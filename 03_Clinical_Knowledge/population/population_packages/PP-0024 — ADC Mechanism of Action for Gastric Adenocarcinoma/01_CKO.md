# Knowledge Block 1 — The Basic Idea Behind ADC Therapy

## Patient Explanation

An antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) works like a **guided delivery system**.

Instead of allowing cancer medicine to circulate broadly throughout the body, the antibody helps carry the medicine toward cancer cells that display a specific biomarker.

This targeted approach is one reason ADCs are considered part of precision medicine.

---

## Clinical Importance

ADCs are designed to improve treatment precision by directing therapy toward selected cancer cells.

---

## Key Concepts

- Guided drug delivery
- Precision oncology
- Targeted treatment

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does the Antibody Do?

## Patient Explanation

The antibody acts like a guide.

It recognizes a specific marker on cancer cells and helps direct the treatment toward those cells.

Without the correct target, the ADC cannot work as intended.

---

## Clinical Importance

The antibody determines which cancer cells the treatment is designed to recognize.

---

## Key Concepts

- Finds the target
- Biomarker recognition
- Selective treatment

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Is the Linker?

## Patient Explanation

The linker connects the antibody to the cancer-fighting medicine.

Patients do not need to understand the chemical details.

Its general role is simply to keep the different parts of the ADC connected until the treatment reaches its intended target.

---

## Clinical Importance

The linker is an essential structural component of every ADC.

---

## Key Concepts

- Connects components
- Structural bridge
- Part of every ADC

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — What Is the Payload?

## Patient Explanation

The payload is the cancer-fighting medicine carried by the antibody.

Once the ADC reaches the appropriate cancer cells, the payload is intended to help destroy those cells.

Exactly how this happens differs among medicines and is discussed in later Population Packages.

---

## Clinical Importance

The payload provides the anti-cancer activity of the ADC.

---

## Key Concepts

- Cancer-fighting medicine
- Delivered to target cells
- Different payloads exist

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Is Biomarker Testing Necessary?

## Patient Explanation

Before an ADC can be considered, doctors usually need to know whether the cancer has the appropriate biomarker.

Biomarker testing helps identify patients who may benefit from a particular ADC.

Without the correct biomarker, the treatment may not be appropriate.

---

## Clinical Importance

Biomarker testing is essential for selecting appropriate ADC therapy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Test before treatment
- Precision medicine
- Patient selection

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Why Are ADCs More Selective?

## Patient Explanation

Traditional chemotherapy affects many rapidly growing cells.

ADCs are designed to direct treatment toward cancer cells carrying a specific biomarker.

Although ADCs are still cancer medicines and can cause side effects, this targeted approach aims to improve treatment precision.

---

## Clinical Importance

ADC technology combines targeted recognition with delivery of anti-cancer therapy.

---

## Key Concepts

- More selective
- Target-guided treatment
- Precision approach

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Common Questions About ADC Mechanisms

## Patient Explanation

Some patients think an ADC can recognize every cancer cell.

In reality, an ADC is designed to work only when the appropriate biomarker is present.

This is why biomarker testing is an essential part of treatment planning.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

ADCs work for every patient with gastric cancer.

**Fact**

ADCs are intended only for selected patients whose tumors have the appropriate biomarker.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Should Patients Remember?

Understanding how ADCs work helps explain:

- why biomarker testing is necessary;
- why different patients receive different treatments;
- why ADCs are considered precision medicine;
- why treatment decisions remain individualized.

Patients should discuss available treatment options with their healthcare team.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding the mechanism helps patients participate more confidently in shared decision-making.

---

# Key Messages

- ADCs work as guided drug delivery systems.
- The antibody recognizes specific cancer cells.
- The linker connects the medicine to the antibody.
- The payload provides the cancer-fighting effect.
- Biomarker testing is essential before treatment.
- ADCs are generally more selective than conventional chemotherapy but still require careful clinical monitoring.
