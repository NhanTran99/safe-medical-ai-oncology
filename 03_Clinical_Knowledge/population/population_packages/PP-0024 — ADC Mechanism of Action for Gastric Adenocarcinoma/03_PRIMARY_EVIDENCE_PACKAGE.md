# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> How do antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) work, and why is their mechanism different from conventional chemotherapy in gastric adenocarcinoma?

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an evidence-based understanding of the overall mechanism of action of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

This Population Package explains, in plain language, how an ADC recognizes cancer cells, transports anti-cancer medicine toward the target, and why biomarker testing is essential before treatment.

The package intentionally avoids molecular biology and focuses on helping patients understand the concept of targeted drug delivery within precision oncology.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- ADCs function as targeted drug-delivery systems.
- The antibody provides target recognition.
- The linker joins the components together.
- The payload delivers the anti-cancer effect.
- Biomarker testing is essential before treatment selection.
- ADCs improve treatment precision but still require careful medical supervision.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary authoritative references.

All major references consistently describe ADCs as biomarker-guided targeted therapies that combine selective recognition with delivery of anti-cancer treatment.

Differences among references primarily involve:

- individual ADC medicines;
- intracellular mechanisms;
- toxicity management;
- treatment sequencing.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- clinical recommendations from NCCN;
- evidence interpretation from JNCCN;
- patient-oriented explanations from NCI and ACS;
- internationally harmonized terminology from ESMO.
