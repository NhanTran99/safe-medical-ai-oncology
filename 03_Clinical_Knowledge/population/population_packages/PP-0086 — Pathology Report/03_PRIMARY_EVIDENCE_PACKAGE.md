# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a pathology report?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of a **pathology report** as one of the most important medical documents used in cancer diagnosis and treatment planning.

This Population Package explains that a pathology report summarizes the microscopic examination of tissue or cell specimens performed by a pathologist and should always be interpreted together with clinical history, imaging findings, physical examination, and other investigations.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- A pathology report is the official report describing laboratory examination of tissue or cell specimens.
- Pathologists prepare pathology reports.
- Pathology reports play a central role in diagnosis.
- Pathology findings support treatment planning.
- Pathology reports are interpreted together with other clinical information.
- Medical explanation is often necessary because pathology terminology is specialized.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

All authoritative organizations consistently recognize the pathology report as the definitive laboratory document describing microscopic evaluation of biopsy or surgical specimens. The evidence also consistently emphasizes that pathology findings should always be interpreted within the broader clinical context rather than as isolated results.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI and ACS;
- pathology reporting principles from CAP;
- oncology guidance consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
