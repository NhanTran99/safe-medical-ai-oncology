# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is RNA Sequencing (RNA-seq)?

## Patient Explanation

**RNA Sequencing (RNA-seq)** is a laboratory test that analyzes **RNA molecules** in a biological sample.

Unlike DNA sequencing, which studies genetic information that is stored in cells, RNA sequencing helps show **which genes are active and being used by the cells** at the time the sample is collected.

---

## Clinical Importance

RNA sequencing provides information about gene activity rather than simply the DNA sequence.

---

## Key Concepts

- RNA
- Gene expression
- Active genes

# Knowledge Block 2 — Is RNA Sequencing the Same as NGS?

## Patient Explanation

No.

**NGS (Next-Generation Sequencing)** is the technology used to sequence genetic material.

**RNA-seq** is one application of that technology.

In simple terms:

- **NGS = how sequencing is performed**
- **RNA-seq = what is analyzed (RNA)**

---

## Clinical Importance

Technology and sequencing application are different concepts.

---

## Key Concepts

- NGS
- RNA sequencing
- Sequencing technology

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — How Is RNA Sequencing Different from DNA Sequencing?

## Patient Explanation

DNA sequencing examines the genetic information stored inside cells.

RNA sequencing examines the RNA produced from genes, helping doctors understand **which genes are currently active**.

These two tests provide different types of information and often complement each other.

---

## Clinical Importance

RNA sequencing provides functional information that complements DNA-based testing.

---

## Key Concepts

- DNA
- RNA
- Gene activity

# Knowledge Block 4 — When Might RNA Sequencing Be Used?

## Patient Explanation

Depending on the clinical situation, RNA sequencing may help doctors:

- evaluate gene expression;
- support tumor classification;
- investigate complex cancers;
- contribute to precision medicine;
- support specialized clinical care or research.

The decision to perform RNA sequencing depends on the individual patient and current medical guidelines.

---

## Clinical Importance

RNA sequencing is used selectively according to clinical indications.

---

## Key Concepts

- Precision medicine
- Clinical indications
- Gene expression

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does Everyone Need RNA Sequencing?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Most patients do **not** routinely require RNA sequencing.

Doctors recommend RNA sequencing only when it is appropriate for the specific cancer type and clinical question.

Many patients receive excellent cancer care without undergoing RNA sequencing.

---

## Clinical Importance

Routine RNA sequencing is not indicated for every patient.

---

## Key Concepts

- Appropriate testing
- Individualized care
- Evidence-based practice

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

RNA sequencing is simply another name for DNA sequencing.

**Fact**

RNA sequencing and DNA sequencing answer different biological questions. DNA shows the genetic blueprint, whereas RNA helps show which genes are active at the time of testing.

---

# Key Messages

- RNA sequencing analyzes RNA rather than DNA.
- RNA sequencing evaluates gene activity (gene expression).
- RNA sequencing is an application of NGS technology.
- RNA sequencing complements DNA-based genomic testing.
- RNA sequencing is not routinely needed for every patient.
- RNA sequencing supports precision medicine in selected clinical situations.
