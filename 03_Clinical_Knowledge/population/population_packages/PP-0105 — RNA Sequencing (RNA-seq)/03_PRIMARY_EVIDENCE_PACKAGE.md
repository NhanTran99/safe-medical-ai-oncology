# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is RNA Sequencing (RNA-seq)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **RNA Sequencing (RNA-seq)** as a molecular sequencing approach that analyzes **RNA molecules** to evaluate **gene expression (gene activity)** within a biological sample.

This Population Package introduces RNA sequencing as an advanced molecular testing application within precision oncology while emphasizing that RNA sequencing complements, rather than replaces, DNA-based genomic testing and is not routinely indicated for every patient.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- RNA sequencing analyzes RNA rather than DNA.
- RNA sequencing evaluates gene activity through gene expression.
- RNA sequencing is an application of NGS technology.
- RNA sequencing complements DNA sequencing rather than replacing it.
- RNA sequencing is used selectively according to the clinical indication.
- RNA sequencing findings should always be interpreted together with other clinical information.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Major oncology organizations consistently describe RNA sequencing as a transcriptomic sequencing approach that evaluates RNA molecules to better understand gene expression and tumor biology. Clinical guidance consistently emphasizes that RNA sequencing is complementary to DNA-based genomic testing, is generally performed using Next-Generation Sequencing technology, and should be used according to the clinical indication, cancer type, available resources, and evidence-based practice.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- terminology from NCI and NHGRI;
- oncology guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- molecular pathology principles from CAP and AMP;
- patient education principles from ACS;
- international guidance consistent with ESMO and JNCCN.
