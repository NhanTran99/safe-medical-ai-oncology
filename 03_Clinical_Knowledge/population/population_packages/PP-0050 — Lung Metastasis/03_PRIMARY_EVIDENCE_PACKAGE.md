# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is lung metastasis?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **lung metastasis**, including what the lungs are, how gastric cancer can spread to the lungs, why lung metastasis remains gastric cancer rather than primary lung cancer, and why appropriate medical evaluation is required before confirming lung metastasis.

This Population Package introduces the concept of lung metastasis without discussing imaging studies, bronchoscopy, biopsy, surgery, systemic therapy or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of the lungs
- Definition of lung metastasis
- Relationship between gastric cancer and the lungs
- Difference between primary lung cancer and lung metastasis
- Relationship between lung metastasis and metastatic gastric cancer
- Importance of appropriate medical evaluation

---

## Excluded

- Primary lung cancer
- Small cell lung cancer
- Non-small cell lung cancer
- M1 classification
- CT chest
- PET-CT
- Bronchoscopy
- Lung biopsy
- Pulmonary metastasectomy
- Radiation therapy
- Systemic therapy
- Molecular mechanisms of lung metastasis
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The lungs are essential organs that may become involved when gastric cancer spreads.
- Lung metastasis means gastric cancer has spread to the lungs.
- Lung metastasis remains gastric cancer because it originated in the stomach.
- Lung metastasis should not be confused with primary lung cancer.
- Not every patient develops lung metastasis.
- Doctors determine whether lung metastasis is present through appropriate clinical assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- lung metastasis as a form of distant metastatic disease;
- the distinction between metastatic gastric cancer and primary lung cancer;
- the role of lung metastasis in gastric cancer staging;
- the importance of confirming metastatic disease through appropriate clinical evaluation.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
