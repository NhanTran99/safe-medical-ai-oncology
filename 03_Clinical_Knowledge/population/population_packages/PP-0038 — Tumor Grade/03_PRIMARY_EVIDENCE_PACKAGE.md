# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is tumor grade and what should patients understand about it?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of tumor grade, including what it represents, how it is determined from pathological examination, why it appears in pathology reports, and how it differs from cancer stage.

This Population Package introduces tumor grade without discussing detailed grading criteria, prognostic implications or treatment recommendations.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of tumor grade
- Purpose of tumor grading
- Relationship between tumor grade and microscopic examination
- Well differentiated tumors
- Moderately differentiated tumors
- Poorly differentiated tumors
- Relationship to pathology reports
- Difference between tumor grade and cancer stage
- Role within the overall pathological assessment

---

## Excluded

- Histologic grading criteria
- Histologic differentiation mechanisms
- WHO grading methodology
- Lauren Classification
- WHO Classification
- TNM staging details
- Molecular classification
- MSI subtype
- EBV-associated gastric cancer
- Prognostic interpretation
- Treatment recommendations

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Tumor grade reflects the microscopic appearance of cancer cells.
- Tumor grade is determined by pathological examination of tissue.
- Common grading terminology includes well differentiated, moderately differentiated and poorly differentiated.
- Tumor grade is commonly reported in pathology reports.
- Tumor grade is distinct from cancer staging.
- Tumor grade contributes to diagnosis but does not independently determine prognosis or treatment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition and purpose of tumor grade;
- pathological determination through microscopic examination;
- standardized grading terminology;
- distinction between tumor grade and TNM staging;
- integration of tumor grade into comprehensive pathological assessment.

Differences between references are limited to terminology, educational emphasis and explanatory depth.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
