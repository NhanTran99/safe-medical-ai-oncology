# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> What is the payload in an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), and why is it important in the treatment of gastric adenocarcinoma?

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an evidence-based understanding of the payload component of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

This Population Package explains, in plain language, what the payload is, why it is attached to an antibody instead of being administered alone, how it contributes to targeted treatment, and why it is an essential component of precision oncology.

The package intentionally avoids detailed pharmacology, molecular biology and drug-specific mechanisms.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The payload is the therapeutic component responsible for the anti-cancer effect of an ADC.
- The antibody guides the payload toward cancer cells expressing the appropriate biomarker.
- Payload delivery contributes to the precision of ADC therapy.
- Biomarker testing is necessary before many ADC treatments.
- Payload-containing therapies still require careful clinical monitoring.
- Patients benefit from understanding both the advantages and limitations of payload-guided treatment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary authoritative references.

All major references consistently describe the payload as the therapeutic component delivered through antibody-guided targeting within ADC technology.

Differences among references primarily involve:

- specific payload classes;
- molecular pharmacology;
- intracellular mechanisms;
- toxicity management;
- treatment sequencing.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- clinical recommendations from NCCN;
- evidence interpretation from JNCCN;
- patient-oriented explanations from NCI and ACS;
- internationally harmonized terminology from ESMO.
