# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is Whole Exome Sequencing (WES)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Whole Exome Sequencing (WES)** as genomic sequencing that analyzes the **protein-coding regions (exons)** of nearly all genes.

This Population Package introduces WES as an advanced genomic testing application within molecular diagnostics while emphasizing that WES is not routinely indicated for every patient and should be performed according to the clinical situation and professional guidelines.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- WES evaluates the protein-coding regions of nearly all genes.
- WES is an application of NGS technology.
- WES provides broader genomic information than gene panel testing.
- WES analyzes less of the genome than WGS.
- WES is used selectively according to the clinical indication.
- WES results should always be interpreted together with other clinical findings.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Major oncology organizations consistently describe Whole Exome Sequencing as comprehensive sequencing of the coding regions of the genome. They distinguish WES from targeted gene panels by its broad exome-wide coverage and from WGS by its narrower sequencing scope. Guidance consistently describes WES as an application of Next-Generation Sequencing technology that may be appropriate in selected clinical settings according to the clinical question, available resources, and evidence-based recommendations.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- terminology from NCI and NHGRI;
- oncology guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- molecular pathology principles from CAP and AMP;
- patient education principles from ACS;
- international guidance consistent with ESMO and JNCCN.
