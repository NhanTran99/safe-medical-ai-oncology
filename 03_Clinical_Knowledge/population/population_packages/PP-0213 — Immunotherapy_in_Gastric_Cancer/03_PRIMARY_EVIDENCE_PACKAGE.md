- MSI-H/dMMR immunotherapy independent of PD-L1;

- RATIONALE-305;
- MATTERHORN;
- guideline-linked immune checkpoint toxicity governance.

### Key Source Findings

The current algorithm identifies FLOT + durvalumab as a preferred perioperative option for selected patients, including a category 1 context for PD-L1 CPS ≥1 or TAP ≥1% and a specified clinical-node-negative context. It also identifies selected category 2B contexts and notes that diffuse-type tumors did not show an event-free survival advantage in MATTERHORN.

The same guideline identifies several first-line advanced-disease immunotherapy combinations, including nivolumab, pembrolizumab, and tislelizumab with chemotherapy in HER2-negative disease, and pembrolizumab with trastuzumab plus chemotherapy in selected HER2-positive disease.

The guideline separately identifies MSI-H/dMMR tumors as an immunotherapy-relevant group independent of PD-L1 status.

# Landmark Evidence

## CheckMate-649

### Population

Previously untreated advanced gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma.

### Intervention

Nivolumab + chemotherapy.

### Comparator

Chemotherapy.

### Key reported outcomes

In PD-L1 CPS ≥5:

- OS: 14.4 vs 11.1 months;
- HR: 0.70;
- PFS: 8.3 vs 6.1 months;
- HR: 0.70.

The supplied NCCN evidence also reports 3-year objective response of 60% versus 45% and complete response of 13% versus 7%.

### Interpretation

This is strong randomized evidence that adding a checkpoint inhibitor to chemotherapy can improve outcomes in a defined advanced-disease population.

It does not establish that every patient with gastric cancer will benefit.

---

## KEYNOTE-859

### Population

Previously untreated advanced HER2-negative gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

### Intervention

Pembrolizumab + chemotherapy.

### Key reported outcomes

PD-L1 CPS ≥10:

- OS 15.7 vs 11.8 months;
- HR 0.65;
- PFS 8.1 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.62;
- ORR 61% vs 43%.

PD-L1 CPS ≥1:

- OS 13.0 vs 11.4 months;
- HR 0.74;
- PFS 6.9 vs 5.6 months;
- HR 0.72;
- ORR 52% vs 43%.

### Interpretation

The trial supports pembrolizumab + chemotherapy in defined HER2-negative advanced populations and illustrates the importance of PD-L1-defined evidence contexts.

---

## RATIONALE-305

### Population

Previously untreated unresectable locally advanced or metastatic gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.

### Intervention

Tislelizumab + chemotherapy.

### Comparator

Placebo + chemotherapy.

### Key reported outcomes

All randomized:

- OS 15.0 vs 12.9 months;
- HR 0.80;
- PFS 6.9 vs 6.2 months;
- HR 0.78.

PD-L1 TAP ≥5%:

- OS 17.2 vs 12.6 months;
- HR 0.74;
- PFS 7.2 vs 5.9 months;
- HR 0.67.

### Interpretation

The trial supports another checkpoint-inhibitor + chemotherapy strategy and demonstrates the importance of the biomarker-scoring context.

---

## KEYNOTE-811

### Clinical role

Supports the integration of pembrolizumab with trastuzumab and chemotherapy in selected HER2-positive advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

### Guideline context

Current NCCN v2.2026 lists fluoropyrimidine + platinum + trastuzumab + pembrolizumab for PD-L1 CPS ≥1 as a preferred category 1 first-line pathway.

### Interpretation

The major educational point is combination logic:

**HER2-targeted therapy + chemotherapy + immunotherapy**

can form an evidence-supported regimen in a defined population.

Detailed HER2 treatment belongs to PP-0210.

---

## MATTERHORN

### Population

Untreated resectable gastric or EGJ adenocarcinoma.

### Intervention

Durvalumab + FLOT perioperatively.

### Comparator

Placebo + FLOT.

### Key reported outcomes

- n = 948;
- 24-month EFS: 67.4% vs 58.5%;
- HR for event/death: 0.71;
- PD-L1 TAP ≥1% subgroup HR: 0.70;
- PD-L1 TAP <1% subgroup HR: 0.77 with wide uncertainty;
- diffuse-type subgroup HR: 0.93.

The NCCN update explicitly notes that diffuse-type gastric or EGJ adenocarcinoma did not show an event-free survival advantage from adding durvalumab to FLOT.

### Interpretation

MATTERHORN demonstrates both:

1. the expansion of immunotherapy into perioperative treatment; and
2. why subgroup-specific evidence matters.

It should not be translated into:

> “All resectable gastric cancers should receive immunotherapy.”

---

# MSI-H/dMMR Evidence

The supplied NCCN discussion reports that among randomized MSI-H tumors in CheckMate-649:

- median OS was 38.7 vs 12.3 months;
- HR 0.34.

For nivolumab + ipilimumab versus chemotherapy in MSI-H tumors:

- median OS not reached vs 10 months;
- HR 0.28;
- ORR 70% vs 57%.

The microsatellite-stable cohort did not show the same survival benefit with nivolumab + ipilimumab.

### Evidence interpretation

This is important subgroup evidence supporting the concept that tumor biology can materially affect immunotherapy sensitivity.

It is not a guarantee of benefit for every MSI-H/dMMR patient.

Detailed MSI-H/dMMR ownership is PP-0215.

Current NCCN includes:

- FLOT + durvalumab in selected contexts;
- MSI-H/dMMR neoadjuvant/perioperative immunotherapy in selected circumstances.

Selected PD-L1 CPS ≥1:

- fluoropyrimidine + platinum + trastuzumab + pembrolizumab.

## Advanced / Metastatic HER2-negative

- fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + nivolumab;

- fluoropyrimidine + oxaliplatin + tislelizumab.

# Safety Evidence

## Commonly described effects

ACS identifies:

- fatigue/weakness;
- fever;
- cough;
- nausea;
- itching;
- skin rash;
- appetite loss;
- muscle/joint pain;
- constipation or diarrhea.

## Serious immune-mediated effects

ACS describes immune-mediated attacks involving:

- lungs;
- intestines;
- liver;
- hormone-producing glands;
- kidneys;
- skin;
- other organs.

ESMO-ASCO similarly identifies immune-related adverse events involving:

- skin;
- endocrine system;
- gastrointestinal system;
- lungs;
- liver.

### Safety interpretation

Immune-related toxicity can be different from conventional chemotherapy or classical targeted-therapy toxicity.

New or worsening symptoms during immunotherapy should be brought promptly to the treating team.

# Response Evidence

ESMO-ASCO identifies:

- late response;
- regression after apparent progression;
- pseudoprogression;
- mixed response;
- hyperprogression.

### Evidence interpretation

These concepts justify careful clinical assessment when imaging changes occur.

They do not justify assuming that any apparent progression is pseudoprogression.

Detailed response assessment is delegated to PP-0217–PP-0219.

---

# Resistance Evidence

ESMO-ASCO identifies:

- primary resistance;
- adaptive/acquired resistance;
- combination strategies intended to overcome resistance.

### PP-0213 ownership

Only the conceptual statement:

> Immunotherapy does not work for every patient, and tumors can be intrinsically resistant or become resistant after an initial response.

Detailed resistance mechanisms and management are delegated.

# Evidence Gaps

The following gaps are intentionally preserved rather than filled with unsupported project-external assumptions:

1. The project Source Materials do not establish one universal biomarker rule applicable to every immunotherapy agent.
2. PD-L1 thresholds and scoring frameworks are treatment-specific and cannot be generalized across all checkpoint inhibitors.
3. MSI-H/dMMR subgroup analyses can involve relatively small populations.
4. TMB-H evidence is less directly gastric/GEJ-specific in the supplied material.
5. Perioperative immunotherapy evidence is evolving and subgroup effects matter.
6. The project sources do not support an individualized probability of response for a specific patient.
7. No single trial establishes immunotherapy as universally superior for every gastric-cancer population.
8. Detailed toxicity-management algorithms are outside PP-0213.
9. Detailed response criteria are outside PP-0213.
10. Detailed sequencing after progression is outside PP-0213.

These gaps do not block the package because the approved scope is an umbrella clinical education package rather than an individualized treatment algorithm.

The ACS source includes references to NCCN v3.2025 and other sources in its own bibliography. For current gastric-cancer treatment positioning, this PP prioritizes the project-provided NCCN v2.2026 source.

1. Immunotherapy helps the immune system attack cancer.
2. Checkpoint inhibition is the principal established immunotherapy paradigm represented in the gastric-cancer project sources.
3. PD-1/PD-L1 signaling is an important checkpoint mechanism.
4. Immunotherapy can be combined with chemotherapy.
5. Immunotherapy can be combined with selected targeted therapy.
6. PD-L1 is important in several treatment pathways but is not universally required.

8. Immunotherapy is relevant to selected perioperative as well as advanced/metastatic gastric-cancer settings.

10. KEYNOTE-811 provides evidence for selected HER2-positive combination treatment.

12. Some patients experience durable responses.
13. Primary and acquired resistance occur.
14. Immune-related adverse events can affect multiple organ systems.
15. Immunotherapy can produce unconventional response patterns.
16. None of these population-level findings should be converted into individualized treatment advice without clinical assessment.
