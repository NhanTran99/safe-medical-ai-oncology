# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is BP4?

## Patient Explanation

**BP4** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant is likely to be **benign (not disease-causing).**

BP4 represents one type of **Supporting Benign Evidence**.

It is based on information generated by multiple computational prediction methods.

---

## Clinical Importance

BP4 provides supporting benign evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- BP4
- Supporting benign evidence
- ACMG Evidence Codes

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Is Computational Evidence?

## Patient Explanation

Computational evidence comes from specialized computer programs that analyze genetic variants using biological knowledge and scientific data.

These programs estimate whether a variant is likely to affect the function of a gene or its protein.

---

## Clinical Importance

Computational predictions provide one source of information during variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Computational evidence
- Prediction tools
- Bioinformatics

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Are Multiple Computational Methods Used?

## Patient Explanation

No computer program is perfect.

Instead of relying on a single prediction, genetics professionals often consider whether **multiple independent computational methods** reach the same conclusion.

When several methods consistently suggest that a variant is unlikely to affect the gene or its protein, this may contribute **supporting benign evidence**.

---

## Clinical Importance

Using multiple independent sources can improve confidence in computational predictions.

---

## Key Concepts

- Multiple predictions
- Independent evidence
- Computational consistency

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does BP4 Mean the Variant Is Definitely Benign?

## Patient Explanation

No.

BP4 **does not prove** that a variant is benign.

It provides one piece of supporting evidence that must always be interpreted together with laboratory findings, clinical information, family history, and other ACMG evidence categories.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification depends on integrating multiple independent types of evidence.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Professional interpretation
- Benign classification

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Does the ACMG Use BP4?

## Patient Explanation

Computational prediction tools can help experts evaluate genetic variants more consistently.

The ACMG framework allows these predictions to contribute to variant interpretation, but only as one part of a much larger evidence-based assessment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Computational evidence supports standardized and reproducible genomic interpretation.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

If a computer predicts that a variant is harmless, the variant is definitely benign.

**Fact**

Computer predictions are only **one source of evidence**.

BP4 provides **supporting benign evidence**, but the final classification always depends on integrating many different types of evidence reviewed by qualified professionals.

---

# Key Messages

- BP4 is one Supporting Benign Evidence criterion.
- BP4 uses multiple computational predictions.
- Consistent computational predictions may contribute supporting benign evidence.
- Computer predictions do not replace expert interpretation.
- Final variant classification always requires integration of multiple evidence categories.
