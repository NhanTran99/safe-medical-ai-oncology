# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is BP4 (Multiple Lines of Computational Evidence Suggest No Impact on Gene or Gene Product)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **BP4 (Multiple Lines of Computational Evidence Suggest No Impact on Gene or Gene Product)** as one of the standardized **Supporting Benign Evidence** criteria within the **ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework**.

This Population Package explains that **BP4 applies when multiple independent computational prediction methods consistently suggest that a genetic variant is unlikely to affect the normal function of a gene or its protein product.** Because different computational tools analyze biological information in different ways, agreement among several methods may contribute **supporting benign evidence** during professional variant interpretation. However, **BP4 represents supporting evidence only** and must always be interpreted together with clinical findings, laboratory evidence, functional studies, population data, and other ACMG evidence categories before a final variant classification is made.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- BP4 is one individual ACMG evidence criterion.
- BP4 uses multiple computational prediction methods.
- Consistent computational predictions may contribute supporting benign evidence.
- Computational evidence alone cannot establish benign classification.
- Final variant classification always requires comprehensive professional evaluation.

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **BP4** as one of the **Supporting Benign Evidence** criteria within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that **multiple independent computational prediction methods** suggesting that a variant is unlikely to affect gene or protein function may contribute supporting benign evidence. ClinGen SVI provides additional implementation recommendations regarding computational evidence, but these technical refinements intentionally remain outside the scope of this introductory educational package.
