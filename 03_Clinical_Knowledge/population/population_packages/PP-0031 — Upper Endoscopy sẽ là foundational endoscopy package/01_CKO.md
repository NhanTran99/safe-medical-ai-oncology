# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what an upper endoscopy is.
- Understand why doctors recommend upper endoscopy.
- Understand what doctors can examine during upper endoscopy.
- Understand that upper endoscopy may help detect stomach cancer and other stomach diseases.
- Understand that a biopsy may be taken if an abnormal area is found.
- Understand that having an upper endoscopy does not mean a person has cancer.
- Understand the general role of upper endoscopy in medical evaluation.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Upper Endoscopy?

## Patient Explanation

Upper endoscopy is a medical examination that allows doctors to look directly inside the upper part of the digestive system.

A thin, flexible tube called an **endoscope** is gently passed through the mouth into the esophagus, stomach and the first part of the small intestine (duodenum).

The endoscope has a small camera and light that allow doctors to examine these organs in detail.

---

## Clinical Importance

Upper endoscopy allows direct visualization of the lining of the upper digestive tract and is one of the most important tools for evaluating stomach diseases.

---

## Key Concepts

- Upper endoscopy is a visual examination.
- It uses a flexible tube with a camera.
- It allows doctors to examine the upper digestive tract directly.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Is Upper Endoscopy Performed?

## Patient Explanation

Doctors may recommend upper endoscopy for several reasons.

It can help investigate symptoms such as:

- difficulty swallowing;
- persistent stomach pain;
- unexplained bleeding;
- persistent nausea or vomiting;
- unexplained weight loss.

It may also be used to evaluate abnormal findings from other tests or to screen certain people who have a higher risk of stomach cancer.

---

## Clinical Importance

Upper endoscopy provides information that cannot be obtained from symptoms alone and often guides the next steps in diagnosis.

---

## Key Concepts

- Upper endoscopy helps evaluate symptoms.
- It may be recommended because of increased cancer risk.
- It helps doctors identify abnormalities inside the stomach.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Can Doctors See?

## Patient Explanation

During upper endoscopy, doctors carefully examine the lining of the:

- esophagus;
- stomach;
- duodenum.

They look for abnormalities such as:

- inflammation;
- ulcers;
- bleeding;
- polyps;
- suspicious masses or lesions.

Finding an abnormal area does **not** automatically mean cancer is present.

---

## Clinical Importance

Direct visualization improves the ability to identify abnormalities that require further investigation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Doctors examine several organs.
- Many abnormalities are not cancer.
- Additional evaluation may sometimes be required.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — What Is a Biopsy?

## Patient Explanation

If the doctor sees an abnormal area, a **biopsy** may be performed.

A biopsy means removing a very small sample of tissue so it can be examined under a microscope.

The biopsy itself does **not** spread cancer.

Only examination of the biopsy can determine whether cancer or another condition is present.

---

## Clinical Importance

Biopsy provides tissue needed for an accurate diagnosis when appropriate.

---

## Key Concepts

- A biopsy is a tissue sample.
- Not everyone undergoing endoscopy needs a biopsy.
- A biopsy helps establish a diagnosis.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does Having an Endoscopy Mean I Have Cancer?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Many people undergo upper endoscopy for conditions that are **not cancer**, including ulcers, gastritis, reflux disease and other digestive disorders.

Doctors use upper endoscopy to investigate symptoms and identify the cause.

The examination itself does not diagnose cancer unless biopsy results confirm it.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding this distinction helps reduce unnecessary anxiety before the procedure.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

If my doctor recommends an upper endoscopy, I probably have cancer.

**Fact**

Most people who undergo upper endoscopy do **not** have stomach cancer. The examination is used to investigate many different digestive conditions.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — What Should Patients Expect?

## Patient Explanation

Upper endoscopy is usually performed by a specially trained doctor.

Healthcare professionals monitor patients throughout the examination.

After the examination, the doctor explains the initial findings.

If a biopsy was taken, the final results usually become available after the tissue has been examined in the laboratory.

---

## Clinical Importance

Patients should understand that visual findings and biopsy results may become available at different times.

---

## Key Concepts

- Doctors explain the initial findings after the examination.
- Biopsy results require additional laboratory analysis.
- Final diagnosis may not be available immediately.

---

# Key Messages

- Upper endoscopy allows doctors to examine the upper digestive tract directly.
- It is commonly used to investigate digestive symptoms and evaluate stomach abnormalities.
- It may help detect stomach cancer, but many people undergoing endoscopy do not have cancer.
- A biopsy may be taken if needed.
- A biopsy is required to confirm many diagnoses.
- Upper endoscopy is an important step in evaluation but does not by itself establish every diagnosis.
