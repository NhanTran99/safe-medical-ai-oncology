# 2. Clinical Question

> **How is treatment response assessed in gastric cancer, and how is the assessment used to understand whether treatment is helping, controlling, or failing to control the disease?**

The Evidence Package addresses response assessment as a clinical integration process rather than as a technical RECIST manual.

---

# 3. Educational Intent

The package supports understanding of:

- treatment response assessment;
- baseline-to-follow-up comparison;
- clinical and imaging integration;
- standardized response categories;
- restaging;
- treatment-setting differences;
- response reassessment;
- limitations and uncertainty;
- immunotherapy-specific response complexity.

# 9. Detailed Evidence Notes

## Evidence Note 1 — NCCN Explicitly Uses Treatment Response Assessment

The current NCCN gastric-cancer algorithm contains a specific **TREATMENT RESPONSE ASSESSMENT** stage.

This is especially explicit in the pathway for peritoneal carcinoma as the only disease.

The pathway uses response/restaging information to distinguish situations such as:

- stable or improved disease;
- progression;
- extraperitoneal disease;

and then directs the clinical pathway toward continued systemic therapy, clinical trial, cytoreductive approaches in selected circumstances, or best supportive care.

This directly supports PP-0217 as a clinical integration package rather than a purely technical imaging package.

---

## Evidence Note 2 — Restaging Can Be Multimodal

In the NCCN peritoneal-only pathway, restaging can include:

- CT scan;
- diagnostic laparoscopy with washings and documented PCI, with biopsy when appropriate;
- consideration of FDG-PET/CT;
- consideration of EGD.

This demonstrates an important principle:

> **The clinically relevant response question can require more than a single measurable lesion.**

The exact technical use of these modalities remains delegated.

---

## Evidence Note 3 — Post-neoadjuvant Assessment

NCCN's perioperative pathway includes post-treatment reassessment after preoperative therapy.

The supplied guideline includes:

- chest/abdomen CT with oral and IV contrast;
- FDG-PET/CT as clinically indicated;
- EGD and biopsy in the relevant pathway;
- assessment after completion of preoperative therapy;
- differentiation between no evidence of disease, persistent local disease and new metastatic disease.

This supports PP-0217's ownership of:

> **clinical reassessment after preoperative treatment.**

Detailed imaging and surgical management remain outside scope.

---

## Evidence Note 4 — Response Is Not an Imaging-only Concept

ESMO-ASCO response-evaluation material distinguishes standardized response evaluation from broader individual-patient clinical judgment.

RECIST is particularly important in clinical trials, where standardized definitions are needed.

For an individual patient, however, the clinical assessment must consider the overall context rather than relying mechanically on one standardized response label.

This supports the PP-0217 principle:

> **Response assessment is broader than formal RECIST calculation.**

---

## Evidence Note 5 — RECIST as a Standardized Framework

RECIST provides standardized rules for evaluating changes in solid tumors.

The foundational PP architecture already assigns dedicated ownership to:

- RECIST;
- RECIST 1.1;
- target lesions;
- measurable disease;
- non-target lesions;
- CR;
- PR;
- SD;
- PD;
- response assessment algorithm.

Therefore PP-0217 should integrate these concepts rather than duplicate their technical content.

---

## Evidence Note 6 — Stable Disease

The project Registry describes Stable Disease as a state in which cancer remains relatively unchanged according to standardized assessment and explicitly emphasizes that SD should not automatically be interpreted as treatment failure.

This supports patient-facing explanation that:

> Stable disease may represent disease control in an appropriate treatment context.

The package intentionally avoids claiming that SD is always beneficial.

---

## Evidence Note 7 — Progressive Disease

The project Registry describes Progressive Disease as disease progression under standardized assessment and explicitly emphasizes that PD should not automatically be interpreted as meaning that all treatment options have been exhausted.

This is clinically important because progression should trigger reassessment rather than an automatic assumption of therapeutic nihilism.

---

## Evidence Note 8 — Measurement Error

The ESMO-ASCO response-evaluation framework identifies measurement error as a relevant limitation.

Therefore small changes should not automatically be interpreted as biologically meaningful changes in tumor burden.

This supports:

> **Longitudinal standardized assessment reduces, but does not eliminate, uncertainty.**

---

## Evidence Note 9 — Immunotherapy

The ESMO-ASCO framework includes iRECIST and immune-related response-evaluation concepts.

This supports only the following PP-0217 claim:

> Immunotherapy may require specialized response interpretation.

Detailed iRECIST rules, confirmation requirements and pseudo-progression algorithms remain PP-0218 content.

---

## Evidence Note 10 — Peritoneal Disease

The NCCN pathway is particularly useful for illustrating why response assessment can be more complex in peritoneal disease.

A patient may require:

- radiologic reassessment;
- laparoscopic assessment;
- washings;
- PCI documentation;
- selected biopsy;
- clinical interpretation.

Therefore response assessment cannot always be reduced to:

> “measure the largest tumor.”

# 10. Clinical Integration Model

The evidence supports the following model:

```text
Baseline disease state
        ↓
Treatment
        ↓
Follow-up assessment
        ↓
Comparison with baseline / prior assessment
        ↓
Standardized response interpretation where applicable
        ↓
Clinical integration
        ↓
Treatment reassessment
        ↓
Next clinical decision
```

The important distinction is:

**Response assessment = information-generating and interpretive process**

while:

**Treatment decision = broader clinical decision-making process.**

# 12. Evidence-to-Patient Translation

## Patient statement

“My CT says stable disease.”

### Correct translation

The cancer has not changed enough on the standardized assessment to meet criteria for response or progression. Whether that is a good treatment result depends on the treatment goal and your overall clinical situation.

---

## Patient statement

“My scan says progression, so there is nothing else to do.”

### Correct translation

Progression means the disease has met criteria for progression. Your care team should reassess the overall situation and available options.

---

## Patient statement

“The tumor did not shrink, so the treatment failed.”

### Correct translation

Tumor shrinkage is only one possible treatment outcome. Stable disease can be meaningful in some settings.

---

## Patient statement

“I had a complete response, so I am cured.”

### Correct translation

Complete response is a standardized response category. It does not by itself prove permanent cure.

# 14. Evidence Limitations

## Limitation 1 — Not every disease site is equally measurable

Some disease manifestations cannot be represented adequately by a simple measurable-lesion approach.

## Limitation 2 — Measurement variability

Small changes may reflect measurement variability rather than true biologic progression or response.

## Limitation 3 — Treatment-related changes

Treatment can alter tissues in ways that complicate imaging interpretation.

## Limitation 4 — Disease heterogeneity

Different disease sites may behave differently.

## Limitation 5 — Timing

Response depends partly on when assessment is performed.

## Limitation 6 — Clinical context

A response category does not contain all information needed for an individual treatment decision.

## Limitation 7 — Immunotherapy

Immune-related response patterns can complicate conventional progression interpretation.

## Limitation 8 — Cross-setting differences

Response assessment in neoadjuvant, adjuvant and metastatic settings cannot be treated as identical.

---

# 15. Evidence Gaps

1. No single universal response-assessment schedule applies to every gastric-cancer treatment setting.
2. The project Source Materials do not support a single universal imaging modality for all response assessments.
3. Detailed individualized interpretation requires patient-specific imaging and clinical information.
4. The exact handling of complex immunotherapy response patterns belongs to formal iRECIST methodology.
5. Response assessment does not by itself define optimal treatment sequencing after progression.
6. Response categories cannot independently determine prognosis.

These gaps do not block PP-0217 because they are explicitly outside the package's atomic clinical ownership.
