# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a CT scan and why is it used during cancer care?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Computed Tomography (CT)**, including what it is, why it is commonly used in cancer care, how it helps doctors evaluate cancer over time, and why CT findings are interpreted together with other clinical information.

This Population Package introduces the purpose of CT imaging without discussing CT physics, radiation dose, technical protocols, image interpretation, or treatment decision algorithms.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of CT scan
- Purpose of CT during cancer care
- Role in diagnosis, staging and treatment monitoring
- Importance of repeated CT examinations
- Basic concept of contrast material
- Integration of CT findings into comprehensive clinical assessment

---

## Excluded

- CT scanner physics
- Radiation dose and radiation risk
- Contrast reactions
- Contrast safety
- Patient preparation
- CT examination procedure
- CT image interpretation
- RECIST measurements
- Comparison with MRI or PET/CT
- Treatment decisions based on CT findings

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- CT is one of the most frequently used imaging tests in oncology.
- CT provides detailed anatomical images.
- CT is used throughout the cancer journey for diagnosis, evaluation and monitoring.
- Repeated CT examinations enable longitudinal assessment.
- CT findings are interpreted within the complete clinical context.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the role of CT in cancer evaluation;
- repeated CT imaging during treatment monitoring;
- the importance of longitudinal comparison;
- integration of CT findings into overall clinical management.

Differences between references primarily concern disease-specific imaging protocols, preferred imaging intervals and technical CT acquisition methods, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- imaging recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- general radiology education principles from ACR and RSNA.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- CT radiation dose;
- radiation safety;
- contrast safety;
- CT preparation;
- CT report interpretation;
- comparison between CT, MRI and PET/CT;
- RECIST measurements.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
