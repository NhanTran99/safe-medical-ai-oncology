# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **How can I read and understand a genomic or molecular test report in gastric cancer?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with a source-grounded framework for understanding the structure and meaning of a genomic or molecular test report without turning the report into an individualized treatment recommendation.

The package is deliberately positioned after NGS/molecular testing and before downstream biomarker-guided treatment application.

---

## Included

- Molecular-report definition and purpose.

- Specimen context.
- Broad assay scope.
- Types of genomic/molecular findings.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Direct Guideline / Professional Curriculum

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

Highest-priority disease-specific source for PP-0189.

Key supported points:

- IHC/ISH/targeted gene PCR are preferred initial approaches for specified biomarkers.
- Validated NGS may be considered later in selected clinical circumstances when sufficient tumor tissue is available.
- NGS can assess several mutations and other molecular events simultaneously.
- Molecular events may include amplification, deletions, TMB, and MSI.
- Blood-based ctDNA testing is a form of liquid biopsy and may be considered in selected settings including limited tissue or advanced/metastatic disease where traditional biopsy is not feasible.

### ESMO-ASCO Global Curriculum 2023

Highest-priority cross-cutting genomic-testing framework.

Key supported points:

- assays may be directed toward DNA, RNA, or protein;
- testing may target a specific analyte, a panel, or a genome-wide assay;
- genomic aberrations include multiple mutation classes, indels, copy-number changes, fusions, transcript-level information, and protein-level information;
- variant interpretation must occur in the proper clinical context;
- pre-analytic variables can influence test results;
- post-analytic variables include bioinformatic processing and interpretation;
- possible germline variants detected during somatic profiling may require confirmatory germline testing and genetic counselling.

# Evidence Notes

## Note 1 — Molecular report versus molecular test

The project Source Materials strongly support the distinction between the **testing approach** and the **information generated by testing**.

PP-0187 owns NGS biomarker testing. PP-0189 therefore uses NGS as an upstream source of report information rather than teaching the technology itself.

This is an architecture decision supported by the Registry and Gold workflow rather than a claim that a single universal report format exists.

---

## Note 2 — Report structure is not universal

Different laboratories may format reports differently.

Therefore, PP-0189 deliberately teaches a **common conceptual reading sequence** rather than claiming that every laboratory report contains identical headings or fields.

The sequence is:

**test → specimen → scope → finding → interpretation → relevance → limitation → clinical context**.

---

## Note 3 — Negative results

The source materials document assay scope, specimen limitations, sensitivity-related variables, and pre-analytic influences. The patient-facing implication is that a negative finding must be understood within those boundaries.

The PP therefore avoids absolute statements such as “negative means no mutation exists.”

---

## Note 4 — VUS

The NCI Genetics PDQ explicitly identifies VUS as a possible test outcome. PP-0189 uses that evidence only to explain the patient-facing meaning of uncertainty.

It does not reproduce variant-classification criteria.

---

## Note 5 — Germline implications

ESMO-ASCO and NCI both support the possibility of clinically relevant germline findings emerging during or after tumor/somatic profiling.

The PP therefore alerts patients to the issue but explicitly avoids concluding that a tumor finding is inherited.

---

## Note 6 — Actionability

Actionability is deliberately treated as a **context-dependent clinical concept**, not a binary laboratory property that automatically determines treatment.

NCCN, NCI and ACS support the role of biomarker information in selected treatment contexts, but treatment selection remains downstream.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence supports the following major educational claims:

1. A molecular report should be understood in relation to the test that generated it.
2. The specimen is an essential part of interpreting a molecular result.
3. Molecular assays differ in biological target and analytical scope.
4. Genomic testing can identify multiple classes of molecular alterations.
5. NGS can assess multiple molecular events simultaneously in selected gastric-cancer contexts.
6. A detected molecular alteration is not automatically an actionable treatment target.
7. A VUS represents uncertainty rather than an established harmful or actionable finding.
8. A negative result applies to the test's scope and limitations.
9. An inadequate/failed test is different from a negative test.
10. Different tests may produce discordant findings because they can examine different biological features using different specimens and methods.
11. Tumor testing can sometimes raise questions about possible inherited findings.
12. Molecular results should be interpreted within the broader clinical context.
13. Biomarker results may contribute to treatment selection, but the molecular report is not itself a treatment order.

# Evidence Consistency Review

## NCCN versus ESMO-ASCO

No material conflict identified for the scope of PP-0189.

NCCN provides the disease-specific gastric-cancer testing context, while ESMO-ASCO provides the cross-cutting genomic-testing framework.

The two source sets are complementary:

**NCCN = gastric-cancer clinical testing context**

**ESMO-ASCO = genomic-testing concepts and interpretation context**

## NCI versus professional guidance

NCI patient-facing material is consistent with the principle that biomarker information contributes to treatment planning but is integrated with broader clinical factors.

NCI Genetics PDQ and ESMO-ASCO are complementary regarding possible germline implications of tumor testing.

## ACS versus NCCN/NCI

ACS provides patient-facing treatment examples that are directionally consistent with the guideline framework while remaining outside the detailed testing and treatment ownership of PP-0189.

# Evidence Gaps

The following gaps are acknowledged and intentionally not filled with unsupported general knowledge:

1. There is no single universal molecular-report template applicable to every laboratory.
2. Assay-specific detection limits and technical thresholds vary by platform and laboratory.
3. Exact interpretation of an individual patient's report requires the original laboratory report and clinical context.
4. Actionability is not reducible to one universal rule across all molecular alterations.
5. The project Source Materials do not provide a universal patient-facing dictionary for every possible molecular-report term.
6. Detailed biomarker-specific scoring and thresholds are delegated to dedicated PPs.
7. Detailed variant classification systems are delegated to dedicated PPs.

These gaps do not block PP-0189 because the locked scope is deliberately conceptual and report-literacy focused.

3. Definitions or major clinical frameworks for molecular actionability.

# Boundary Verification

## Core

Molecular-report literacy: test context, specimen context, assay scope, reported molecular findings, basic result terminology, high-level clinical relevance/actionability, limitations, clinical-context integration, and patient-facing interpretation questions.

## Supporting

Tissue-versus-liquid context, tumor cellularity, assay limitations, discordance, possible germline implications, molecular tumor board concept, and clinical-trial relevance.

## Explicitly Excluded

Detailed NGS methodology, sequencing/bioinformatics, variant calling, detailed variant interpretation/classification, individual biomarker testing/scoring, detailed ctDNA biology, germline testing algorithms, treatment selection, individualized prognosis, and individualized report interpretation.

## Delegated-to PP

PP-0107, PP-0108, PP-0110, PP-0111, PP-0178, PP-0179, PP-0180, PP-0181–PP-0188, PP-0190, PP-0191, relevant hereditary/genetic-testing PPs, ctDNA/liquid-biopsy PPs, and downstream treatment PPs.
