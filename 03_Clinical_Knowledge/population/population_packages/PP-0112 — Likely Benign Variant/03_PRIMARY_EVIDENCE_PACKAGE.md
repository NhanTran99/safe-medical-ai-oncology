# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a Likely Benign Variant?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of a **Likely Benign Variant** as a genetic variant supported by **strong scientific evidence indicating that it is unlikely to cause disease**, while recognizing that the available evidence has not yet reached the highest level required for a **Benign** classification.

This Population Package explains that a likely benign classification reflects the **strength of available scientific evidence**, emphasizes that it should not be confused with absolute certainty, and reinforces that genomic findings should always be interpreted within the patient's complete clinical context.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Likely benign is an evidence-based scientific classification.
- Strong evidence supports that the variant is unlikely to cause disease.
- The distinction from benign reflects the amount of available evidence rather than biological effect.
- Scientific classifications may evolve as new evidence becomes available.
- Clinical interpretation always considers the broader medical context.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently describe a **Likely Benign Variant** as a genetic variant supported by strong evidence indicating that it is unlikely to be disease-causing, while recognizing that the available evidence has not yet reached the highest level required for a benign classification. Guidance consistently emphasizes that classification reflects the current state of scientific knowledge and that periodic review may occur as additional evidence accumulates.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- classification standards from ACMG and AMP;
- clinical genomics guidance from ClinGen;
- molecular pathology principles from CAP;
- oncology implementation guidance from NCCN and ASCO;
- patient education principles from NCI and ACS;
- international consistency with ESMO.
