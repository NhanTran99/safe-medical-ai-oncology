- Understand that not every patient requires molecular testing.
- Feel more confident discussing molecular testing with the healthcare team.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Molecular Testing?

## Patient Explanation

**Molecular testing** is laboratory testing that looks for changes in the DNA, RNA, or other molecular features of cancer cells or other biological samples.

These tests may provide information that helps doctors better understand a cancer and guide medical care.

---

## Clinical Importance

Molecular testing identifies clinically relevant molecular alterations.

---

## Key Concepts

- DNA.
- RNA.
- Molecular alterations.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Is Molecular Testing Performed?

## Patient Explanation

Depending on the clinical situation, molecular testing may help doctors:

- better understand the cancer;
- identify certain biomarkers;
- support treatment planning;
- contribute to precision medicine.

The purpose of testing depends on the type of cancer and the clinical question being asked.

---

## Clinical Importance

Molecular testing provides clinically useful molecular information when appropriate.

---

## Key Concepts

- Biomarkers.
- Precision medicine.
- Clinical decision-making.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Is Molecular Testing the Same as Biomarker Testing?

## Patient Explanation

Not exactly.

Many molecular tests are used to identify biomarkers, but **molecular testing** and **biomarker testing** are not identical terms.

Some biomarker tests involve molecular testing, while others use different laboratory methods.

---

## Clinical Importance

Molecular testing represents one important category within biomarker testing.

---

## Key Concepts

- Biomarker testing.
- Molecular diagnostics.
- Laboratory methods.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — What Samples Can Be Used?

## Patient Explanation

Molecular testing may use:

- tissue collected during a biopsy or surgery;
- blood samples in certain situations;
- other biological specimens when appropriate.

The type of sample depends on the specific test and the clinical situation.

---

## Clinical Importance

Appropriate specimen selection depends on the intended molecular analysis.

---

## Key Concepts

- Tissue specimen.
- Blood specimen.
- Sample selection.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Does Everyone Need Molecular Testing?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Whether molecular testing is recommended depends on:

- the type of cancer;
- the stage of disease;
- the clinical question;
- current medical guidelines.

Your healthcare team will determine whether molecular testing is appropriate for your situation.

---

## Clinical Importance

Molecular testing should be performed according to evidence-based clinical indications.

---

## Key Concepts

- Individualized testing.
- Clinical guidelines.
- Appropriate use.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Every person with cancer automatically needs molecular testing.

**Fact**

The need for molecular testing depends on the cancer type, the clinical situation, and current medical guidelines.

---

# Key Messages

- Molecular testing looks for changes at the molecular level.
- Molecular testing is a group of laboratory tests rather than a single test.
- Molecular testing often supports biomarker testing and precision medicine.
- Not every patient requires molecular testing.
- Doctors recommend molecular testing only when it is clinically appropriate.
