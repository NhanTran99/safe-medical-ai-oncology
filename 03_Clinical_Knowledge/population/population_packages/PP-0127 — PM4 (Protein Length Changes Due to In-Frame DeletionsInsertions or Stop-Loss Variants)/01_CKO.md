# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what PM4 is.
- Understand the basic concept of protein length changes.
- Recognize that PM4 is one individual ACMG Evidence Code within the PM evidence-code family.
- Understand why certain protein-length changes may provide moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- Recognize that not every protein-length change is harmful.
- Understand that PM4 contributes one category of evidence rather than determining the final variant classification.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is PM4?

## Patient Explanation

**PM4** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant may cause disease.

It belongs to the **PM (Moderate Pathogenic Evidence)** family and represents one specific type of **moderate scientific evidence**.

---

## Clinical Importance

PM4 contributes one category of moderate evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- PM4
- Moderate evidence
- ACMG Evidence Code

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Does Protein Length Mean?

## Patient Explanation

Proteins normally have a specific length that helps them function properly.

Some genetic variants can cause a protein to become **slightly shorter or longer than expected**.

These changes may affect how well the protein works.

---

## Clinical Importance

Changes in protein length may influence biological function and therefore contribute to variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Protein
- Protein length
- Biological function

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Types of Variants Can Lead to PM4?

## Patient Explanation

PM4 may be considered for certain genetic variants that change the normal length of a protein.

Examples include:

- **In-frame deletions**
- **In-frame insertions**
- **Stop-loss variants**

These types of variants do not automatically cause disease, but in some situations they may provide moderate scientific evidence.

---

## Clinical Importance

Certain protein-length alterations may provide clinically meaningful evidence during variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- In-frame deletion
- In-frame insertion
- Stop-loss variant

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does PM4 Mean the Variant Definitely Causes Disease?

## Patient Explanation

No.

PM4 is **not the final classification**.

Healthcare professionals always evaluate PM4 together with many other categories of scientific evidence before determining whether a variant should be classified as pathogenic.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification depends on the complete body of available evidence.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Professional interpretation
- Final classification

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Is PM4 Important?

## Patient Explanation

PM4 helps genetics professionals evaluate whether a change in the normal length of a protein may contribute useful scientific evidence during variant interpretation.

Using standardized evidence codes allows laboratories around the world to interpret genetic findings more consistently.

---

## Clinical Importance

Standardized evaluation of protein-length changes improves consistency and reproducibility in clinical genomics.

---

## Key Concepts

- Protein-length change
- Standardization
- Clinical genomics

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Any change in the length of a protein automatically means the variant causes disease.

**Fact**

Many protein-length changes do **not** cause disease.

PM4 provides **moderate supporting evidence** only and must always be interpreted together with many other categories of scientific evidence.

---

# Key Messages

- PM4 is one ACMG Evidence Code.
- PM4 belongs to the PM (Moderate Pathogenic Evidence) family.
- Some genetic variants can change the normal length of a protein.
- Protein-length changes may contribute moderate supporting evidence in appropriate situations.
- PM4 contributes one category of evidence but is not the final variant classification.
