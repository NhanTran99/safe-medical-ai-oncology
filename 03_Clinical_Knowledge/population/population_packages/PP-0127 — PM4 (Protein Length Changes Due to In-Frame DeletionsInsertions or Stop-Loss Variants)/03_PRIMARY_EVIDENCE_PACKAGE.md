# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is PM4 (Protein Length Changes Due to In-Frame Deletions/Insertions or Stop-Loss Variants)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **PM4 (Protein Length Changes Due to In-Frame Deletions/Insertions or Stop-Loss Variants)** as one of the standardized **ACMG/AMP Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes**.

This Population Package explains that **PM4 represents moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity when a genetic variant changes the normal length of a protein through mechanisms such as in-frame deletions, in-frame insertions, or stop-loss variants, according to accepted professional standards.** It emphasizes that changes in protein length may contribute one category of scientific evidence but **do not independently establish that a variant causes disease**.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- PM4 is one standardized ACMG/AMP evidence code.
- Certain genetic variants can change the normal length of a protein.
- Protein-length changes may provide moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity in appropriate situations.
- Protein-length changes alone do not prove that a variant causes disease.
- Final variant classification always requires comprehensive professional evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **PM4** as one of the standardized **Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes** within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that PM4 may be applied when a variant changes the expected length of a protein through mechanisms such as **in-frame deletions**, **in-frame insertions**, or **stop-loss variants**, according to accepted professional standards. ClinGen SVI recommendations further refine the application of PM4 while emphasizing that **protein-length alteration represents one component of structured evidence-based interpretation rather than an independent diagnostic conclusion.**
