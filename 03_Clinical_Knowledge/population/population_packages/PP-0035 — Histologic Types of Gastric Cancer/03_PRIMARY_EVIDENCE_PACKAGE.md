# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What are the histologic types of gastric cancer?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the concept of histologic types in gastric cancer, including why different histologic types exist, how they are identified, and why they are important as one component of the overall pathological assessment.

This Population Package introduces histologic classification without discussing detailed classification systems, prognostic implications or treatment decisions.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of histology
- Definition of histologic type
- Microscopic classification of gastric cancer
- Adenocarcinoma as the predominant histologic type
- Introduction to intestinal type
- Introduction to diffuse type
- Introduction to signet ring cell carcinoma
- Relationship between biopsy, pathology report and histologic type
- Role of histologic type in overall diagnosis

---

## Excluded

- Lauren Classification
- WHO Classification
- Histologic grading
- Tumor differentiation
- Molecular classification
- MSI subtype
- EBV-associated gastric cancer
- HER2 testing
- PD-L1 testing
- Prognostic interpretation
- Treatment selection

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Histologic type describes the microscopic appearance of gastric cancer.
- Histologic type is determined through pathological examination of biopsy tissue.
- Adenocarcinoma is the most common histologic type of gastric cancer.
- Additional histologic patterns may be identified within gastric adenocarcinoma.
- Histologic type is reported as part of the pathology report.
- Histologic type contributes to diagnosis but does not independently determine prognosis or treatment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of histologic type;
- the role of pathology in histologic classification;
- adenocarcinoma as the predominant gastric cancer type;
- the importance of histologic classification within the overall diagnostic process.

Differences between references are limited to terminology, educational emphasis and depth of explanation.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- Lauren Classification;
- WHO Classification;
- tumor grade;
- molecular classification;
- biomarker testing;
- prognostic significance of histologic subtype;
- treatment recommendations based on histology.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
