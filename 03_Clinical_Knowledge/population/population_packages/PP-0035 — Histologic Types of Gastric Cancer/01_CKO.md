# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what a histologic type is.
- Understand that gastric cancer is not a single disease under the microscope.
- Recognize that adenocarcinoma is the most common histologic type of gastric cancer.
- Understand that pathology reports identify the histologic type.
- Understand that histologic type is one part of the overall medical evaluation.
- Understand that histologic type alone does not determine prognosis or treatment.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Does "Histologic Type" Mean?

## Patient Explanation

Histology is the study of cells and tissues under a microscope.

When a tissue sample is examined after a biopsy, the pathologist identifies what type of cells are present.

The **histologic type** describes how the cancer looks under the microscope.

Different cancers may appear different even if they arise in the same organ.

---

## Clinical Importance

Histologic type is a standard part of the pathological evaluation of gastric cancer.

---

## Key Concepts

- Histology examines tissue under a microscope.
- Histologic type describes the appearance of cancer cells.
- Histologic type is identified by the pathologist.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Are All Gastric Cancers the Same?

## Patient Explanation

No.

Although they all begin in the stomach, gastric cancers are not identical.

Under the microscope, doctors recognize several different histologic types.

These differences help doctors describe the tumor more accurately.

---

## Clinical Importance

Histologic classification improves communication between healthcare professionals and supports standardized diagnosis.

---

## Key Concepts

- Gastric cancer includes several histologic types.
- Histologic differences are identified by microscopic examination.
- Classification helps describe the disease.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Is the Most Common Histologic Type?

## Patient Explanation

The most common type of stomach cancer is **adenocarcinoma**.

This type develops from the cells that line the inside of the stomach.

Most people diagnosed with stomach cancer have this type.

Within adenocarcinoma, doctors may describe additional histologic patterns, which are explained in separate Population Packages.

---

## Clinical Importance

Recognizing adenocarcinoma as the predominant histologic type provides an important foundation for understanding gastric cancer pathology.

---

## Key Concepts

- Adenocarcinoma is the most common gastric cancer.
- It begins in the stomach lining.
- Additional classifications may be used.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — What Are Intestinal Type, Diffuse Type and Signet Ring Cell Carcinoma?

## Patient Explanation

You may see terms such as:

- **intestinal type**;
- **diffuse type**;
- **signet ring cell carcinoma**.

These terms describe different microscopic appearances of stomach cancer.

They help pathologists classify the tumor.

This Population Package introduces these names only.

Their detailed meaning and clinical significance are explained in separate Population Packages.

---

## Clinical Importance

Patients should recognize these terms without assuming they determine prognosis or treatment by themselves.

---

## Key Concepts

- Several histologic types exist.
- These names describe microscopic appearance.
- Detailed interpretation requires additional context.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — How Is the Histologic Type Determined?

## Patient Explanation

The histologic type cannot usually be determined by symptoms, blood tests or imaging alone.

Instead, it is identified after a pathologist examines tissue obtained during a biopsy.

The histologic type is then included in the pathology report.

---

## Clinical Importance

Microscopic examination remains essential for histologic classification.

---

## Key Concepts

- Histologic type requires biopsy.
- Pathologists determine the histologic type.
- The result appears in the pathology report.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Does Histologic Type Decide Everything?

## Patient Explanation

No.

The histologic type is only one part of your overall medical evaluation.

Your healthcare team also considers:

- your symptoms;
- imaging studies;
- tumor stage;
- biomarker testing;
- your overall health;
- and many other clinical factors.

For this reason, patients should not try to predict their prognosis or treatment based only on the histologic type.

---

## Clinical Importance

Clinical management depends on integration of multiple sources of information rather than histologic type alone.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

Once I know my histologic type, I already know my prognosis and treatment.

**Fact**

Histologic type is important, but doctors also consider many other clinical and pathological factors before making treatment recommendations.

---

# Key Messages

- Histologic type describes how stomach cancer appears under the microscope.
- Gastric cancer includes several histologic types.
- Adenocarcinoma is the most common type.
- Histologic type is determined from a biopsy by a pathologist.
- Histologic type is one part of the pathology report.
- Histologic type alone does not determine prognosis or treatment.
