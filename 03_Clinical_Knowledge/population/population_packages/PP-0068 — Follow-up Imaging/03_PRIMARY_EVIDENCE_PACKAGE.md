# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **Why do I need repeated imaging scans during and after cancer treatment?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **follow-up imaging**, including why repeated imaging studies are performed, how doctors compare imaging over time, why a single scan is usually insufficient for treatment monitoring, and how imaging contributes to—but does not solely determine—clinical decision-making.

This Population Package introduces the purpose of follow-up imaging without discussing imaging techniques, technical protocols, radiation exposure, RECIST measurements, or disease-specific surveillance schedules.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of follow-up imaging
- Purpose of repeated imaging during and after treatment
- Longitudinal comparison of imaging findings
- Monitoring treatment response
- Individualized imaging schedules
- Integration of imaging findings into overall clinical assessment

---

## Excluded

- CT technical protocol
- MRI technical protocol
- PET/CT technical protocol
- Ultrasound technique
- RECIST measurements
- Radiation exposure
- Imaging frequency recommendations
- Treatment decisions based on imaging
- Cancer recurrence surveillance
- Prognostic significance

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Follow-up imaging is an essential component of cancer monitoring.
- Doctors compare imaging studies over time rather than interpreting one scan in isolation.
- Imaging helps evaluate treatment response and disease progression.
- Imaging schedules differ among patients.
- Imaging findings contribute to, but do not replace, comprehensive clinical assessment.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the importance of repeated imaging;
- longitudinal comparison of imaging findings;
- the role of imaging in treatment monitoring;
- integration of imaging results into broader clinical decision-making.

Differences between references relate primarily to disease-specific imaging schedules and preferred imaging modalities, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- internationally accepted concepts regarding serial imaging and treatment assessment.
