## Clinical Question

> **What is special about gastric cancer whose metastatic disease is confined to the peritoneal compartment, how is this state established and reassessed, and how does the specialized management pathway work?**

## Evidence Intent

PP-0225 translates the current project Source Materials into a patient-facing, evidence-traceable representation of the **dedicated peritoneal-only metastatic pathway**.

The key architecture is:

**Metastatic gastric cancer**
→ **peritoneal carcinomatosis**
→ **peritoneal-only metastatic disease**
→ **systemic therapy**
→ **restaging**
→ **PCI / response / extraperitoneal disease**
→ **cytoreduction feasibility**
→ **multidisciplinary decision**
→ **selected local treatment / clinical trial / continued systemic therapy / supportive pathway**

## Detailed Evidence Notes

### Evidence Note 1 — Dedicated pathway

The most important source finding is that NCCN does not merely mention peritoneal metastasis inside the general metastatic algorithm.

It provides a separate pathway explicitly titled:

**PERITONEAL CARCINOMA AS ONLY DISEASE**

This validates PP-0225 as a distinct Population Package rather than a duplicate of PP-0224. fileciteturn33file14

### Evidence Note 2 — Positive cytology

The pathway begins with:

> peritoneal only metastatic disease, including positive cytology.

This is essential because a patient can have clinically relevant peritoneal metastatic disease without obvious macroscopic implants.

PP-0225 therefore must not define the population solely by visible peritoneal deposits.

### Evidence Note 3 — Absence of extraperitoneal disease

The favorable branch explicitly requires:

> no extraperitoneal disease.

Therefore the phrase “only disease” is an ownership-defining feature, not merely a descriptive adjective.

### Evidence Note 4 — Minimum systemic treatment

NCCN v2.2026 specifies:

> systemic therapy for a minimum of 3 months.

This is a direct quantitative pathway anchor and is therefore retained in PP-0225. fileciteturn33file14

### Evidence Note 5 — Restaging

Restaging includes:

- CT;
- diagnostic laparoscopy;
- washings;
- documented PCI;
- ± biopsy;
- selected PET/CT;
- selected EGD.

The purpose is to reassess disease extent and response before additional treatment.

### Evidence Note 6 — PCI

The guideline uses:

**PCI ≤10**

as the lower-burden branch and:

**PCI >10**

as the higher-burden branch.

This should not be converted into an automatic treatment threshold.

### Evidence Note 7 — Response

Stable/improved disease is part of the favorable branch.

Progression is routed away from the selected local-treatment pathway.

Therefore response is a core management variable.

### Evidence Note 8 — Cytoreduction

The pathway distinguishes:

**complete cytoreduction predicted**

from

**incomplete cytoreduction predicted**.

This is clinically meaningful because surgery/IC-HIPEC appears only in the complete-cytoreduction branch. fileciteturn33file14

### Evidence Note 9 — HIPEC evidence

The supplied NCCN v2.2025 evidence discussion reports heterogeneous results.

CYTO-CHIP suggested a survival benefit for surgery plus HIPEC compared with surgery alone in a phase III setting, while GASTRIPEC-I did not demonstrate a significant OS difference, although some secondary outcomes favored HIPEC. PERISCOPE II is an ongoing study. fileciteturn33file19

The guideline therefore concludes that IC/HIPEC should be used only in selected circumstances and after multidisciplinary discussion.

This evidence uncertainty is important to preserve.

### Evidence Note 10 — Clinical trials

Clinical trials appear repeatedly in the pathway.

This is not an incidental option.

It reflects the evolving evidence base for selected local and intraperitoneal strategies.

### Evidence Note 11 — Best supportive care

Best supportive care appears in the high-PCI/progression/extraperitoneal branch.

This should be presented as an active clinical pathway rather than as abandonment of care.

## Clinical Claims Summary

1. Peritoneal-only disease is a distinct metastatic state.
2. Positive cytology can be included.
3. Extraperitoneal metastatic disease excludes the patient from the clean peritoneal-only state.
4. Initial systemic therapy is central.
5. The current NCCN pathway specifies a minimum 3-month systemic-treatment period.
6. Restaging is required before selected additional treatment.
7. PCI is a core measure of peritoneal disease burden.
8. PCI ≤10 is part of the favorable branch.
9. PCI >10 moves toward a different branch.
10. Stable/improved disease is favorable.
11. Progression is unfavorable for the selected local-treatment pathway.
12. Complete cytoreduction must be considered.
13. Incomplete predicted cytoreduction does not automatically lead to surgery.
14. Selected patients may be considered for gastrectomy + cytoreduction + IC/HIPEC.
15. Clinical trials remain important.
16. IC/HIPEC evidence is limited/evolving.
17. Best supportive care is a legitimate pathway in selected patients.

## Evidence Limitations

1. The peritoneal-only population is heterogeneous.
2. PCI is a useful burden measure but not a complete representation of patient fitness or biology.
3. Imaging may underestimate small-volume disease.
4. Restaging laparoscopy may identify disease not fully characterized by imaging.
5. Cytoreduction feasibility is technically and clinically dependent.
6. HIPEC evidence remains heterogeneous.
7. Randomized evidence has not established universal benefit for HIPEC.
8. PIPAC remains investigational in the supplied guideline.
9. Individual patient decisions require information beyond this package.

## Evidence-to-Patient Translation

### Why do I need systemic treatment first?

Because the team needs to see how the cancer behaves before considering highly selected local treatment.

### Why do I need restaging?

Because the disease may respond, remain stable, progress or reveal additional metastatic sites.

### Why is PCI important?

Because the amount and distribution of peritoneal disease affect whether selected local treatment can even be considered.

### Why does complete cytoreduction matter?

Because a local surgical approach depends on whether the visible peritoneal disease can realistically be removed.

### Why isn't HIPEC automatic?

Because the evidence and guideline pathway support only selected use after multidisciplinary evaluation.
