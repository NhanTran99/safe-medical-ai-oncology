## Atomic Clinical Question

> **What is special about gastric cancer whose metastatic disease is confined to the peritoneal compartment, how is this state established and reassessed, and how does the specialized management pathway work?**

## Educational Objectives

After reading this package, the reader should be able to:

1. Explain what “peritoneal carcinoma as only disease” means in gastric cancer.
2. Understand that positive peritoneal cytology can belong to this pathway even when visible implants are absent.
3. Distinguish peritoneal-only metastatic disease from peritoneal disease accompanied by extraperitoneal metastases.
4. Understand the information needed to establish the peritoneal-only state.
5. Understand why systemic therapy is used before reassessment in the NCCN pathway.
6. Understand the significance of the minimum 3-month systemic-treatment framework.
7. Understand why restaging is a decision point rather than simply another scan.
8. Understand why response or progression changes the pathway.
9. Understand what PCI represents and why disease burden matters.
10. Understand why PCI is not, by itself, an automatic indication for surgery or HIPEC.
11. Understand the importance of predicted complete cytoreduction.
12. Understand why multidisciplinary consultation is central to selected treatment decisions.
13. Understand when continued systemic therapy, clinical trials, best supportive care, or selected cytoreductive surgery with IC/HIPEC may enter consideration.
14. Understand why HIPEC is a downstream specialized treatment topic rather than an automatic treatment.
15. Understand the patient-facing limitations and common misconceptions around this disease state.

## Scope

### Included

PP-0225 owns the **specialized management pathway for gastric cancer with metastatic disease confined to the peritoneal compartment**, including positive cytology. It covers confirmation of the disease state, absence of documented extraperitoneal metastases, initial systemic treatment, the NCCN minimum 3-month framework, restaging, response, PCI, disease burden, extraperitoneal reassessment, predicted cytoreduction, multidisciplinary decision-making, selected local-treatment pathways, clinical trials, and patient-facing interpretation.

### Not Included

PP-0225 does not own the foundational definition of peritoneal carcinomatosis, detailed response-assessment methodology, RECIST, detailed systemic regimens, detailed surgery or HIPEC methodology, palliative/supportive-care protocols, or individualized treatment decisions.

## Boundary

**Core =** The specialized clinical state in which gastric cancer has metastatic disease confined to the peritoneal compartment, including positive peritoneal cytology, without documented extraperitoneal metastatic disease; pathway-level confirmation and baseline characterization; systemic therapy as the initial management backbone; the NCCN minimum 3-month systemic-treatment framework; restaging; response/stability versus progression; PCI and peritoneal disease burden; reassessment for extraperitoneal disease; predicted complete versus incomplete cytoreduction; multidisciplinary selection; and the resulting high-level pathways of continued systemic therapy, clinical-trial participation, best supportive care, or selected gastrectomy with cytoreductive surgery and IC/HIPEC.

**Supporting =** CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; diagnostic laparoscopy; peritoneal washings/cytology; biopsy when indicated; selected FDG-PET/CT and EGD; performance status and treatment fitness; symptoms/nutritional context; gastrectomy as part of a selected local-treatment pathway; evidence limitations surrounding cytoreduction/IC-HIPEC; clinical-trial context; PIPAC as investigational context.

**Explicitly Excluded =** Detailed peritoneal-carcinomatosis definition; detailed cytology laboratory methodology; detailed laparoscopy technique; detailed CT/PET/EGD methodology; detailed PCI scoring methodology; RECIST methodology; detailed systemic regimens/dosing; biomarker-specific treatment; detailed gastrectomy or cytoreductive-surgery technique; dedicated HIPEC evidence/technical methodology; PIPAC technical methodology; detailed palliative care; detailed best supportive care; detailed toxicity management; individualized treatment eligibility; individualized prognosis.

**Delegated-to PP =** PP-0223 Metastatic Gastric Cancer for the overall metastatic framework; PP-0224 Peritoneal Carcinomatosis for foundational peritoneal-disease characterization; PP-0217 Response Assessment and PP-0218 RECIST-based Assessment for response methodology; PP-0219 Post-treatment Imaging for post-treatment imaging; PP-0226 HIPEC for dedicated HIPEC/intraperitoneal-treatment evidence and technique; PP-0227 Palliative Care; PP-0228 Best Supportive Care; PP-0231 Treatment-related Toxicity and Supportive Care; PP-0232 Multidisciplinary Management; plus dedicated systemic-treatment, surgery, diagnostic and clinical-trial packages.

## Clinical Knowledge Blocks

### 1. What Does “Peritoneal Carcinoma as Only Disease” Mean?

In this project, the term refers to a specific metastatic pattern of gastric cancer:

**Gastric cancer**
→ **metastatic disease involving the peritoneal compartment**
→ **no documented extraperitoneal metastatic disease**

The current NCCN v2.2026 algorithm explicitly labels this state **“Peritoneal carcinoma as only disease”** and includes **positive cytology** within the pathway. fileciteturn33file14

The phrase “only disease” is therefore important.

It does not mean:

- the peritoneum is merely the largest metastatic site;
- the peritoneum is the clinically dominant site while liver metastases are also present;
- there are other distant metastases that have simply not yet been considered.

It means the metastatic disease being considered in this pathway is confined to the peritoneal compartment.

### 2. Why Is This Different From General Metastatic Gastric Cancer?

PP-0223 owns the broad question:

> What does metastatic gastric cancer mean?

PP-0225 is narrower.

It asks:

> What changes when the peritoneum is the only documented metastatic compartment?

This distinction matters because NCCN provides a dedicated pathway for this population rather than treating every M1 pattern identically. fileciteturn32file6

The dedicated pathway introduces additional considerations such as:

- PCI;
- response after initial systemic treatment;
- absence of extraperitoneal disease;
- predicted complete cytoreduction;
- selected cytoreductive surgery;
- IC/HIPEC.

### 3. Positive Cytology Belongs to the Pathway

A critical concept is that visible peritoneal implants are not required for the patient to enter the peritoneal-only metastatic framework.

NCCN explicitly includes **positive cytology** in the peritoneal-only disease pathway. fileciteturn32file6turn33file14

This means:

**No obvious visible peritoneal implant**

does not necessarily mean:

**No peritoneal metastatic disease.**

PP-0224 explains the meaning of positive cytology in greater detail.

PP-0225 explains its **pathway consequence**.

### 4. Peritoneal-Only Versus Peritoneal-Plus-Other Metastases

Consider two simplified situations.

#### Situation A

Peritoneal metastatic disease

+

No documented liver, lung, bone or other extraperitoneal metastases.

→ **Peritoneal-only pathway may apply.**

#### Situation B

Peritoneal metastatic disease

+

Liver metastases.

→ **Not peritoneal-only disease.**

The broader metastatic framework becomes more relevant.

NCCN explicitly routes patients with **extraperitoneal metastases** away from the low-PCI peritoneal-only branch. fileciteturn33file14

### 5. Why Establishing the Disease State Requires More Than One Test

The clinical team needs to answer several questions:

1. Is the gastric primary adequately characterized?
2. Is metastatic disease present?
3. Is the peritoneum involved?
4. Is the disease truly confined to the peritoneal compartment?
5. How extensive is the peritoneal disease?
6. Is there positive cytology?
7. What is the PCI?
8. Is there progression or response after systemic therapy?
9. Can complete cytoreduction potentially be achieved?

This is why the pathway uses multiple complementary forms of assessment.

### 6. Initial Workup

NCCN v2.2026 lists pathway-level evaluation including:

- history and physical examination;
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with oral and IV contrast;
- consideration of diagnostic laparoscopy;
- FDG-PET/CT as clinically indicated;
- pathology review.

fileciteturn33file14

PP-0225 does not teach how to perform these tests.

It explains why the tests are assembled into a **disease-distribution assessment**.

### 7. Why CT Is Important

CT provides a broad assessment of:

- the primary gastric tumor;
- abdominal disease;
- distant organs;
- lymph nodes;
- peritoneal findings;
- other potential metastatic sites.

For PP-0225, the key question is not merely:

> “What does the CT show?”

It is:

> **“Does the overall staging assessment support disease confined to the peritoneal compartment?”**

### 8. Why Diagnostic Laparoscopy May Be Added

Small-volume peritoneal disease can be difficult to characterize completely using cross-sectional imaging.

Diagnostic laparoscopy can provide direct visualization of the peritoneal cavity and may permit:

- washings;
- cytology;
- biopsy;
- direct assessment of distribution;
- PCI documentation.

NCCN incorporates diagnostic laparoscopy with washings and documented PCI into restaging in the dedicated pathway. fileciteturn33file14

### 9. Peritoneal Washings and Cytology

Peritoneal washings can be examined for malignant cells.

In PP-0225, their significance is not merely diagnostic.

A positive result can establish clinically important metastatic disease even when obvious implants are not seen.

The package therefore preserves the distinction:

**PP-0224**
→ What is positive peritoneal cytology?

**PP-0225**
→ Why does positive cytology matter for the peritoneal-only management pathway?

### 10. Why Extraperitoneal Disease Must Be Rechecked

A patient may initially appear to have peritoneal-only disease, but subsequent assessment may reveal disease outside the peritoneum.

This can occur:

- at baseline;
- during restaging;
- after treatment;
- because previously occult disease becomes detectable.

Therefore “peritoneal-only” is not a label that should be assumed permanently without reassessment.

### 11. Systemic Therapy Is the Initial Treatment Backbone

For patients entering the NCCN peritoneal-only pathway, systemic therapy is used before the specialized restaging step.

NCCN v2.2026 specifies systemic therapy for a **minimum of 3 months**. fileciteturn33file14

This is an important pathway anchor.

The package does not prescribe a specific regimen.

The principle is:

> **The patient's disease is first treated systemically, then reassessed before selected additional local approaches are considered.**

### 12. Why Systemic Therapy Comes Before Further Local Treatment

Systemic therapy provides an opportunity to assess:

- whether the cancer is treatment-sensitive;
- whether disease is stable;
- whether disease is progressing;
- whether new metastatic sites emerge;
- whether a selected local approach remains plausible.

This creates a clinically meaningful separation between:

**initial disease characterization**

and

**selection for additional local treatment**.

### 13. Why the “Minimum 3 Months” Detail Matters

The current NCCN algorithm does not simply say:

> “Give some systemic therapy and operate if the disease looks limited.”

It specifies a minimum systemic-treatment period before restaging.

Therefore the Gold package must retain this quantitative anchor.

It should not, however, be interpreted as:

> “Every individual patient must receive exactly three months.”

The correct patient-facing formulation is:

> **The NCCN pathway uses systemic therapy for a minimum of 3 months before reassessment and consideration of additional treatment.**

### 14. Restaging Is a Major Decision Point

After initial systemic therapy, the pathway moves to **restaging**.

NCCN specifies:

- CT;
- diagnostic laparoscopy with washings;
- documented PCI ± biopsy;
- consideration of FDG-PET/CT;
- consideration of EGD.

fileciteturn33file14

Restaging therefore asks more than:

> “Did the tumor shrink?”

It asks:

> **“What is the current disease distribution, burden and treatment response, and does the patient still belong to the selected peritoneal-only pathway?”**

### 15. Response Versus Progression

Restaging can lead to fundamentally different pathways.

#### Stable or improved disease

This keeps selected local-treatment consideration open if the other criteria are met.

#### Progressive disease

This moves the patient away from the low-burden local-treatment branch.

NCCN routes high PCI, progression, or extraperitoneal metastases toward systemic therapy, clinical trial, or best supportive care. fileciteturn33file14

### 16. Why RECIST Is Not Reproduced Here

Response assessment is important to PP-0225.

However:

**PP-0225**
→ uses the result of response assessment.

**PP-0217**
→ owns response-assessment concepts.

**PP-0218**
→ owns RECIST methodology.

**PP-0219**
→ owns post-treatment imaging.

This preserves package ownership.

### 17. What Is PCI?

The **Peritoneal Cancer Index (PCI)** is a structured measure of the distribution and burden of peritoneal disease.

In PP-0225, PCI is important because the dedicated NCCN pathway uses it to distinguish patients with lower versus higher peritoneal disease burden.

NCCN v2.2026 uses:

- **PCI ≤10**
- **PCI >10**

as pathway-level categories. fileciteturn33file14

PP-0225 does not provide the technical PCI scoring manual.

### 18. PCI Is a Measure, Not a Standalone Treatment Decision

This is one of the most important safety messages.

A patient should not interpret:

> **PCI ≤10**

as:

> **“I automatically qualify for HIPEC.”**

The NCCN pathway requires additional conditions:

- stable or improved disease;
- no metastatic progression;
- no extraperitoneal disease;
- predicted complete cytoreduction.

Only then does the selected local-treatment branch become relevant. fileciteturn33file14

### 19. Low PCI With Controlled Disease

NCCN identifies the lower-burden branch as:

**PCI ≤10**

+

**stable or improved disease**

+

**no metastatic progression**

+

**no extraperitoneal disease**.

fileciteturn33file14

This creates an important clinical concept:

> **Disease burden must be interpreted together with disease behavior and distribution.**

### 20. High PCI

NCCN identifies:

**PCI >10**

as a different pathway branch.

Together with progression or extraperitoneal metastases, the pathway leads toward:

- systemic therapy;
- clinical trial;
- best supportive care.

fileciteturn33file14

The exact choice depends on the broader clinical context.

### 21. Why Disease Behavior Matters

Two patients can have a similar disease burden but different clinical trajectories.

One may have:

- stable disease after systemic therapy.

Another may have:

- clear progression.

This is why PCI cannot be interpreted in isolation.

The pathway combines:

**burden**

+

**response**

+

**distribution**

+

**technical feasibility**

+

**patient fitness**

+

**multidisciplinary judgment**.

### 22. What Is Complete Cytoreduction?

**Cytoreduction** means surgically removing visible tumor deposits.

The key question is whether the surgeon expects that a **complete cytoreduction** can realistically be achieved.

NCCN specifically separates:

- complete cytoreduction predicted;
- incomplete cytoreduction predicted.

fileciteturn33file14

### 23. Why Complete Cytoreduction Matters

If complete cytoreduction is predicted, selected patients may be considered for:

- clinical trial;
- gastrectomy with cytoreductive surgery and IC/HIPEC;
- continued systemic therapy.

If incomplete cytoreduction is predicted, the pathway instead identifies:

- clinical trial;
- continued systemic therapy.

fileciteturn33file14

Thus:

> **The goal is not simply to operate on patients with low PCI. The feasibility of removing all visible disease is part of the decision.**

### 24. Multidisciplinary Consultation

NCCN places **multidisciplinary consultation** in the additional-treatment pathway.

This is important because the decision combines:

- oncology;
- surgery;
- radiology;
- pathology;
- disease-distribution assessment;
- treatment response;
- patient fitness;
- technical resectability;
- treatment goals.

PP-0225 owns the principle.

PP-0232 owns the dedicated MDT package.

### 25. What If Complete Cytoreduction Is Predicted?

The NCCN pathway allows consideration of:

> **Gastrectomy with cytoreductive surgery and intraperitoneal chemotherapy (IC)/HIPEC**

in selected patients.

It also allows:

- clinical trial;
- continued systemic therapy.

fileciteturn33file14

This should be interpreted as a **selected option**, not a universal recommendation.

### 26. What If Complete Cytoreduction Is Not Predicted?

The pathway does not automatically proceed to surgery.

NCCN identifies:

- clinical trial;
- continue systemic therapy.

fileciteturn33file14

This is an important boundary against the misconception that the existence of peritoneal disease automatically creates a surgical indication.

### 27. What If There Is Progression?

If the disease progresses during or after systemic therapy, the patient no longer fits the favorable stable/improved low-PCI branch.

NCCN routes:

- high PCI;
- progression;
- extraperitoneal metastases

toward:

- systemic therapy;
- clinical trial;
- best supportive care.

fileciteturn33file14

### 28. What If Extraperitoneal Metastases Are Found?

The defining feature of PP-0225 is lost.

The patient should no longer be conceptually treated as having isolated peritoneal metastatic disease.

The broader metastatic framework of PP-0223 becomes important.

### 29. What If the Patient Remains Peritoneal-Only but Has High PCI?

The pathway remains within the specialized peritoneal-disease context, but the high-burden branch does not automatically proceed to cytoreduction/HIPEC.

The NCCN pathway identifies:

**systemic therapy**

or

**clinical trial**

or

**best supportive care**.

fileciteturn33file14

### 30. What Is the Role of HIPEC Here?

HIPEC is **downstream** from PP-0225.

PP-0225 explains:

> **when the disease pathway may reach a point at which HIPEC becomes a selected consideration.**

PP-0226 explains:

- what HIPEC is;
- how it is performed conceptually;
- evidence;
- benefits;
- limitations;
- risks;
- selection;
- clinical trials.

The supplied NCCN evidence emphasizes that IC/HIPEC should be pursued only in selected circumstances and after multidisciplinary discussion. fileciteturn33file19

### 31. Why HIPEC Is Not Automatic

The current evidence does not support:

> peritoneal disease = HIPEC.

The NCCN framework incorporates:

- limited disease burden;
- disease control;
- absence of extraperitoneal disease;
- predicted complete cytoreduction;
- multidisciplinary assessment.

The evidence base remains selective and evolving.

### 32. Evidence Behind the HIPEC Boundary

The supplied NCCN v2.2025 discussion describes studies including:

- CYTO-CHIP;
- GASTRIPEC-I;
- PERISCOPE II.

The guideline notes that evidence is limited and randomized evidence is insufficient to make IC/HIPEC a universal strategy, recommending selected use after multidisciplinary discussion. fileciteturn33file19

This supports the PP-0225 wording:

> **Selected consideration, not automatic treatment.**

### 33. PIPAC

PIPAC is a different intraperitoneal treatment approach.

Within the supplied NCCN framework, PIPAC is investigational and belongs in clinical-trial context.

PP-0225 should mention this only to prevent patients from interpreting every intraperitoneal treatment as standard care.

Detailed PIPAC methodology belongs elsewhere.

### 34. Clinical Trials

Clinical trials have a meaningful role in this pathway.

NCCN includes clinical-trial options in multiple branches, including selected low-PCI disease and situations where complete cytoreduction is not predicted. fileciteturn33file14

Clinical trials are especially relevant when:

- evidence is evolving;
- treatment strategies are specialized;
- the patient does not fit a clearly established branch;
- investigational intraperitoneal approaches are being evaluated.

### 35. Best Supportive Care

Best supportive care appears as one of the pathway destinations for patients with:

- high PCI;
- progression;
- extraperitoneal disease.

This does **not** mean:

> “There is no treatment.”

It means the treatment strategy may prioritize:

- symptom control;
- quality of life;
- functional status;
- patient goals;
- avoidance of burdensome treatment when expected benefit is limited.

Detailed best-supportive-care content belongs to PP-0228.

### 36. Palliative Care

Palliative care can be integrated at any point when symptom burden or patient goals warrant it.

PP-0225 only establishes the interface.

PP-0227 owns detailed palliative-care content.

### 37. Performance Status and Treatment Fitness

The suitability for intensive treatment depends on more than disease anatomy.

Relevant considerations include:

- performance status;
- nutritional status;
- comorbidities;
- organ function;
- prior therapy;
- treatment tolerance;
- patient goals.

PP-0225 should explain this at selection level.

Detailed toxicity and supportive-care management belongs to PP-0231.

### 38. Nutrition

Peritoneal disease can be associated with:

- ascites;
- abdominal distension;
- early satiety;
- reduced oral intake;
- bowel symptoms;
- weight loss.

These factors can affect treatment readiness and recovery.

Therefore nutrition is relevant as **supporting context**, not as a second clinical ownership domain.

### 39. Gastrectomy in This Setting

Gastrectomy is not introduced here as routine surgery for metastatic gastric cancer.

It appears only within the selected branch of the dedicated peritoneal-only pathway:

**low PCI**

+

**controlled disease**

+

**no extraperitoneal disease**

+

**complete cytoreduction predicted**

→ selected consideration of gastrectomy + cytoreductive surgery + IC/HIPEC.

fileciteturn33file14

Detailed gastrectomy technique belongs to surgical PPs.

### 40. The Most Important Clinical Logic

The pathway should be remembered as:

**Confirm peritoneal-only disease**

↓

**Systemic therapy**

↓

**Minimum 3-month framework**

↓

**Restaging**

↓

**Assess response**

↓

**Reassess extraperitoneal disease**

↓

**Document PCI**

↓

**Assess cytoreduction feasibility**

↓

**Multidisciplinary decision**

↓

**Selected local treatment / clinical trial / continued systemic therapy / supportive pathway**

This is the core of PP-0225.

## Patient-Facing Questions

### “Does peritoneal-only disease mean the cancer is only in the abdomen?”

It means that, within the metastatic disease being considered in this pathway, the documented metastatic spread is confined to the peritoneal compartment. It does not mean the original stomach cancer is absent.

### “Can positive cytology count even if no tumor deposits are visible?”

Yes. Positive peritoneal cytology is explicitly included in the NCCN peritoneal-only pathway. fileciteturn33file14

### “Why do I need treatment before another laparoscopy?”

The pathway uses systemic treatment first and then reassesses the disease to see how it behaves and how extensive it remains.

### “Why is three months mentioned?”

The NCCN v2.2026 pathway specifies systemic therapy for a minimum of 3 months before restaging.

### “If my PCI is 8, do I automatically get HIPEC?”

No. PCI is only one part of the decision. Disease response, absence of extraperitoneal disease, predicted complete cytoreduction and multidisciplinary assessment also matter.

### “What if my disease grows during treatment?”

Progression moves the patient away from the favorable low-PCI branch. The pathway may then involve continued systemic therapy, a clinical trial or best supportive care depending on the situation.

### “What if the scans find liver metastases?”

Then the disease is no longer confined to the peritoneum, so the broader metastatic gastric-cancer framework becomes important.

### “Does everyone with peritoneal-only disease need surgery?”

No. Surgery is considered only for selected patients after reassessment.

### “Does everyone with low PCI receive HIPEC?”

No. Low PCI alone is not sufficient.

### “Why does the surgeon need to know whether complete cytoreduction is possible?”

Because the potential value of cytoreductive surgery depends on whether visible disease can realistically be removed to the intended extent.

### “Is HIPEC proven for everyone with peritoneal disease?”

No. The supplied NCCN evidence supports selected use and multidisciplinary discussion rather than universal treatment. fileciteturn33file19

## Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1

**“Peritoneal-only disease means the cancer is easy to remove.”**

**Fact:**  
Peritoneal-only disease is a specialized metastatic state, but its extent and technical resectability can vary greatly.

### Myth 2

**“If the peritoneum is the biggest metastatic site, it counts as peritoneal-only.”**

**Fact:**  
The defining concept is absence of documented extraperitoneal metastatic disease, not simply which site has the largest tumor burden.

### Myth 3

**“Positive cytology does not matter if there are no visible implants.”**

**Fact:**  
Positive cytology is explicitly included in the NCCN peritoneal-only pathway.

### Myth 4

**“Three months of treatment means exactly three months and then surgery.”**

**Fact:**  
The NCCN pathway uses a minimum 3-month systemic-treatment framework followed by reassessment. It does not guarantee surgery afterward.

### Myth 5

**“PCI ≤10 means I qualify for HIPEC.”**

**Fact:**  
PCI is only one criterion. Disease control, absence of extraperitoneal disease, predicted complete cytoreduction and multidisciplinary assessment also matter.

### Myth 6

**“If the disease is peritoneal-only, surgery is always better than systemic treatment.”**

**Fact:**  
The pathway includes continued systemic therapy and clinical trials as well as selected surgery.

### Myth 7

**“If complete cytoreduction is not possible, surgery should still be attempted.”**

**Fact:**  
The NCCN pathway does not automatically direct such patients to cytoreductive surgery/HIPEC.

### Myth 8

**“HIPEC is the standard next step for all peritoneal disease.”**

**Fact:**  
HIPEC is a selected downstream treatment approach.

### Myth 9

**“PIPAC is another standard version of HIPEC.”**

**Fact:**  
PIPAC is a distinct approach and is investigational in the supplied NCCN framework.

### Myth 10

**“If supportive care is discussed, it means doctors have stopped caring.”**

**Fact:**  
Supportive care is an active treatment approach focused on symptoms, function, quality of life and patient goals.

+

Potential pathways:

- clinical trial;

Potential pathways:

- clinical trial;

2. The NCCN pathway includes positive peritoneal cytology.
3. “Only disease” means no documented extraperitoneal metastatic disease.
4. Systemic therapy is the initial management backbone.
5. NCCN v2.2026 specifies a minimum 3-month systemic-treatment framework before restaging.
6. Restaging evaluates both disease response and current disease distribution.
7. PCI is a structured measure of peritoneal disease burden.
8. PCI ≤10 is not, by itself, an indication for HIPEC.
9. Stable/improved disease and absence of extraperitoneal metastases are important prerequisites for the favorable branch.
10. Predicted complete cytoreduction is a major consideration.
11. Multidisciplinary consultation is central to selected local-treatment decisions.
12. Selected patients may be considered for gastrectomy with cytoreductive surgery and IC/HIPEC.
13. Continued systemic therapy and clinical trials remain important pathways.
14. High PCI, progression or extraperitoneal metastases move patients away from the favorable local-treatment branch.
15. HIPEC remains a selected, evidence-limited treatment approach rather than an automatic treatment.
