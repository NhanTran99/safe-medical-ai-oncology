After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Recognize why the relative position of two variants can matter during variant interpretation.
- Understand why BP2 may provide supporting benign evidence.
- Recognize that BP2 alone cannot determine that a variant is benign.
- Feel more confident understanding genomic laboratory reports.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is BP2?

## Patient Explanation

**BP2** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant is likely to be **benign (not disease-causing).**

BP2 represents one type of **Supporting Benign Evidence**.

It considers how two genetic variants are positioned relative to one another.

---

## Clinical Importance

BP2 provides supporting benign evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- BP2
- Supporting benign evidence
- ACMG Evidence Codes

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Do "In Cis" and "In Trans" Mean?

## Patient Explanation

Most people have **two copies** of each gene—one inherited from each parent.

If two variants are located on the **same copy** of a gene, they are described as **in cis**.

If they are located on **different copies** of the gene, they are described as **in trans**.

---

## Clinical Importance

The relative position of variants can influence how genetics professionals interpret laboratory findings.

---

## Key Concepts

- In cis
- In trans
- Gene copies

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Is BP2 Important?

## Patient Explanation

Sometimes, knowing whether two variants are **in cis** or **in trans** provides additional information about whether one of the variants is likely to be harmless.

In certain situations defined by professional guidelines, this information can contribute **supporting benign evidence**.

---

## Clinical Importance

Variant location is one factor that can contribute to evidence-based interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Variant location
- Supporting evidence
- Variant interpretation

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does BP2 Mean the Variant Is Definitely Benign?

## Patient Explanation

No.

BP2 **does not prove** that a variant is benign.

It provides one piece of supporting evidence that must always be interpreted together with many other clinical and scientific findings.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification depends on integrating multiple evidence categories.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Professional interpretation
- Benign classification

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Does the ACMG Use BP2?

## Patient Explanation

Looking only at one genetic variant is not always enough.

The ACMG framework also considers how variants relate to one another because this can improve the accuracy and consistency of variant interpretation.

---

## Clinical Importance

Considering variant context helps produce more reliable genomic interpretations.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

The position of two variants has no effect on how they are interpreted.

**Fact**

In some situations, whether two variants are **in cis** or **in trans** provides useful information that may contribute supporting benign evidence.

However, BP2 alone never determines the final classification.

---

# Key Messages

- BP2 is one Supporting Benign Evidence criterion.
- BP2 considers whether two variants are **in cis** or **in trans**.
- The relative position of variants can contribute supporting benign evidence.
- BP2 alone cannot establish benign classification.
- Final variant classification always requires integration of multiple evidence categories.
