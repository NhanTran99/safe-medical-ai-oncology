1. Explain what HER2 means in gastric adenocarcinoma.
2. Understand the relationship between the HER2 protein and the ERBB2 gene.
3. Explain why HER2 testing may be clinically important.
4. Understand when HER2 testing is recommended in the gastric-cancer pathway.
5. Understand that HER2 testing is performed on tumor tissue.
6. Understand the difference between biopsy tissue and a surgical specimen at a conceptual level.
7. Understand the role of immunohistochemistry (IHC).
8. Understand the gastric-cancer IHC categories 0, 1+, 2+, and 3+.

10. Understand that IHC 2+ is equivocal and generally requires ISH/FISH clarification.

14. Understand what HER2-positive and HER2-negative results mean clinically.

# 1. Scope

## Core

PP-0181 explains the complete patient-facing HER2 testing and interpretation pathway in gastric adenocarcinoma:

**clinical context → tumor specimen → HER2 IHC → 0/1+/2+/3+ interpretation → reflex ISH/FISH when indicated → HER2-positive/HER2-negative classification → clinical relevance and limitations.**

The package includes:

- purpose and clinical rationale of HER2 testing;
- timing of testing in the appropriate advanced/metastatic clinical setting;
- tumor tissue and specimen concepts;
- gastric-specific HER2 IHC scoring;
- IHC 0, 1+, 2+, and 3+;
- equivocal IHC 2+;
- reflex ISH/FISH;
- ERBB2 amplification;
- interpretation of positive and negative results;
- heterogeneity and specimen limitations;
- selected repeat-testing considerations;
- relationship with other biomarkers;
- predictive versus prognostic interpretation;
- patient-facing pathology terminology.

## Not Included

The package does not provide:

- detailed HER2 receptor biology;
- detailed IHC laboratory methodology;
- antibody clones or staining-platform validation;
- detailed ISH/FISH laboratory procedures;
- detailed NGS laboratory methodology;
- variant interpretation;
- treatment regimens;
- trastuzumab dosing;
- T-DXd treatment;
- HER2 resistance mechanisms;
- treatment toxicity management;
- individualized treatment recommendations;
- individualized prognosis.

# 2. Clinical Knowledge Blocks

## CKB-01 — What Is HER2?

HER2 is a protein associated with the ERBB2 gene. In gastric adenocarcinoma, increased HER2 protein expression or ERBB2 gene amplification can occur.

The important patient-facing distinction is:

- **HER2 protein expression** is primarily assessed by IHC.
- **ERBB2 gene amplification** can be assessed by ISH/FISH.

These measurements are related, but they are not literally the same test.

### Clinical importance

HER2 status can identify a molecular feature that may be relevant to HER2-directed treatment in the appropriate clinical setting.

### Key message

> HER2 testing asks whether the gastric cancer has a HER2 abnormality that may have treatment relevance.

---

## CKB-02 — Why Is HER2 Testing Done?

HER2 testing is performed because HER2 status can influence treatment options.

NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026 recommends HER2 testing for patients with gastric cancer at diagnosis when advanced/metastatic disease is documented or suspected.

The purpose is not simply to label the tumor. The result can help determine whether HER2-directed treatment may be relevant.

### Key message

> HER2 testing is a treatment-selection biomarker assessment, not merely a descriptive pathology test.

---

## CKB-03 — When Is HER2 Testing Recommended?

The project source guideline specifies HER2 testing in the context of advanced/metastatic gastric cancer at diagnosis when advanced/metastatic disease is documented or suspected.

NCCN also states that repeat biomarker testing may be considered at clinical or radiologic progression of advanced/metastatic disease.

This does not mean that every patient must undergo repeated HER2 testing automatically. Reassessment is a clinical-context decision.

### Key message

> Timing of HER2 testing follows the clinical setting; repeat testing may be considered when the disease changes or progresses.

---

## CKB-04 — What Specimen Is Used?

HER2 testing is performed on tumor tissue.

The tissue may come from:

- an endoscopic biopsy;
- a surgical/resection specimen;
- another clinically appropriate tumor specimen.

The source matters because gastric cancer can show heterogeneous HER2 expression, and a small biopsy samples only part of a tumor.

### Key message

> HER2 testing is based on tumor tissue, and the sampled tissue may not represent every part of a heterogeneous tumor.

---

## CKB-05 — Why Does Biopsy Versus Surgical Specimen Matter?

The gastric-cancer HER2 scoring framework contains specific criteria for both surgical specimens and biopsy specimens.

For surgical specimens, the scoring system incorporates the proportion of cancer cells showing the relevant membranous staining pattern.

For biopsy specimens, the framework uses clusters of cancer cells and does not simply apply the surgical-specimen percentage rule.

### Clinical importance

This is one reason why gastric HER2 interpretation should be performed using a gastric-specific framework rather than importing rules from another cancer type.

---

## CKB-06 — What Is HER2 IHC?

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) is a tissue-based test that uses staining to evaluate HER2 protein expression in tumor cells.

The gastric-cancer framework evaluates membranous immunostaining, considering:

- intensity;
- pattern;
- extent;
- proportion or cluster characteristics of immunoreactive tumor cells.

IHC produces a score from 0 to 3+.

### Key message

> IHC measures HER2 protein expression in the tumor tissue.

---

## CKB-07 — What Does IHC 0 Mean?

In the gastric scoring framework, IHC 0 is HER2-negative.

For a surgical specimen, this corresponds to no reactivity or membranous reactivity in less than 10% of cancer cells.

For a biopsy specimen, the criterion is more specific to the absence of relevant membranous reactivity in the sampled cancer cells.

### Patient interpretation

> HER2 IHC 0 means the tumor does not meet the gastric-cancer criteria for HER2 overexpression by IHC.

---

## CKB-08 — What Does IHC 1+ Mean?

IHC 1+ is also HER2-negative.

It represents faint or barely perceptible membranous reactivity under the gastric scoring framework.

The important educational point is:

> 1+ does not mean “slightly HER2-positive.”

### Key message

> HER2 IHC 1+ is classified as negative in the gastric-cancer framework.

---

## CKB-09 — What Does IHC 2+ Mean?

IHC 2+ is **equivocal**.

It is not automatically HER2-positive.

Because the IHC result is not definitive, additional ISH/FISH testing is used to clarify HER2 status.

### Key message

> IHC 2+ means “uncertain by IHC,” not “positive.”

---

## CKB-10 — What Does IHC 3+ Mean?

IHC 3+ represents strong membranous HER2 expression meeting the gastric-cancer scoring criteria.

It is classified as HER2-positive.

A positive IHC result does not generally require reflex ISH in the NCCN pathway.

### Key message

> IHC 3+ is HER2-positive under the gastric-cancer scoring framework.

---

## CKB-11 — What Is the IHC → ISH/FISH Pathway?

The core pathway is:

**HER2 IHC**

→ **0 / 1+**

→ HER2-negative

**HER2 IHC**

→ **2+**

→ equivocal

→ **ISH/FISH**

→ final HER2 classification

**HER2 IHC**

→ **3+**

→ HER2-positive

This is the most important interpretation pathway in the package.

---

## CKB-12 — What Is ISH/FISH?

In situ hybridization (ISH), including fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), evaluates the ERBB2 gene at the DNA level.

It is especially important when IHC is equivocal.

NCCN describes HER2 ISH/FISH results using the relationship between ERBB2 gene copies and chromosome 17 centromere copies (CEP17), or the average ERBB2 copy number per cell.

### Key message

> ISH/FISH helps determine whether the ERBB2 gene is amplified when IHC alone is not definitive.

---

## CKB-13 — What Makes ISH/FISH Positive?

NCCN v2.2026 identifies ISH/FISH positivity using criteria including:

- an ERBB2:CEP17 ratio ≥2, or
- average ERBB2 copy number ≥6.0 signals/cell.

These criteria are used in the relevant gastric-cancer testing framework.

Detailed laboratory signal-counting methodology is outside this package.

### Patient-facing interpretation

> A positive ISH/FISH result supports HER2-positive classification when it follows an equivocal IHC result.

---

## CKB-14 — What Does HER2-Positive Mean?

HER2-positive status can be established by:

- IHC 3+, or
- IHC 2+ followed by a positive ISH/FISH result.

HER2-positive does not mean:

- the cancer is cured;
- the cancer is automatically advanced;
- the cancer has a particular prognosis;
- one specific treatment must be given.

It means that the tumor meets the relevant HER2 criteria and may therefore be eligible for HER2-directed treatment in the appropriate clinical setting.

---

## CKB-15 — What Does HER2-Negative Mean?

HER2-negative includes:

- IHC 0;
- IHC 1+;
- appropriate ISH/FISH-negative classification following equivocal IHC.

HER2-negative means the tumor does not meet the defined criteria for HER2-positive status in the tested specimen.

It does not mean:

- no cancer is present;
- no molecular abnormalities exist;
- no other biomarkers are relevant;
- no treatment options remain.

---

## CKB-16 — What Is an Equivocal Result?

An equivocal result means that the available test does not establish HER2 status definitively.

For gastric cancer, the classic example is:

**IHC 2+ → equivocal → ISH/FISH**

The purpose of reflex testing is to resolve the uncertainty.

---

## CKB-17 — Negative Is Not the Same as Inadequate

A valid HER2-negative result is different from:

- insufficient tissue;
- inadequate tumor content;
- uninterpretable staining;
- failed assay;
- inconclusive testing;
- no HER2 test performed.

A negative result means the tested specimen was interpretable and did not meet HER2-positive criteria.

### Key message

> “HER2-negative” and “HER2 could not be reliably assessed” are not the same conclusion.

---

## CKB-18 — Why Does HER2 Heterogeneity Matter?

HER2 expression can vary within a gastric tumor.

Different regions of the tumor may show different levels or patterns of expression.

Therefore:

- a biopsy samples only a portion of the tumor;
- a resection specimen may provide more tissue;
- different specimens can occasionally yield different results.

This is one reason specimen selection and pathology interpretation matter.

---

## CKB-19 — Can HER2 Testing Be Repeated?

NCCN states that repeat biomarker testing may be considered at clinical or radiologic progression of advanced/metastatic disease.

This is not an instruction to repeat HER2 testing automatically for every patient.

The decision depends on:

- disease course;
- available tissue;
- previous testing;
- clinical question;
- whether a new result could affect management.

---

## CKB-20 — Why Can HER2 Results Differ Between Specimens?

Potential explanations include:

- tumor heterogeneity;
- sampling differences;
- biological evolution;
- technical/test-quality factors.

A changed result does not automatically mean that one laboratory made an error.

The interpretation must consider the specimen and testing context.

---

## CKB-21 — Why Is Gastric HER2 Scoring Different From Breast HER2 Scoring?

Gastric cancer has a disease-specific HER2 scoring framework.

NCCN provides separate scoring criteria for:

- surgical specimens;
- biopsy specimens.

Therefore a gastric-cancer HER2 result should be interpreted using the appropriate gastric-cancer criteria.

### Key message

> HER2 scoring is disease-specific; breast-cancer rules should not simply be transferred to gastric cancer.

---

## CKB-22 — Does Lauren Type Determine HER2 Status?

No.

HER2 positivity is reported more frequently in intestinal than diffuse gastric adenocarcinoma in the cited NCCN evidence.

However:

> intestinal type does not mean HER2-positive, and diffuse type does not mean HER2-negative.

Histologic classification and HER2 testing provide different types of information.

---

## CKB-23 — Is HER2 a Prognostic Marker?

The prognostic significance of HER2 in gastric cancer remains unclear.

NCCN notes conflicting evidence regarding whether HER2 positivity independently predicts outcome.

Therefore PP-0181 should not teach patients:

> “HER2-positive means a poor prognosis.”

The major practical role of HER2 testing is its relevance to treatment selection.

---

## CKB-24 — Predictive Versus Prognostic Meaning

A **prognostic** marker provides information about expected disease outcome.

A **predictive** marker helps identify whether a particular treatment may be relevant.

HER2 testing is clinically important because HER2-positive disease can create eligibility for HER2-directed treatment in the appropriate setting.

### Key message

> A HER2 result helps guide treatment decisions; it is not, by itself, a complete prediction of outcome.

---

## CKB-25 — Does HER2-Positive Automatically Mean One Specific Drug?

No.

A HER2-positive result is one component of treatment selection.

Actual treatment depends on:

- disease setting;
- stage/resectability;
- prior therapy;
- other biomarkers;
- guideline recommendations;
- patient factors;
- treatment availability and suitability.

Detailed treatment selection belongs to HER2-targeted therapy and drug-specific packages.

---

## CKB-26 — Is HER2 the Only Biomarker That Matters?

No.

Gastric adenocarcinoma may require assessment of several biomarkers, including HER2, MSI/MMR, PD-L1, CLDN18.2 and other molecular features depending on the clinical setting.

Therefore:

> HER2 testing does not replace the rest of the biomarker assessment.

---

## CKB-27 — Where Does NGS Fit?

NCCN states that IHC/ISH/targeted PCR is preferred for initial biomarker assessment, while validated NGS may be considered later in the clinical course when sufficient tumor tissue is available.

NGS can assess multiple mutations and other molecular events simultaneously.

For PP-0181, NGS is a contextual concept only.

Detailed NGS methodology and interpretation belong to PP-0187 and PP-0189.

---

## CKB-28 — What Will a Patient See in a HER2 Report?

Possible terminology includes:

- HER2;
- ERBB2;
- IHC;
- 0;
- 1+;
- 2+;
- 3+;
- equivocal;
- ISH;
- FISH;
- amplification;
- positive;

The report may contain more technical language, but the central clinical question is:

> **Does this tumor meet the criteria for HER2-positive status?**

# 3. Patient Explanation

## In simple terms

HER2 is a protein-related marker that can be increased in some gastric cancers. Doctors can test a tumor sample to see whether the cancer has increased HER2 expression or an associated ERBB2 gene abnormality.

Usually, the first test is an IHC test.

The result is reported on a scale from 0 to 3+:

- **0 or 1+ → HER2-negative**
- **2+ → uncertain/equivocal, so another test called ISH/FISH is used**
- **3+ → HER2-positive**

If a tumor is HER2-positive, this may open the possibility of HER2-directed treatment when that treatment is appropriate for the patient's disease setting.

A HER2 result is only one part of the overall cancer assessment. It does not by itself determine the exact treatment or predict the patient's entire prognosis.

# 4. Clinical Importance

HER2 testing connects pathology with precision treatment.

The sequence is:

**Tumor tissue**

→ **HER2 testing**

→ **HER2 status**

→ **treatment relevance**

The quality of this pathway depends on:

- appropriate specimen;
- disease-specific scoring;
- appropriate reflex testing;
- correct interpretation;
- integration with the broader clinical picture.

# 6. Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1 — “HER2 2+ means HER2-positive.”

**Fact:** IHC 2+ is equivocal and requires ISH/FISH clarification.

### Myth 2 — “HER2 1+ means weakly positive.”

**Fact:** IHC 1+ is classified as HER2-negative in the gastric-cancer scoring framework.

### Myth 3 — “HER2-positive means IHC 3+ only.”

**Fact:** HER2 positivity can also be established when IHC is 2+ and ISH/FISH is positive.

### Myth 4 — “HER2-negative means HER2 was not tested.”

**Fact:** A valid negative result means the tested specimen was interpretable and did not meet positive criteria.

### Myth 5 — “HER2-positive means the patient will definitely receive trastuzumab.”

**Fact:** Treatment depends on the clinical setting and broader treatment criteria.

### Myth 6 — “HER2-positive means the cancer has a poor prognosis.”

**Fact:** The prognostic significance of HER2 in gastric cancer remains uncertain.

### Myth 7 — “HER2 testing is the same in gastric and breast cancer.”

**Fact:** Gastric cancer has its own HER2 scoring framework.

### Myth 8 — “A biopsy always represents the HER2 status of the whole tumor.”

**Fact:** Tumors can be heterogeneous and a biopsy samples only part of the tumor.

### Myth 9 — “A negative HER2 result means no other biomarker testing is needed.”

**Fact:** HER2 is only one component of gastric-cancer biomarker assessment.

### Myth 10 — “NGS always makes IHC/ISH unnecessary.”

**Fact:** NCCN identifies IHC/ISH/targeted PCR as preferred initial approaches, with validated NGS used as appropriate later in the clinical course.

### Myth 11 — “HER2-positive means the cancer is curable.”

**Fact:** HER2 status is a biomarker result, not a statement about cure.

### Myth 12 — “ERBB2 amplification and HER2 protein overexpression are exactly the same measurement.”

**Fact:** They are biologically related but assessed at different molecular levels.

### Myth 13 — “A HER2-negative result can never be different later.”

**Fact:** Repeat testing may be considered at progression in selected advanced/metastatic cases.

### Myth 14 — “If a HER2 test fails, the result is negative.”

**Fact:** An inadequate or uninterpretable test is different from a valid negative result.

### Myth 15 — “HER2 determines the whole treatment plan.”

**Fact:** Treatment decisions integrate HER2 with stage, other biomarkers, prior treatment, clinical factors and guideline recommendations.
