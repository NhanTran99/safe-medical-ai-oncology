# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **When can endoscopic resection be used to treat early gastric cancer, and how is its curative role determined?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a patient-facing, source-grounded framework explaining:

1. why endoscopic resection can be definitive treatment for selected early gastric cancers;
2. why not every early gastric cancer is appropriate for ER;
3. how depth, lymph-node risk, histology, size and ulceration influence selection;
4. the strategic roles of EMR and ESD;
5. why ESD is preferred in the current NCCN framework;
6. why the resection specimen is essential for curative assessment;
7. why a non-curative result can lead to additional treatment;
8. why surveillance remains necessary.

The package deliberately stops before detailed procedural technique, detailed pathology methodology, and individualized treatment.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Disease-Specific Guideline

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

NCCN is the highest-priority disease-specific evidence source for this PP.

The relevant framework establishes that:

- ER (EMR or ESD) is a primary treatment option for selected early-stage Tis/T1a tumors;
- ESD is preferred in the ER framework;
- accurate clinical staging is especially important when ER is being considered;
- the resection specimen should be assessed by an experienced GI pathologist;
- curative assessment incorporates size, invasion depth, differentiation, margins and lymphovascular invasion in the relevant framework;
- post-ER surveillance is required.

---

## Level I / Patient-Facing Clinical Evidence

### NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ

NCI supports:

- EMR in selected early-stage tumors;
- favorable-risk patterns including Tis/T1a, small size and predominantly differentiated histology without ulcerative findings in the cited experience;
- lower nodal risk for intramucosal tumors compared with submucosal tumors;
- careful patient selection;
- experienced endoscopist;
- close surveillance.

NCI also describes historical Asian experience and clinical outcomes from selected EMR cohorts.

# Clinical Claims Summary

## Claim 1 — ER is a treatment, not merely a biopsy

The evidence supports ER as a therapeutic option in selected early gastric cancers.

The package therefore frames ER as an oncologic treatment with a defined eligibility logic.

## Claim 2 — Early-stage disease is necessary but not sufficient

Tis/T1a status places the tumor within the central ER framework, but other features remain relevant.

## Claim 3 — Depth of invasion matters because nodal risk changes with deeper invasion

NCI specifically distinguishes intramucosal from submucosal nodal risk.

This provides the biological rationale for why ER selection is restrictive.

## Claim 4 — Pathology after ER is central

The resection specimen provides the information needed to determine whether the lesion meets curative criteria.

## Claim 5 — ESD is preferred in the current NCCN framework

This is a current guideline-supported statement.

It should not be converted into a universal statement that ESD is appropriate for every patient.

## Claim 6 — Non-curative ER is a decision point

A non-curative result signals that endoscopic treatment alone may not be sufficient and that additional management may need to be considered.

## Claim 7 — Surveillance remains part of the treatment pathway

Curative ER does not eliminate the need for follow-up.

# Evidence Notes

## Note 1 — Classic favorable-risk EMR pattern

NCI describes historical EMR experience in early-stage tumors with:

- Tis/T1a disease;
- diameter ≤2 cm;
- predominantly differentiated type;
- no ulcerative findings.

These characteristics should be treated as evidence context rather than as a universal self-assessment checklist.

---

## Note 2 — Expanded endoscopic indications

The NCCN framework contains selected expanded indications involving superficial submucosal invasion and other combinations of histology, size and ulceration.

Because these criteria are clinically nuanced, this PP explains the concept but deliberately does not turn the package into a patient-applied eligibility calculator.

---

## Note 3 — ESD preference

NCCN currently prefers ESD in the ER framework.

The evidence and guideline discussion also acknowledge greater technical demands and procedural risks.

Therefore the educational statement is:

> ESD is preferred within the current framework, but the appropriate modality remains dependent on lesion characteristics, expertise and clinical context.

---

## Note 4 — Final pathology is decisive

Pre-treatment biopsy and endoscopic appearance cannot fully replace the information obtained from the resection specimen.

The final pathology establishes whether the assumptions that supported local treatment remain valid.

---

## Note 5 — ER and surgery are not interchangeable

ER and gastrectomy represent different oncologic strategies.

ER is appropriate when the risk of residual/nodal disease is sufficiently low for local treatment.

Gastrectomy provides surgical removal and regional lymph-node assessment when greater oncologic control is required.

# Evidence Consistency Review

## NCCN and NCI

The two sources are complementary.

NCCN provides the current guideline framework.

NCI provides patient-facing explanation and historical clinical evidence, including Asian EMR experience and the importance of careful selection.

No material conflict was identified for the locked PP scope.

## Guideline Versus Modality-Specific Detail

NCCN provides sufficient evidence for the strategic distinction between EMR and ESD.

Detailed procedural technique is not required to answer the PP's atomic educational question and is therefore delegated.

## ER Versus Surgery

The source set supports the conceptual distinction between local endoscopic treatment and surgical treatment.

Detailed surgical technique and treatment algorithms remain outside PP-0193.

# Evidence Gaps

1. No single source provides a universal patient-facing ER eligibility calculator.
2. Exact eligibility depends on the interaction of multiple tumor and pathology features.
3. Exact procedural risks vary by lesion, technique, operator and center.
4. Detailed modality-specific outcomes depend on lesion characteristics and healthcare setting.
5. Expanded indications require expert interpretation and should not be converted into simplified universal rules.
6. Detailed pathology interpretation is outside this package.
7. Detailed post-ER surveillance and recurrence algorithms are outside this package.
8. Individualized treatment decisions cannot be made from the population-level source set alone.

These gaps do not block the PP because the locked scope is strategic and patient-facing rather than individualized or procedural.
