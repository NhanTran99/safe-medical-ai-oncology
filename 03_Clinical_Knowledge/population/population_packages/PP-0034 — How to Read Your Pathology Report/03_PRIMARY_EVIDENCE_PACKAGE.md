# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **How should patients read and understand a pathology report?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of how to navigate a pathology report, understand its general structure, recognize its most important sections, and use it effectively during discussions with their healthcare team.

This Population Package focuses on **pathology report literacy** rather than pathology interpretation.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Purpose of a pathology report
- General report structure
- Common report sections
- Final Diagnosis section
- Comments and supplementary reports
- Report updates
- Patient communication
- Questions patients may ask

---

## Excluded

- Interpretation of histologic subtype
- Tumor grading
- Lauren classification
- WHO classification
- TNM staging
- HER2 interpretation
- MSI interpretation
- PD-L1 interpretation
- Molecular pathology
- Treatment recommendations

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Pathology reports communicate the results of microscopic tissue examination.
- Most pathology reports contain a structured set of sections with specific purposes.
- The Final Diagnosis generally provides the principal pathological conclusion.
- Additional laboratory studies may require subsequent report updates.
- Pathology findings must be interpreted within the overall clinical context.
- Healthcare professionals should explain pathology reports to patients before major medical decisions are made.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the purpose of pathology reports;
- the role of pathologists;
- the importance of Final Diagnosis;
- integration of pathology with the broader clinical assessment;
- physician-guided interpretation.

Differences between references are limited to educational wording and presentation style.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

- staging;
- biomarker interpretation;
- molecular pathology;
- treatment planning.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
