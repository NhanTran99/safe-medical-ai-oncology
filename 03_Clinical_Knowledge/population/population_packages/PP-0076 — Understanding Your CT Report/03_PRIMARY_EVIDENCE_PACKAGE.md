# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **How should I understand my CT report?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **the purpose and role of a CT report**, including who writes it, how it is used by the healthcare team, why it should be interpreted within the complete clinical context, and why patients should discuss the report with their treating clinician rather than attempting to interpret it independently.

This Population Package introduces the role of the CT report without explaining radiology terminology, staging, treatment decisions, or individual imaging findings.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of a CT report
- Role of the radiologist
- Purpose of the report
- Clinical use of imaging findings
- Integration with other clinical information
- Appropriate patient interpretation

---

## Excluded

- Individual radiology terminology
- Interpretation of specific CT findings
- Cancer staging
- Treatment recommendations
- Comparison of serial CT reports
- Artificial intelligence interpretation
- Second-opinion imaging review

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- CT reports summarize the radiologist's interpretation of CT images.
- CT reports are communication tools within the healthcare team.
- Imaging findings represent only one component of comprehensive clinical evaluation.
- Medical decisions require integration of multiple sources of clinical information.
- Professional explanation improves patient understanding and reduces misinterpretation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the professional role of the radiologist;
- the purpose of radiology reports;
- multidisciplinary interpretation of imaging findings;
- the importance of discussing imaging reports with treating clinicians.

Differences between references primarily concern reporting style and institutional workflow, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- oncology recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- radiology communication principles from ACR and RSNA.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- interpretation of individual CT findings;
- radiology terminology;
- TNM staging;
- treatment planning;
- second-opinion imaging review;
- AI-assisted interpretation.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
