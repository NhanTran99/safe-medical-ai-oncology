# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what a CT report is.
- Know who writes a CT report.
- Understand the purpose of the CT report in cancer care.
- Recognize that a CT report is only one part of the overall clinical assessment.
- Understand why CT reports should not be interpreted without professional medical guidance.
- Feel confident discussing CT results with their healthcare team.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is a CT Report?

## Patient Explanation

A **CT report** is a medical document written after a radiologist carefully reviews your CT images.

The report describes what the radiologist observed during the examination.

---

## Clinical Importance

The CT report communicates imaging findings to the healthcare professionals responsible for your care.

---

## Key Concepts

- Medical document.
- Based on CT images.
- Written after image review.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Who Writes the CT Report?

## Patient Explanation

A **radiologist** is a doctor who specializes in interpreting medical images.

After reviewing your CT scan, the radiologist prepares a report describing the imaging findings.

---

## Clinical Importance

Radiologists provide expert interpretation that supports diagnosis and treatment planning.

---

## Key Concepts

- Radiologist.
- Image interpretation.
- Professional medical report.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Is the Purpose of the CT Report?

## Patient Explanation

The CT report helps your healthcare team understand what was seen on the scan.

It provides important information that may help answer the clinical question for which the CT scan was performed.

---

## Clinical Importance

The CT report supports communication between specialists involved in patient care.

---

## Key Concepts

- Communication tool.
- Imaging findings.
- Clinical support.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Is the CT Report My Final Diagnosis?

## Patient Explanation

Not usually.

Your doctor considers the CT report together with:

- your symptoms;
- physical examination;
- laboratory tests;
- pathology results (if available);
- other imaging studies;
- your medical history.

Only after considering all of this information can your healthcare team determine what the CT findings mean for you.

---

## Clinical Importance

Clinical decisions are based on comprehensive evaluation rather than imaging findings alone.

---

## Key Concepts

- One part of assessment.
- Clinical context.
- Comprehensive evaluation.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — Should I Interpret My CT Report by Myself?

## Patient Explanation

CT reports often contain technical medical language.

Reading the report on your own may lead to unnecessary worry or misunderstanding.

If you have questions, ask your healthcare team to explain the report in the context of your overall health.

---

## Clinical Importance

Professional interpretation improves patient understanding and reduces misinterpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- Technical language.
- Ask your doctor.
- Avoid self-diagnosis.

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

Everything written in a CT report is my final diagnosis.

**Fact**

A CT report describes imaging findings. Your healthcare team combines the report with many other clinical factors before making diagnostic or treatment decisions.

---

# Key Messages

- A CT report is written by a radiologist.
- It summarizes what is seen on the CT images.
- The report is one part of your overall medical evaluation.
- Do not interpret the report on your own.
- Discuss your CT report with your healthcare team to understand what it means for your situation.
