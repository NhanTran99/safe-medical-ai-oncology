After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Explain what an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) is.

- Understand the role of ADCs in modern gastric cancer treatment.
- Recognize the general benefits and limitations of ADC therapy.
- Understand why treatment decisions should always be individualized.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Are Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)?

## Patient Explanation

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are a newer type of targeted cancer treatment.

An ADC combines a targeted antibody with a cancer-fighting medicine.

The antibody helps guide the treatment toward cancer cells with a specific biomarker, while reducing exposure to many normal cells.

---

## Clinical Importance

ADCs represent an important advancement in precision oncology.

---

## Key Concepts

- Targeted treatment
- Combines two treatment approaches
- Precision medicine

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Are the Main Parts of an ADC?

## Patient Explanation

Although different ADCs vary, they all contain three basic parts:

- an antibody that recognizes a specific target;
- a connector that joins the components together;
- a cancer-fighting medicine.

Patients do not need to understand the technical details to understand how ADC treatment is used.

---

## Clinical Importance

These three components work together to deliver targeted therapy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Three-part medicine
- Designed for targeted delivery
- Different ADCs share the same general concept

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — How Do ADCs Work?

## Patient Explanation

An ADC first recognizes cancer cells carrying a specific target.

It then delivers treatment mainly to those cancer cells.

This approach is intended to make treatment more precise than conventional chemotherapy.

The detailed biological mechanisms are discussed in separate Population Packages.

---

## Clinical Importance

ADCs combine targeted treatment with cancer medicine in a single therapeutic strategy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Targets specific cancer cells
- Delivers treatment more precisely
- Supports personalized cancer care

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Why Is Biomarker Testing Important?

## Patient Explanation

Many ADCs only work when the cancer has a specific biomarker.

Before treatment is considered, doctors usually perform biomarker testing to determine whether the tumor has the required target.

Without the correct biomarker, an ADC may not be appropriate.

---

## Clinical Importance

Biomarker testing is essential for selecting appropriate ADC therapy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Test before treatment
- Biomarker-guided therapy
- Personalized treatment

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — What Is the Role of ADCs in Gastric Cancer?

## Patient Explanation

ADCs have become an important addition to treatment options for selected patients with gastric adenocarcinoma.

They expand the number of precision treatments available when appropriate according to current clinical guidelines.

Not every patient will require or benefit from an ADC.

---

## Clinical Importance

ADCs continue to expand personalized treatment options in gastric cancer.

---

## Key Concepts

- Additional treatment option
- Precision oncology
- Individualized care

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — What Are the Benefits and Limitations?

## Patient Explanation

Potential benefits include:

- more personalized treatment;
- additional treatment options;
- targeted delivery of therapy.

However, ADCs also have limitations.

They require appropriate biomarkers and may still cause side effects that require monitoring.

---

## Clinical Importance

Every treatment requires balancing potential benefits and risks.

---

## Key Concepts

- Benefits vary between patients
- Biomarker-dependent
- Requires monitoring

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Common Questions About ADC Therapy

## Patient Explanation

Some patients believe ADCs have no side effects because they are targeted treatments.

This is incorrect.

Although ADCs are designed to target cancer cells more precisely, they can still cause important side effects.

Your healthcare team will monitor treatment carefully.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

Targeted treatment means there are no side effects.

**Fact**

All cancer treatments, including ADCs, may cause side effects and require appropriate monitoring.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Should Patients Expect?

Patients considering ADC therapy should discuss:

- biomarker test results;
- expected benefits;
- possible risks;
- treatment goals;
- alternative treatment options.

Treatment decisions should always be made together with the healthcare team.

---

## Clinical Importance

Patient education supports informed shared decision-making.

---

# Key Messages

- ADCs are a newer form of targeted cancer treatment.
- ADCs combine an antibody with a cancer-fighting medicine.
- Biomarker testing is usually required before treatment.
- ADCs provide additional precision treatment options for selected patients.
- ADCs still have potential side effects and require monitoring.
- Patients should discuss treatment options with their healthcare team.
