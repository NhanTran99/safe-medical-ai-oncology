# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is Targeted Therapy?

## Patient Explanation

Targeted therapy is a type of cancer treatment that acts on specific molecules or biological pathways that help cancer cells grow and survive.

Unlike traditional chemotherapy, targeted therapy is designed to act on particular features of cancer cells rather than affecting all rapidly dividing cells.

Not every gastric cancer has these targets. Therefore, targeted therapy is appropriate only for selected patients.

---

## Clinical Importance

Targeted therapy is an important component of precision medicine and is recommended only when an appropriate treatment target has been identified.

---

## Key Concepts

- Targeted therapy acts on specific cancer targets.
- It is different from chemotherapy.
- Not every patient is eligible.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — Why Is Biomarker Testing Needed?

## Patient Explanation

Before targeted therapy is considered, doctors usually perform biomarker testing on the tumor.

These tests look for specific biological features that indicate whether a targeted medicine is likely to work.

If the required target is not present, the treatment is unlikely to provide benefit.

---

## Clinical Importance

Biomarker testing guides appropriate patient selection and helps avoid ineffective treatment.

---

## Key Concepts

- Biomarker testing comes first.
- Treatment depends on test results.
- Different biomarkers guide different therapies.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Common Biomarkers

## Patient Explanation

Several biomarkers are currently used in the treatment of gastric adenocarcinoma.

Examples include:

- HER2
- CLDN18.2
- VEGFR2-related pathways

Each biomarker identifies a different group of patients who may benefit from specific targeted medicines.

Patients do not need to remember these names, but they should understand that testing helps doctors choose the most appropriate treatment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Modern gastric cancer treatment increasingly relies on biomarker-directed therapy.

---

## Key Concepts

- Different biomarkers exist.
- Different biomarkers lead to different treatments.
- Testing is essential.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — When May Targeted Therapy Be Used?

## Patient Explanation

Targeted therapy is recommended only in certain clinical situations.

It may be used:

- together with chemotherapy;
- after previous treatments in selected patients;
- according to current clinical guidelines and biomarker results.

Not everyone with gastric cancer requires targeted therapy.

---

## Clinical Importance

Treatment recommendations depend on disease stage, previous treatments and biomarker status.

---

## Key Concepts

- Selected patients only.
- Biomarker-directed treatment.
- Individualized treatment planning.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — How Is Targeted Therapy Different From Chemotherapy?

## Patient Explanation

Chemotherapy attacks rapidly growing cells throughout the body.

Targeted therapy works differently.

Instead of attacking all rapidly dividing cells, it focuses on specific biological targets found in certain cancer cells.

Because of this difference, targeted therapy may produce a different pattern of side effects.

---

## Clinical Importance

Understanding the distinction between systemic chemotherapy and biomarker-directed therapy helps patients understand modern treatment strategies.

---

## Key Concepts

- Different mechanism.
- Different patient selection.
- Different side-effect profile.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — Benefits and Limitations

## Expected Benefits

For appropriate patients, targeted therapy may:

- improve treatment effectiveness;
- prolong survival;
- help control cancer for longer;
- work together with other cancer treatments.

Benefits vary among individuals.

---

## Limitations

Targeted therapy is not suitable for every patient.

Some tumors do not have the required biomarker.

Even when treatment is initially effective, cancer may eventually become resistant.

Treatment decisions therefore need regular reassessment.

---

## Clinical Importance

Patient selection is essential to maximize benefit while avoiding unnecessary treatment.

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Common Side Effects

## Patient Explanation

Different targeted medicines cause different side effects.

Possible side effects may include:

- fatigue;
- diarrhea;
- skin changes;
- high blood pressure with some medicines;
- infusion-related reactions.

Many side effects can be monitored and treated by the healthcare team.

---

## Common Misconception

**Myth**

Targeted therapy has no side effects.

**Fact**

Targeted therapy usually has a different side-effect profile from chemotherapy, but it can still cause important adverse effects that require monitoring.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Should Patients Expect?

Patients receiving targeted therapy are monitored regularly.

Doctors evaluate:

- treatment response;
- side effects;
- laboratory results when necessary;
- overall health.

Treatment may be continued, changed or stopped according to the patient's response and tolerance.

---

## Clinical Importance

Regular follow-up helps ensure that treatment remains both effective and safe.

---

# Key Messages

- Targeted therapy attacks specific biological targets in cancer cells.
- Not every patient is eligible for targeted therapy.
- Biomarker testing is required before treatment selection.
- Targeted therapy is different from chemotherapy.
- Treatment decisions are individualized.
- Side effects differ among medicines and are often manageable.
- Ongoing monitoring is an important part of treatment.
