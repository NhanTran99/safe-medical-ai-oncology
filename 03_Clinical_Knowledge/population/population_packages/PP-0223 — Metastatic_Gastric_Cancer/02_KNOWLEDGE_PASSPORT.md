## Intended Runtime Usage

PP-0223 is intended to support:

- patient education about Stage IV/M1 gastric cancer;
- explanation of the meaning of distant metastatic disease;
- orientation before oncology consultations;
- explanation of why imaging, biopsy and biomarkers may be needed;
- explanation of why treatment differs between patients;
- retrieval of the high-level metastatic-management framework;
- safe routing to downstream therapy-specific and supportive-care PPs.

It is not intended to provide individualized treatment recommendations.

## Clinical Scope

### Core

**Core =** Metastatic gastric cancer as a distinct clinical disease state; Stage IV/M1 concept; distinction between distant metastatic disease and local/locoregional disease; metastatic distribution and burden; high-level characterization and confirmation of metastatic disease; patient fitness and performance status; nutritional and symptom context; tumor-biology and biomarker context; systemic therapy as the principal disease-directed strategy at architectural level; selected local interventions and clinical-trial context; treatment goals; reassessment/progression concepts; integration of supportive and palliative care; patient-facing explanation; misconceptions; prognosis concepts without individualized prediction.

### Supporting

Necessary contextual material includes metastatic-site orientation, imaging and biopsy interfaces, biomarkers, prior treatment, nutrition, symptoms, quality of life, MDT context and selected local-intervention concepts.

### Not Included

Detailed treatment regimens, treatment-specific algorithms, technical testing methodology, response criteria, detailed imaging protocols, organ-specific metastatic management, peritoneal-specific treatment, HIPEC/PIPAC, detailed palliative/supportive-care protocols, toxicity management and individualized decisions.

## Knowledge Units

### KU-01 — Definition
Metastatic gastric cancer is gastric cancer with distant spread.

### KU-02 — Stage IV / M1
M1 disease corresponds to Stage IV in the TNM framework.

### KU-03 — Disease Characterization
Metastatic work-up establishes disease distribution, burden and clinically relevant features.

### KU-04 — Patient Fitness
Performance status, nutrition, organ function, symptoms and comorbidity affect treatment feasibility.

### KU-05 — Tumor Biology
HER2, PD-L1, MSI/MMR, CLDN18.2 and potentially other molecular findings can influence treatment options.

### KU-06 — Systemic Treatment
Systemic therapy is central for many patients with unresectable/metastatic disease when local therapy is not the primary strategy.

### KU-07 — Selected Local Treatment
Local interventions may be used for specific symptoms, complications or highly selected disease patterns.

### KU-08 — Clinical Trials
Clinical trials are part of the treatment landscape.

### KU-09 — Longitudinal Management
Treatment is reassessed and adapted according to response, progression, toxicity, patient fitness and goals.

### KU-10 — Supportive and Palliative Care
Supportive and palliative care can accompany active treatment.

### KU-11 — Prognosis
M1 status is prognostically important but does not determine an individual's outcome by itself.

### KU-12 — Patient Safety
Population-level information must not be converted into individualized treatment advice.

## Evidence Classification

### Established / guideline-supported

- Stage IV is defined by M1 disease.
- Metastatic gastric cancer requires characterization of distant disease.
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis is central to the metastatic work-up.
- FDG-PET/CT may be clinically indicated.
- Biopsy of metastatic disease may be clinically indicated.
- HER2, PD-L1, MSI/MMR and CLDN18.2 testing are relevant in advanced/metastatic disease.
- NGS should be considered via a validated assay according to NCCN.
- Systemic therapy is a central pathway for appropriate patients.
- Best supportive care is an explicit management option.
- Peritoneal-only disease has a dedicated pathway.

### Context-dependent

- Local treatment of selected metastatic sites.
- Radiation or procedures for symptoms/complications.
- Metastasectomy or ablation in selected circumstances.
- Clinical-trial selection.
- Intensity and sequencing of systemic treatment.

### Update-sensitive / emerging

- Molecular monitoring strategies.
- ctDNA-guided treatment adaptation.
- Oligometastatic approaches.
- New biomarker-directed therapies and combinations.

## Patient Journey Classification — Detailed

### Trigger

Metastatic disease is suspected or established during:

- initial staging;
- post-treatment evaluation;
- recurrence work-up;
- evaluation of new symptoms;
- imaging performed for another clinical reason.

### Immediate Knowledge Need

The patient needs to understand:

- what “metastatic” means;
- where disease is located;
- whether it is confirmed;
- why additional tests are needed.

### Decision Interface

The care team then integrates:

**Disease map + tumor biology + patient fitness + prior treatment + goals.**

### Longitudinal Interface

After treatment begins:

**response → stability/progression → treatment continuation/change → supportive care**

## Knowledge Unit Expansion

### KU-13 — Metastatic versus recurrent disease

Recurrence describes cancer returning after a period in which it was not clinically evident. Metastatic disease describes distant spread.

A recurrent gastric cancer can be metastatic.

A newly diagnosed gastric cancer can also be metastatic.

Therefore recurrence and metastasis are related but not synonymous concepts.

### KU-14 — Unresectable versus metastatic

A cancer can be unresectable because of local extent without necessarily having distant metastasis.

Metastatic disease is defined by distant spread.

The two concepts may coexist, but they should not be used interchangeably.

### KU-15 — Why disease distribution matters

Different metastatic distributions can create different symptoms, complications and treatment considerations.

This is why a complete disease map is more informative than the Stage IV label alone.

### KU-16 — Why prior therapy matters

A metastatic presentation after previous perioperative treatment is clinically different from a de novo metastatic presentation.

Prior exposure affects:

- treatment choices;
- cumulative toxicity;
- resistance considerations;
- expected benefit of previously used agents.

### KU-17 — Why performance status matters

Treatment benefit depends partly on whether the patient can tolerate treatment.

Functional status therefore belongs in the metastatic-disease framework.

### KU-18 — Why biomarker testing matters

Modern advanced gastric-cancer treatment is partly biomarker-informed. Testing may identify biological characteristics relevant to therapy.

This does not mean that every biomarker has the same clinical role.

### KU-19 — Why clinical trials matter

Clinical trials may offer a treatment option when standard choices are limited or may evaluate newer strategies.

Trial participation remains voluntary and requires individual eligibility assessment.

### KU-20 — Why supportive care remains active

Metastatic disease can produce symptoms and treatment can produce toxicity.

Supportive care addresses both disease-related and treatment-related needs.
