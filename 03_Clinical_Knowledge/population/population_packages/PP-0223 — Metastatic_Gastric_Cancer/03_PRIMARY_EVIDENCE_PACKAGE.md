# Primary Evidence Package — PP-0223 Metastatic Gastric Cancer

## Identity

- PP ID: PP-0223
- Clinical Question: What is metastatic gastric cancer, how is it characterized, and how is it managed as a clinical disease state?
- Version: 1.0.0

## Clinical Question

The package addresses metastatic gastric cancer as a distinct clinical state rather than as a simple list of metastatic organs. It explains the relationship between M1/Stage IV disease, metastatic distribution, patient fitness, tumor biology, systemic treatment, selected local treatment, clinical trials and supportive/palliative care.

## Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

### A. Stage IV / M1

NCI PDQ defines Stage IV as Any T, Any N, M1. This provides the cleanest foundational definition for the package.

The clinical implication is that distant spread is established. However, the stage label alone does not describe metastatic burden, symptoms, biomarkers or fitness.

### B. Metastatic work-up

NCCN v2.2026 places chest/abdomen/pelvis CT, with FDG-PET/CT when clinically indicated, within the assessment of advanced/metastatic disease. Biopsy of metastatic disease is listed when clinically indicated.

The work-up therefore serves two purposes:

1. establish the extent of disease;
2. provide clinically relevant information for treatment planning.

### C. Biomarker characterization

NCCN v2.2026 specifically identifies HER2, PD-L1, MSI/MMR and CLDN18.2 testing as relevant when advanced/metastatic disease is documented or suspected, if not previously performed, and recommends considering NGS via a validated assay.

This supports the PP's statement that metastatic disease management is increasingly biology-informed.

### D. Performance status

NCCN GAST-9 differentiates palliative-management pathways by performance status. This supports inclusion of patient fitness as a core management variable.

Performance status should be explained as functional context rather than as a numerical label that independently dictates treatment.

### E. Systemic therapy

NCCN GAST-9 places systemic therapy within palliative management for unresectable locally advanced or metastatic disease in appropriate patients. NCI PDQ similarly describes cytotoxic, targeted and immunotherapy options for Stage IV/inoperable/recurrent disease.

The evidence supports a high-level statement that systemic therapy is central. It does not justify reproducing every regimen in PP-0223 because those are owned by downstream treatment packages.

### F. Peritoneal disease

NCCN GAST-10 creates a distinct pathway for peritoneal carcinoma as only disease. This is a direct architecture signal that PP-0223 should mention peritoneal metastasis but delegate detailed peritoneal management.

### G. Supportive and palliative care

NCCN GAST-9 includes best supportive care. NCI describes palliative locoregional treatment options. Therefore supportive/palliative care should remain visible in the metastatic-disease framework.

## Clinical Claims Summary

1. Metastatic gastric cancer is gastric cancer with distant spread.
2. M1 disease corresponds to Stage IV.
3. Metastatic disease requires characterization beyond a stage label.
4. Imaging and, when indicated, metastatic-site biopsy establish disease extent.
5. Biomarker characterization is clinically relevant.
6. Patient fitness and performance status influence treatment feasibility.
7. Systemic therapy is central for many patients with metastatic disease.
8. Selected local interventions can have symptom-directed or highly selected roles.
9. Clinical trials may be appropriate.
10. Supportive and palliative care remain important.
11. Detailed treatment selection belongs to therapy-specific packages.
12. Individual prognosis cannot be inferred from M1 status alone.

## Evidence Consistency Review

The principal sources are concordant on the following architecture:

**Metastatic disease → characterize disease → assess patient → characterize tumor → select treatment → reassess → adapt/support.**

The NCCN 2025 and 2026 materials are also consistent on the central role of systemic therapy and best supportive care, while the 2026 version provides the current project reference.

## Evidence Limitations

- Metastatic gastric cancer is biologically and clinically heterogeneous.
- The same M1 label can represent very different disease burdens.
- Local-treatment evidence is context-dependent.
- Treatment regimens and biomarker-directed options evolve rapidly.
- Organ-specific metastatic evidence differs by site.
- Patient-level treatment selection cannot be safely derived from population-level package content.

## Evidence Gaps

- Universal criteria for oligometastatic gastric cancer are not established in the supplied core materials.
- ctDNA-guided treatment adaptation remains update-sensitive and is not a core requirement of this package.
- Universal metastasectomy rules are not supported.
- A single prognostic model cannot represent all metastatic presentations.

## Evidence-to-Patient Translation

### What does “metastatic” mean?

It means the stomach cancer has spread to a distant part of the body.

### Why are more tests needed after metastasis is found?

Because the team needs to know how much disease is present, where it is, what the tumor's biological features are, and whether the patient is fit enough for particular treatments.

### Why can two people with Stage IV disease receive different treatments?

Because their disease distribution, biomarkers, previous treatment, symptoms, general health and goals may differ.

### Does treatment still have a purpose?

Yes. Treatment may aim to control cancer, relieve symptoms, preserve function, prolong life and support quality of life.

## Clinical Reasoning Chain

The evidence supports the following non-prescriptive chain:

**M1 / Stage IV**
→ **confirm and map disease**
→ **assess patient fitness**
→ **characterize tumor biology**
→ **review prior treatment**
→ **select appropriate management**
→ **reassess disease and patient**
→ **adapt treatment and supportive care**

Each arrow represents a clinical decision domain, not an automatic algorithm.

## Why Regimen-Level Detail Is Delegated

The supplied NCI PDQ contains specific regimen examples for Stage IV disease. Those details are clinically useful, but including them comprehensively in PP-0223 would create substantive duplication with PP-0208 and therapy-specific PPs.

The Gold architecture therefore preserves the distinction:

**PP-0223 = metastatic disease-state framework**

**PP-0208 and therapy PPs = detailed treatment ownership**

This is an architectural decision, not an evidence gap.

## Evidence Gaps — Expanded

### Gap 1 — Oligometastatic disease

The supplied core materials do not establish a single universal oligometastatic treatment algorithm.

### Gap 2 — Metastasectomy

No universal metastasectomy rule should be derived from the disease-state package.

### Gap 3 — Molecular monitoring

ctDNA and related longitudinal strategies are update-sensitive and should not be converted into a standard metastatic-management requirement without dedicated evidence.

### Gap 4 — Organ-specific management

The project deliberately distributes organ-specific knowledge across dedicated packages.
