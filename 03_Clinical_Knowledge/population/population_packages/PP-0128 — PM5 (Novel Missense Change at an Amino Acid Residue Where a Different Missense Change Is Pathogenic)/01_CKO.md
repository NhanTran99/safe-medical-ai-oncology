# Educational Objectives

After reading this Population Package, the reader should be able to:

- Understand what PM5 is.
- Understand the basic concept of amino acid positions in proteins.
- Recognize that PM5 is one individual ACMG Evidence Code within the PM evidence-code family.
- Understand why a new missense variant occurring at the same amino acid position as a known pathogenic missense variant may provide moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity.
- Recognize that different amino acid substitutions at the same position may have different biological effects.
- Understand that PM5 contributes one category of evidence rather than determining the final variant classification.

# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is PM5?

## Patient Explanation

**PM5** is one of the standardized **ACMG Evidence Codes** used by genetics professionals when evaluating whether a genetic variant may cause disease.

It belongs to the **PM (Moderate Pathogenic Evidence)** family and represents one specific type of **moderate scientific evidence**.

---

## Clinical Importance

PM5 contributes one category of moderate evidence during professional variant interpretation.

---

## Key Concepts

- PM5
- Moderate evidence
- ACMG Evidence Code

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Is an Amino Acid Position?

## Patient Explanation

Proteins are built from many **amino acids** arranged in a specific order.

Each amino acid occupies a particular **position** within the protein.

Sometimes, that specific position is especially important for the protein to function normally.

---

## Clinical Importance

The location of an amino acid change can influence how a genetic variant is interpreted.

---

## Key Concepts

- Amino acid
- Protein
- Amino acid position

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — Why Does PM5 Matter?

## Patient Explanation

Sometimes researchers already know that **one missense variant** at a particular amino acid position causes disease.

If a **different missense variant** is later found at the **same amino acid position**, this information may provide **moderate scientific evidence** that the new variant also deserves careful evaluation.

However, the new variant is **not automatically considered pathogenic**.

---

## Clinical Importance

Knowledge gained from previously studied variants can contribute to interpretation of newly identified variants.

---

## Key Concepts

- Novel missense variant
- Previously known pathogenic variant
- Scientific evidence

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — Does PM5 Mean the Variant Definitely Causes Disease?

## Patient Explanation

No.

PM5 is **not the final classification**.

Different genetic changes at the same amino acid position can have different biological effects.

Healthcare professionals always evaluate PM5 together with many other categories of scientific evidence before determining whether a variant should be classified as pathogenic.

---

## Clinical Importance

Final variant classification depends on the complete body of available evidence.

---

## Key Concepts

- Combined evidence
- Professional interpretation
- Final classification

# Knowledge Block 5 — Why Is PM5 Important?

## Patient Explanation

PM5 allows genetics professionals to use existing scientific knowledge when evaluating newly discovered variants.

Using standardized evidence codes helps laboratories interpret variants in a more consistent and transparent way.

---

## Clinical Importance

Standardized use of prior clinical knowledge improves consistency and reproducibility in clinical genomics.

---

## Key Concepts

- Existing evidence
- Standardization
- Clinical genomics

---

# Common Misconception

**Myth**

If two variants affect the same amino acid position, they must have the same clinical effect.

**Fact**

Different amino acid substitutions at the same position may behave differently.

PM5 provides **moderate supporting evidence** only and must always be interpreted together with many other categories of scientific evidence.

---

# Key Messages

- PM5 is one ACMG Evidence Code.
- PM5 belongs to the PM (Moderate Pathogenic Evidence) family.
- Different variants can affect the same amino acid position.
- A previously known pathogenic variant may provide moderate supporting evidence when evaluating a new variant at the same position.
- PM5 contributes one category of evidence but is not the final variant classification.
