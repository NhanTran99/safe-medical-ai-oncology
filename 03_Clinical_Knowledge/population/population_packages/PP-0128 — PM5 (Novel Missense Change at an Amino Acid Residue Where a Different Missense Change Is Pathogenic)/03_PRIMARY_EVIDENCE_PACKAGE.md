# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is PM5 (Novel Missense Change at an Amino Acid Residue Where a Different Missense Change Is Pathogenic)?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **PM5 (Novel Missense Change at an Amino Acid Residue Where a Different Missense Change Is Pathogenic)** as one of the standardized **ACMG/AMP Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes**.

This Population Package explains that **PM5 represents moderate evidence supporting pathogenicity when a novel missense variant affects the same amino acid residue as a different missense variant that has already been established as pathogenic, according to accepted professional standards.** It emphasizes that knowledge gained from previously characterized pathogenic variants may help evaluate newly discovered variants, while recognizing that **different amino acid substitutions at the same position can have different biological effects**.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- PM5 is one standardized ACMG/AMP evidence code.
- Different missense variants may occur at the same amino acid position.
- Existing knowledge about one pathogenic variant may provide moderate supporting evidence when evaluating another variant at the same position.
- Different substitutions at the same amino acid residue may have different biological effects.
- Final variant classification always requires comprehensive professional evaluation.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

Professional organizations consistently recognize **PM5** as one of the standardized **Moderate Pathogenic Evidence Codes** within the ACMG/AMP variant interpretation framework. Guidance consistently explains that PM5 may be applied when a **novel missense variant** affects the **same amino acid residue** as another missense variant already established as pathogenic, according to accepted professional standards. ClinGen SVI recommendations further refine the application of PM5 while emphasizing that **evidence from previously characterized variants contributes to structured evidence-based interpretation rather than serving as an independent diagnostic conclusion**, and that different amino acid substitutions may have different functional consequences.
