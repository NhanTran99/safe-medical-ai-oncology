# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is a biopsy during upper endoscopy and why is it performed?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of biopsy during upper endoscopy, including why doctors perform biopsies, what information a biopsy provides, why pathology examination is necessary, and why patients should wait for the official pathology report before drawing conclusions.

This Population Package establishes the foundational educational concept of biopsy before introducing pathology interpretation, biomarker testing and definitive gastric cancer diagnosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of biopsy
- Purpose of biopsy
- Relationship between upper endoscopy and biopsy
- Reasons doctors perform biopsy
- Role of biopsy in evaluating stomach disease
- Basic explanation of pathology examination
- Why biopsy results require time
- Importance of waiting for the official pathology report

---

## Excluded

- Biopsy techniques
- Number of biopsy samples
- Histopathological interpretation
- Histologic classification
- HER2 testing
- MSI testing
- PD-L1 testing
- Molecular pathology
- Next-generation sequencing
- Cancer staging
- Treatment planning

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- A biopsy is a small tissue sample obtained for laboratory examination.
- Doctors perform biopsies only when clinically appropriate.
- Most biopsy procedures are performed to clarify abnormal findings rather than confirm cancer.
- Many biopsy results identify benign conditions.
- Definitive diagnosis frequently depends on microscopic examination by a pathologist.
- Patients should wait for the official pathology report before interpreting the results.
