# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **How does gastric cancer spread?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of the major pathways through which gastric cancer can spread, including local invasion, lymphatic spread, hematogenous spread and an introductory overview of peritoneal spread.

This Population Package introduces the concept of cancer dissemination without discussing molecular mechanisms, organ-specific metastases, TNM classifications, diagnostic investigations or treatment recommendations.

---

# Scope

## Included

- General concept of cancer spread
- Local (direct) invasion
- Lymphatic spread
- Hematogenous (bloodstream) spread
- Introductory explanation of peritoneal spread
- Relationship between cancer spread and disease staging
- Importance of medical evaluation

---

## Excluded

- Molecular mechanisms of metastasis
- EMT (epithelial–mesenchymal transition)
- Metastatic cascade
- Peritoneal metastasis in detail
- Liver metastasis
- Lung metastasis
- Bone metastasis
- Brain metastasis
- N classifications
- M classifications
- Imaging investigations
- Treatment recommendations
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Gastric cancer begins in one location but may spread over time.
- Cancer may spread by direct growth into nearby tissues.
- Cancer cells may spread through lymphatic vessels to regional lymph nodes.
- Cancer cells may spread through the bloodstream to distant organs.
- Gastric cancer may also spread within the abdominal cavity.
- The pattern of spread varies between patients.
- Appropriate medical evaluation is required to determine the extent of spread.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the principal pathways of gastric cancer spread;
- the distinction between local invasion and metastatic spread;
- the clinical importance of lymphatic and hematogenous dissemination;
- recognition of peritoneal spread as an important dissemination pathway in gastric cancer;
- the need for comprehensive clinical evaluation before determining disease extent.

Differences between references are limited to terminology and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-friendly educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
