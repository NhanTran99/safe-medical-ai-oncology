# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What is HIPEC, how does it fit with cytoreductive surgery for gastric cancer with peritoneal disease, which patients may be considered in the current guideline pathway, what does the evidence show, and what are the limitations and uncertainties?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide a patient-facing, evidence-traceable explanation of HIPEC that:

- defines the treatment accurately;
- explains its local-regional role;
- prevents confusion with systemic chemotherapy and PIPAC;
- explains selection without creating an individualized eligibility algorithm;
- presents evidence both supporting and challenging HIPEC;
- preserves numerical evidence where it changes interpretation;
- distinguishes observational from randomized evidence;
- communicates morbidity and treatment burden;
- preserves current guideline uncertainty;
- supports multidisciplinary discussion.

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Level I — Current Disease-Specific Guideline

### NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

This is the highest-priority source for the package.

The guideline states that HIPEC involves continuous circulation of a heated sterile chemotherapy-containing solution throughout the peritoneal cavity following cytoreductive surgery.

It states that, because evidence is limited, IC/HIPEC should be pursued only in selected circumstances and after multidisciplinary discussion.

For certain patients with PCI ≤10 who are candidates for complete cytoreduction, IC/HIPEC with cytoreductive surgery is an option.

For certain patients with PCI >10, IC/HIPEC may be considered in a clinical trial.

The guideline also requires pretreatment evaluation and references systemic therapy, restaging, CT, diagnostic laparoscopy/washings, PCI, possible PET, and multidisciplinary review.

These statements establish the clinical framework of PP-0226.

# Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

## Evidence 1 — HIPEC Definition and Rationale

### Source

NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026.

### Supported claim

HIPEC is continuous circulation of a heated sterile chemotherapy-containing solution through the peritoneal cavity after cytoreductive surgery.

### Clinical meaning

The method is intended to expose the peritoneal surfaces directly to chemotherapy.

### Boundary

This evidence supports conceptual explanation only.

It does not support inclusion of:

- device engineering;
- exact perfusion settings;
- exact drug doses;
- technical operative instructions.

### Evidence maturity

**Established / guideline-supported.**

---

## Evidence 2 — Selected Patient Pathway

### Source

NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026.

### Supported pathway

Selected patients with peritoneal-only disease undergo:

1. workup;
2. systemic therapy for a minimum of 3 months;
3. restaging;
4. multidisciplinary assessment;
5. evaluation of PCI;
6. assessment of disease stability/improvement;
7. assessment of extraperitoneal disease;
8. assessment of complete cytoreduction.

### Current selected-use framework

For certain patients:

**PCI ≤10**

+

**candidate for complete cytoreduction**

→ IC/HIPEC with cytoreductive surgery is an option.

For higher PCI:

**PCI >10**

→ IC/HIPEC may be considered in a clinical trial.

### Evidence maturity

**Established / guideline-supported framework.**

### Important caveat

The framework is not a stand-alone individualized eligibility calculator.

# Evidence 3 — CYTO-CHIP

## Study population

The NCCN source reports 277 patients with gastric-cancer peritoneal metastases.

Groups:

- CRS + HIPEC: 180
- CRS alone: 97

## Findings

NCCN reports improved:

- overall survival;
- recurrence-free survival.

The guideline also states there was no increase in morbidity or mortality in the analysis.

## Important limitation

Median PCI remained higher in the HIPEC group following treatment.

The study is a propensity-score analysis rather than a randomized trial.

Therefore:

### What the evidence supports

HIPEC may provide benefit in strictly selected patients with limited peritoneal metastases.

### What it does not prove

HIPEC is universally superior for every patient.

---

# Evidence 4 — Badgwell Phase II

## Population

20 patients with gastric adenocarcinoma and:

- positive peritoneal cytology or carcinomatosis;
- completed systemic chemotherapy;
- laparoscopic HIPEC;
- cytoreduction;
- gastrectomy;
- HIPEC.

## Outcomes reported by NCCN

- 90-day morbidity: **70%**
- 90-day mortality: **0%**
- median OS from cytoreduction/gastrectomy/HIPEC: **16.1 months**
- 1-year OS from metastatic diagnosis: **90%**
- 2-year OS: **50%**
- 3-year OS: **28%**

## Appraisal

This study contributes:

- feasibility evidence;
- safety signal;
- early outcome information.

It does not establish comparative efficacy because it is a small phase II study.

The 70% morbidity figure should be interpreted as a study-specific observation in a 20-patient cohort undergoing a complex combined treatment.

---

# Evidence 5 — Yang Randomized Phase III

## Population

68 patients with gastric cancer and peritoneal carcinomatosis.

## Comparison

CRS alone

versus

CRS + HIPEC.

## Results

At median follow-up of 32 months:

- deaths: 97% vs 85%;
- median survival: **6.5 vs 11 months**;
- **P=.046**.

Serious adverse events:

- 11.7% CRS alone;
- 14.7% CRS + HIPEC;
- **P=.839**.

## Appraisal

This is randomized evidence and therefore has stronger causal interpretability than a retrospective propensity-score analysis.

However:

- sample size was small;
- treatment era was older;
- contemporary systemic therapy may differ;
- subsequent randomized evidence is not uniformly positive for OS.

Therefore the result should be treated as one part of a heterogeneous evidence base.

---

# Evidence 6 — GASTRIPEC-I

## Population

Patients with gastric or EGJ cancers and peritoneal metastasis after preoperative chemotherapy.

## Comparison

Cytoreductive surgery

versus

cytoreductive surgery + HIPEC.

## Overall survival

**HR 0.72; P=.1647**

Interpretation:

No statistically significant OS difference was demonstrated.

## Progression-free survival

**7.1 vs 3.5 months; P=.0472**

Interpretation:

PFS was improved with HIPEC.

## Other distant metastasis-free survival

**10.2 vs 9.2 months; P=.0286**

Interpretation:

This endpoint also favored HIPEC.

## Important trial-context issue

The NCCN guideline notes that some patients did not proceed to further treatment and complete cytoreduction after preoperative chemotherapy because of progression and other causes, and almost half had PCI ≥7.

## Appraisal

GASTRIPEC-I is critical because it demonstrates that:

- disease-control endpoints may improve;
- OS may nevertheless remain statistically non-significant.

This is a central evidence-maturity message for PP-0226.

---

# Evidence 7 — PERISCOPE II

## Study concept

Gastrectomy + cytoreductive surgery + HIPEC

versus

systemic treatment.

Population:

- resectable cT3–cT4 gastric cancer;
- limited peritoneal carcinoma.

## Endpoints

Primary:

- OS.

Secondary:

- PFS;
- toxicity;
- quality of life.

## Appraisal

PERISCOPE II represents ongoing evidence generation.

It should be described as:

**Emerging / investigational evidence.**

It should not be used to claim a confirmed benefit before definitive results are available.

## Observational versus Randomized Evidence

The evidence must not be flattened into a single category.

### Propensity-score evidence

CYTO-CHIP:

- large;
- comparative;
- clinically informative;
- vulnerable to residual confounding.

### Randomized evidence

Yang:

- randomized;
- small;
- historical.

GASTRIPEC-I:

- randomized;
- contemporary;
- no statistically significant OS benefit.

Therefore:

> The evidence base supports selected use while leaving the magnitude and generalizability of benefit uncertain.

---

# Evidence Limitations

1. Some supportive evidence is observational.
2. Randomized trials have relatively limited sample sizes.
3. Historical trials were performed before the full modern systemic-treatment era.
4. Trial populations differ in disease burden and treatment sequence.
5. Gastric and EGJ populations may be combined in some evidence.
6. Complete cytoreduction feasibility strongly influences selection.
7. PCI distributions differ across studies.
8. HIPEC is often combined with major surgery, complicating attribution of morbidity to HIPEC alone.
9. OS, PFS and recurrence outcomes do not always move in the same direction.
10. Ongoing trials indicate that the optimal role remains under investigation.
11. Study-specific morbidity/mortality cannot be converted into individual risk estimates.
12. The supplied source set does not establish a universal HIPEC eligibility calculator.

---

# Evidence Gaps

## Gap 1 — Optimal patient selection

The evidence does not define one universally sufficient combination of:

- PCI;
- response;
- fitness;
- surgical feasibility.

## Gap 2 — Modern systemic-treatment integration

The effect of HIPEC when integrated with contemporary systemic regimens remains an active evidence question.

## Gap 3 — Overall-survival magnitude

Randomized evidence is not uniformly positive.

## Gap 4 — Higher PCI

The current guideline places higher-PCI HIPEC consideration primarily in clinical-trial context.

## Gap 5 — Quality of life

Quality-of-life evidence remains less mature than survival/end-point reporting.

## Gap 6 — Technical heterogeneity

HIPEC practice can differ by center and protocol; detailed technical standardization is outside this PP.

# Evidence-to-Patient Translation

### Evidence statement

GASTRIPEC-I did not show a statistically significant OS difference.

### Patient-facing translation

> “One modern randomized study did not show a statistically significant overall-survival improvement from adding HIPEC, although some measures of disease control improved.”

### Evidence statement

CYTO-CHIP showed better OS and recurrence-free survival.

### Patient-facing translation

> “A large comparative study found better outcomes with HIPEC, but because it was not a randomized trial, the result cannot prove that HIPEC itself caused all of the observed difference.”

### Evidence statement

Badgwell phase II reported 70% 90-day morbidity.

### Patient-facing translation

> “In one small study of 20 patients undergoing this complex treatment, complications within 90 days were common. That number should not be treated as an individual prediction.”
