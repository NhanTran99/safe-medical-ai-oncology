# Knowledge Classification

## Primary Knowledge Type

**Supportive / palliative care framework knowledge**

PP-0227 is an atomic knowledge node explaining what palliative care means in gastric cancer, why it is important, when it can be integrated, how it relates to active cancer treatment, and how goals and priorities change across the cancer trajectory.

It is not a symptom-management manual.

It is not a hospice-administration package.

It is not a metastatic-treatment package.

# Patient Journey Classification

Palliative care is longitudinal.

It can be relevant during:

1. diagnosis;
2. active treatment;
3. response/stable disease;
4. recurrence;
5. advanced/metastatic disease;
6. rehabilitation;
7. end of life.

The presence of palliative care does not determine the disease stage.

# Intended Runtime Usage

## Primary Uses

1. Explain palliative care in plain language.
2. Correct the misconception that palliative care means stopping cancer treatment.
3. Explain that palliative care can be integrated early.
4. Explain the distinction between palliative care and best supportive care.
5. Explain quality of life and relief of suffering.
6. Explain goals of care.
7. Explain shared decision-making.
8. Explain advance care planning.
9. Explain family/caregiver support.
10. Explain end-of-life and hospice concepts.
11. Provide a safe framework for patient questions.
12. Connect patients to detailed supportive-care packages.

## Runtime Safety

Do not use PP-0227 to:

- decide hospice eligibility;
- decide when anticancer therapy should stop;
- prescribe medication;
- give terminal-care drug doses;
- recommend individualized procedures;
- predict individual survival;
- replace clinical assessment.

# Knowledge Units

## KU-01 — Definition

Palliative care is an approach focused on preventing/relieving suffering and supporting quality of life for patients and families.

## KU-02 — Timing

Palliative care can occur from diagnosis through end of life.

## KU-03 — Concurrent Treatment

Palliative care can coexist with active anticancer treatment.

## KU-04 — Patient-Centered Goals

Care should incorporate what matters most to the patient.

## KU-05 — Multidimensional Needs

Needs include physical, emotional, social, cultural, spiritual, nutritional, functional and caregiver dimensions.

## KU-06 — Assessment

Symptoms may have disease-related, treatment-related, psychosocial or mixed causes.

## KU-07 — Best Supportive Care Boundary

PP-0227 owns the framework; PP-0228 owns detailed supportive interventions.

## KU-08 — Goals of Care

Goals may change as disease trajectory and patient priorities change.

## KU-09 — Advance Care Planning

Future-care preferences can be discussed and documented conceptually.

## KU-10 — End of Life

End-of-life care is part of palliative care but does not define the whole field.

## KU-11 — Hospice

Hospice is a distinct care/service model associated with end-of-life care.

## KU-12 — Continuity

Stopping anticancer therapy does not mean stopping care.

# Safety / Runtime Rules

1. Do not equate palliative care with hospice.
2. Do not equate palliative care with stopping anticancer treatment.
3. Do not use the package to determine when treatment should stop.
4. Do not provide individualized prognosis.
5. Do not provide medication dosing.
6. Do not provide detailed terminal-care protocols.
7. Do not provide jurisdiction-specific hospice rules.
8. Do not convert conceptual symptom assessment into an individualized treatment algorithm.
9. Preserve patient autonomy and shared decision-making.
10. Direct urgent symptoms to appropriate clinical assessment.
11. Preserve uncertainty where evidence is not specific.
12. Maintain the PP-0227/PP-0228 boundary.
