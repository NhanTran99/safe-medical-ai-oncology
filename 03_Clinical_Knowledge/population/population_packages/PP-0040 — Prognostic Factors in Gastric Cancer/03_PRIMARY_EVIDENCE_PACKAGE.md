# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What are prognostic factors in gastric cancer and what should patients understand about them?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of prognostic factors in gastric cancer, including what prognosis means, which categories of clinical information contribute to prognosis, why prognosis is based on multiple factors rather than a single result, and why prognostic assessment changes as new clinical information becomes available.

This Population Package introduces the concept of prognostic factors without discussing survival statistics, prognostic models, or individualized prediction tools.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of prognostic factors
- Purpose of prognostic assessment
- Major prognostic factor categories
- Cancer stage
- Pathological findings
- Biomarker results (introductory)
- Response to treatment
- General health and performance status
- Prognosis as an estimate
- Dynamic nature of prognosis

- Kaplan–Meier curves

- Nomograms
- Prognostic prediction models
- AJCC Prognostic Stage
- AI prediction models

- Personalized survival prediction

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Prognostic factors help doctors estimate the likely course of gastric cancer.
- Prognosis is determined by integrating multiple clinical, pathological and patient-related factors.
- Cancer stage, pathology, biomarkers, treatment response and overall health all contribute to prognosis.
- Prognosis is an estimate rather than a certainty.
- Prognostic assessment may change as additional clinical information becomes available.
- Clinical decisions are based on the complete clinical picture rather than a single prognostic factor.

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition of prognostic factors;
- the multifactorial nature of prognostic assessment;
- the central role of cancer staging;
- the contribution of pathology, biomarkers and patient factors;
- the importance of interpreting prognosis as an estimate rather than an exact prediction.

Differences between references are limited to educational emphasis and the amount of clinical detail provided.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.
