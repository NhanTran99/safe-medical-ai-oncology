# 2. Clinical Question

> What treatment-related toxicities can occur during or after gastric-cancer therapy, how are they recognized and assessed, what supportive/preventive principles apply, and when can toxicity affect safe continuation or modification of treatment?

---

# 3. Educational Intent

The Evidence Package supports a patient-facing and clinician-informed knowledge layer that explains:

- what treatment toxicity is;
- how it differs from disease symptoms;
- how toxicity is assessed;
- how common toxicity domains vary by treatment modality;
- why prevention and supportive care matter;
- why serious toxicity may change treatment delivery;
- how long-term treatment effects connect with survivorship and follow-up.

It intentionally does not become a treatment-prescribing or toxicity-protocol manual.

# 5. Primary Evidence Sources

## Source A — ESMO-ASCO Recommendations for a Global Curriculum in Medical Oncology Edition 2023

**Evidence role:** General oncology toxicity-management framework.

Key supported claims:

- clinicians should assess, diagnose and treat treatment toxicities;
- preventive counseling is part of toxicity management;
- cytotoxic, targeted and immunotherapy toxicities differ and overlap;
- toxicity may be acute or chronic;
- severity and chronicity influence management;
- individual risk varies;
- clinical assessment is critical;
- diagnostic tests have strengths and limitations;
- patient-reported and clinician-reported toxicity differ;
- patient education and patient-reported outcomes can improve symptom management;
- standardized toxicity grading is important;
- multidisciplinary management is common;
- evidence-based prophylaxis can reduce toxicity;
- toxicity can affect relative-dose intensity;
- toxicity affects physical and psychological well-being;
- dose delay, modification or cessation may be relevant when toxicity is unacceptable.

**Evidence classification:** Guideline/consensus curriculum-level authoritative framework.

---

## Source B — NCCN Gastric Cancer

**Evidence role:** Gastric-cancer-specific treatment and safety context.

The supplied NCCN material links systemic-treatment choices with toxicity considerations and directs clinicians to the dedicated NCCN guideline for management of immunotherapy-related toxicities.

It also provides treatment-specific context for systemic therapy and targeted therapies.

**Evidence classification:** Disease-specific clinical practice guideline.

## Source D — NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment PDQ®

The PDQ material provides treatment-evidence context and reports adverse-event information in treatment studies, including systemic regimens and combinations.

For example, the supplied material reports grade ≥3 adverse events in KEYNOTE-811 and identifies immune-related reactions as a component of the adverse-event profile.

**Evidence classification:** NCI evidence summary.

- mouth sores;

- infection risk;
- bleeding risk;
- anemia;
- neuropathy;
- hand-foot syndrome.

**Evidence classification:** Patient-facing disease-specific cancer information.

---

## Source F — ACS Stomach Cancer / Radiation Therapy

Supports gastric-cancer radiation toxicity including:

- skin changes;
- nausea/vomiting;
- diarrhea;
- fatigue;
- low blood counts;
- eating/hydration problems;
- possible late organ effects.

It also notes that toxicity may be greater when radiation is combined with chemotherapy.

**Evidence classification:** Patient-facing disease-specific cancer information.

---

## Source G — ACS Immunotherapy for Stomach Cancer

Supports:

- immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment context;
- serious immune-related side effects;
- possibility that serious immune toxicity may require treatment interruption/stopping;
- corticosteroid-based immune suppression in serious cases;
- patient-facing need to recognize and report symptoms.

**Evidence classification:** Patient-facing disease-specific cancer information.

# 8. Detailed Evidence Notes / Appraisal

## 8.1 Systemic toxicity framework

ESMO-ASCO is the strongest project source for the architecture of PP-0231. It treats toxicity management as a competency involving recognition, diagnosis, treatment, prevention, grading, patient education, multidisciplinary management and treatment modification.

This supports the decision that PP-0231 must be a **management layer**, not a side-effect dictionary.

---

## 8.2 Chemotherapy

The ACS gastric-cancer chemotherapy material supports multiple patient-facing toxicity domains.

The evidence is appropriate for explaining common categories and why different regimens can have different toxicity burdens.

It should not be used to imply that every patient receiving chemotherapy experiences all listed effects.

---

## 8.3 Radiation

The ACS gastric-cancer material directly supports radiation toxicity domains.

The distinction between radiation alone and chemoradiation is important because combined treatment can increase side-effect burden.

The material also supports the nutrition/hydration interface.

---

## 8.4 Targeted therapy

NCI and NCCN establish the targeted-treatment landscape.

The PP should use representative toxicity examples rather than converting PP-0231 into multiple drug monographs.

This preserves atomicity with PP-0210, PP-0211 and PP-0212.

---

## 8.5 Immunotherapy

The supplied ESMO-ASCO material provides the strongest conceptual framework for immune-related toxicity.

The ACS material provides patient-facing gastric-cancer context, including serious side effects and possible treatment interruption.

The NCCN gastric-cancer guideline links immunotherapy treatment with its dedicated immunotherapy-toxicity guidance.

This supports a **high-priority but bounded** immune-toxicity component within PP-0231.

---

# 11. Evidence Limitations

1. The supplied clinical materials are not a single comprehensive toxicity guideline.
2. Exact management varies by treatment, toxicity, severity and patient factors.
3. ACS provides patient-facing summaries and is not a substitute for detailed specialty toxicity guidelines.
4. The NCCN gastric-cancer source points certain immunotherapy toxicity decisions to a dedicated toxicity guideline rather than reproducing all details.
5. PP-0231 therefore deliberately avoids exhaustive treatment protocols.
6. Some long-term toxicity domains require dedicated survivorship or rehabilitation evidence.
7. Exact emergency thresholds depend on treatment and clinical context and should not be universalized from this package.

---

# 12. Evidence Gaps

- Dedicated drug-by-drug toxicity management is outside the project core source set for this PP.
- Full CTCAE mapping is outside the supplied educational package.
- Full immune-checkpoint-toxicity algorithms are delegated.
- Detailed rehabilitation and nutrition pathways may require dedicated source sets.
- Detailed cancer-pain protocols are outside this package.

These are **non-blocking gaps** because they are explicitly delegated rather than essential to PP-0231 identity.

# 16. Boundary Verification


Core = treatment-related toxicity and treatment-related sequelae across chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy; recognition, assessment, severity/grading concepts, prevention, supportive management, monitoring, patient education, toxicity-related treatment modification concepts, acute versus chronic toxicity, multidisciplinary toxicity management, and safety-oriented escalation;
