# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **Why is cancer stage one of the most important prognostic factors in gastric cancer?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of why cancer stage is one of the most important prognostic factors in gastric cancer, how staging helps physicians understand disease extent, supports treatment planning, and contributes to prognosis while emphasizing that stage is only one component of the overall clinical assessment.

This Population Package introduces the prognostic role of cancer staging without discussing the TNM staging system, AJCC stage groups, stage-specific survival statistics or treatment recommendations.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of cancer stage
- Purpose of cancer staging
- Cancer stage as a prognostic factor
- Relationship between stage and disease extent
- Role of staging in treatment planning
- Relationship between stage and prognosis
- Why prognosis varies among patients with the same stage
- Stage as one component of comprehensive clinical assessment

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- Cancer stage describes how far gastric cancer has spread.
- Cancer stage is one of the strongest prognostic factors in gastric cancer.
- Cancer staging is essential for treatment planning and clinical communication.
- Earlier stages generally have a more favorable prognosis than more advanced stages.
- Patients with the same stage may still experience different outcomes.
- Prognostic assessment always integrates staging with pathology, biomarkers, treatment response and patient-related factors.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- the definition and purpose of cancer staging;
- the central role of stage in prognosis;
- the importance of stage for treatment planning;
- the need to combine stage with additional prognostic factors;
- the principle that stage alone cannot predict an individual's outcome with certainty.

Differences between references are limited to educational emphasis and the amount of clinical detail provided.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- scientific concepts from NCI PDQ;
- patient-centered educational language from ACS;
- terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- TNM staging methodology;
- AJCC stage grouping;
- stage-specific survival statistics;
- treatment recommendations by stage;
- clinical versus pathological staging;
- restaging after treatment.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
