# Clinical Question

## Primary Educational Question

> **What is endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR), when can it be used for early gastric cancer, how is the EMR specimen assessed, and what are the limitations of EMR compared with other endoscopic or surgical treatment?**

## Clinical-Educational Intent

Provide a source-grounded, patient-facing explanation of EMR as a specific endoscopic treatment modality for selected early gastric cancer.

The package is designed to help a patient understand:

**why EMR is being considered**

→ **what makes a lesion potentially suitable**

→ **what EMR contributes as treatment and specimen acquisition**

→ **what pathology determines**

→ **what curative versus non-curative means**

→ **why additional treatment or surveillance may follow**

The package deliberately avoids turning guideline criteria into an individualized treatment algorithm.

# Primary Evidence Sources

## Priority 1 — NCCN Gastric Cancer v2.2026

**Source:** Gastric Cancer v2.2026 — NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology.

### Role

Highest-priority direct gastric-cancer guideline source for PP-0194.

### Relevant content

The supplied NCCN document states that:

- endoscopy is important in diagnosis, staging, and treatment of gastric cancer;
- EMR or ESD can be used for evaluation of small lesions;
- focal nodules ≤2 cm can be resected endoscopically to provide a larger specimen for assessment of differentiation, LVI and depth of infiltration;
- accurate clinical staging is especially important when ER is being considered;
- early-stage gastric cancer can be treated endoscopically in selected cases;
- curative ER features include small size, superficial invasion, favorable differentiation, complete excision and negative lateral/deep margins;
- current NCCN treatment text considers EMR or ESD adequate therapy for selected lesions ≤2 cm with favorable histology, superficial invasion ≤500 μm, no LVI and clear margins, while preferring ESD;
- expanded indications described by the guideline primarily concern ESD;
- poor differentiation, LVI, deep submucosal invasion, positive margins or lymph-node metastases indicate incomplete endoscopic treatment and may require additional therapy;
- EMR pathology reporting should include invasion, histologic type, grade, depth of submucosal invasion where possible, vascular/lymphatic invasion and mucosal/deep margins;
- experienced/high-volume centers are recommended for ER.

### Source location

- GAST-A 1 of 4 — PDF p.18.
- GAST-A 2 of 4 — PDF p.19.
- GAST-A 3 of 4 — PDF p.20.
- GAST-A 4 of 4 / references — PDF p.21.
- GAST-B pathology table — PDF p.22.

## Priority 2 — NCI Gastric Cancer Treatment (PDQ®)

**Source:** Gastric Cancer Treatment (PDQ®) — National Cancer Institute.

### Role

Primary supporting treatment-evidence source.

### Relevant content

The supplied PDQ states that:

- EMR is a treatment option for stage 0 gastric cancer;
- selected stage IA patients may receive EMR;
- classic good-risk EMR experience includes Tis/T1a tumors ≤2 cm, predominantly differentiated, without ulcerative findings;
- intramucosal tumors have lower nodal-metastasis risk than submucosal tumors;
- careful patient selection, experienced endoscopists and close surveillance should be considered;
- a prospective EMR trial included 445 patients with 479 intramucosal tumors;
- complete resection was recommended when submucosal invasion, blood-vessel involvement or positive margins were present;
- among 405 patients with intramucosal disease, 278 underwent complete resection, with 2% local recurrence treated with curative intent and 100% disease-free survival at median follow-up of 38 months;
- among incomplete or non-evaluable resections, 18 of 127 patients had local recurrence and underwent curative surgery.

### Source location

- PDF p.17 — stage 0 / EMR evidence.
- PDF p.18 — stage I selected EMR.
- PDF p.19 — additional EMR evidence.

## Priority 3 — NCI Treatment of Stomach Cancer

**Source:** Treatment of Stomach Cancer — National Cancer Institute.

### Role

Patient-facing definition and treatment-context source.

### Relevant content

The supplied patient-facing NCI material describes EMR as a procedure using an endoscope to remove carcinoma in situ and early-stage cancer from the lining of the digestive tract.

It also frames treatment decisions as a discussion between the patient and cancer care team, taking into account stage, overall health, preferences and treatment goals.

### Source location

- PDF p.1.

## Priority 4 — Vietnamese Guideline

**Source:** Hướng dẫn chẩn đoán và điều trị ung thư dạ dày.

### Role

Regional clinical source providing explicit endoscopic-resection criteria and curative-resection concepts.

### Relevant content

The supplied guideline describes:

- early gastric cancer cTis/cT1a as a setting for endoscopic treatment;
- EMR and ESD as endoscopic-resection modalities;
- the principle that lesions considered for endoscopic treatment should have very low probability of lymph-node metastasis and be suitable for en-bloc removal;
- absolute indications including differentiated, nonulcerated, clinically T1a lesions <2 cm;
- relative/expanded indications;
- ESD being more frequently used in expanded situations because it can resect more broadly in the submucosal layer;
- careful pathology review of the resected specimen;
- curative assessment based on complete primary-tumor removal and absence of significant nodal-metastatic potential;
- margins, histologic differentiation, pT1a/pT1b depth, lymphatic/venous invasion as relevant features;
- consideration of further gastrectomy when endoscopic resection is not curative.

### Source location

- PDF p.13 — early gastric cancer and treatment framework.
- PDF p.14 — endoscopic resection indications and pathology/curative criteria.

# Clinical Claims Summary

## Established / Guideline-Supported

1. EMR is a recognized endoscopic treatment for selected early gastric cancer.
2. Classic favorable EMR candidates include superficial, small, differentiated, nonulcerated lesions.
3. Low expected nodal risk is central because EMR does not remove regional lymph nodes.
4. Depth of invasion is central to endoscopic-treatment suitability.
5. The EMR specimen is important for definitive pathology assessment.
6. Curative assessment depends on multiple pathology and lesion features.
7. Negative lateral and deep margins are important.
8. Absence of LVI is part of current NCCN curative ER criteria.
9. ESD has greater en-bloc capability and is preferred in the current NCCN framework.
10. Expanded ESD criteria should not automatically be interpreted as expanded EMR criteria.
11. Non-curative features may lead to additional treatment.
12. Experienced endoscopists and high-volume centers matter.
13. Surveillance remains important after definitive endoscopic treatment.

# Evidence Interpretation

## Current guideline versus historical evidence

The supplied NCCN v2.2026 document is the primary current guideline reference.

The NCI PDQ contains historical EMR evidence, much of it originating from Japan and other Asian settings.

These sources are complementary:

**Current NCCN**

→ current framework for clinical interpretation.

**NCI PDQ**

→ historical evidence demonstrating the clinical experience and outcomes underlying the role of EMR.

Historical cohort outcomes should not override current guideline selection criteria.

---

# Evidence Conflict / Nuance Review

## EMR versus ESD

There is no material conflict in the supplied sources.

The sources consistently support:

- EMR for selected favorable-risk early lesions.
- ESD as a more extensive endoscopic resection option.
- Greater en-bloc capability of ESD.
- Greater technical complexity of ESD.
- Current NCCN preference for ESD within ER.

The Vietnamese guideline similarly states that ESD is often used more in expanded-indication settings because it can resect more broadly in the submucosal layer.

## Absolute versus expanded indications

The Vietnamese guideline provides explicit absolute and expanded indication categories.

The current NCCN document provides a current ER framework and presents expanded indications primarily for ESD.

Therefore PP-0194 preserves a conservative distinction:

> **Classic EMR suitability should not be expanded by simply importing every expanded ESD criterion.**

# Safety / Interpretation Guardrails

## Guardrail 1 — Do not equate small size with eligibility

Size is only one selection variable.

## Guardrail 2 — Do not equate T1a with automatic EMR

Histology, ulceration, size, LVI, margins and nodal risk still matter.

## Guardrail 3 — Do not equate successful removal with cure

Final pathology is required.

## Guardrail 4 — Do not equate negative margins with complete oncologic adequacy

Curative assessment is multidimensional.

## Guardrail 5 — Do not transfer ESD indications automatically to EMR

Expanded ESD criteria are modality-specific.

## Guardrail 6 — Do not use historical outcome percentages as personal predictions

Published cohorts represent selected populations.

## Guardrail 7 — Do not replace specialist assessment with this PP

The package is educational, not an individualized treatment tool.

---

# Evidence Gaps

1. No universal patient-specific EMR eligibility algorithm is provided.
2. No complete EMR procedural technique is provided.
3. No universal EMR-specific complication rate is established in the supplied sources.
4. Exact surveillance timing varies by treatment modality, stage, pathology and current guideline.
5. Individual pathology interpretation requires the actual specimen/report and expert review.
6. The source set is stronger for EMR selection and outcomes than for technical procedural detail.
7. Much of the historical evidence comes from Asian high-incidence settings, which may limit direct generalizability to lower-incidence systems.

These gaps are intentionally retained rather than filled with unsupported general medical knowledge.
