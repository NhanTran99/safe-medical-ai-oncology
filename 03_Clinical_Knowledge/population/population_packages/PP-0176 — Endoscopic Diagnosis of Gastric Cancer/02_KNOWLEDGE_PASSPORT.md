## Intended Runtime Usage

### Primary Runtime Usage

Explain what happens during diagnostic endoscopy when gastric cancer is suspected.

### Secondary Runtime Usage

- Explain what endoscopists look for.
- Explain why location and lesion description matter.
- Explain why endoscopy does not equal final pathology.
- Explain why biopsy follows suspicious findings.
- Explain limitations of endoscopic detection.
- Explain why diffuse/microscopic disease can be difficult to see.
- Explain discordance between endoscopy and an initial biopsy.
- Route detailed biopsy and pathology questions to downstream PPs.
- Prevent confusion between screening, diagnosis, surveillance and treatment endoscopy.

# Clinical Scope

## Core

Gastric-cancer-specific diagnostic endoscopy, including direct visualization and recognition of suspicious gastric neoplasia, lesion morphology at a conceptual level, focal versus diffuse/infiltrative patterns, lesion location, size, extent, relationship to the esophagogastric junction, systematic examination and documentation, distinction between endoscopic suspicion and confirmed diagnosis, limitations of visual detection, and the transition from suspicious findings to tissue sampling.

## Supporting

Selected discussion of subtle/early lesions, microscopic or diffuse disease, endoscopy–pathology discordance, selected EUS and EMR/ESD diagnostic-staging interfaces, and the role of endoscopic findings in downstream planning.

## Explicitly Excluded

General endoscopy preparation, sedation/anesthesia, post-procedure care, procedural complications, detailed biopsy number/site/technique, histopathologic classification, Lauren classification, molecular/biomarker testing, detailed EUS or imaging methodology, full TNM staging, EMR/ESD technique, therapeutic endoscopy, hereditary gastric-cancer surveillance protocols, and individualized diagnosis or treatment.

# Evidence Classification

## Established / guideline-supported

- Diagnostic endoscopy is an important component of gastric-cancer diagnosis.
- The diagnostic endoscopic examination aims to determine the presence and location of neoplastic disease.
- Suspicious lesions should be biopsied.
- Tumor location within the stomach and relationship to the EGJ should be carefully recorded.
- EUS provides additional local-staging information.
- EMR/ESD can provide larger specimens for selected small lesions and can contribute to staging.
- Diffuse and microscopic gastric cancer can be difficult to identify visually.
- Superficial biopsy can be negative in selected diffuse gastric-cancer presentations.

## Supported but context-dependent

- Enhanced endoscopic imaging may help evaluate subtle mucosal abnormalities.
- Repeat or additional evaluation may be required when endoscopic suspicion and initial pathology are discordant.
- EMR/ESD may have diagnostic and therapeutic implications in selected early lesions.
- The intensity and sequence of endoscopic evaluation depend on the clinical context.

## Not established universally in the supplied source set

- One universal enhanced-imaging protocol.
- One universal repeat-endoscopy interval after a nondiagnostic biopsy.
- One universal size threshold for endoscopic treatment across all contexts.
- One universal endoscopic appearance that confirms gastric cancer.
- One universal diagnostic sequence for every patient.
