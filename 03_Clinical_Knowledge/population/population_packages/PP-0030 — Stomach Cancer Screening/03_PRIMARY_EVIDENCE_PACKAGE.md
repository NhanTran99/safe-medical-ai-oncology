# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **Should people be screened for stomach cancer?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of stomach cancer screening, including its purpose, who may benefit from screening, the principal screening method, and the benefits and limitations of screening.

This Population Package establishes the foundational educational concept of stomach cancer screening and supports informed, shared decision-making between patients and healthcare professionals.

---

# Scope

## Included

* Definition of stomach cancer screening
* Purpose of screening
* Early detection
* Average-risk versus higher-risk individuals
* Individuals who may benefit from screening
* Upper endoscopy as the principal screening test
* Benefits of screening
* Limitations of screening
* Potential risks of screening
* Shared decision-making

---

## Excluded

* Symptoms of stomach cancer
* Diagnostic work-up
* Technical aspects of upper endoscopy
* Biopsy
* Histopathology
* Cancer staging
* Treatment
* National screening programs
* Surveillance protocols
* Hereditary cancer surveillance recommendations

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

* Screening aims to detect stomach cancer before symptoms develop.
* Routine screening is not recommended for average-risk individuals in the United States.
* Certain higher-risk individuals may benefit from screening after individualized assessment.
* Upper endoscopy is the principal screening test used for higher-risk individuals.
* Screening may improve opportunities for early detection but cannot detect every cancer.
* Patients should understand both the benefits and limitations of screening before making decisions.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

* the purpose of stomach cancer screening;
* identification of higher-risk populations;
* the role of upper endoscopy;
* the absence of routine population screening for average-risk individuals in the United States;
* the importance of informed decision-making.

Differences between references are limited to educational wording and the amount of explanatory detail.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

* scientific recommendations from NCI PDQ;
* patient-friendly educational language from ACS;
* terminology consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

* screening implementation in individual countries;
* detailed endoscopic techniques;
* biopsy procedures;
* surveillance intervals;
* hereditary gastric cancer screening protocols;
* management after abnormal screening findings;
* treatment following diagnosis.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
