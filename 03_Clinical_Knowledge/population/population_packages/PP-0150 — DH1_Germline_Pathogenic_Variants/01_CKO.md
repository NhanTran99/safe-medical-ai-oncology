# Knowledge Block 1 — What Is CDH1?

CDH1 is a gene that encodes **E-cadherin**, a protein involved in cell-to-cell adhesion and tissue organization.

Inherited pathogenic variants in CDH1 are strongly associated with hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (HDGC) and increased risk of lobular breast cancer.

---

# Knowledge Block 2 — What Is a Germline CDH1 Variant?

A germline variant is present in constitutional DNA and can therefore be present throughout the body and potentially inherited by biological offspring.

A germline CDH1 pathogenic variant represents inherited cancer susceptibility.

It is different from a CDH1 alteration found only in tumor tissue.

---

# Knowledge Block 3 — What Does “Pathogenic” Mean?

A pathogenic variant is a genetic alteration for which the available evidence supports a disease-causing effect.

The important distinction is:

> **CDH1 variant ≠ CDH1 pathogenic variant.**

CDH1 variants exist across a spectrum of clinical significance, including:

- pathogenic;
- likely pathogenic;
- variant of uncertain significance (VUS);
- likely benign;
- benign.

---

# Knowledge Block 4 — What Types of CDH1 Variants Can Occur?

CDH1 pathogenic variants can include different molecular classes.

Examples include:

- truncating/loss-of-function variants;
- nonsense variants;
- splice-affecting variants;
- selected missense variants with demonstrated functional effects.

The package does not provide an exhaustive variant catalogue.

---

# Knowledge Block 5 — How Is a CDH1 Variant Interpreted?

Variant interpretation considers multiple lines of evidence.

At a high level this can include:

- the type and location of the variant;
- population frequency;
- predicted or demonstrated functional effect;
- segregation/family evidence;
- phenotype;
- other established evidence.

CDH1-specific interpretation criteria have been developed to complement general variant-classification frameworks.

Detailed variant-classification methodology is delegated to the Variant Interpretation branch.

---

# Knowledge Block 6 — What Is a VUS?

A **variant of uncertain significance (VUS)** is a variant for which current evidence is insufficient to classify it confidently as pathogenic or benign.

Therefore:

> **A VUS is not the same as a pathogenic CDH1 variant.**

A VUS should not be treated as proof that a person has HDGC.

---

# Knowledge Block 7 — Does the Exact CDH1 Variant Predict the Exact Cancer Risk?

Not reliably.

Although some genotype–phenotype relationships have been investigated, current evidence does not allow precise prediction of:

- whether a particular carrier will develop cancer;
- which cancer will occur;
- when cancer will occur.

Therefore, a specific CDH1 variant should not be used as a stand-alone individualized risk calculator.

---

# Knowledge Block 8 — What Does a Pathogenic CDH1 Result Mean?

A pathogenic germline CDH1 result indicates inherited cancer susceptibility.

It does **not** by itself mean:

- that cancer is currently present;
- that cancer will definitely develop;
- that the exact age of cancer onset can be predicted.

The result may have implications for gastric-cancer and lobular breast-cancer risk management and for biological relatives.

Detailed management belongs to PP-0149.

---

# Knowledge Block 9 — What If CDH1 Is Found Unexpectedly?

A CDH1 pathogenic variant can sometimes be discovered during multigene testing performed for another cancer history or clinical indication.

Such a result can have implications beyond the original reason for testing and may require appropriate genetic counseling and HDGC-focused evaluation.

---

# Knowledge Block 10 — What Does a CDH1 Result Mean for Family Members?

Because a pathogenic germline CDH1 variant is inherited, it may be relevant to biological relatives.

Cascade testing can help identify relatives who may carry the same familial pathogenic variant.

Detailed family counseling and testing workflows are outside this package.

# Common Misconceptions

### Myth 1
**Any CDH1 variant means HDGC.**

**Fact:** CDH1 variants have different clinical significance. Only appropriately classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic findings indicate inherited susceptibility.

### Myth 2
**A VUS means the person probably has HDGC.**

**Fact:** A VUS is an uncertain result and should not be treated as proof of disease.

### Myth 3
**A pathogenic CDH1 result means the person already has cancer.**

**Fact:** It indicates inherited susceptibility, not a cancer diagnosis.

### Myth 4
**The exact CDH1 variant tells us exactly when cancer will develop.**

**Fact:** Current evidence does not support reliable individualized prediction from genotype alone.

### Myth 5
**Germline CDH1 and tumor CDH1 alterations mean the same thing.**

**Fact:** Germline findings indicate inherited susceptibility; somatic tumor findings answer a different clinical question.

### Myth 6
**A CDH1 pathogenic variant found on a breast-cancer panel is unrelated to gastric cancer.**

**Fact:** An unexpected germline CDH1 pathogenic variant can have implications for HDGC-related gastric risk.

# Key Messages

- CDH1 encodes E-cadherin and is central to HDGC biology.
- A germline CDH1 pathogenic variant indicates inherited cancer susceptibility.
- Not every CDH1 variant is pathogenic.
- VUS should not be treated as pathogenic.
- Different CDH1 variant types can have different evidence profiles.
- Current evidence does not allow precise individual cancer-risk prediction from the exact CDH1 variant alone.
- A pathogenic result is not itself a diagnosis of current cancer.
- Unexpected CDH1 findings may have important hereditary implications.
- Biological relatives may need genetic evaluation.
- Detailed HDGC risk management belongs to PP-0149.
