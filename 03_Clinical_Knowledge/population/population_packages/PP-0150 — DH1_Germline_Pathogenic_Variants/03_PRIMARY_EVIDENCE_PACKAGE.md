# Core Evidence Statements

## 1. CDH1 Gene

CDH1 encodes E-cadherin, a cell-adhesion protein involved in maintaining epithelial organization and tissue integrity.

Germline pathogenic variants in CDH1 are strongly associated with HDGC and increased lobular breast-cancer risk.

---

## 2. Germline CDH1 Pathogenic Variants

A germline pathogenic CDH1 variant is an inherited genetic alteration that increases susceptibility to CDH1-associated cancers.

It is distinct from a somatic tumor alteration.

---

## 3. CDH1 Variant Spectrum

CDH1 sequence variants range from pathogenic to benign.

Reported pathogenic variants include different molecular classes, particularly loss-of-function/truncating variants, while selected missense and splice-affecting variants may require additional evidence for classification.

---

## 4. Pathogenicity

A CDH1 variant should not be called pathogenic simply because:

- it changes the DNA sequence;
- it is rare;
- it is located in a particular part of the gene;
- it is described in a research paper.

Clinical classification requires integrated evidence.

CDH1-specific criteria have been developed to complement general variant-classification frameworks.

---

# Variant Classification Concept

| Category | Patient-Level Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pathogenic | Evidence supports disease-causing effect |
| Likely pathogenic | Strong evidence supports disease-causing effect but not all criteria for pathogenic classification are met |
| VUS | Evidence is insufficient for confident classification |
| Likely benign | Evidence supports a non-disease-causing interpretation |
| Benign | Evidence supports a non-disease-causing interpretation |

This table is educational and does not replace laboratory reporting or expert variant interpretation.

---

# VUS Evidence

A VUS is not equivalent to a pathogenic variant.

CDH1-specific analyses have shown that many missense VUS do not demonstrate the positive association with HDGC-related phenotypes that would be expected for pathogenic variants.

Therefore:

> **A VUS should not be used as proof of HDGC.**

---

# Variant Classes

Evidence includes:

### Truncating / Loss-of-Function

Often associated with loss of functional E-cadherin.

### Nonsense

Can introduce premature termination and contribute to loss of functional protein.

### Splice-Affecting

Can disrupt normal RNA processing; functional evidence may be relevant to classification.

### Missense

Can range from benign to pathogenic. Some require functional or other evidence to establish clinical significance.

The package intentionally avoids a complete mutation catalogue.

---

# Genotype–Phenotype Evidence

Different CDH1 variants have been studied for associations with:

- cancer type;
- age at onset;
- variant location;
- other phenotypic features.

However, available evidence does not reliably support using the exact CDH1 genotype to predict an individual's cancer type, age of onset, or optimal management.

This uncertainty is clinically important.

---

# Cancer-Risk Implications

A pathogenic germline CDH1 variant is associated with increased risk of:

- diffuse gastric cancer;
- invasive lobular breast cancer in women.

The magnitude of risk is substantial at population level but varies between studies and does not function as a precise individual risk calculator.

Detailed risk management is delegated to PP-0149.

---

# Positive Result: What It Does and Does Not Mean

## It means

- inherited cancer susceptibility;
- potential implications for gastric and breast risk management;
- possible implications for biological relatives.

## It does not mean

- cancer is definitely present now;
- cancer will definitely develop;
- exact cancer age can be predicted;
- one genetic result alone determines all clinical management.

---

# Unexpected CDH1 Findings

CDH1 pathogenic variants may be identified unexpectedly during multigene testing for another clinical indication.

An unexpected pathogenic finding can reveal hereditary cancer susceptibility that was not the original testing question.

This may trigger:

- appropriate genetic counseling;
- HDGC-focused evaluation;
- assessment of relevant cancer risks;
- consideration of testing for relatives.

Detailed incidental/secondary findings architecture remains outside this package unless separately commissioned.

---

# Family / Cascade Evidence

A germline pathogenic variant can be transmitted within a family.

Once a familial CDH1 pathogenic variant is established, targeted testing can be considered for appropriate relatives.

The purpose is to identify relatives who may benefit from hereditary cancer risk assessment.

Detailed cascade-testing workflows are delegated to the genetic counseling/testing architecture.

# Evidence Gaps

The source materials do not support:

- exact variant-specific individual cancer risk;
- reliable genotype-based prediction of cancer onset;
- universal management based on variant location;
- complete classification of every possible CDH1 variant;
- detailed laboratory/NGS methods;
- a comprehensive CDH1 mutation catalogue for patient education.

These gaps are intentionally preserved.
