# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is an MRI scan and why is it used during cancer care?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)**, including what MRI is, why it is used in selected situations during cancer care, why it does not use ionizing radiation, how it complements other imaging methods, and why MRI findings are interpreted together with the complete clinical picture.

This Population Package introduces the purpose of MRI without discussing MRI physics, pulse sequences, safety precautions, contraindications, examination protocols, or image interpretation.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of MRI
- MRI uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves
- MRI does not use X-rays
- Role of MRI in cancer diagnosis, evaluation and treatment monitoring
- Basic introduction to MRI contrast agents
- MRI as one component of comprehensive clinical assessment

---

## Excluded

- MRI physics
- MRI pulse sequences
- MRI protocols
- MRI safety
- MRI contraindications
- MRI preparation
- MRI examination procedure
- MRI report interpretation
- Comparison between MRI, CT and PET/CT
- Treatment decisions based on MRI

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- MRI is a major imaging modality in oncology.
- MRI produces detailed images without using X-rays.
- MRI is selected when it best answers the clinical question.
- Some MRI examinations require contrast enhancement.
- MRI findings contribute to, but do not independently determine, clinical decision-making.

---

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- MRI technology using magnetic fields and radio waves;
- absence of ionizing radiation;
- MRI's role in cancer evaluation;
- individualized selection of MRI according to clinical need.

Differences between references primarily concern disease-specific imaging protocols, technical MRI sequences and imaging indications, which remain intentionally outside the scope of this Population Package.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:

- educational principles from NCI PDQ and ACS;
- imaging recommendations consistent with NCCN, JNCCN and ESMO;
- general radiology education principles from ACR and RSNA.

---

# Evidence Gaps

This Population Package intentionally does **not** discuss:

- MRI pulse sequences;
- MRI safety;
- MRI contraindications;
- MRI preparation;
- MRI report interpretation;
- MRI versus CT;
- MRI versus PET/CT.

These subjects require independent Population Packages.
