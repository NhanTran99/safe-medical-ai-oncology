## Educational Objectives

- Explain what CLDN18.2 is at a level sufficient to understand why it can be a therapeutic target.
- Explain why CLDN18.2 testing is relevant before considering CLDN18.2-targeted treatment.
- Explain the treatment context supported by the project Source Materials: HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive, unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma.
- Explain the role of zolbetuximab as the established CLDN18.2-targeted therapy supported by the supplied evidence.
- Explain why zolbetuximab is used with fluoropyrimidine/platinum-based chemotherapy in the established first-line paradigm.
- Describe the clinical evidence from the SPOTLIGHT and GLOW phase III trials without converting population-level results into individualized predictions.
- Distinguish biomarker eligibility from guaranteed treatment response.
- Recognize major treatment burdens, especially nausea and vomiting, and understand why treatment monitoring matters.
- Understand the boundaries between CLDN18.2 testing, targeted-therapy selection, drug-specific management, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and resistance.
- Identify useful questions to discuss with the cancer care team.

## Clinical Knowledge Blocks

### 1. What is CLDN18.2?

CLDN18.2 is a form of the claudin-18 protein associated with gastric epithelial tissue.

The NCCN source describes CLDN18.2 as a tight-junction transmembrane protein normally expressed in gastric epithelium and notes that tumor biology can make the protein more accessible as a therapeutic target.

For this Population Package, the biology is intentionally limited to what is needed to understand treatment relevance.

### 2. Why can CLDN18.2 become a treatment target?

A targeted therapy aims at a defined molecular feature rather than treating every cancer cell through the same mechanism.

The project evidence identifies CLDN18.2 as a target for zolbetuximab.

The clinical value of the target is therefore linked to a validated biomarker result and to the disease setting in which the therapy has been studied.

### 3. Why is testing important before treatment?

NCCN recommends CLDN18.2 assessment in untreated patients with unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma when zolbetuximab is being considered.

Testing is an upstream knowledge node; this PP does not reproduce the technical IHC/scoring rules owned by the dedicated CLDN18.2 testing packages.

A positive biomarker result is a prerequisite for the treatment pathway but is not by itself a guarantee that treatment will be chosen or will work.

### 4. What treatment context is supported?

The 2026 NCCN guideline identifies fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy plus oxaliplatin and zolbetuximab as a category 1 preferred first-line option for patients with unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic gastric cancer whose tumors are HER2-negative and CLDN18.2-positive.

The NCI PDQ similarly lists zolbetuximab plus CAPOX or mFOLFOX6 for CLDN18.2-positive tumors in the first-line palliative systemic-treatment context.

The treatment context is therefore specific; it should not be generalized to every stage of gastric cancer.

### 5. What is zolbetuximab?

Zolbetuximab is a monoclonal antibody targeting CLDN18.2.

The NCI drug list includes zolbetuximab-clzb and Vyloy (zolbetuximab) among drugs approved for stomach cancer.

The ACS describes zolbetuximab as an intravenous targeted drug used with chemotherapy in the relevant HER2-negative/CLDN18.2-positive advanced setting.

### 6. Why is chemotherapy part of the established regimen?

The established clinical evidence is not a targeted-agent-alone paradigm.

SPOTLIGHT evaluated zolbetuximab with mFOLFOX6.

GLOW evaluated zolbetuximab with CAPOX.

Therefore the patient-facing explanation should not imply that targeted therapy means chemotherapy is unnecessary.

### 7. What did SPOTLIGHT show?

SPOTLIGHT enrolled 565 treatment-naive patients with HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic gastric/EGJ tumors.

The experimental group received zolbetuximab plus mFOLFOX6; the control group received placebo plus mFOLFOX6.

Median PFS was 10.61 months with zolbetuximab versus 8.67 months with placebo; HR for progression or death was 0.75.

Median OS was 18.23 months versus 15.54 months; HR for death was 0.75.

ORR and median duration of response were similar between groups in the NCCN discussion.

### 8. What did GLOW show?

GLOW enrolled 507 untreated patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive gastric or EGJ adenocarcinoma.

The experimental group received zolbetuximab plus CAPOX; the control group received placebo plus CAPOX.

Median PFS was 8.21 months versus 6.80 months; HR was 0.687.

Median OS was 14.39 months versus 12.16 months; HR was 0.771.

The trial therefore provides independent phase III support for a zolbetuximab-containing first-line strategy in the biomarker-defined population.

### 9. What do these results mean for a patient?

The trials show that, in the studied population, adding zolbetuximab to the chemotherapy backbone improved time-to-progression and overall survival compared with chemotherapy alone.

Trial medians describe groups of patients; they do not predict an individual's exact survival time.

A biomarker result identifies a treatment-relevant population, but treatment choice still depends on disease status, health, prior therapy, other biomarkers, preferences, and the oncology team's assessment.

### 10. What are the important treatment burdens?

NCCN identifies nausea and vomiting as particularly prominent adverse events with zolbetuximab-containing treatment.

Grade 3 or higher events in the cited evidence include anemia and neutropenia; decreased appetite was also observed in SPOTLIGHT.

ACS lists nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fatigue, decreased appetite, abdominal pain, weight loss, constipation, and sensory neuropathy among common effects.

ACS also describes low blood cell counts, abnormal liver tests, and electrolyte abnormalities as potentially serious effects.

This PP provides awareness rather than a toxicity-management algorithm.

### 11. What does progression mean?

Not every patient responds, and a cancer can progress despite treatment.

If disease progression occurs, the treatment plan needs reassessment.

This PP does not define a detailed post-progression algorithm or resistance-guided sequence.

### 12. What is the key patient message?

CLDN18.2-targeted therapy is a biomarker-directed treatment strategy, not simply a drug given because a cancer is gastric.

The strongest project-supported clinical paradigm is HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive, unresectable locally advanced/recurrent/metastatic disease treated in the first-line setting with zolbetuximab plus fluoropyrimidine/platinum-based chemotherapy.

The decision remains individualized and should be made with the cancer care team.

## Common Misconceptions

### CLDN18.2-positive means zolbetuximab is automatically required.

False. The result identifies a treatment-relevant population; treatment choice depends on disease setting, other biomarkers, clinical fitness, prior treatment, preferences, and the care team's assessment.

### Targeted therapy means chemotherapy is not needed.

False for the established zolbetuximab evidence summarized here. The pivotal trials combined zolbetuximab with chemotherapy.

### A positive CLDN18.2 test guarantees response.

False. Biomarker positivity does not guarantee individual benefit.

### CLDN18.2-targeted therapy is for every stage of gastric cancer.

False. The strongest supplied evidence is for unresectable locally advanced, recurrent, or metastatic disease in the specified first-line context.

### HER2 status does not matter if CLDN18.2 is positive.

False. The NCCN treatment pathway described here is specifically for HER2-negative and CLDN18.2-positive tumors.

### Targeted therapy has no serious side effects.

False. The supplied sources describe clinically important gastrointestinal, hematologic, hepatic, and electrolyte adverse effects.

### Trial median survival is an individual's expected survival.

False. Trial medians describe groups and cannot predict an individual patient's exact outcome.

## Extended Clinical Explanation

### A. The treatment pathway in one sentence

CLDN18.2-targeted therapy is a biomarker-directed treatment approach in which a clinically relevant CLDN18.2-positive gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma is considered for a CLDN18.2-targeted agent, with the current project evidence centered on zolbetuximab combined with fluoropyrimidine/platinum chemotherapy in the first-line advanced/unresectable/recurrent/metastatic setting.

### B. The pathway as a sequence

**Gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma**

↓

**Disease extent and treatment setting established**

↓

**Relevant biomarker testing performed**

↓

**CLDN18.2 status established**

↓

**HER2 status and other treatment-relevant biomarkers considered**

↓

**If the clinical setting and biomarker profile fit the evidence-supported pathway**

↓

**Zolbetuximab-containing treatment may be considered**

↓

**Zolbetuximab + chemotherapy**

↓

**Treatment response and tolerability assessed**

↓

**Treatment continued, modified, or changed according to the clinical situation**

This sequence is educational. It is not a patient-specific prescription algorithm.

### C. Why the word “targeted” matters

Targeted therapy is called “targeted” because the treatment is designed to interact with a particular biological feature. In PP-0211 that feature is CLDN18.2.

However, “targeted” does not mean:

- the treatment affects only cancer cells;
- the treatment has no side effects;
- the treatment works in every biomarker-positive tumor;
- chemotherapy is unnecessary;
- treatment is appropriate regardless of disease stage or patient health.

The project sources support a more careful interpretation: the biomarker helps identify a population in which a targeted treatment has demonstrated benefit.

### D. Why the biomarker is clinically important

The clinical importance of CLDN18.2 is not simply that the protein can be measured.

The important chain is:

**measurable tumor feature**

→

**validated clinical definition**

→

**treatment-relevant population**

→

**therapy with clinical evidence**

The last step is what distinguishes a treatment-relevant biomarker from a merely interesting molecular finding.

### E. Why HER2-negative status is preserved

The NCCN v2.2026 treatment pathway specifically places the preferred zolbetuximab-containing regimen under the HER2-negative branch.

Therefore the PP should not teach:

> “CLDN18.2-positive automatically means zolbetuximab.”

The more accurate conceptual statement is:

> “In the evidence-supported advanced gastric/EGJ setting, HER2-negative and CLDN18.2-positive disease can identify a population in which zolbetuximab-containing first-line treatment is an established option.”

This wording protects against overgeneralization.

### F. Why disease setting is preserved

The project evidence is concentrated in:

- unresectable locally advanced disease;
- recurrent disease;
- metastatic disease.

The package therefore does not extrapolate the same treatment evidence to:

- early gastric cancer;
- endoscopically resectable early cancer;
- routinely resectable localized cancer;
- perioperative therapy;
- neoadjuvant therapy;
- adjuvant therapy.

A biomarker may remain biologically relevant across stages, but evidence for a treatment must be established in the specific population before the treatment claim is generalized.

## Extended Patient Explanation

### If a patient asks: “What is CLDN18.2?”

A patient-facing answer can be:

> CLDN18.2 is a protein found in cells of the stomach lining. Some stomach cancers have a substantial amount of CLDN18.2 on their surface. Because it can be present on cancer cells, it can serve as a target for a medicine such as zolbetuximab.

The answer should then explain that testing and treatment context matter.

### If a patient asks: “Why did my doctor order CLDN18.2 testing?”

The educational answer is:

> The test can help determine whether a treatment pathway directed at CLDN18.2 may be relevant. The meaning of the result depends on your cancer type, stage, other biomarkers, and the treatments being considered.

### If a patient asks: “My result is positive. Does that mean I will receive zolbetuximab?”

The safe answer is:

> Not automatically. The result is one part of treatment selection. The established evidence summarized here concerns a particular disease setting, including HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive advanced or unresectable gastric/EGJ adenocarcinoma. Your oncology team also considers your overall health, disease extent, previous treatments, other biomarkers, treatment goals, and potential risks.

### If a patient asks: “Does zolbetuximab replace chemotherapy?”

The answer is:

> No. The main clinical trials that established zolbetuximab benefit studied it together with chemotherapy. The combination, rather than zolbetuximab alone, is the treatment paradigm emphasized in this package.

### If a patient asks: “Will it cure my cancer?”

The answer is:

> A treatment can be very useful without guaranteeing cure. The trials show improved outcomes for groups of patients with the relevant disease and biomarker profile. They cannot predict exactly what will happen to one individual.

## Clinical Context Blocks

### Block 1 — Biomarker status is necessary but not sufficient

A biomarker result should be understood as a decision input.

It is not equivalent to a treatment order.

The relevant clinical context includes:

- tumor type;
- disease extent;
- resectability;
- recurrence/metastatic status;
- HER2 status;
- other clinically relevant biomarkers;
- prior therapy;
- performance status and overall health;
- treatment goals;
- potential treatment burden;
- patient preferences.

### Block 2 — Treatment evidence is population-specific

SPOTLIGHT and GLOW enrolled defined patient populations.

The fact that both trials support zolbetuximab-containing therapy strengthens the evidence for the studied population.

It does not establish that:

- every CLDN18.2-positive tumor behaves identically;
- every disease stage benefits;
- every patient tolerates treatment similarly;
- the same treatment sequence is correct after progression.

### Block 3 — Combination therapy is part of the evidence

The treatment should be conceptualized as:

**targeted agent + chemotherapy backbone**

rather than:

**targeted agent instead of chemotherapy**.

The chemotherapy backbone differed between the two pivotal trials:

- mFOLFOX6 in SPOTLIGHT;
- CAPOX in GLOW.

The PP intentionally describes these at the evidence level and delegates regimen management.

### Block 4 — Trial endpoints are different from patient goals

PFS asks about the time until disease progression or death in the trial population.

OS asks about survival in the trial population.

ORR describes tumor responses according to the trial's response assessment framework.

DOR describes how long responses lasted.

These endpoints are useful for judging a treatment, but they do not tell an individual patient exactly what will happen.

## SPOTLIGHT Evidence Interpretation

### Study population

The NCCN v2.2026 summary describes 565 treatment-naive patients with:

- HER2-negative disease;
- CLDN18.2-positive disease;
- unresectable locally advanced or metastatic gastric/EGJ tumors.

### Intervention

Zolbetuximab + mFOLFOX6.

### Comparator

Placebo + mFOLFOX6.

### PFS

Median PFS:

- zolbetuximab: 10.61 months;
- placebo: 8.67 months.

Reported HR:

- 0.75.

### OS

Median OS:

- zolbetuximab: 18.23 months;
- placebo: 15.54 months.

Reported HR:

- 0.75.

### Interpretation

The trial supports improved disease-control duration and overall survival in the studied population.

It does not support a statement such as:

> “A CLDN18.2-positive patient will live 18.23 months.”

That would incorrectly transform a trial median into individualized prognosis.

## Why Two Trials Matter

SPOTLIGHT and GLOW are important together because they are not simply duplicate descriptions.

They used different chemotherapy backbones:

- SPOTLIGHT: mFOLFOX6;
- GLOW: CAPOX.

Both evaluated zolbetuximab against placebo in CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced gastric/EGJ disease.

Together they strengthen the evidence that the observed benefit is associated with the CLDN18.2-targeted strategy rather than being explained only by one specific chemotherapy backbone.

## Safety Explanation

### Nausea and vomiting

These are particularly important because the NCCN discussion identifies them as prominent treatment-emergent adverse events.

A patient should understand:

- these symptoms can be treatment-related;
- they can affect nutrition and daily functioning;
- the oncology team has supportive strategies;
- severe or persistent symptoms should be discussed promptly.

This package does not prescribe antiemetic regimens.

### Hematologic effects

The NCCN summary identifies anemia and neutropenia among grade 3 or higher adverse events in GLOW.

The patient-facing implication is:

> blood counts may need monitoring during combination treatment.

Detailed thresholds and management are delegated.

### Appetite and weight

Decreased appetite was observed in SPOTLIGHT, while ACS describes decreased appetite and weight loss among common effects.

This matters because gastric-cancer patients can already be vulnerable to nutritional problems.

The package therefore treats nutritional burden as a clinically meaningful patient-facing issue without replacing the dedicated nutrition/supportive-care pathway.

### Liver and electrolyte abnormalities

ACS describes abnormal liver tests and changes in electrolytes among potentially serious effects.

The package does not provide laboratory thresholds.

It does emphasize:

> abnormal test results may require clinical assessment and management by the treating team.

## What PP-0211 Should Never Imply

It should never imply:

1. CLDN18.2 positivity alone determines the treatment plan.
2. Zolbetuximab is appropriate for every gastric-cancer stage.
3. Zolbetuximab is a chemotherapy substitute.
4. Zolbetuximab is free of meaningful toxicity.
5. Trial medians predict an individual's survival.
6. SPOTLIGHT and GLOW prove benefit for every possible CLDN18.2-positive population.
7. A negative CLDN18.2 result means the cancer has no other treatment options.
8. Progression after zolbetuximab has one universally correct next treatment.
9. Testing methodology can be inferred from the treatment package.
10. A patient can determine treatment eligibility without the complete clinical context.

## Extended Clinical Reasoning Blocks

### Block 13 — Why testing and therapy are separate knowledge steps

A patient may encounter two apparently similar questions:

> “What does my CLDN18.2 test say?”

and

> “What does that result mean for treatment?”

These are different questions.

The first belongs to the testing/interpretation layer.

The second belongs to the treatment-application layer.

PP-0211 is the second layer.

This separation prevents the package from repeating laboratory scoring rules while still explaining why the result matters clinically.

### Block 14 — Why the same biomarker can have different clinical meaning

A biomarker does not operate independently of:

- disease type;
- stage;
- resectability;
- treatment line;
- prior therapy;
- coexisting biomarkers;
- clinical fitness;
- evidence base.

Therefore the same molecular label should not be interpreted as carrying one universal treatment instruction across all gastric-cancer settings.

### Block 15 — Why “HER2-negative” is not a minor footnote

The project evidence places the zolbetuximab-containing preferred regimen under the HER2-negative branch.

This is clinically meaningful because gastric cancers can have multiple treatment-relevant biomarkers.

A patient may therefore need more than one biomarker result before a systemic-treatment pathway is clear.

PP-0211 does not own the detailed HER2 testing package.

It preserves HER2-negative status because removing that qualifier would change the meaning of the evidence.

### Block 16 — Why treatment line matters

SPOTLIGHT and GLOW studied patients before systemic treatment in the relevant advanced setting.

Therefore the evidence is strongest as first-line evidence.

The package does not silently convert first-line evidence into a claim that the same regimen should be used:

- after multiple lines of therapy;
- after progression on other targeted agents;
- after prior zolbetuximab;
- in the adjuvant setting.

### Block 17 — Why gastric and EGJ disease are retained

The pivotal evidence summarized by NCCN includes gastric and EGJ adenocarcinoma populations.

Therefore EGJ disease is retained where supported by the source.

The package does not broaden this to every esophageal cancer.

### Block 18 — Why patient preferences remain part of the treatment context

NCI patient-facing treatment material emphasizes that treatment plans are developed with the cancer care team and consider:

- the cancer;
- stage;
- overall health;
- treatment goals;
- possible side effects;
- patient preferences.

A biomarker can make a treatment relevant without making the treatment mandatory.

### Block 19 — Why treatment benefit and treatment burden must be taught together

A patient-facing targeted-therapy package should not present efficacy without safety.

The evidence shows benefit.

The same sources show clinically meaningful adverse effects.

Therefore the package deliberately presents:

**potential benefit**

and

**potential burden**

as two parts of the same treatment decision.

### Block 20 — Why the PP does not contain dosing

Dosing is a drug-management question.

It depends on:

- product information;
- treatment schedule;
- combination regimen;
- clinical circumstances;
- toxicity;
- dose modifications.

Because PP-0211 is a modality-level package, detailed dosing would create overlap with the drug-specific node and could become unsafe if separated from prescribing context.

### Block 21 — Why trial evidence is summarized rather than reproduced

The purpose of PP-0211 is to explain the clinical meaning of the evidence.

It therefore extracts:

- population;
- intervention;
- comparator;
- major efficacy endpoints;
- principal safety signal;
- interpretation.

It does not reproduce an entire trial publication.

This preserves evidence traceability while maintaining patient-centered educational focus.

### Block 22 — Why two chemotherapy backbones are shown

SPOTLIGHT and GLOW provide evidence with different backbones.

Showing both helps the reader understand that the targeted therapy was evaluated in more than one chemotherapy context.

It does not mean every chemotherapy backbone has equivalent evidence.

### Block 23 — Why response and progression are handled carefully

A patient may ask:

> “If the scan shows the tumor is smaller, does that mean the treatment worked?”

Response is one way treatment effect can be assessed.

However, the pivotal evidence also evaluates PFS and OS.

PP-0211 therefore avoids defining treatment success using a single patient-level concept.

Formal imaging-response assessment is delegated.

### Block 24 — Why a negative CLDN18.2 result is not a “nothing can be done” result

This package is about one treatment pathway.

A negative result means that the CLDN18.2-directed pathway described here may not apply under the relevant testing framework.

It does not mean:

- no treatment exists;
- no other biomarker matters;
- chemotherapy cannot be used;
- immunotherapy cannot be relevant;
- clinical trials are impossible.

Other treatment packages retain ownership of those branches.

### Block 25 — Why a patient may hear “targeted therapy” and “immunotherapy” together

Both are systemic anticancer treatment modalities, but they are conceptually different.

Targeted therapy in this package is directed at CLDN18.2.

Immunotherapy uses a different therapeutic principle.

The project architecture therefore keeps them as separate treatment nodes.

### Block 26 — Why this package is not a complete treatment plan

A complete treatment plan requires integration of:

- pathology;
- staging;
- biomarker results;
- performance status;
- organ function;
- previous treatment;
- goals;
- preferences;
- multidisciplinary assessment.

PP-0211 contributes one part of that knowledge graph.

### Example 1 — Positive biomarker with no treatment decision yet

A patient says:

> “My report says CLDN18.2 positive. Why haven't I been given the drug yet?”

> A positive result can make a CLDN18.2-targeted treatment pathway relevant, but treatment decisions depend on the full clinical situation. The evidence summarized here applies to a defined advanced gastric/EGJ population, particularly HER2-negative disease. Your team also considers your disease extent, health, other biomarkers, prior treatment and treatment goals.

### Example 2 — Patient asks whether treatment is chemotherapy or targeted therapy

Appropriate response:

> It is a targeted-treatment strategy that is used together with chemotherapy in the established evidence. The targeted component is zolbetuximab, which targets CLDN18.2.

### Example 3 — Patient asks why two drugs are needed

Appropriate response:

> The pivotal studies did not test zolbetuximab as a replacement for chemotherapy. They tested adding zolbetuximab to a chemotherapy backbone. That combination is what established the treatment benefit.

### Example 4 — Patient asks about nausea

Appropriate response:

> Nausea and vomiting are important adverse effects reported with zolbetuximab-containing treatment. Your oncology team can explain how they will monitor and manage these symptoms. This package does not replace the treatment team's symptom-management instructions.

### Example 5 — Patient asks about prognosis

Appropriate response:

> The clinical trials provide group-level results, including median progression-free and overall survival. Those numbers cannot be used by themselves to predict exactly how long one individual will live.

### Example 6 — Patient asks about recurrence after treatment

Appropriate response:

> If the cancer progresses, your team will reassess the situation and consider the treatment options that are appropriate at that time. This package does not define a single universal post-progression sequence.
