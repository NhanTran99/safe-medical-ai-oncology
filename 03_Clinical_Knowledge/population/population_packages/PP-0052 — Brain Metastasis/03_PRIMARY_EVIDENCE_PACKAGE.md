# Clinical Question

**Primary Educational Question**

> **What is brain metastasis?**

---

# Educational Intent

Provide patients and caregivers with an accurate, evidence-based explanation of **brain metastasis**, including what the brain is, how gastric cancer can spread to the brain, why brain metastasis remains gastric cancer rather than primary brain cancer, and why appropriate medical evaluation is required before confirming brain metastasis.

This Population Package introduces the concept of brain metastasis without discussing neuroimaging, biopsy, neurosurgery, radiation therapy, systemic treatment or prognosis.

---

# Scope

## Included

- Definition of the brain
- Definition of brain metastasis
- Relationship between gastric cancer and the brain
- Difference between primary brain cancer and brain metastasis
- Recognition that brain metastasis is possible but relatively uncommon
- Relationship between brain metastasis and metastatic gastric cancer
- Importance of appropriate medical evaluation

---

## Excluded

- Primary brain tumors
- Glioblastoma
- Meningioma
- Brain MRI
- Brain CT
- Lumbar puncture
- Brain biopsy
- Neurosurgery
- Stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS)
- Whole-brain radiation therapy (WBRT)
- Systemic therapy
- M1 classification
- Molecular mechanisms of brain metastasis
- Stage-specific prognosis

These topics require dedicated Population Packages.

# Clinical Claims Summary

The evidence consistently supports the following educational messages:

- The brain controls many essential body functions and may occasionally become involved when gastric cancer spreads.
- Brain metastasis means gastric cancer has spread to the brain.
- Brain metastasis remains gastric cancer because it originated in the stomach.
- Brain metastasis should not be confused with primary brain cancer.
- Brain metastasis is possible but relatively uncommon compared with several other metastatic sites in gastric cancer.
- Doctors determine whether brain metastasis is present through appropriate clinical assessment.

# Evidence Consistency Review

No clinically meaningful disagreement was identified among the primary references.

The evidence is highly consistent regarding:

- brain metastasis as a possible manifestation of distant metastatic gastric cancer;

Differences between references are limited to terminology and educational emphasis.

The Clinical Knowledge Object follows:
