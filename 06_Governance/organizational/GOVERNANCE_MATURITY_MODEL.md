# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the Governance Maturity Model of the Safe Medical AI System.

The Governance Maturity Model establishes a standardized framework for evaluating the maturity of organizational governance capabilities across the complete system lifecycle while preserving governance integrity, strategic planning, and technology independence.

---

# 2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The Governance Maturity Model follows the principles of:

- Organizational governance maturity
- Strategic organizational learning
- Continuous governance evolution
- Objective assessment
- Long-term planning
- Technology independence

---

# 3. ROLE

The Governance Maturity Model is responsible for:

- assessing governance capability
- evaluating organizational maturity
- identifying governance gaps
- supporting long-term planning
- generating improvement roadmaps
- supporting executive governance

The model is not responsible for:

- runtime operations
- release approval
- incident management
- implementation activities

---

# 4. MATURITY PHILOSOPHY

Governance Maturity evaluates organizational governance capability.

The objective is to understand how effectively governance processes are institutionalized, standardized, measured, and continuously improved.

Maturity assessment evaluates the governance system rather than individual software versions.

---

# 5. MATURITY LEVELS

The standardized maturity levels are:

Level 1 — Initial

Level 2 — Managed

Level 3 — Standardized

Level 4 — Measured

Level 5 — Optimizing

The maturity model remains independent of external maturity frameworks while supporting future mapping if required.

---

# 6. ASSESSMENT DOMAINS

Governance Maturity evaluates:

- Foundation
- Architecture
- Knowledge Governance
- Runtime Governance
- Operational Governance
- Organizational Governance

Additional domains may be introduced through governed amendments.

---

# 7. ASSESSMENT CRITERIA

Every domain shall be evaluated using standardized criteria.

Minimum criteria include:

- Completeness
- Standardization
- Traceability
- Repeatability
- Measurability
- Continuous Improvement

Assessment criteria shall remain objective and reproducible.

---

# 8. MATURITY ASSESSMENT RECORD

Every Governance Maturity Assessment shall generate a standardized Assessment Record.

Minimum metadata include:

- Assessment Identifier
- Assessment Date
- Assessor
- Assessment Scope
- Maturity Level
- Findings

Additional metadata may be introduced through governed amendments.

---

# 9. IMPROVEMENT ROADMAP

Every assessment shall generate a governed Improvement Roadmap.

Minimum roadmap components include:

- Current Maturity Level
- Target Maturity Level
- Required Improvements
- Expected Timeline

The roadmap supports strategic governance planning.

---

# 10. ASSESSMENT INDEPENDENCE

Governance Maturity Assessment remains independent from:

- Release Approval
- Incident Management
- Monitoring
- Runtime Operations

The assessment focuses on long-term organizational capability rather than operational performance.

---

# 11. PERIODIC ASSESSMENT

Governance Maturity shall be evaluated periodically.

Assessment schedules remain independent from software release cycles.

Periodic assessment supports longitudinal organizational development.

---

# 12. ORGANIZATIONAL BENCHMARKING

The Governance Maturity Model supports benchmarking across:

- organizational development phases
- system generations
- governance programs
- development teams (where applicable)

The model shall not be used to benchmark individual personnel.

---

# 13. STRATEGIC GOVERNANCE

Governance Maturity supports:

- Strategic Planning
- Resource Allocation
- Investment Prioritization
- Long-term Governance Roadmapping

Final strategic decisions remain the responsibility of Governance Authority.

---

# 14. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

The Governance Maturity Model follows:

- Governance-first assessment
- Organizational learning
- Strategic planning
- Objective evaluation
- Traceability
- Continuous improvement
- Technology independence
