# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the Regulatory Readiness Framework of the Safe Medical AI System.

The framework establishes organizational preparedness for future regulatory adaptation while preserving governance integrity, patient safety, quality management, traceability, and technology independence.

The framework is intentionally independent from any specific regulatory jurisdiction or certification scheme.

---

# 2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Regulatory Readiness follows the principles of:

- Architecture before compliance
- Governance-first readiness
- Organizational preparedness
- Regulatory adaptability
- Future-proof governance
- Technology independence

---

# 3. ROLE

The Regulatory Readiness Framework is responsible for:

- governing regulatory preparedness
- assessing organizational readiness
- supporting regulatory planning
- coordinating readiness assessments
- supporting regulatory gap analysis
- preserving regulatory traceability

The framework is not responsible for:

- regulatory compliance certification
- legal interpretation
- regulatory approval
- regulatory affairs execution

---

# 4. REGULATORY READINESS PHILOSOPHY

Regulatory Readiness represents organizational preparedness rather than regulatory compliance.

Its objective is to ensure that governance architecture can be efficiently mapped to future regulatory requirements without redesigning the system architecture.

---

# 5. REGULATORY SCOPE

Regulatory Readiness supports future adaptation for domains including:

- Medical AI Governance
- Medical Device Software
- Clinical Decision Support
- AI Quality Systems
- Data Governance

The framework does not claim compliance with any specific regulatory requirement.

---

# 6. REGULATORY DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Regulatory Readiness is based on standardized governance principles.

Core principles include:

- Traceability
- Explainability
- Accountability
- Risk Management
- Quality Management
- Documentation Completeness

These principles remain regulation-independent.

---

# 7. REGULATORY MAPPING

Regulatory Mapping is governed independently.

The Regulatory Readiness Framework defines the capability to support future mapping but does not perform or maintain mappings to individual regulations.

Mappings may subsequently be developed for specific regulatory frameworks through separate governed documentation.

---

# 8. REGULATORY EVIDENCE

Regulatory Readiness utilizes existing governed organizational evidence.

Illustrative evidence sources include:

- Governance Records
- Quality Records
- Audit Records
- Risk Records
- Release Records

Dedicated regulatory evidence repositories are not required.

---

# 9. REGULATORY READINESS ASSESSMENT

Periodic Regulatory Readiness Assessments shall evaluate:

- Documentation Readiness
- Traceability Readiness
- Risk Governance
- Quality Governance
- Organizational Governance

Assessment results support strategic planning rather than certification.

---

# 10. REGULATORY GAP ANALYSIS

Regulatory Gap Analysis identifies organizational gaps between the current governance architecture and future regulatory objectives.

Gap Analysis shall generate governed Improvement Proposals.

Gap Analysis shall not directly modify governance frameworks.

---

# 11. REGULATORY INDEPENDENCE

The Regulatory Readiness Framework remains independent from:

- jurisdictions
- regulatory authorities
- certification bodies
- individual regulations

Regulatory independence preserves long-term architectural stability.

---

# 12. CONTINUOUS REGULATORY READINESS

Regulatory Readiness evolves through:

- Continuous Improvement
- Risk Management
- Audit
- Governance Review

Readiness improvements shall follow governed organizational processes.

---

# 13. REGULATORY STRATEGY

The Regulatory Readiness Framework supports:

- Regulatory Planning
- Certification Planning
- Market Expansion Planning
- Organizational Readiness

Final regulatory decisions remain the responsibility of Regulatory Affairs, legal counsel, and Governance Authority.

---

# 14. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

The Regulatory Readiness Framework follows:

- Architecture before compliance
- Governance-first readiness
- Organizational preparedness
- Future adaptability
- Traceability
- Separation of concerns
- Technology independence
